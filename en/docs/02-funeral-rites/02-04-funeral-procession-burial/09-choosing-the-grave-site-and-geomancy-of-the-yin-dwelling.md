# Choosing the Grave Site and Geomancy of the Yin Dwelling

![阴宅堪舆与罗盘 / Geomancy and the luopan compass](../../../../assets/images/09-geomancy-luopan.png)


## 1. Definition

Geomancy of the yin dwelling (*yinzhai kan yu*, "reading the grave", "reading fengshui") is the art of choosing the grave's position and orientation by the traditional geomatic doctrine.

Geomancy has two great schools:

- **The form school** (*xingshi pai*, Jiangxi school): chiefly observes the forms of mountains and rivers, stressing the matching of "dragon, cave, sand, water, orientation" (*long, xue, sha, shui, xiang*), pursuing "sheltering the wind and gathering the qi".
- **The qi school** (*liqi pai*, Fujian school): chiefly the compass's "dividing the metal" (*fenjin*), the eight trigrams and nine stars, and the sexagenary qi, stressing the calculation of "sitting-degree" and "water method".

In practice the two are often combined.

(This entry records folklore and esoteric art, and makes **no scientific claim**; the fengshui idea had a real component of site-selection, planning and environmental experience in traditional society, and must be understood critically as history.)

## 2. Historical Origins

- The fengshui idea took shape in the Han and matured after the Wei and Jin. The **Burial Book** (*Zangshu*, falsely attributed to Guo Pu of the Jin, actually a work of the Tang and after) is the summative text of yin-dwelling theory, proposing core theses such as "burial rides the living qi" and "qi scatters when it meets wind, and stops where it meets water".
- After the Tang and Song geomancy flourished and became a profession ("geography master", "fengshui master").
- The Ming and Qing were the height of grave-reading; books such as the **Five Secrets of Geography** (*Dili Wujue*, by Zhao Jiufeng of the Qing) systematised the operation of "dragon-cave-sand-water-orientation".
- Through the ages there were anti-fengshui voices (e.g. Wang Chong's *Lunheng* of the Eastern Han, critiquing "burial almanacs" and "taboos"), and also the social ill of "keeping the corpse unburied to seek an auspicious site" — delaying burial too long for a site was a serious abuse of the Ming and Qing.
- Contemporary villages still widely read graves; urban cemeteries replace site-reading with planning.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

### Reading the land (form)

1. **Seeking the dragon**: observe the coming and going, rising and falling of the mountain trend (dragon vein) to find where the cave is formed.
2. **Examining the sand**: observe the sand hills around the cave (the hills before, behind, left and right), stressing the configuration of the four spirit-sands "Azure Dragon, White Tiger, Vermilion Bird, Black Tortoise".
3. **Observing the water**: observe the water's direction and gathering/scattering, stressing "where water gathers, qi gathers" and "water embracing is auspicious".
4. **Fixing the cave**: at the formed cave determine the specific spot for lowering the coffin.
5. **Setting the orientation**: fix the grave's sitting and facing (see "Fixing the Apex, Orienting the Grave and the Compass Divisions").

### Avoidances (the "ten non-burials", etc.)

Common avoidance principles (slightly different across books):

1. Not on land rushed by a swift current;
2. Not on a lone peak with no shelter;
3. Not on barren land, bare of grass, with exposed gravel;
4. Not with no support behind the cave (no backing mountain);
5. Not with no bright hall before the cave (no open ground);
6. Not where water rushes straight or leaps back ("water leaves without looking back");
7. Not on old battlefields, execution grounds or slaughterhouses;
8. Not on the foundation of an old shrine or abandoned temple;
9. Not on muddy, waterlogged, rotting-vegetation land;
10. Not on a path trampled by people and beasts.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Jiangxi, Fujian**: the strongest geomancy tradition, birthplace of the form school (Gan) and the qi school (Min), with many practitioners.
- **Guangdong, coastal Fujian**: stress the "water method" and "orientation", valuing facing and escort.
- **North China**: stress "backed by a mountain, open before", with simpler site-reading.
- **Ethnic minorities**: the Tibetan choose a sky-burial platform with religious considerations (e.g. facing a sacred mountain), without fengshui calculation; the Yi and others have their own site-choice (by divination); the Miao choose grave land by clan rule and divination. Each people's site logic differs and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Compass (luopan)**: the core instrument for fixing sitting and facing, in three-harmony and three-yuan types.
- **Geomancy books**: *Zangshu*, *Dili Wujue*, *Yangzhai Sanyao*, etc.
- **Land deed, earth-purchase contract**: the written proof of the grave site.
- **Grave map in the lineage register**: the map of the family grave site.
