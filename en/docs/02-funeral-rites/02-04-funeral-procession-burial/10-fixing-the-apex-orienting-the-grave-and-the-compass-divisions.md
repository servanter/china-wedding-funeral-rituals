# Fixing the Apex, Orienting the Grave and the Compass Divisions

## 1. Definition

- **Fixing the apex** (*dianxue*): determining the specific spot for lowering the coffin (or ashes) on the chosen auspicious land, called "fixing the apex". A difference of a few feet in the apex's position was traditionally held to mean a wholly different fortune.
- **Setting the orientation** (*lixiang*, *dingxiang*): fixing the coffin's (grave's) facing, mostly expressed as "sitting mountain, facing direction", e.g. "sitting north, facing south" (sitting *zi*, facing *wu*).
- **Dividing the metal** (*fenjin*): dividing the compass's 360° into the twenty-four mountains, each mountain again into five positions (120 *fenjin* in all), to fix a finer sitting-degree.
- **Sitting-degree** (*zuodu*): the concrete angle obtained by dividing the metal, e.g. "sitting *jia*, facing *geng*", "sitting *ren*, facing *bing*", with an error of no more than half a fen (about 3°).

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* ("Jixi li") records the orientation rule of burial ("head to the north"), the earliest ritual requirement of "orientation".
- After the Han geomancy rose, and orientation changed from "ritual rule" to "numerological calculation".
- The Tang's Yang Yunsong (Master Yang) is honoured as ancestor by the form school; his art is said to fix the apex and set the orientation by "dragon-cave-sand-water".
- After the Song the qi school rose, the compass's layers multiplied (the three-harmony board reaching over thirty layers), and "dividing the metal" became a technical term.
- The Ming and Qing stressed "dividing the metal must not err", and gazetteers and notebooks record reburials for a half-degree error in dividing the metal.
- Contemporary grave-reading still uses the compass, but mostly simplifies to "fixing the great orientation"; fine dividing of the metal is mostly not practised.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Locate**: by "fixing the apex" determine the coffin spot on the chosen land (mostly marked with a wooden stake).
2. **Set the board**: lay the compass level before the cave (or in it), taking the horizontal.
3. **Take true north**: with the magnetic needle in the compass's central pool fix south-north, adjusting the disc.
4. **Set the orientation**: fix the sitting mountain and facing direction (e.g. sitting *zi* facing *wu*, i.e. sitting due north facing due south).
5. **Divide the metal**: among the five *fenjin* of the sitting mountain (e.g. *zi* mountain has the five dragons "jia-zi, bing-zi, wu-zi, geng-zi, ren-zi"), choose one combined with the "immortal fate" (the deceased's birth) and the mountain-facing.
6. **Run the line**: fix the orientation with a line, as the basis for lowering the coffin and building the mound.
7. **Re-check**: when lowering the coffin re-check, insisting "the coffin errs not in orientation".
8. **Record**: the sitting and facing are recorded in the lineage grave-map or the grave-site deed.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Fujian-Guangdong**: most insistent on dividing the metal and sitting-degree, with many compass layers and strict trade transmission.
- **Jiangxi**: the form school leads, valuing "fixing the apex" while dividing the metal is relatively simplified.
- **North China**: mostly takes "sitting north facing south" as the general rule, simple, with fine dividing of the metal little practised.
- **Ethnic minorities**: the Tibetan site and facing carry religious meaning (facing a sacred mountain or holy lake); the Yi facing is decided by a *bimo*'s divination; the Mongolian traditionally takes "by the mountain and beside the water" as the site principle. These orientation logics are not part of the geomatic system.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Compass (luopan)**: the core instrument, in three-harmony and three-yuan types.
- **Level, wooden stake, line**: for on-site locating.
- **Lineage grave-map, grave-site deed**: documents recording the orientation.
- **Geomancy books**: *Dili Wujue*, *Luojing Toujie*, etc.
