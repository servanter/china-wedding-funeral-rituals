# Burial: Lowering the Coffin and Raising the Mound

## 1. Definition

Burial (*luozang*, *anzang*) is the rite of placing the coffin in the grave pit and covering it with earth to build the mound. It is the endpoint of the whole funeral, and the realisation of the idea of "rest in peace once in the earth".

The core steps are: **orientation** (fixing the coffin's bearing by compass) → **lowering** (into the pit) → **covering** (filling earth) → **building the mound** (covering earth into a grave hill) → **erecting the stele**.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* ("Jixi li") records the "lowering" (*bian*, i.e. interment) rite: "then they lower it, and the host wails and leaps without count." *Bian* is the ancient name for interment.
- The *Liji* ("Tan'gong") records "to be buried in the north, head to the north, is the universal rite of the three dynasties", fixing the coffin's orientation (head-north being the common practice of the three dynasties).
- The *Liji* ("Wangzhi") records "the commoner suspends and seals, and burial is not stopped for rain" — the commoner lowers the coffin by rope into the pit, and does not stop for rain, showing burial's "cannot be delayed" nature.
- After the Han the burial procedure grew complex, adding the compass-orientation step of geomancy (see "Fixing the Apex, Orienting the Grave and the Compass Divisions").
- From the Tang and Song, "covering earth into a mound" and "erecting a stele" became the norm; the Ming and Qing grave system stressed the matching of "grave head", "grave ring", "tombstone" and "offering table".
- Under the contemporary cremation system, burial becomes "ashes placement" (cemetery, ashes hall), and the traditional earth-covering rite mostly does not apply.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Open the pit**: dig the grave pit in advance (breaking the earth needs an auspicious day; old custom had a "breaking-earth rite"); the pit is called the "golden well".
2. **Warm the pit**: some areas burn paper, place a rooster or five grains in the pit, called "warming" or "towning" the pit.
3. **Orient**: fix the coffin's bearing and sitting-degree by compass (see "Fixing the Apex, Orienting the Grave and the Compass Divisions").
4. **Lower by rope**: lower the coffin slowly into the pit by rope (or pole), avoiding a hard drop or striking the pit wall.
5. **Set the position**: adjust the coffin's bearing to meet the orientation; the chief mourner kneels before the pit and wails and leaps.
6. **Fill earth (cover)**: the chief mourner first casts a handful of earth ("breaking earth", "casting earth"), then the functionaries fill; some scatter five grains on the coffin.
7. **Cover into a mound**: fill to ground level, heap earth into a grave head ("grave head", "grave hill"); some build a stone ring.
8. **Erect the stele**: set the tombstone; its inscription is treated in "Grave Stelae, Epitaphs and Spirit-Way Stelae".
9. **Sacrifice**: set an offering before the grave, burn paper money and paper effigies, kneel and take leave.
10. **Take leave of the grave**: the family returns; old custom "take leave of the grave without turning back" (must not turn to look at the grave).
11. **Third-day return**: three days later "return on the third" (*fusan*, rounding the grave) to tidy the grave head (see "The Third-Day Rite and Rounding the Grave").

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: insists on "deep earth, deep water", with tall grave heads; mostly buries a "five-grain store" in the pit.
- **Jiangnan**: insists "covering earth without showing the coffin", with lower grave heads, often planting pines and cypresses.
- **Fujian-Guangdong**: insists on the completeness of "grave hill" and "grave ring", with high-grade steles, and the custom of "making fengshui".
- **Hakka (secondary-burial areas)**: three years after first burial "pick the bones" (gather bones into an urn), then choose another site for the bone-urn burial — the "secondary burial" system (see "Joint Burial and Reburial").
- **Ethnic minorities**: the modes differ entirely. Tibetan sky-burial (body to vultures, bones to vultures), water burial (casting into water), stupa burial (high lamas), tree burial (some); the Hui and Uyghur are earth-buried but **use no coffin, raise no mound, erect no stele** (the law sets the grave no higher than a hand-span); the Miao and Dong are mostly earth-buried with varied grave forms; some Yao areas have "cliff-coffin burial". "Covering earth into a mound and erecting a stele" is the Han and some-peoples' system, not a universal norm.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Compass**: the instrument for orienting the burial.
- **Rope, pole**: for lowering the coffin into the pit.
- **Five grains, rooster**: to warm or town the pit.
- **Paper money, paper effigies**: burned before the grave.
- **Tombstone, offering table, grave ring**: the grave's composition.
- **Land deed (earth-purchase contract)**: some areas burn a "land-purchase contract" in the grave (see "Land Purchase Deeds and Notices to the Underworld").
- **Grave-site deed (yin-dwelling deed)**: the written proof of the family grave site.
