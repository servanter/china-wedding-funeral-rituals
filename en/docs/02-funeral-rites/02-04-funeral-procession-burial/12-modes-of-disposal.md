# Modes of Disposal: Earth, Fire, Water, Sky, Tree, Cliff and Stupa Burial

## 1. Definition

The mode of disposal (*zangshi*, the form of burial) is the final form of handling the body. This entry compares the principal modes of disposal within China, explaining their geographic distribution, ethnic belonging and conceptual basis.

**Important note**: the mode of disposal is directly bound to ethnicity and religion, and **must not be judged as "advanced vs. backward"**, nor may Han earth-burial be taken as a universal standard.

## 2. The Principal Modes

### 1. Earth burial

- **Distribution**: the Han (except cremation zones), the Hui, Uyghur and most other peoples.
- **Form**: the Han as "coffin burial + earth-covered mound + stele"; the **Hui, Uyghur and other Muslim peoples as "wrapped in white cloth + no coffin + no mound + no stele"**, the grave no higher than a hand-span.
- **Idea**: the Han "rest in peace once in the earth", "hair and skin return to the earth"; the Muslim "from earth, return to earth".

### 2. Cremation

- **Distribution**: historically practised by some Buddhist monks and some southwestern peoples (e.g. the Yi formerly cremated); today, through funeral reform, it is the mainstream in Han areas.
- **Idea**: Buddhism takes "cremation" (*chupi*) as routine, fitting the doctrine of "the four elements dispersing"; today the chief considerations are saving land and hygiene.

### 3. Sky burial

- **Distribution**: the Tibetan, and some Mongolian areas.
- **Form**: the body is carried by the "corpse-bearer" to the sky-burial platform, a lama chants, and vultures (the sacred bird) peck it; the bones are likewise dealt with (pounded with tsampa and fed to the vultures).
- **Idea**: **"offering the body in charity"** — with the last flesh giving alms to all beings, the utmost expression of the Tibetan-Buddhist view of compassion. This mode has strict religious meaning and must not be judged "barbaric".

### 4. Water burial

- **Distribution**: some Tibetan areas (mostly the deceased who died young or of contagion), and some Monba and Lhoba areas.
- **Form**: the body is cast into river or sea.
- **Idea**: mostly the handling of "abnormal death", also holding the idea of "passing away with the water".

### 5. Tree burial

- **Distribution**: some Tibetan areas (infants), some northeastern peoples (e.g. the Evenki formerly had "wind burial"), and some southern peoples.
- **Form**: the body is placed in a tree (or on a frame between trees), to decompose naturally.
- **Idea**: tied to "returning to nature" and a forest-based mode of production; the Tibetan infant tree-burial carries the meaning of "returning early".

### 6. Cliff-coffin burial

- **Distribution**: historically in the Three Gorges, Sichuan, Fujian's Wuyi Mountains, Jiangxi, Guangxi, etc., a mode of the ancient southern peoples (the Liao, the Baiyue).
- **Form**: the coffin is placed in a cliff cave or nailed to the cliff wall.
- **Idea**: variously explained as "rising near heaven", "avoiding beasts and disturbance", "ancestor worship"; scholarship has no final verdict. No longer practised.

### 7. Stupa burial

- **Distribution**: Tibetan-Buddhist and Han-Buddhist high lamas.
- **Form**: the body, after embalming, is placed in a stupa (relic stupa), or cremated and the ashes placed in a stupa.
- **Idea**: the stupa as the abode of the "dharma-body", with religious veneration.

### Others

- **Stupa / niche burial**: exclusive to monks.
- **Secondary burial (bone-picking burial)**: the Han Hakka, some Guangfu areas, and some southern peoples; years after the first burial the bones are picked and placed in an urn for reburial (see "Joint Burial and Reburial").
- **Tree (wind) burial and stone-coffin burial**: practised by some southwestern and ancient peoples.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

The procedures of the different modes differ vastly; this entry only compares them. For concrete rites see the relevant entries: earth burial — "Burial: Lowering the Coffin and Raising the Mound"; cremation — "Modern Funeral Services and Cremation"; sky, water and tree burial — the relevant ethnic-minority entries.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

| Mode | Main peoples | Main distribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Earth (coffin + mound) | Han | Nationwide (mainly rural) |
| Earth (white cloth, no mound) | Hui, Uyghur, Kazakh, etc. | Northwest and Muslim areas nationwide |
| Cremation | Contemporary Han; historically some monks, Yi, etc. | Nationwide (mainly urban) |
| Sky burial | Tibetan, some Mongolian | Qinghai-Tibet Plateau, Inner Mongolia |
| Water burial | Some Tibetan, Monba, Lhoba | Southern Tibet, Yarlung Zangbo basin |
| Tree / wind burial | Evenki, Tibetan (infants), etc. | Northeast, Qinghai-Tibet |
| Cliff-coffin burial | Ancient southern peoples (Liao, Baiyue, etc.) | Three Gorges, Wuyi, Sichuan-Yunnan |
| Stupa burial | Tibetan- and Han-Buddhist high lamas | Tibetan area, Han temples |

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Sky-burial platform, sky-burial master's (corpse-bearer's) implements**: sky burial.
- **Stupa (relic stupa, stupa of the dharma-body)**: stupa burial.
- **Coffin, outer coffin, ashes casket, bone urn (gold-dipper urn)**: earth burial and secondary burial.
- **Ethnographies and ethnological surveys**: the main documentary source.
