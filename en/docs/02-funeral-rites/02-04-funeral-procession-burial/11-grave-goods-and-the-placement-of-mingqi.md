# Grave Goods and the Placement of Mingqi

## 1. Definition

Grave goods (*suizang pin*) are the objects buried with the dead, of two kinds:

- **Mingqi** (spirit objects): objects made specially for burial, not actually usable ("prepared but unusable").
- **Used objects** (*yongqi*, "living objects"): the dead's own possessions in life, buried to "image the living".

The proportion and placement of mingqi and used objects is a double expression of rank and idea within the funeral code.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Liji* ("Tan'gong"): "It is called mingqi because it treats them as spiritual." Again: "Confucius said: he who makes mingqi knows the way of mourning — prepared but unusable." Thus the definition of mingqi was established.
- The *Yili* ("Jixi li") records in detail the kinds and laying-out of grave goods: the *bao* (reed pack for fish and meat), *shao* (vessel for millet), *weng* (jar for relish), *wu* (jar for wine), bows and arrows, the plough (farm tool), armour (war tool), each in its place; the "writing-set" of grave goods had a list-like order of display.
- The bronze ritual vessels and chariot-horse pits of Shang and Zhou tombs, the terracotta armies and jade suits of Qin and Han, the "mingqi spirits-and-demons" of Tang and Song (e.g. pottery figures, tomb-guardian beasts), and the "five-grain store" and "treasure vase" of the Ming and Qing form the long evolution of grave goods.
- After the Han, paper mingqi (paper effigies) rose and real-object burial lessened; after the Tang and Song, "paper effigies" and "real-object burial" went in parallel.
- Under the contemporary cremation system, grave goods are mostly small items in the ashes casket (beloved possessions, photos).

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Prepare objects**: by family means and rank, prepare grave goods in the two classes, mingqi and used objects.
2. **Open a list**: some areas open a list of grave goods (the "dispatch register" *qiance* seen in archaeology is this kind of document).
3. **Lay in the coffin** (see "Encoffining"): in the dead's hands, ingots, copper coins, five-grain bags; beside the body, beloved possessions, comb, mirror, scissors; at the coffin corners, five-grain store, treasure vase.
4. **Lay in the pit** (after the coffin is lowered): before the coffin, pottery and glazed jars ("grain jar"); beside the coffin, a lamp (eternal lamp), mirror, tomb-town objects; at the pit bottom, five grains, copper coins ("pillow money").
5. **Burn paper mingqi**: paper effigies (paper house, paper figures, paper horse, paper chest) are burned before the grave, not buried.
6. **Fill earth**: after the goods are placed, fill the earth.
7. **Sacrifice**: set an offering before the grave, telling the dead the grave goods are ready.
8. **Register**: the grave-goods list is buried with (the "dispatch register") or kept by the family (the "register").

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: stresses "five-grain store", "treasure vase", "pillow money".
- **Jiangnan**: stresses "grain jar", "eternal lamp", "bronze mirror".
- **Fujian-Guangdong**: stresses "gold dipper", "seven-star coins", with especially rich paper mingqi.
- **Hakka (secondary burial)**: the first burial is simple, and grave goods are placed anew when the bones are gathered into the urn (silver ornaments, copper coins).
- **Ethnic minorities**: grave goods reflect each people's belief. The Yi bury farm tools and silver (to "keep labouring in the land of ancestors"); the Miao bury silver (both wealth and ethnic mark); the Tibetan sky-burial places no grave goods; the **Hui and Uyghur, by religious law, place no grave goods at all** (the law holds the dead rely only on good deeds, and grave goods are useless). This is a difference at the doctrinal level, not to be read as "frugality".

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Mingqi**: pottery figures, pottery vessels, tomb-guardian beasts, porcelain jars, grain jars, treasure vases.
- **Used objects**: the dead's possessions in life (comb, mirror, brush, pipe, etc.).
- **Paper mingqi (paper effigies)**: paper house, paper figures, paper horse, paper chest.
- **Five-grain store, five-grain bag, pillow money**.
- **Eternal lamp, bronze mirror**.
- **Dispatch register / grave-goods list**: the document recording the goods (archaeology knows it as the "dispatch register").
