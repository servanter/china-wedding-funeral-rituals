# Soul-Guiding Banners and Scattered Paper Money

## 1. Definition

- **Name banner** (*mingjing*): a flag bearing the deceased's name, rank and age, fixed by the ritual classics, leading the procession.
- **Soul-guiding banner** (*yinlu fan*, also *yinhun fan*, *zhaohun fan*): the flag that leads the dead soul to the grave, held by the eldest grandson or a designated person. It is mostly a long strip of white paper (or white cloth) cut into shape, inscribed "soul-guiding banner" and the like.
- **Paper money** (*zhiqian*, "road-buying money"): paper scraps scattered along the way, symbolising "buying the road" for the dead soul's passage. Two kinds: scattered paper money (thrown, not burned) and burned paper money (burned at crossings and bridges).
- There is also **punched money** (*daqian*), money-shaped paper made by punching holes, the most ancient form of paper money.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Name banner**: explicitly recorded in the *Yili* ("Jixi li") as the leading flag before the procession, inscribed "the coffin of so-and-so of such-and-such clan".
- **Paper money**: very early in origin. The Tang already used "paper money" (the *Fengshi Wenjian Ji* records it as "a thing that began from the Wei and Jin"); punched money, made by punching holes in paper into coin shape, was common in the Tang and Song.
- **Road-buying money**: its idea relates to the folk belief that "spirits guard the road". Scattering paper money both "buys the road" and "dismisses the wild ghosts along the way".
- **Soul-guiding banner**: a combination of the "recall rite" (*fuli*) and the name banner, gradually fixed as the "soul-guiding banner" form after the Tang and Song.
- Ming and Qing gazetteers record the customs of "scattering paper money" and "raising the banner in the lead" abundantly. Contemporary villages still scatter paper money; cities mostly replace it with burning.
- Note: paper money and road-buying money are materialised forms of folk belief; this corpus records them as folklore, and does not endorse them.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Prepare the banner**: before the procession prepare the soul-guiding banner, inscribe the deceased's name, and have the eldest grandson (or designated person) hold it.
2. **Prepare the money**: prepare paper money (both scattered and burned kinds).
3. **Lead the way**: as the column sets out, the soul-guiding banner goes at its front (after or before the name banner, varying by place).
4. **Scatter along the way**: a specialist throws paper money along the route, and must scatter at: crossings (where the soul might lose its way); bridges (where the road must be "bought"); before temples, shrines and memorial arches; the grave entrance.
5. **Burn money**: at crossings and bridges set small heaps to burn paper money.
6. **After interment**: burn paper money and paper effigies before the grave.
7. **Return**: on the way back no more paper money is scattered (some places do the opposite; follow local record).

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: mostly scatters "punched money" (round-holed scraps), insisting "scatter all the way to the grave mound".
- **Jiangnan**: scatters paper money together with underworld coins; must burn at bridges.
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: paper money is divided strictly into "silver paper" (for the dead), "gold paper" (for the gods), "treasury money" (to repay underworld debt), etc. — the most refined paper-money system.
- **Guangdong**: many kinds of paper money, a whole set of "gold-silver-clothing paper", plus "stream money" (for scattering on the road).
- **Ethnic minorities**: paper money is a Han product. The Tibetan replace it with "wind-horse flags" (prayer flags) and butter lamps; the Dai with offering to the Buddha and flowers; Muslim peoples do not use paper money (the law deems it useless). The paper-money system does not apply to other peoples.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Name banner**: the flag bearing the deceased's name, rank and age.
- **Soul-guiding banner (guiding banner)**: a long strip of white paper or cloth, cut into teeth, with inscriptions.
- **Paper money**: punched money, underworld coins, treasury money, silver paper, stream paper, etc., whose forms and uses vary by region.
- **Brazeros, matches**: for burning.
- **Bamboo pole, wooden pole**: to hold the banner.
