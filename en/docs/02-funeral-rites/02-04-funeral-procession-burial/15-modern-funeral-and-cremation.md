# Modern Funeral Services and Cremation

## 1. Definition

The modern funeral service is the contemporary way of handling a death, centred on the funeral home and ending in cremation. Its fundamental differences from the traditional funeral are:

1. **The venue moves out**: from the home (mourning hall) to the funeral home (mourning hall, farewell hall).
2. **The cycle compresses**: from several days or tens of days to 1–3 days.
3. **The endpoint changes**: from "into the earth" to "cremation + ashes placement".
4. **The subject changes**: from "family + neighbours" to "funeral service agency + family".

**But the function of the rite has not vanished**: farewell, condolence, confirmation of social relations, and grief healing remain needs, only the form changes.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **1956**: the state advocated cremation; funeral reform began.
- **1985**: the *Provisional Funeral Management Rules* were issued; **1997**: the *Funeral Management Regulations* took effect, clearly "actively and step by step practising cremation, reforming earth burial".
- Everywhere "cremation zones" and "earth-burial reform zones" were drawn; in cremation zones the body must be cremated.
- After the 1990s, funeral homes, cemeteries and funeral-service companies formed an industrial system.
- After the 2010s, ecological burial (tree, flower, lawn, sea burial) was gradually promoted; everywhere carried out "beneficial funeral" (free basic funeral services).
- At the same time, the social advocacy of "simple funerals" and "rear nourishment, thin burial" coexists with problems such as "funeral profiteering" and "luxury graves".

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

(The typical contemporary urban funeral)

1. **Issue the death certificate**: a medical institution or public-security organ issues the *Resident Death Medical Certificate (inferred)*.
2. **Transport the body**: contact the funeral home to transport the body, register transport and refrigeration.
3. **Cosmetise and dress the body**: the funeral home washes, cosmetises and dresses the body (replacing the traditional washing and dressing).
4. **Set the mourning hall**: rent the funeral home's mourning hall (1–3 days), arranging the portrait, wreaths, couplets, and an electronic screen biography.
5. **Vigil and condolence**: kin keep vigil; friends come to condole, send wreaths, give condolence money.
6. **Memorial meeting / body-farewell ceremony** (the core link): the host announces the start; mourn in silence, play funeral music; deliver the eulogy (unit leader or family representative); the family gives a thanks speech; all bow thrice; circle the body to take leave.
7. **Cremate**: the body is sent into the cremator; the family waits in a designated area; after cremation receive the ashes and the *Cremation Certificate*.
8. **Place the ashes**: choose an ashes casket, send to a cemetery or ashes hall, or choose sea or tree burial and other ecological placements.
9. **Thanks feast / return gifts**: feast kin and friends, distribute towels, sweets and other return gifts (some areas).
10. **Later sweeping**: sweep the grave or ashes hall at Qingming and on the death day.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Cities (mainly Han)**: the above procedure prevails; only "farewell", "return gifts" and "sweeping" of the traditional rites survive.
- **Rural (some earth-burial reform zones)**: still keep some traditional rites (basin-smashing, bearing, roadside offering, seven-day rites); after cremation the ashes are earth-buried.
- **Ethnic minorities**: **there are special policies**. The *Funeral Management Regulations* provide "respect for the funeral customs of ethnic minorities"; the customs of the ten Islamic peoples (Hui etc.) and the Tibetan sky-burial are protected by law, and cremation is not forced; everywhere special sites such as Hui cemeteries and sky-burial platforms are set.
- **Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan**: cremation rates are high, while complete traditional rites are kept (e.g. Taiwan's "seven-day rites" and "farewell ceremony") — a form where "cremation + traditional ritual" proceed in parallel.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Resident Death Medical Certificate (inferred)**: the first document for handling the funeral.
- **Cremation Certificate**: the proof after cremation, used to cancel household registration and claim funeral subsidy.
- **Ashes casket, cemetery plot certificate (grave-site certificate)**.
- **Funeral-service contract, fee list**.
- **Wreaths, couplets, hangings, black armband, white flower**.
- **Obituary, eulogy, thanks speech**: modern funeral documents (see the "Document Templates" section).
