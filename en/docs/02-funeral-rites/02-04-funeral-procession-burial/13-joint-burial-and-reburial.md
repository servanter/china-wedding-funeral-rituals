# Joint Burial and Reburial

## 1. Definition

- **Joint burial** (*hezang*): burying husband and wife (or mother and child, or family members) in the same pit or the same grave area.
- **Subordinate burial** (*fuzang*): burial beside the ancestral grave ("*fu*" means "to attach"), part of the lineage burial system.
- **Reburial** (*qianzang*, *gaizang*): moving an already-buried coffin or remains to another place for burial.
- **Secondary burial** (*erci zang*, bone-picking burial): years after the first burial, picking the bones, placing them in a bone urn (gold-dipper urn), and burying again — a special system of some southern areas.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Liji* ("Tan'gong"): "To be buried in the north, head to the north, is the universal rite of the three dynasties", and records the joint-burial rite: "The men of Wei in their *fu* kept apart, the men of Lu in their *fu* joined — well done!" — showing that "joint burial" (same pit) and "apart burial" (same area, different pits) already coexisted in the pre-Qin, and Confucius praised the Lu men's joint burial.
- The *Shijing* ("Wang feng", "Great Cart") "die then same pit" is the earliest literary expression of husband-wife joint burial.
- The *Yili* ("Sangfu") mourning code gives *fu* a clear place: the newly dead's tablet is attached to the ancestral temple, matching the burial *fu*.
- After the Han, husband-wife joint burial became the norm; Han tombs archaeologically are mostly one pit with two coffins or twin parallel chambers.
- **Secondary burial (bone-picking)**: common among the Hakka, Guangfu and parts of Fujian-Guangdong, abundantly recorded in Ming and Qing gazetteers, tied to the historical background of "choosing an auspicious site for reburial" and "the Hakka's frequent migrations".
- Since modern times, the urban cemetery system has made joint burial mostly "same-pit joint burial" (ashes joint burial) or "same-stele, twin pits".

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

### Joint burial

1. **Reserve the pit**: when the first buried is interred, reserve the later one's spot ("reserving the pit").
2. **Choose the day**: after the later one's death, choose an auspicious day.
3. **Open the pit**: carefully excavate the first buried's grave (old custom had the "opening the coffin to see the bones" rite).
4. **Inter**: place the new coffin (or ashes) in the reserved pit, oriented relative to the first buried (mostly "man left, woman right" or "man first, woman after").
5. **Joint seal**: fill earth, cover into one grave head.
6. **Erect (or alter) the stele**: add the later one's name to the tombstone, or erect a new one.
7. **Sacrifice**: set an offering before the grave; the rite of the "two souls dwelling together" is done.

### Reburial (changing burial)

1. **Request and announce**: first announce at the ancestral temple (or before the grave), stating the reason (new site, a project, a lineage decision).
2. **Choose the day**: choose an auspicious day to break earth and open the coffin.
3. **Open the coffin (pick the bones)**: open the coffin and pick the remains (or a living person's relics), wash with white liquor, wipe, dry.
4. **Fill the urn**: place the bones in the bone urn (gold-dipper urn) in a fixed order — skull on top.
5. **Choose a new site**: choose another auspicious site (see "Choosing the Grave Site and Geomancy of the Yin Dwelling").
6. **Re-bury**: by the burial rite inter in the new pit, cover earth, erect a stele.
7. **Sacrifice and thank the earth**: set an offering, thank the earth, burn paper.
8. **Mourning code**: the *Yili* ("Sangfu") sets the reburial mourning as *sima* (three months), because "once buried, the mourning is already spent", and only *sima* is re-donned to exhaust the grief.

### Secondary burial (bone-picking)

1. **First burial**: first bury in a coffin (the "first", "inauspicious" burial).
2. **Wait**: three to seven years (by local rule), until the body has decayed.
3. **Pick the bones**: choose an auspicious day, open the coffin, pick the bones, wash and wipe with liquor.
4. **Fill the urn**: place the bones in the "gold-dipper urn" (earthen urn) in a fixed order.
5. **Re-bury**: choose another auspicious site (the "auspicious", "gold" burial), bury the urn.
6. **Sacrifice**: set an offering, erect a stele, burn paper.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: husband-wife joint burial is the norm, the "reserving the pit" custom widespread; secondary burial is rare.
- **Jiangnan**: stresses "same-pit joint burial", with both names on the stele.
- **Hakka**: secondary burial (bone-picking) is the core system; the "gold-dipper urn" is its most representative object.
- **Guangfu and parts of Fujian-Guangdong**: also practise secondary burial, stressing "first inauspicious, then auspicious".
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples have multi-person joint burial (family graves); the Tibetan sky-burial leaves no bones, so no joint burial; in the Muslim system husband and wife are buried nearby but each in his own pit (no joint pit). The concrete form of joint burial must follow each people's burial system.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Bone urn (gold-dipper urn)**: the core object of secondary burial.
- **Ashes casket**: the carrier of contemporary joint burial after cremation.
- **Tombstone (double inscription)**: the mark of joint burial.
- **Liquor, white cloth, brush**: bone-picking implements.
- **Reburial announcement / changing-burial announcement**: the document to the spirits at reburial.
- **Grave-site deed, lineage grave-map**: documents recording the burial position.
