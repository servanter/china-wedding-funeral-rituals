# Choosing the Day and Hour of the Procession

## 1. Definition

The procession (chubin — also fayin, chusang, songzang) requires the choice of an auspicious day and hour, and is the most weightily regarded act of date-selection in the funeral. The selection covers four things: the **burial date**, the **hour of raising the coffin**, the **direction of leaving the house**, and the **hour of interment**.

The core taboo is "repeated bereavement" (*zhongsang*): folk belief holds that a procession on an inauspicious day will bring another death to the family (the *zhongsang*, *sansang* and *furi* days).

## 2. Historical Origins

- Statutory burial periods by rank: the *Liji* ("Wangzhi") says "the Son of Heaven is coffined on the seventh day and buried in the seventh month; the feudal lords … are buried in the fifth month; the great officers, knights and commoners are coffined on the third day and buried in the third month".
- The *Yili* ("Jixi li") records the "moving the coffin" (*qianjiu*) and "farewell offering" (*zudian*) of the day before the procession, and the "dispatch offering" (*qianmian*) and "starting the procession" (*fayin*) on the day itself, all at fixed times.
- From the Tang and Song onward the art of date-selection intervened, forming concrete rules such as "avoid the *zhongsang* day", "avoid the three-sha direction" and "avoid the yellow-sand day", systematised in the *Xiejie Bianfang Shu*.
- Ming and Qing gazetteers record the custom of choosing the procession day extensively, e.g. "one must invite a *yinyang* master to read the day; if it offends *zhongsang* one cannot proceed".
- Means of breaking the taboo: folk practices such as "pressing the coffin with a rooster", "adding a Daoist priest (or monk)", or "writing a demon-dispelling talisman" (*jiesha fu*), said to dissolve repeated bereavement.
- Under the contemporary cremation system the procession day is mostly a combination of the funeral home's schedule and date-selection, and the influence of traditional taboos has diminished.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Invite a day-reader**: a *yinyang* master fixes the day by the almanac, the deceased's eight characters, and the grave's orientation.
2. **Fix the four elements**: the burial day (avoiding *zhongsang* and *sansang* days); the hour of raising the coffin (mostly before dawn or in the morning, not the afternoon); the direction of leaving the house (which way to exit first, avoiding a direct clash with certain directions); the hour of interment (lowering and covering the earth).
3. **Post the avoidance list**: write the zodiac signs and persons to be avoided, and post it at the door.
4. **Notify kin and friends**: put the schedule into the obituary.
5. **Prepare taboo-breaking objects** (if an inauspicious day is unavoidable): rooster, talisman, five grains, mirror, etc.
6. **Fire crackers to raise the coffin**: at the fixed hour, crackers signal the start of the procession.
7. **Mid-route taboos**: avoid turning back, avoid letting the coffin touch the ground, avoid lingering on a bridge.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China (Jing-Jin-Ji-Shanxi)**: insist "the procession not pass noon", mostly at dawn; avoid the *zhongsang* day, press the coffin with a rooster.
- **Jiangnan**: insist on "reading the hour", raising the coffin mostly in the *yin-mao* or *chen* hours.
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: the hour is strictly fixed by the *sigong* and *tugong* together; avoid the "yellow-sand day".
- **Guangdong**: insist "the procession not meet the three-sha", with periods of "three mornings", "five mornings", "seven mornings".
- **Ethnic minorities**: the time of procession is mostly decided by religious specialists. The Yi consult a *bimo*; Tibetan sky-burial dates are fixed by lamas by the Tibetan calendar; the Miao and Dong have their own methods (e.g. reading chicken bones). Muslim peoples do **not** choose a day — speedy burial is a requirement of the law, only avoiding burial after sunset, entirely different from the Han sexagenary date-selection.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Almanac, date-selection books, compass**: tools for fixing the day and direction.
- **Obituary**: records the schedule.
- **Rooster, talisman, five grains, mirror**: taboo-breaking objects.
- **Avoidance list**: a notice posted at the door.
- **Firecrackers**: the signal to raise the coffin.
