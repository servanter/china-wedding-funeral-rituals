# Coffin Canopy and Bearing the Coffin (Gangfang)

## 1. Definition

- **Coffin canopy** (*guanzhao*): the embroidered cover laid over the coffin, made of silk and satin, covering the lid and four walls, serving to conceal, decorate and display rank.
- **Bearing the coffin**: raising and carrying the coffin with poles (wooden bars) and ropes (carrying cords); the bearers are called *gangfu*.
- **Gangfang** (the bearing house): the old trade that specialised in bearing the coffin, insignia and sedan-chair rental, most famous in Beijing.

The number and grade of bearers ("how many bearers") is a direct mark of the funeral's scale: **eight, sixteen, twenty-four, thirty-two bearers**, even "sixty-four" for the imperial system.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* ("Jixi li") records the "roller-axle" (*gongzhou*), "hearse-sledge" (*chun*) and the like as coffin-bearing implements, and the "holding the rope" system — the ritual source of the bearing system.
- After the Han the "hearse cart" (*erche*) was used, and also men bearing the coffin.
- The Ming and Qing matured the bearing system; the *gangfang* became an important urban trade. Beijing's *gangfang* had "great pole" and "small pole" distinctions, each with fixed grades and prices.
- The *gangfang* business included: sending the pole (bearing), the functionaries (flags, tablets, umbrellas, fans), the coffin canopy, drums and music, and the shed-shop (erecting the mourning shed) — a "one-stop" service, the precursor of the modern funeral industry.
- On the way the rule was "the coffin does not touch the ground": if a rest was needed, it was propped on wooden stools ("setting the pad"), never letting the wood touch earth directly.
- Contemporary cities mainly use vehicles for the coffin (or body); bearing becomes a ritual act (e.g. from the hall to the hearse).

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Engage the gangfang**: the family agrees the date and price with the gangfang by grade (eight bearers, sixteen, etc.).
2. **Prepare the poles**: the gangfang prepares the great and small poles, carrying cords, coffin canopy and functionary implements.
3. **Canopy the coffin**: before raising the coffin, cover it with the canopy and tie it fast.
4. **Mount the poles**: the bearers lift the coffin onto the poles, each to his station.
5. **Raise the poles**: fire crackers, the bearers raise in unison (with a "raise-pole chant").
6. **March**: the bearers keep step; at crossings and bridges the "pole-head" calls the cadence to adjust.
7. **Set the pad**: at rest on the way, prop the poles on wooden stools, the coffin not touching ground.
8. **Lower the poles**: at the grave lower the poles, moving the coffin beside the pit.
9. **Inter**: perform the burial rite (see "Burial: Lowering the Coffin and Raising the Mound").
10. **Reward money**: the family gives reward money to the bearers and functionaries.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Beijing, Tianjin**: the gangfang trade is most systematic, with trade-convention grades and prices; "sixteen" and "twenty-four bearers" are common grades.
- **North China villages**: mostly the village's young men serve as bearers, not hiring a gangfang, insisting "kin bear it".
- **Jiangnan**: mostly vehicle-borne (hearse), short bearing distance.
- **Fujian-Guangdong**: by "tugong" and professional bearers; in the watery lands coffins are ferried by boat.
- **Ethnic minorities**: the Tibetan sky-burial is carried on the back by the "corpse-bearer" to the platform, no coffin, no poles; the Miao and Yi have their own bearing ways (e.g. the "single-log coffin" borne by several); the Hui and Uyghur carry on a *tabut* (bier) by four, without stopping. The *gangfang* is a specific urban Han trade form.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Great pole, small pole**: the wooden bars for bearing.
- **Carrying cord, pole rope**: the tying ropes.
- **Coffin canopy**: the embroidered cover (dragon, crane, cloud patterns, etc.).
- **Pad stool**: for propping the poles on the way.
- **Functionary implements**: flags, tablets, umbrellas, fans.
- **Gangfang receipt**: the rental and settlement document.
