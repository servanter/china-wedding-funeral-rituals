# Pumi Funeral Customs

## 1. Definition

The Pumi are mainly concentrated in Nujiang, Yunnan (Lanping), Lijiang (Ninglang) and Liangshan, Sichuan (Yanyuan, Muli). Traditionally they practised **cremation**, and in modern times some areas switched to **earth burial**.

**Core elements**:
- **Presided over by the "Hangui" (priest)** — the hangui chants to send the soul, guiding the deceased back to the ancestral homeland (in keeping with the Pumi migration memory).
- **"Jar burial"** — after cremation the ashes are collected into a pottery jar (the "jar") and buried, a characteristic of the traditional Pumi burial.
- **"Giving the sheep"** — in some areas a "giving the sheep" rite (offering a sheep to lead the way) is performed for the deceased, a distinctive ceremony.
- **Funeral songs and wake-jumping** — sing funeral songs through the night and form a circle to jump the wake.
- **Tibetan-Buddhist influence** — in some areas lamas are invited to chant.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Tang–Song to Ming–Qing**: the Pumi forebears ("Xifan") appear in histories and local gazetteers (Lijiang Prefecture gazetteer, etc.).
- **Modern**: intermingled with Naxi, Tibetan and Yi; customs mutually influenced.
- **Contemporary**: cremation and earth burial proceed side by side; the traditional rites are preserved in the settlement areas.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Dying**: family keep watch; fire a gun to announce the death.
2. **Cleanse and dress**: cleanse the body, dress in new clothes.
3. **Keep the spirit and chant**: lay out the corpse, set up the spirit; the hangui chants to send the soul.
4. **Wake-jumping and condolence**: relatives and friends offer condolences, sing funeral songs and jump the wake; some areas perform "giving the sheep".
5. **Cremation (or earth burial)**: choose a day to send the body for cremation, collect the ashes into a jar for burial.
6. **After-burial sacrifice**: settle the jar and worship the ancestors; sweep tombs at the New Year festival and Qingming.

## 4. Regional and Subgroup Variants

- **Lanping and Ninglang, Yunnan**: the settlement areas, preserving the hangui system and cremation tradition more fully.
- **Yanyuan and Muli, Sichuan**: intermingled with Naxi and Tibetan; customs interpenetrate.
- **Relation to other peoples**: the Pumi cremation and "jar burial" tradition are not the same source as the Han earth-burial ritual system, and mourning also does not adopt the five-degree system; it must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Religious objects**: hangui ritual implements, scriptures.
- **Funeral supplies**: firewood (cremation), ash pottery jar, sacrificial livestock (sheep), wine.
- **Documents**: orally transmitted scriptures, clan genealogy.
