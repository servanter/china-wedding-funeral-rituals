# Jino Funeral Customs

## 1. Definition

The Jino are concentrated in the Jino Mountain (Jino Township) of Jinghong, Xishuangbanna, Yunnan. Earth burial is the main mode; the village keeps a **communal cemetery**, divided into zones by village and clan.

**Core elements**:
- **The "Zhuoba" and "Zhuose" preside** — the village heads "Zhuoba" and "Zhuose" (village elders) participate in mourning and site selection.
- **"Soul-sending" and funeral songs** — the "Mopi" or village elder chants to send the soul and sings funeral songs, guiding the deceased back to the ancestral home.
- **Burial of production tools** — bury tools such as knife and sickle and bamboo implements with the dead.
- **Cattle-impaling sacrifice for the dead** — a bull is sacrificed (old custom); "cattle-impaling" is an important Jino rite.
- **Mourning taboos** — the village forbids farming and song/dance; purification rites after burial.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Qing dynasty**: the Jino ("Youle") appear in local gazetteers (the *Pu'er Prefecture gazetteer*, etc.).
- **Modern**: because of the closed forests, traditional customs were preserved longer.
- **Contemporary**: after being recognised as a distinct ethnicity in 1979, cultural recording and research began; customs grew simpler.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Dying**: family keep watch; fire a gun or strike bamboo to announce the death.
2. **Cleanse and dress**: cleanse the body, dress in new clothes (or wrap in white cloth).
3. **Keep the spirit and chant**: lay out the corpse, set up the spirit; the village elder or mopi chants, sing funeral songs.
4. **Cattle-impaling sacrifice for the dead** (old custom): sacrifice a bull.
5. **Funeral and burial**: choose a day to send the burial, interred in the village communal cemetery.
6. **After-burial sacrifice**: round the grave and conclude the soul-sending; worship ancestors at the New Year festival (the Temaoke Festival).

## 4. Regional and Subgroup Variants

- **Jino Mountain (Baya, Bapo and other villages)**: the core settlement area, fairly complete custom records.
- **Other parts of Jinghong**: intermingled with Dai and Hani; customs interpenetrate.
- **Relation to other peoples**: the Jino have no five-degree mourning system; mourning is bounded by taboos and the mourning period, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Religious objects**: sacrificial vessels, divination tools.
- **Funeral supplies**: white cloth, bamboo/wood burial gear, sacrificial livestock (ox, pig, chicken), wine.
- **Distinctive items**: bamboo-woven implements, chopper (buried with the dead).
