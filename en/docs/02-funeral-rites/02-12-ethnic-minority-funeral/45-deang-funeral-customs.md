# De'ang Funeral Customs

## 1. Definition

The De'ang are mainly concentrated in Dehong, Yunnan (Mangshi, Ruili, Longchuan) and Lincang (Zhenkang, Gengma). They believe in **Theravada Buddhism** alongside traditional ghost-and-spirit worship. Earth burial is the main mode, with cremation in some areas.

**Core elements**:
- **Monk chanting and "water pouring"** — invite temple monks to chant for deliverance; relatives perform "water pouring" to transfer the merit.
- **The "Dagan" (headman) and village elders preside** — the village headman participates in mourning and site selection.
- **Zoned burial** — those who died natural deaths and those who died violently are buried in separate graveyards, a notable taboo.
- **Mourning taboos** — the village forbids song, dance and marriage; purification rites are performed after burial.
- **Sacrifice to the village deity** — the village "village deity" sacrifice relates to funeral taboos.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Tang–Song to Ming–Qing**: the De'ang forebears ("Pu people", "Benglong") and their burial customs appear in histories and local gazetteers.
- **Modern**: Theravada Buddhist influence deepened; temples took on the role of chanting for deliverance.
- **Contemporary**: traditional customs are better preserved in mountainous areas; towns have grown simpler.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Dying**: family keep watch; invite monks to chant.
2. **Cleanse and dress**: cleanse the body, dress in new clothes.
3. **Keep the spirit and chant**: lay out the corpse, set up the spirit; monks chant.
4. **Funeral and burial**: choose a day to send the burial, interred in the village communal cemetery (zoned by cause of death).
5. **Water-pouring merit transfer**: after burial, perform "water pouring" at the temple to transfer the merit.
6. **After-burial sacrifice**: sacrifice to the village deity; seasonal observances at the Water-Splashing Festival, Door-Closing Festival and the like.

## 4. Regional and Subgroup Variants

- **Dehong (Mangshi, Ruili)**: intermingled with Dai and Jingpo; customs interpenetrate.
- **Lincang (Zhenkang, Gengma)**: more concentrated settlement, with obvious Buddhist influence.
- **Relation to other peoples**: De'ang mourning is bounded by Buddhist taboos and the mourning period, and does not adopt the five-degree mourning system; it must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Religious objects**: Buddha statues, palm-leaf scriptures, monk robes, water-pouring vessels.
- **Funeral supplies**: white cloth, wax tapers, glutinous-rice meals, offerings.
- **Temple architecture**: temple, stupa.
