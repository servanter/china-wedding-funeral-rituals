# Tajik Funeral Customs

## 1. Definition

The Tajik are mainly concentrated in Kashgar, Xinjiang (the Tashkurgan Tajik Autonomous County). They believe in Islam (mostly the Ismaili branch), and their funerals follow the norms of **prompt burial, earth burial and a simple burial**, while preserving a distinctive **lamp sacrifice** tradition.

**Core elements**:
- **Prompt burial and earth burial** — interment on the day of or the day after death, wrapped in white cloth, the grave with a side chamber.
- **Presided over by the ahong** — cleansing and the funeral prayer are led by the ahong.
- **Women sing the elegy** — relatives (especially women) sing elegies to express grief.
- **The "third day", "seventh day", "fortieth day"** — invite the ahong to chant and receive guests.
- **The "Lamp Festival" (Barat)** — an important Tajik seasonal observance for sacrificing to the dead, commemorating deceased relatives by lighting lamps and chanting, a marked feature.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **After the spread of Islam**: the funerary norms were established, suited to village life on the Pamir Plateau.
- **Qing dynasty**: the Tashkurgan area belonged to the Sarik-col "Hui manor" (selekui huizhuang); the funeral customs continued.
- **Contemporary**: the rites continue; the seasonal sacrifices persist.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Dying**: invite the ahong to chant; family keep watch.
2. **After "returning to truth"**: close the eyes, cover with white cloth.
3. **Cleanse and wrap**: specialists cleanse the body, wrap it in white cloth.
4. **Funeral prayer**: hold the funeral prayer at the mosque or the graveyard.
5. **Send the burial**: convey to the graveyard for interment, oriented toward Mecca (west).
6. **After-burial memorial**: chant and receive guests on the third, seventh and fortieth days; light lamps at the "Lamp Festival" to sacrifice to the dead.

## 4. Regional and Subgroup Variants

- **Tashkurgan**: the core settlement area, with complete rites and seasonal sacrifices.
- **Other parts of southern Xinjiang**: customs adjacent to those of the Uyghur and Kyrgyz.
- **Relation to other peoples**: details differ from other peoples who also follow Islamic burial, and its source is not the same as the Han five-degree system; it must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Funeral supplies**: white cloth (shroud), washing bed, corpse-carrying implements, spices.
- **Religious objects**: the *Quran*, prayer beads, mosque.
- **Sacrificial objects**: the lamps, oil and offerings used at the "Lamp Festival".
