# Achang Funeral Customs

## 1. Definition

The Achang are mainly concentrated in Dehong, Yunnan (Longchuan, Lianghe) and Baoshan (Tengchong). Earth burial is the main mode; some areas practise **cremation**.

**Core elements**:
- **Presided over by the "Huopo" (priest)** — the "huopo" chants to send the soul and guide the deceased back to the ancestors.
- **Singing the funeral song and "jumping the wake"** — sing funeral songs during the wake; some villages form a circle to jump the wake.
- **"Woluo" ancestor worship** — the Achang "Woluo" Festival (around the fourth day of the first lunar month) relates to ancestor worship and ancestor veneration.
- **Theravada Buddhist influence** — some Achang believe in Theravada Buddhism, and the funeral also includes Buddhist rites.
- **Mourning taboos** — the village forbids song, dance and marriage.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Tang–Song to Ming–Qing**: the Achang forebears ("Echang") appear in histories (the *Man Shu*, etc.) and local gazetteers.
- **Modern**: intermingled with Dai, Jingpo and Han; customs interpenetrate.
- **Contemporary**: traditional rites are better preserved in mountainous areas; towns have grown simpler.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Dying**: family keep watch; fire a gun to announce the death.
2. **Cleanse and dress**: cleanse the body, dress in new clothes.
3. **Encoffin and keep the spirit**: place in the coffin, set up the spirit; the huopo chants.
4. **Condolence and wake-jumping**: relatives and friends offer condolences, sing funeral songs and jump the wake.
5. **Funeral and burial**: choose a day to send the burial, earth burial (or cremation).
6. **After-burial sacrifice**: round the grave and conclude the soul-sending; sweep tombs at the Woluo Festival and Qingming.

## 4. Regional and Subgroup Variants

- **Longchuan (Husa, Lasa)**: the Achang settlement, with a fairly complete huopo system.
- **Lianghe, Tengchong**: intermingled with Dai and Han; customs merge.
- **Relation to other peoples**: the Achang have no five-degree mourning system; mourning is bounded by taboos and the mourning period, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Religious objects**: huopo ritual implements, sacrificial vessels.
- **Funeral supplies**: coffin, sacrificial livestock (ox, pig), wine, offerings.
- **Distinctive craft**: the Husa knife (a famous Achang craft, sometimes buried with the dead or used in rites).
