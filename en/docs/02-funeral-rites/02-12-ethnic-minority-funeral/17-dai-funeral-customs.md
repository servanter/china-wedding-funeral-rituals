# Dai Funeral Customs

## 1. Definition

The Dai mainly distribute in Yunnan's Xishuangbanna, Dehong and Pu'er, Lincang, believing in "**Theravada Buddhism (Southern Buddhism)**". The burial is "**earth burial**" main, with "**cremation and water burial**" too.

**Core elements**:
- **Monks chant for deliverance** — invite temple monks to chant, "transferring merit" for the dead.
- **"Dripping water"** — kin "drip water" in the rite, to transfer merit to the dead; the marked act of Dai funerals.
- **"Dan"** — giving alms to monks ("dan") is an important link of the funeral and sacrifice.
- **"Village-god" sacrifice** — the village "village-god" ("Phi Man") faith ties to funeral taboos.
- **No entertainment in mourning** — in mourning the village suspends song-dance and weddings.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Tang-Song**: Theravada Buddhism entered via Southeast Asia, merging with the Dai native faith.
- **Yuan-Ming-Qing**: under the tusi system, the temple and village jointly bore the funeral; gazetteers record the custom.
- **Modern**: Buddhist rites stable; the "drip-water", "dan" customs continue.
- **Contemporary**: cremation gradually promoted; the temple still acts in village funerals.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Dying**: family keep watch; invite monks to chant.
2. **Cleanse and dress**: cleanse with clear water; wear new clothes or white-cloth wrap.
3. **Keep the spirit and chant**: invite monks to chant; kin condole.
4. **Procession and bury**: choose a day to send; earth burial (or cremation, water burial, by cause and status).
5. **Drip-water and transfer**: after the burial at the temple do "drip-water" and "dan", transferring merit.
6. **After-burial sacrifice**: sacrifice the village-god; at the Water-Splashing Festival (ancestor worship in the festival), the Door-Closing and Door-Opening festivals hold seasonal sacrifices.

## 4. Regional and Subgroup Variants

- **Xishuangbanna (Dai Lü)** : deepest Theravada influence; the drip-water, dan-Buddha customs complete.
- **Dehong (Dai Na)** : neighbour Jingpo, Achang; customs penetrate.
- **Pu'er, Lincang (Dai Ya, Dai Beng etc.)** : branch customs differ.
- **Relation to other peoples** : the Dai mourning is limited by Buddhist taboos and the mourning term, taking no five-degree system, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Religious objects** : Buddha statue, scriptures (palm-leaf manuscripts), monk's robe, drip-water vessel, incense and candle.
- **Funeral goods** : white cloth, offerings (glutinous rice, wax tapers), Buddha-giving things.
- **Temple buildings** : temple, stupa (tying to the funeral and dan-Buddha).
