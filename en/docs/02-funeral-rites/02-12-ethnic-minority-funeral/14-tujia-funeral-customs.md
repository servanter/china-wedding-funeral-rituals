# Tujia Funeral Customs

## 1. Definition

The Tujia mainly gather in west Hunan, west Hubei, southeast Chongqing, northeast Guizhou. The burial is "**earth burial**" main; the most marked sign is "**jumping the mourning (Saying Er He)**" — seeing off the dead with song and dance, called "joyfully seeing off the dead".

**Core elements**:
- **"Saying Er He" (jumping the mourning)** — people circle the coffin, drum and dance, sing the funeral song, all night; embodying the unique idea "sing instead of cry, dance to see off the dead".
- **"Tu teacher" (tima) presides** — the Tujia priest "tima" does rites, sends the soul.
- **"Circle the coffin"** — before and after the procession circle the coffin singing.
- **"Mourning drum"** — the drum leads the dance and song, the core instrument of jumping the mourning.
- **Han influence** — some areas also do the "sevens", "do the altar".

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the Tujia ancestors (Ba people) favoured song and dance; the *Chu Ci* and Ba-Chu shamanism tie.
- **Tang-Song**: the "Ba people" funeral song and tread-song custom recorded.
- **Ming-Qing**: gazetteers (Yongshun prefecture gazetteer, Shinan prefecture gazetteer) record "jumping the mourning", "circling the coffin".
- **Contemporary**: "Saying Er He" is listed as national intangible heritage; the custom best kept in the Qingjiang valley.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Dying**: family keep watch; fire a cannon to report death.
2. **Cleanse and dress**: cleanse; wear grave clothes.
3. **Encoffin and keep the spirit**: into the coffin, set the spirit.
4. **Jump the mourning (Saying Er He)**: kin circle the coffin, drum and dance, sing the funeral song, all night.
5. **Procession and bury**: choose a day to send, earth burial; the grave by the mountain.
6. **After-burial sacrifice**: round the mound; "fifth seven", "anniversary"; sweep at Qingming, Zhongyuan, New-Year.

## 4. Regional and Subgroup Variants

- **Qingjiang valley (Changyang, Wufeng)**: jumping the mourning best kept.
- **West Hunan (Longshan, Yongshun)**: mixed with Miao; with Miao custom.
- **Southeast Chongqing (Youyang, Xiushan)**: near Chongqing; like Sichuan-Chongqing.
- **Northeast Guizhou (Tongren)**: mixed with Dong, Miao; customs penetrate.
- **Relation to other peoples**: the Tujia do not take the five degrees; their "joyfully see off the dead" idea differs in orientation from the Han "grief" rite, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Instruments**: mourning drum, gong, cymbal.
- **Religious objects**: tima instruments, mask.
- **Funeral goods**: coffin, grave clothes, paper effigies, paper money, offerings.
- **Documents**: obituary, funeral-song manuscript, genealogy.
