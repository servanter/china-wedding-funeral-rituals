# Wa Funeral Customs

## 1. Definition

The Wa mainly distribute in Yunnan's Lincang, Pu'er (Cangyuan, Ximeng etc.). The burial is "**earth burial**" main; the village has a public graveyard.

**Core elements**:
- **"Moba" presides** — the priest "moba" hosts the funeral, chants scripture, sends the soul, chooses the burial ground.
- **"Fell the ox" to sacrifice** — some areas stab and fell the ox to offer the dead; the blood and ox-head used in sacrifice.
- **"Wooden drum"** — the wooden drum is the Wa's great sacrifice vessel (old tied to "head-hunting" sacrifice, now abolished); the funeral keeps the beating-drum relic.
- **Mourning taboos** — village taboos (no farming, no song-dance); after burial a "calling the soul" rite restores purity.
- **Theravada-Buddhist influence** — some Wa believe in Theravada Buddhism; the funeral has Buddhist rites too.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Tang-Song to Ming-Qing** : the Wa ancestors' burial appears in the *Man Shu* and Ming-Qing gazetteers ("Kawa" etc.).
- **Modern** : some areas' old custom "head sacrifice for grain" was abolished in mid-20th century.
- **Contemporary** : traditional rites simplified; wooden-drum making and sacrifice listed as intangible heritage.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Dying** : family keep watch; fire a gun to report death.
2. **Cleanse and dress** : cleanse; wear new clothes.
3. **Encoffin and keep the spirit** : into the coffin, set the spirit; the moba chants.
4. **Fell the ox to sacrifice** (some areas) : stab and fell the ox to offer; the whole village joins.
5. **Procession and bury** : choose a day to send; bury in the village public graveyard.
6. **After-burial sacrifice** : round the mound, "call the soul" to restore village purity; sacrifice at festivals and the "grain-soul" season.

## 4. Regional and Subgroup Variants

- **Ximeng, Cangyuan** : Wa concentrated; the moba system and wooden-drum culture better kept.
- **Other Lincang, Pu'er areas** : neighbour Dai, Lahu; under Theravada influence.
- **Relation to other peoples** : the Wa have no five-degree system; mourning is limited by taboos and term, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Religious objects** : moba instruments, wooden drum, sacrificial vessels.
- **Funeral goods** : coffin, sacrifice animal (ox, pig, chicken), wine.
- **Grave marks** : wooden post, grave marker, bamboo-weave sacrificial vessel.
