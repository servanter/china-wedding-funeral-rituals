# Mongolian Funeral Customs

## 1. Definition

Mongolians are mainly distributed in the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region, with branches in the Northeast and Northwest. The traditional modes of burial are "**wild burial (sky burial), cremation and earth burial**"; wild burial is the most ethnically marked:

| Mode | For | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Wild burial (sky burial)** | Commoners | The body is carried by an ox-cart or horse, abandoned in the wild; birds and beasts eating it all is auspicious |
| **Cremation** | Lamas, nobles, the upper class | Burn, collect the bones; ashes into a stupa or scattered on mountains and rivers |
| **Earth burial** | Farming, semi-farming-herding areas | Under Han influence, coffined and buried |

**Faith basis**: Mongolians traditionally believed in Shamanism, later widely converted to Tibetan Buddhism, so the funeral has the rite of "**inviting the lama to chant for deliverance**". Folk taboo loud wailing, believing it disturbs the dead soul and delays its "rebirth" journey.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Yuan period**: the custom of "no mound or tomb" appears in envoys' travel records; nobles cremated, deeply buried.
- **Ming-Qing**: Tibetan Buddhism spread; chanting for deliverance became the core of the funeral; farming areas sinicised, earth burial grew.
- **Modern**: pastoral areas still keep wild burial; farming areas mainly earth burial; after funeral reform cremation was promoted.
- **Sources**: the *Secret History of the Mongols*, Ming-Qing Mongolian-language histories, brief histories and folk-custom records of each people.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Dying**: family keep watch; invite the lama to chant to "send the soul"; taboo crying or noise in the tent.
2. **Cleanse and dress**: cleanse with clear water; wear plain clothes or wrap in white cloth.
3. **Keep the spirit and chant**: invite the lama to chant for deliverance, usually one to three days, or the 49-day count.
4. **Send the burial**: wild-burial by ox-cart to the wild; cremation prepares firewood to burn; earth burial coffins and buries.
5. **After-burial sacrifice**: invite the lama to chant "dedication"; set offerings on the death-day, Qingming, New-Year; nomadic families use the "oboo" as the sacrifice place.

## 4. Regional and Subgroup Variants

- **Inner Mongolia pastoral areas**: more wild-burial and cremation relics; solemn chanting.
- **Inner Mongolia farming areas (Tumed, Hetao)**: mainly earth burial; rites near Han.
- **Northeast Mongols (Liaoning, Jilin, Heilongjiang)**: mixed with Manchu, Han; earth burial main.
- **Qinghai, Gansu, Xinjiang Mongols**: deeper Tibetan-Buddhist influence; sky and cremation coexist.
- **Relation to other peoples**: the Mongolian funeral is not the same source as the Han five-degree system, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Religious objects**: rosary, prayer-wheel, scripture, butter-lamp, offerings.
- **Burial gear**: wooden coffin (farming areas), white cloth (wrap), yak-cart (wild-burial transport).
- **Sacrifice objects**: oboo offerings, wine, milk-food (milk tofu, milk).
