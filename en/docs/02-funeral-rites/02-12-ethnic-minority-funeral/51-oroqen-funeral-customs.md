# Oroqen Funeral Customs

## 1. Definition

The Oroqen are distributed across Hulunbuir, Inner Mongolia (the Oroqen Autonomous Banner) and Heilongjiang (Heihe, Greater Khingan). The traditional mode was **wind burial (tree burial)**, later gradually earth burial; cremation is also used (for special cases).

**Core elements**:
- **"Wind burial" (tree burial)** — the body is placed in birch-bark or a wooden coffin, set on a tree or a raised frame to weather; after several years the bones are collected for earth burial.
- **Shaman sends the soul** — in old times a shaman was invited to send the soul, performing the "soul-sending" rite.
- **The old horse-sacrifice custom** — in old custom the deceased's mount was sacrificed or a horse killed for the dead.
- **Birch-bark burial gear** — coffins made of birch bark and logs, a hunting-people characteristic.
- **Mourning taboos** — taboo song/dance; the mourning period has dress markers and taboos.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Ming–Qing**: the Oroqen ("Oroqen", "Biral") were distributed in the Heilongjiang basin; their funeral customs are recorded in ethnographies.
- **Qing dynasty**: the Oroqen incorporated into the Buteha Eight Banners were influenced by Manchu–Han; earth burial grew.
- **Contemporary**: after settling, earth burial is main; the traditional tree-burial custom survives in ethnographic records.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Dying**: family keep watch; inform the clan elders.
2. **Cleanse and dress**: cleanse the body, dress in new clothes.
3. **Keep the spirit and sacrifice**: lay out the corpse, set up the spirit; invite the shaman to send the soul.
4. **Funeral**: the old custom was wind burial (tree burial); later mostly earth burial.
5. **Burial or bone-collecting reburial**: earth burial builds a mound; tree burial collects the bones later for reburial.
6. **After-burial sacrifice**: round the grave, observe the anniversary sacrifice; sweep tombs at the New Year festival.

## 4. Regional and Subgroup Variants

- **Inner Mongolia Oroqen Autonomous Banner**: the settlement area, with fairly complete custom records.
- **Heilongjiang Heihe, Greater Khingan**: intermingled with Han and Daur; customs sinicised.
- **Relation to other peoples**: the Oroqen have no five-degree mourning system; mourning is bounded by taboos and the mourning period, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Religious objects**: shaman drum, shaman robe.
- **Burial gear**: birch-bark coffin, wooden coffin.
- **Funeral supplies**: animal hide, bow and crossbow (buried with men), horse (sacrificed).
