# Yao Funeral Customs

## 1. Definition

The Yao have many branches (Pan Yao, Bunu Yao, White-Trouser Yao, Chashan Yao etc.), distributed in Guangxi, Hunan, Guangdong, Yunnan, Guizhou. The burial is "**earth burial**" main; generally do "**secondary burial**".

**Core elements**:
- **"Shigong" (daogong) presides** — the shigong does the "deliver-the-dead" rite, chanting scripture, breaking the prison, sending the soul.
- **Fell the ox for the dead** — some branches (e.g. White-Trouser Yao) fell the ox to offer the dead, a solemn rite.
- **Sing the funeral song and jump the mourning** — sing the funeral song on the vigil; some branches do the "jump the mourning" dance.
- **"Duijie" tradition** — the Yao men's "duijie" (initiation) and the funeral norms come from the same shigong system.
- **Han-Zhuang influence** — do the "sevens", "hundred days", "anniversary".

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Tang-Song**: names like "Mo Yao" appear in histories; Yao ancestors' burial recorded in gazetteers.
- **Ming-Qing**: gazetteers and the Yao "Guoshan Bang" etc. reflect their funeral and migration; the Dayao Mountain (Jinxiu) Yao custom recorded in detail.
- **Modern**: branch customs differences stable; the shigong, daogong system developed.
- **Contemporary**: cremation promoted; village secondary burial remains.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Dying**: family keep watch; fire a gun or beat a drum to report death.
2. **Cleanse and dress**: cleanse; wear ethnic dress (women in finery).
3. **Encoffin and keep the spirit**: into the coffin, set the spirit; the shigong does rites, sings the funeral song.
4. **Fell the ox for the dead** (some branches): offer the ox; horns hung at the grave-front or door.
5. **Procession and bury**: choose a day to send, earth burial.
6. **Pick bones re-bury**: after years pick the bones into a jar, re-bury.
7. **After-burial sacrifice**: sweep at Qingming, Zhongyuan, New-Year; "hundred days", "anniversary" set the offering.

## 4. Regional and Subgroup Variants

- **Guangxi Jinxiu (Dayao Mountain)**: Chashan, Pan, Ao Yao etc. branches differ.
- **Guangxi Nandan (White-Trouser Yao)**: the fell-the-ox custom famous.
- **Hunan (Jiangyong, Jianghua)**: mixed with Han; higher sinicisation.
- **Yunnan, Guizhou**: mixed with Miao, Zhuang, Bouyei; customs penetrate.
- **Relation to other peoples**: the Yao do not take the five-degree system, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Burial gear**: coffin, bone-picking pottery jar.
- **Religious objects**: shigong instruments (bell, drum, mask, scripture manuscripts).
- **Funeral goods**: ethnic dress, silver ornaments, ox (sacrifice), wine.
- **Documents**: scripture manuscripts, the "Guoshan Bang" (migration document, ties to group memory).
