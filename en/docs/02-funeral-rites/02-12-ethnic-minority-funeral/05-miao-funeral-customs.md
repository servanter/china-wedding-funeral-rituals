# Miao Funeral Customs

## 1. Definition

The Miao are widely distributed (Guizhou, Yunnan, Hunan, Guangxi, Chongqing, Sichuan etc.) with many branches and great funeral differences, but the common faith core is "**sending the soul home to the ancestors**" — by rite send the dead soul back to the ancestral homeland.

**Main mode**: earth burial main; some areas keep "**rock burial (cave burial, hanging coffin)**" relics; a few areas cremate.

**Core rites**:
- **"Opening the road"** — the shaman (priest) chants the *Guide Scripture* or sings the "road-opening song", pointing the dead soul's route back to the homeland.
- **Lusheng and drum** — accompany the mourning with lusheng and wooden drum; the southeast-Guizhou Miao "drum society" ties to the funeral.
- **"Beating the ox" (felling the ox)** — some areas sacrifice an ox for the dead; blood kin share "tuotuo meat".

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin to Tang-Song**: the Miao ancestors' "San Miao", "Nan Man" burial and migration memory merged; the "sending the soul" idea matches the group's migration history.
- **Ming-Qing**: gazetteers (Guizhou, Hunan general gazetteers) "Miao custom" record the rites; "drum society", "yilang" organisations bore the funeral.
- **Modern**: Christianity reached some Miao areas; a few families hold church funerals.
- **Contemporary**: cremation promoted; the "opening the road", "beating the ox" customs remain in southeast Guizhou, west Hunan.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Dying**: family keep watch; fire a gun or blow the lusheng to report death.
2. **Cleanse and dress**: cleanse with clear water; wear festive dress (women wear finery, silver ornaments).
3. **Keep the spirit and open the road**: invite the priest to "open the road", chant the soul-sending scripture; kin condole, blow lusheng, beat the drum.
4. **Beat the ox for the dead** (some areas): slaughter the ox to offer; hang the horns at the door or grave-front.
5. **Procession and bury**: choose a day to bury; the grave mostly by the mountain; some villages set a public graveyard.
6. **After-burial sacrifice**: hold the closing rite of the "sending the soul"; sweep at Qingming, New-Year, the New-Rice Festival.

## 4. Regional and Subgroup Variants

- **Southeast Guizhou (Taijiang, Leishan, Jianhe)**: "drum society" ties closely to the funeral; lusheng, wooden drum flourish.
- **West Hunan (Xiangxi prefecture)**: near Tujia, Han; "opening the road" and "jump the mourning" coexist.
- **Northwest Guizhou, northeast Yunnan (Bijie, Zhaotong)**: near the Yi; rites influenced by Yi custom.
- **South Yunnan, north Guangxi**: mixed with Zhuang, Yao, Bouyei; customs penetrate.
- **Relation to other peoples**: the Miao have no "five degrees" system; kin mourning is limited by taboos and term, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Instruments**: lusheng, wooden drum, bronze drum.
- **Funeral goods**: festive dress, silver ornaments, ox (sacrifice), wine, glutinous rice.
- **Religious objects**: sacrifice gear, the soul-sending scripture (oral).
- **Grave marks**: ox horn, grave marker, stone stele (more in recent times).
