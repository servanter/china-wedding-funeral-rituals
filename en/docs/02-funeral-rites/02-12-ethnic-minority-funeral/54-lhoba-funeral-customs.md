# Lhoba Funeral Customs

## 1. Definition

The Lhoba are concentrated in southeastern Tibet (Mainling, Medog, Zayu, Lhunze). Earth burial is the main mode, with **tree burial** (mostly for the prematurely dead) and cremation (for special status) as well.

**Core elements**:
- **The "Niubo" (shaman) presides** — the shaman "Niubo" or "Bamu" presides over divination and soul-sending.
- **"Divination to choose the site"** — choose the burial site and auspicious day by chicken-bone divination, a marked feature.
- **Burial of production tools and ornaments** — men are buried with bow and crossbow, knife; women with ornaments and weaving tools.
- **"Soul-sending"** — show the deceased's soul the way, sending it back to the ancestors' original home.
- **Mourning taboos** — forbid farming and song/dance; the village performs purification rites.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Tang–Song to Ming–Qing**: the Lhoba forebears' burial customs are scattered in Tibetan-language documents and frontier-defense/geographical records.
- **Modern**: because of the closed gorge, traditional customs were preserved longer.
- **Contemporary**: the traditional rites grow simpler; ethnographic records are important.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Dying**: family keep watch; inform the clan elders.
2. **Cleanse and dress**: cleanse the body, dress in new clothes.
3. **Keep the spirit and divine**: lay out the corpse, set up the spirit; the niubo divines to choose the burial site and auspicious day.
4. **Funeral and burial**: choose a site for earth burial (the prematurely dead may have tree burial).
5. **Burying the goods**: bury the production tools and ornaments with the dead.
6. **After-burial sacrifice**: round the grave and conclude the soul-sending; worship ancestors at the New Year festival.

## 4. Regional and Subgroup Variants

- **Mainling, Lhunze**: more concentrated; fairly complete custom records.
- **Medog, Zayu**: intermingled with Monba and Tibetan; customs interpenetrate.
- **Relation to other peoples**: the Lhoba have no five-degree mourning system; mourning is bounded by taboos and the mourning period, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Religious objects**: niubo ritual implements, divination tools (chicken bones, etc.).
- **Funeral supplies**: wooden coffin, bamboo wrapping, sacrificial livestock, wine.
- **Burial goods**: bow and crossbow, knife, ornaments, weaving tools.
