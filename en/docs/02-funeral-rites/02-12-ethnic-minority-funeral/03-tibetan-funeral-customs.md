# Tibetan Funeral Customs

## 1. Definition

Tibetan funeral is led by "**Tibetan Buddhism**"; the mode of burial is graded by the dead's status and cause of death:

| Mode | For | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Stupa burial** | The Dalai, Panchen and great living Buddhas | The body is treated and enshrined in a stupa |
| **Cremation** | Living Buddhas, high lamas, the upper class | After burning, ashes into a stupa or scattered |
| **Sky burial (bird burial)** | Ordinary believers (most common) | The body is given on the sky-burial platform for vultures; eaten clean is auspicious "rising to heaven" |
| **Water burial** | Some areas, infants, contagious-disease dead | The body thrown into rivers |
| **Earth burial** | Contagious-disease dead, those killed in brawls | Folk see it as an "ill" mode |

**Core idea**: "**deliverance**" is key — the 49 days after death is the "intermediate state" (bardo); kin invite monks to chant, give alms, release life, offer lamps, to help the soul reborn in a good path.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Tubo period**: Bon and Buddhist faiths ran parallel; diverse burial modes.
- **After the later spread**: the Tibetan-Buddhist schools held the deliverance rites; sky burial gradually became mainstream, with fixed regional platforms.
- **Ming-Qing to modern**: under the church-state system, stupa and cremation matched status grades.
- **Contemporary**: sky burial is protected by law and custom; onlookers and filming forbidden; townspeople mostly cremate.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Dying**: invite monks to chant, counsel the dead to let go; family do not wail, lest they disturb the consciousness.
2. **Lay out the body**: wrap in white cloth, place in a corner of the room; monks chant continuously (often three to seven days, or 49).
3. **Procession**: the "body-bearer" (sky-burial master) chooses an auspicious time to carry out, not straight from the door (or open another hole), no turning back.
4. **Perform the burial**: on the sky-burial platform the master dismembers; cremation builds a furnace to burn; water burial throws into the river.
5. **After-burial deliverance**: within 49 days family chant, offer lamps, give alms; invite monks to "dedicate".
6. **Sacrifice**: on the death-day chant and give alms for the dead; "butter lamp" and "tsampa" are common offerings.

## 4. Regional and Subgroup Variants

- **Ü-Tsang (Lhasa, Shigatse)**: sky burial main; large platforms.
- **Amdo (Qinghai, Gansu, northwest Sichuan)**: sky, cremation, water burial coexist.
- **Kham (Garzê Sichuan, Diqing Yunnan)**: cremation and sky burial both stressed.
- **Relation to Han**: Tibetans have no "five degrees" concept; kin mourning is limited by religious taboos and the mourning term (mostly 49 days), and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Religious objects**: rosary, prayer-wheel, butter-lamp, prayer-flag, mani pile, wind-horse flag.
- **Funeral goods**: white cloth (wrap), tsampa (offering), barley wine, juniper (bsang smoke).
- **Documents**: deliverance scriptures, prayer texts, the dead's name-list (for the assembly dedication).
