# Jing Funeral Customs

## 1. Definition

The Jing are concentrated in Fangchenggang, Guangxi (the three islands of Wanwei, Wutou and Shanxin in Dongxing). Earth burial is the main mode, and the **second burial** (collecting the bones into a jar for reburial) is widely practised.

**Core elements**:
- **Presided over by the "Daogong"** — the daogong performs the rites to deliver the dead, choosing the day and site.
- **Singing the funeral song** — sing funeral songs during the wake.
- **The "Ha Festival" ancestor worship** — the Jing "Ha Festival" is an important festival for sacrificing to the gods and ancestors, sharing the same idea as funeral ancestor worship.
- **Han–Vietnamese influence** — the funeral customs combine the Han (observing the seventh days) with the Vietnamese tradition.
- **Mourning taboos** — forbid song, dance and marriage; perform purification rites.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Ming–Qing**: the Jing forebears migrated from the Tuoshan area of Vietnam to the islands of the Beibu Gulf, forming the three-island settlement.
- **Modern**: close contact with the Han; the funeral customs are highly sinicised.
- **Contemporary**: cremation is promoted; the rural second burial still survives.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Dying**: family keep watch; fire crackers to announce the death.
2. **Cleanse and dress**: cleanse the body, dress in new clothes.
3. **Encoffin and keep the spirit**: place in the coffin, set up the spirit; the daogong performs rites and sings the funeral song.
4. **Observe the seventh days and perform rites**: set offerings every seven days.
5. **Funeral and burial**: choose a day to send the burial, earth burial.
6. **Collect the bones for reburial**: after several years, collect the bones into a jar and choose a site for reburial.
7. **After-burial sacrifice**: observe the seventh days and the "anniversary"; sweep tombs at Qingming and the Ha Festival.

## 4. Regional and Subgroup Variants

- **Wanwei, Wutou, Shanxin (the three islands)**: the core settlement area, using the Jing language.
- **Dongxing, Fangcheng**: intermingled with Han; customs merge.
- **Relation to other peoples**: the Jing have no five-degree mourning system; mourning is bounded by taboos and the mourning period, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Burial gear**: coffin, bone-collecting pottery jar.
- **Religious objects**: daogong ritual implements, scriptures.
- **Funeral supplies**: paper effigies, paper money, sacrificial livestock, wine, the single-string zither (a distinctive Jing instrument, used in festivals and sacrificial rites).
