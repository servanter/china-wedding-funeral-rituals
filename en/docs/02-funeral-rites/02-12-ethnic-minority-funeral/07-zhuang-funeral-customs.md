# Zhuang Funeral Customs

## 1. Definition

The Zhuang are the most populous minority, mainly in Guangxi, with branches in Yunnan, Guangdong, Guizhou. The burial is "**earth burial**" main, and generally does "**secondary burial**" (after years "pick the bones" into a jar re-bury), in common with the Han south-China custom.

**Faith and rites**:
- **"Daogong", "Shigong" preside** — the folk-Daoist-coloured "daogong" or "shigong" hosts the funeral liturgy.
- **Sing the funeral song** — sing the funeral song in Zhuang, telling the dead's life and filial duty.
- **Han influence** — generally do the "sevens", "hundred days", "anniversary" sacrifice terms, reflecting Han-Zhuang blend.
- **"Third of March" and ancestor worship** — the song-fair festival ties to ancestor worship.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin to Tang-Song**: the "Luoyue", "Xiou" ancestors' burial archaeological remains (e.g. stone spades, bronze drums as grave goods).
- **Since Tang-Song**: Daoism and Buddhism entered in succession, merged with native belief to form the "daogong", "shigong" tradition.
- **Ming-Qing**: gazetteers (Guangxi general gazetteer, prefecture/county gazetteers) "customs" record the rites; tusi areas had grade differences.
- **Contemporary**: cremation promoted; secondary burial remains in villages.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Dying**: family keep watch; fire a cannon or beat a drum to report death.
2. **Cleanse and dress**: cleanse with clear water or pomelo-leaf water; wear new clothes.
3. **Encoffin and keep the spirit**: into the coffin, set the spirit; invite the daogong for rites, sing the funeral song.
4. **Do the sevens, do the altar**: an offering every seven days; "hit the zhai", "break the prison" liturgy.
5. **Procession and bury**: choose a day to send; earth burial; some areas "keep the coffin awaiting burial", choose another day to bury.
6. **Pick bones re-bury**: after years open the coffin, pick the bones into the "gold jar (gold urn)" to re-bury.
7. **After-burial sacrifice**: sweep at Qingming, Third of March, Zhongyuan; "hundred days", "anniversary" set the offering.

## 4. Regional and Subgroup Variants

- **Central Guangxi (Nanning, Laibin, Liuzhou)**: complete daogong liturgy; doing the sevens and secondary burial parallel.
- **West Guangxi (Baise, Hechi)**: mixed with Yao, Dong, Bouyei; customs penetrate.
- **North Guangxi (Guilin)**: near Hunan; with Hunan traces.
- **Yunnan Zhuang (Wenshan)**: mixed with Miao, Yao, Hani; varied.
- **Relation to other peoples**: Zhuang custom and Han custom influence each other, but kin mourning does not take the five degrees, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Burial gear**: coffin, gold jar (gold urn, for bone-picking).
- **Religious objects**: daogong instruments (bell, drum, scriptures), incense and candle, talisman.
- **Funeral goods**: paper effigies, paper money, offerings (glutinous rice, chicken, wine).
- **Documents**: obituary, liturgy text, genealogy.
