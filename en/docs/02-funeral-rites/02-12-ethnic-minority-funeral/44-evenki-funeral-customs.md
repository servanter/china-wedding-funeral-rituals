# Evenki Funeral Customs

## 1. Definition

The Evenki are distributed across Hulunbuir, Inner Mongolia (the Evenki Autonomous Banner, Genhe, New Barag Left Banner) and Heilongjiang. Because livelihood (herding, hunting, farming) and living environment differ, the funeral customs vary greatly; earth burial is the main mode, with **tree burial and wind burial** (for shamans or special cases) as well.

**Core elements**:
- **Shaman sends the soul** — in old times a shaman was invited to preside over sending the soul; some herding areas, under Tibetan-Buddhist influence, invited lamas to chant.
- **The old horse-sacrifice custom** — in old herding custom the deceased's mount was sacrificed or a symbolic rite performed.
- **"Wind burial / tree burial"** — the hunting areas and the reindeer branch (Aoluguya) wrapped the body in birch bark and placed it on a tree, later collecting the bones for reburial.
- **"Spirit sacrifice" and funeral songs** — keep the spirit and sacrifice; sing funeral songs.
- **Mourning taboos** — taboo song and dance, taboo marriage; perform purification rites.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Ming–Qing**: the Evenki ("Solon", "reindeer branch", etc.) lived separately in Heilongjiang and Hulunbuir; their funeral customs are recorded in ethnographies.
- **Qing dynasty**: the Solon branch, incorporated into the Eight Banners, was influenced by Manchu–Han; herding areas were influenced by Tibetan Buddhism.
- **Contemporary**: cremation is promoted; the traditional hunting-area burial customs draw attention under cultural protection.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Dying**: family keep watch; inform the clan elders.
2. **Cleanse and dress**: cleanse the body, dress in new clothes.
3. **Keep the spirit and sacrifice**: lay out the corpse, set up the spirit; invite the shaman (or lama) to chant.
4. **Funeral**: the hunting areas kept the old tree/wind burial; herding areas practised earth burial.
5. **Burial or bone-collecting reburial**: earth burial builds a mound; tree burial collects the bones later for reburial.
6. **After-burial sacrifice**: round the grave, observe the anniversary sacrifice; sweep tombs at Qingming and the New Year festival.

## 4. Regional and Subgroup Variants

- **Herding areas (Evenki Autonomous Banner)**: mainly earth burial; lama chanting may be seen.
- **Hunting areas (Genhe Aoluguya)**: the reindeer Evenki keep the separate tree-burial and wind-burial tradition.
- **Farming areas and Heilongjiang**: intermingled with Han and Daur; customs sinicised.
- **Relation to other peoples**: the Evenki have no five-degree mourning system; mourning is bounded by taboos and the mourning period, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Religious objects**: shaman drum, shaman robe (old times); lama scriptures, ritual implements.
- **Funeral supplies**: birch bark, animal hide, wooden coffin, sacrificial livestock (reindeer, horse).
- **Documents**: obituary, clan genealogy.
