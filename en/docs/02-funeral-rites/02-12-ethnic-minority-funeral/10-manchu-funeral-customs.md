# Manchu Funeral Customs

## 1. Definition

The Manchu mainly distribute in Liaoning, Jilin, Heilongjiang, Hebei, Beijing. Before entering the pass they cremated; after entering they gradually "**earth-buried**", keeping several native marked rites.

**Core elements**:
- **"Hanging the red banner"** — after death set a red-cloth banner at the door ("red banner", "soul-leading banner"), the most marked sign of Manchu funeral; at the procession the banner leads.
- **Shaman presides** — old custom invited the shaman to dance and send the soul (after entering, mostly replaced by monks and Daoists).
- **"Banner coffin"** — the coffin form has Manchu traits (ridged lid etc.).
- **"Burning the meal"** — old custom burned offerings for the dead; later merged with the Han "burning paper".
- **Han influence** — generally do the "sevens", "hundred days", "anniversary"; rites near North-China Han.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Ming**: the Jurchen tribes had cremation, human sacrifice (horse, servant, later abolished) etc.
- **Qing**: after entering the pass, under Han ritual influence turned to earth burial main; court and banner funerals recorded in the *Comprehensive Rituals of the Great Qing*.
- **Modern**: banner custom merged with Beijing folk custom, forming the "Manchu-Han blended" North-China funeral.
- **Contemporary**: cremation common; the red banner etc. partly remain in Northeast villages (mostly changed to white banner).

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **First passing**: move the body, cleanse, dress; set the red banner at the door.
2. **Keep the spirit**: set the mourning shed, the spirit; invite monks and Daoists to chant (old invited the shaman).
3. **Encoffin**: minor and great encoffining; small grave goods in the coffin.
4. **Open condolence**: the filial son keeps the spirit; kin condole.
5. **Procession**: led by the red banner (or white banner); send, bury.
6. **After-burial sacrifice**: do the sevens, "hundred days", "anniversary"; sweep at Qingming, Zhongyuan, the first of the tenth month; the "burning the meal" (burning offerings) custom merged into burning paper.

## 4. Regional and Subgroup Variants

- **Liaodong, north Liaoning Manchu areas**: more old custom kept; red banner, banner coffin seen.
- **Beijing banners**: merged with Beijing custom; refined rites.
- **Jilin, Heilongjiang**: mixed with Han, Korean; rites simplified.
- **Relation to other peoples**: Manchu and Han customs are deeply blended, but the shaman faith and red banner etc. are still native tradition, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Marks**: red banner (soul-leading banner), banner coffin.
- **Religious objects**: shaman drum, shaman hat (old); monk-Daoist instruments (later).
- **Funeral goods**: paper effigies, paper money, offerings.
- **Documents**: obituary, mourning placard, genealogy ("family tree", "genealogy book").
