# Russian Funeral Customs

## 1. Definition

The Russian people are scattered across Xinjiang (Yining, Tacheng, Urumqi), Inner Mongolia (Hulunbuir) and Heilongjiang. Most believe in **Eastern Orthodoxy**, and the funeral follows the Orthodox liturgy — a rather special case among the peoples.

**Core elements**:
- **Priest presides** — an Eastern Orthodox priest presides over the funeral and chants prayers.
- **"Icon" and prayer** — an icon and candle are placed before the spirit; relatives pray.
- **Coffin burial** — after encoffining, the body is sent to burial and a cross is raised before the grave.
- **"Requiem"** — on the 9th and 40th days after death and the anniversary, requiem prayers (prayers for the repose of the soul) are held.
- **Mourning taboos** — during mourning, song, dance and entertainment are forbidden; relatives wear plain-coloured clothes.
- **Some adopt secular funerals** — in cities, the funeral may also be simplified to a mortuary arrangement.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Qing dynasty**: Russians moved into China (the Yili, Tacheng and Hulunbuir areas) in successive waves.
- **Republican period**: stable Russian settlement communities formed; Orthodox churches took charge of wedding and funeral rites.
- **Contemporary**: religious activity is protected by law; the funeral proceeds between Orthodox tradition and local custom.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Dying**: invite the priest to pray and receive communion (where conditions allow).
2. **Cleanse and dress**: cleanse the body, dress in plain new clothes.
3. **Encoffin and keep the spirit**: place in the coffin; set an icon and candle before the spirit; relatives keep the wake in prayer.
4. **Funeral**: hold the funeral at the church or the graveyard; the priest chants and sprinkles holy water.
5. **Burial**: inter the body in the earth; raise a cross before the grave.
6. **Requiem**: hold prayers for the repose of the soul on the 9th and 40th days and the anniversary.

## 4. Regional and Subgroup Variants

- **Xinjiang (Yining, Tacheng, Urumqi)**: more concentrated; Orthodox rites are better preserved.
- **Inner Mongolia (Hulunbuir)**: intermingled with Han, Mongol and Evenki.
- **Heilongjiang, Beijing**: urban funerals grow simpler.
- **Relation to other peoples**: the Russian funeral belongs to the Orthodox cultural system, unrelated to the Han five-degree system or the native funeral rites; it must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Religious objects**: icon, candle, cross, holy water, prayer book.
- **Funeral supplies**: coffin, plain-coloured clothing, wreath.
- **Graveyard**: Orthodox cemetery, cross tombstone.
