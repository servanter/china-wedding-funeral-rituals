# Behavioural Taboos in the Mourning Period

## 1. Definition

The behavioural restrictions during mourning were called "the rites of dwelling in mourning" in the old rites, and became concrete taboos in folk custom.

| Class | Taboo |
| --- | --- |
| **Body** | Taboo on **haircut, shaving** ("keep the hair a hundred days" — men do not shave head or face, women do not dress) |
| **Adornment** | Taboo on powder, ornaments, coloured clothes |
| **Entertainment** | Taboo on watching plays, listening to music, cards, feasting; taboo on making music at home |
| **Going about** | Taboo on **visiting** (the filial son does not cross others' thresholds), taboo on visiting kin and friends, taboo on going to the new mother's house |
| **Living** | Taboo on conjugal relations ("in mourning, not within"); taboo on sitting others' beds, taboo on sitting the upper seat |
| **Objects** | Taboo on **mirrors** (some areas); taboo on sitting the threshold; taboo on touching others' heads |
| **Speech** | Taboo on saying "goodbye", "congratulations"; taboo on calling by name or surname; taboo on jesting at the bereaved's |
| **Travel** | Some areas taboo on long travel in mourning, taboo on boat (not crossing) |

**"The filial son does not cross the threshold"** is among the most common taboos: the mourner does not enter others' houses, lest he "bring mourning breath" to them.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the *Liji* ("Quli", "Jianzhuan") fixes the behavioural degrees of dwelling in mourning.
- **Han-Tang**: entered the law with penalties; "making music in mourning", "marrying in mourning" had clear articles.
- **Song-Ming**: the *Family Rituals* standardises mourning behaviour; Neo-Confucianism strengthened it.
- **Ming-Qing**: gazetteers heavily record "no shaving in mourning", "the filial son does not visit".
- **Since the near age**: villages keep more; cities only keep "not attending happy events", "no entertainment".

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **After assuming the dress**: stop adornment, entertainment, socialising.
2. **Keep the hair**: men do not shave, women do not dress (a hundred days or until removing the dress).
3. **Dwell in quiet mourning**: stay behind closed doors, no visiting, no long travel.
4. **Stepwise lifting**: the fifth seven, hundred days, anniversary gradually lift some restrictions.
5. **Remove the dress**: behaviour back to normal.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: stresses "no shaving for a hundred days", "the filial son does not visit".
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: in mourning do not attend happy events, do not enter temples (some areas excepted).
- **Jiangnan**: mourning-behaviour restrictions are mostly "no feasting", "no music".
- **Ethnic minorities**: each people's behavioural taboos differ (no hunting, no song-dance, no labour for a set term), their basis the people's own faith and mode of living, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Related objects**: mourning dress, mourning belt, spirit-tablet.
- **Documents**: the mourning-matters sheet (listing taboos and lifting nodes).
- **Literary basis**: *Liji* "Quli", the *Family Rituals*.

## 6. Sources

- *Liji*, "Quli"; *Liji*, "Jianzhuan"
