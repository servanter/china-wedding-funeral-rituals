# Auspice-Seeking and Evil-Warding Customs

## 1. Definition

The funeral is seen as an "inauspicious affair", so one must dissolve it with **evil-warding objects and rites**, praying to "seek the auspicious, avoid the inauspicious".

| Custom | Practice | Explanation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Peach branch, willow twig** | Stuck at the door, placed by the coffin or in the filial son's hand | Peach wood is "the essence of the five woods", drives ghosts |
| **Cinnabar, chicken blood** | Used in dotting the lord, breaking earth, drawing talismans | Red wards evil; blood is living breath |
| **Talismans** | Pasted on door, coffin, mourning dress | Daoist talisman law suppresses evil |
| **Mirror, scissors** | Hung at the door or by the coffin (some areas) | Reflect evil breath |
| **Glutinous rice, white rice, salt** | Scattered in the coffin, on the road | Drive evil, suppress sha |
| **Soul-leading rooster** | A rooster leads the way at the procession | "the golden rooster leads the way" |
| **Cross the fire basin, cross the bridge** | On returning from the burial, cross fire, cross the "peace bridge" | Fire cleanses the body, cuts off ill breath |
| **Clean the house** | After burial, sprinkle the house with incense and talisman water | Clear the mourning breath |
| **Sugar, red egg on returning** | On returning from the burial, give sugar, red eggs | "calm the fright", "turn to auspicious" |
| **Mourning-cloth "return gift"** | Give kin and friends white cloth, sugar | "scatter the mourning" to share the ill breath |

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the *Zhouli*'s Fangxiang driving pestilence, and the peach-whisk (peach-branch broom) rite already show evil-warding use.
- **Han-Wei**: the *Jingchu Suishi Ji* records peach-charms, mugwort, calamus and other evil-warding objects.
- **Tang-Song**: Daoist talismans, Buddhist rites joined the funeral, the evil-warding custom systematised.
- **Ming-Qing**: gazetteers heavily record "cleaning the house after bereavement", "crossing fire", "scattering the mourning".
- **Since the near age**: villages keep it complete; cities keep "crossing fire", "eating sugar", "white-parcel return gift".

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Set the guard**: at first bereavement stick peach branches, paste talismans at the door.
2. **Encoffin**: put five grains, paper money, small peach-wood pieces in the coffin.
3. **Procession**: the soul-leading rooster leads, scatter paper money.
4. **Bury**: at breaking earth sprinkle chicken blood or cinnabar.
5. **Return**: cross the fire basin, cross the bridge, clean the house, scatter sugar — complete "turning to auspicious".

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: on returning from the procession "cross the fire basin"; the bereaved "scatter sugar".
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: on the return "clean the body"; at the door a "pure-water basin" and peach branches.
- **Jiangnan**: "scatter the mourning" and "eat sugar" are common; do not enter the house directly on return.
- **Ethnic minorities**: each people's warding ways are rich (e.g. the Tibetan burn juniper, the Yi *bimo* chant to drive evil, the Dai pour water), their belief system not the same source as the Han warding custom, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Warding objects**: peach branch, willow twig, cinnabar, talisman, mirror, scissors, rooster, rice and salt.
- **Rite objects**: fire basin, pure-water basin, red egg, sugar.
- **Documents**: talisman paper, the house-cleaning petition.
