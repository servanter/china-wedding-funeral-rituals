# Social Taboos for the Mourner

## 1. Definition

The mourner's social intercourse is strictly limited, the core being "**not mixing with happy rites**".

| Occasion | Taboo |
| --- | --- |
| **Marriage** | "**In the parents' mourning, no marriage for three years**"; within mourning do not arrange marriage for children, send betrothal, wed (the old law punished) |
| **Feasts** | Taboo on attending happy feasts, taboo on drinking and making merry ("in mourning, no feasting") |
| **New-Year visits** | The bereaved **do not pay New-Year visits, do not paste red couplets** at Spring Festival (paste white or blue couplets); kin too do not come to congratulate |
| **Congratulating** | Taboo on sending congratulatory gifts, taboo on attending full-month, birthday, house-moving celebrations |
| **Gifts** | Taboo on red-paper parcels, red gift boxes; use a white parcel for the funeral gift |
| **Visiting** | Taboo on entering the new mother's room, the sick's room ("mourning breath dashing joy"); taboo on joining house-beam-raising, opening |
| **Going about** | The filial son does not on his own cross others' thresholds; others too mostly do not come (not to clash) |
| **Sacrifice** | "The mourner sacrifices not" — the mourner does not join temple or hall sacrifices |

**Exceptions and accommodations**: those who objectively cannot keep it have stepwise lifting after "a hundred days", "removing the dress"; also "borrowing mourning" (wed first, make up the mourning dress later).

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the *Liji* ("Quli") "in mourning, speak not of music", "in the parents' mourning, no marriage for three years".
- **Han-Tang**: entered the law — "marrying in mourning" was a crime, the Tang code punished it clearly.
- **Song-Ming**: ritual law and clan rules strengthened; genealogies' "house rules" mostly list this taboo.
- **Ming-Qing**: gazetteers record "the bereaved paste no red couplets for three years", "the filial son does not congratulate the year".
- **Since the near age**: villages keep more; cities simplify to "in mourning, no feasts, no fireworks".

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Assume the dress**: announce the mourning social taboos (the steward informs kin and friends).
2. **Decline happy rites**: politely decline happy invitations.
3. **Spring Festival handling**: paste no red couplets, set off no firecrackers, pay no New-Year visits.
4. **Stepwise lifting**: the fifth seven, hundred days, anniversary, removing the dress as nodes.
5. **Remove the dress**: social intercourse back to normal.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the bereaved paste blue or white couplets at Spring Festival, no New-Year visits for three years.
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: the bereaved "hold no bustle for three years", do not go to others' homes for New-Year.
- **Jiangnan**: in mourning, no feasts, no happy events is main.
- **Ethnic minorities**: social taboos differ (taboo on certain gatherings, on song-dance), their basis the people's own faith, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Marks**: mourning dress, mourning belt, white parcel (funeral-gift parcel), blue/white couplets.
- **Documents**: the declination card, the notice sheet.
- **Law basis**: the "marrying in mourning" articles in the codes of each age.
