# Length of Lying-in-State and the Sevens

## 1. Definition

The number of days of lying in state (staying the coffin) and its relation to the "seven" is an important part of timing the funeral.

**Days of lying in state**:
- The common run is "**three, five, seven**" days (mostly odd, colloquially "arrange three", "arrange five", "arrange seven").
- Elders and wealthy families may stay "seven sevens" (49 days) or even a hundred days ("the coffin period", "putting the wood aside").
- Those taboo on an even-day procession explain it as "odd numbers are yang".

**The "seven" system**: from the day of death, every seven days is one "seven", ending at "seven sevens" (49 days), called "**doing the sevens**" or "burning the sevens". Buddhism takes the "intermediate existence" of 49 days as its basis (the *Shi Shi Yao Lan* etc.); Daoism too has a seven-based arrangement for delivering the dead.

| Node | Rite |
| --- | --- |
| **First seven** | The filial son sets the offering, invites monks and Daoists to chant |
| **Third seven, fifth seven** | Solemn; the "fifth seven" is the key of "passing the sevens" |
| **Seven sevens (breaking the seven)** | Remove the spirit, burn the furnishings, the funeral ends a stage |

**Related taboos**: taboo on "hitting the seven" (the death day clashing with a seven), taboo on holding a happy event on a "seven" day, taboo on "crashing the seven" (the procession day clashing with a seven).

## 2. Historical Origins

- **After Buddhism entered**: the 49-day intermediate-existence teaching joined the native funeral custom, forming "doing the sevens".
- **Tang-Song**: the custom of doing the sevens was already very common (*Dongjing Menghua Lu*, *Mengliang Lu* record it).
- **Ming-Qing**: doing the sevens joined Buddhist and Daoist rites, becoming the backbone of the funeral.
- **Since the near age**: villages do the sevens fully; cities simplify to "first seven", "fifth seven" or only mourn at the funeral home.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Fix the lying-in days**: the yinyang master chooses, usually three, five, seven days.
2. **Start the sevens**: from the death day set the offering for the "first seven".
3. **Offer each seven**: an offering every seven days, inviting monks and Daoists to chant.
4. **Fifth seven**: a solemn offering (some areas treat the "fifth seven" as the "great seven").
5. **Seven sevens, break the seven**: remove the spirit, burn the spirit-tablet, take down the mourning hall.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: stresses the "fifth seven", kin and friends gather at the grave.
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: does the sevens strictly; the "sixth seven" is offered by the daughter ("daughter's seven").
- **Jiangnan**: does the sevens with both Buddhist and Daoist rites; the "fifth seven" is key.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples influenced by Buddhism also do the "seven" offering (e.g. the Tibetan take "seven sevens" as the deliverance period), but most peoples' funeral timing follows their own faith and mode of production, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Offerings and ritual instruments**: incense and candle, offerings, scriptures, ritual instruments.
- **Documents**: the "doing-the-sevens sheet" (listing the seven-sevens dates and rites), the day-sheet.
- **Literary basis**: the *Shi Shi Yao Lan*, the *Xieji Bianfang Shu*.
