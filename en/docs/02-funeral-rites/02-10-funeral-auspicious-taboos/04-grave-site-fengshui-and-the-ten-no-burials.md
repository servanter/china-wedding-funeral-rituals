# Grave-Site Fengshui and the Ten No-Burials

## 1. Definition

The siting of the yin-dwelling (grave) takes "**fengshui**" as its basis, stressing the matching of the five elements "**dragon, sand, water, cave, facing**", with several **absolutely avoided places**.

**Folk "Ten No-Burials"** (phrased variously by place, the common run):

1. Not bury at a **rushing-water shoal**;
2. Not bury **before a god, behind a temple**;
3. Not bury where **left and right are spent** (the terrain is cut off);
4. Not bury on a **tangled hill ridge** (the veins are unclear);
5. Not bury where the **fengshui is mournful** (the wind and water sound bleak);
6. Not bury where the **seat is low and small** (the cave is low and pressed);
7. Not bury at the **sharp head of dragon and tiger** (the left-right sand is sharp);
8. Not bury on a **lone hilltop** (a lone peak with no support);
9. Not bury where **no grass grows**;
10. Not bury in a **tangle of rocks**, **old pits of ditches and kilns**.

**Facing and sand-water**: "sitting north facing south" is common; stress "an open bright hall", "water gathers at heaven's heart"; taboo on "water rushing, road rushing, pagoda rushing" and other "sha".

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Han**: the art of geomancy and the "form-school" works (the *Han Shu* "Yi Wen Zhi" lists the *Gong Zhai Di Xing* etc.).
- **Wei-Jin**: the *Book of Burial* attributed to Guo Pu put forth the "riding the living breath" teaching, the ancestor of later fengshui.
- **Tang-Song**: the form-school (Jiangxi school) and the liqi-school (Fujian school) parted; the yin-dwelling fengshui theory matured.
- **Ming-Qing**: fengshui joined burial extremely closely; cases of contesting fengshui and moving graves were endless.
- **Since the near age**: villages still stress "looking at the land" (inviting a geomancer to fix the cave); city public cemeteries are mostly by planning, the facing idea lingering.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Invite the geomancer**: invite a geomancer to seek the land.
2. **Look at the land**: read the dragon veins, observe the sand-water, fix the cave-field and facing.
3. **Divine the day, break earth**: choose an auspicious day to break earth ("open the mountain").
4. **Lower and orient**: by the compass (luopan) fix the facing, the coffin enters the cave.
5. **Round the grave, raise the stele**: seal the earth, raise the stele, the grave facing matches the stele facing.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Jiangnan**: the fengshui (form-school) is fine, stressing "water methods".
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: the liqi-school prevails, the compass orientation is precise; stress the "ancestral-grave fengshui".
- **North China**: plain areas mostly "read the earth veins", "discuss the direction".
- **Ethnic minorities**: siting ways vary (e.g. the Tibetan choose land by star-calculation, some peoples fix the cave by divination), their belief system not the same source as the Han fengshui, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: compass (luopan), level, boundary stakes.
- **Literature**: the *Book of Burial* attributed to Guo Pu, the *Xieji Bianfang Shu*, the various schools' "Geography" works.
- **Documents**: the land deed, the grave map, the mountain-facing sheet.
