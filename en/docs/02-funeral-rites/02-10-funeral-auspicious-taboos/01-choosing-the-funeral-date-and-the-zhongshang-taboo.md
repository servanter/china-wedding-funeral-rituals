# Choosing the Funeral Date and the Zhongshang Taboo

## 1. Definition

Choosing the funeral date ("picking the day", "selecting the term") is done by a yinyang master (*yinyang xiansheng*) from the almanac and calendar, the chief concern being to avoid "**zhongshang**" (double bereavement) — folk belief holds that if the procession and burial fall on a zhongshang day, the family will meet death again.

| Item | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Zhongshang day** | A taboo day reckoned by the heavenly stems and earthly branches, on which the procession risks another death |
| **Three-Sang day** | Taboo on three successive days each bearing a funeral |
| **Four Li, Four Jue days** | Taboo days just before the two equinoxes and two solstices |
| **Monthly break, yearly break** | Days clashing with the current month's or year's earthly branch |
| **Yanggong taboo days** | Thirteen taboo days traditionally fixed by Yang Yunsong of the Tang |
| **Empty-and-dead day** | The "旬空" day in the stem-branch calendar, taboo for burial |

**Remedies**: folk have "**cutting the zhongshang**" (using a paper figure, paper coffin, or chicken as a substitute for the dead, choosing another moment to burn it), "writing a zhongshang talisman and pasting it", "sprinkling chicken blood on the ground at breaking earth", "smashing the ceramic basin" and the like.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Han**: the art of choosing days already appears in the *Day Book* (Shuihudi Qin slips' *Rishu* has funeral do's and don'ts), an early form of funeral date-choosing.
- **Tang-Song**: the yinyang five-elements date-choosing learning flourished; the *Book of Burial* (attributed to Guo Pu) spread.
- **Qing**: the *Xieji Bianfang Shu* (compiled by imperial order under Qianlong) gathered the peak of auspicious-date learning, becoming the authoritative basis of almanacs.
- **Since the near age**: villages commonly invite a yinyang master to choose the day; cities mostly give "convenience for kin and friends" priority, choosing simply.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Report the time**: give the dead's birth-death times and the family's zodiac signs to the yinyang master.
2. **Reckon the day**: from the almanac choose the auspicious day and hour for encoffining, the procession and burial, avoiding zhongshang, three-sang, monthly break, etc.
3. **Issue the sheet**: the master issues a "funeral day-sheet" (with the hour of each rite).
4. **Remedy**: if a taboo day cannot be avoided, perform remedies such as cutting the zhongshang or a talisman.
5. **Act by the term**: the bereaved act by the sheet.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the zhongshang taboo is heaviest; the custom of "cutting the zhongshang" is common.
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: choose the day from the almanac, taboo on the "three-sha direction" and "zhongshang day".
- **Jiangnan**: also invite a master to choose, but stress more the arrangement of sacrifice terms like "the fifth seven".
- **Ethnic minorities**: each people's date-choosing basis differs (e.g. the Yi *bimo* divination, the Tibetan star-calculation), its system not the same source as the Han stem-branch auspicious dating, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Date-choosing basis**: the *Xieji Bianfang Shu*, almanacs, calendars, the *Book of Burial*.
- **Documents**: the funeral day-sheet, the date-choosing sheet, talismans.
- **Remedy objects**: paper figure, paper coffin, rooster, tile basin, cinnabar, talisman paper.
