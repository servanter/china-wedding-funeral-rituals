# Dietary Taboos in the Mourning Period

## 1. Definition

The restrictions on mourning-period diet come from the *Liji*'s "**rites of dwelling in mourning**" (diet decreasing step by step), which in folk custom became several concrete taboos.

| Class | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **No meat/fish** | The filial son/daughter eat no meat in the mourning period (especially within a hundred days or the fifth seven), only vegetarian |
| **No wine** | "In mourning, no wine"; though the funeral feast has wine, the filial son does not drink or substitutes tea |
| **No salt** | Some areas at first bereavement "taboo salt" ("not eating salt" to show grief) |
| **Vegetarian meal** | At the sevens, "fifth seven", "hundred days" set "vegetarian meals", "vegetarian seats"; strict when monks and Daoists chant |
| **Funeral meal** | The bereaved set "funeral meals", "running tables" for guests, but the filial son often sets a separate vegetarian seat |
| **No festive food** | Taboo on New-Year cakes, glutinous rice balls and other festive foods; taboo on holding a "joyful feast" at the bereaved's |
| **No noodles (some areas)** | Some places taboo "eating noodles", taking "noodles are long" as unfit for the mourning period |

**Ritual meaning**: the *Liji* ("Jianzhuan") shows the decreasing sequence "no food three days → congee → plain food and water → vegetables and fruit → back to normal", embodying "the decreasing of grief"; the folk taboos are the popular form of this rite.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the *Liji* ("Jianzhuan") fixes the diet degrees of dwelling in mourning.
- **Han-Tang**: the dwelling diet entered the law, with penalties for "making music and eating meat in mourning".
- **Song-Ming**: the *Family Rituals* standardises the mourning diet; Buddhist "vegetarian meals" joined the funeral.
- **Ming-Qing**: gazetteers' "customs" sections often record "the bereaved eat no meat", "the filial son eats no meat for a hundred days".
- **Since the near age**: villages keep more; cities only keep "vegetarian seat at the fifth seven", "no toasting at the funeral feast".

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **First bereavement**: the filial son stops eating (formally), then eats congee.
2. **Coffin period**: the bereaved treat guests with a vegetarian seat; the filial son eats no meat or wine.
3. **Doing the sevens**: set "vegetarian meals", monks and Daoists chant, the family vegetarian.
4. **Hundred days / anniversary**: gradually resume meat.
5. **Remove the dress**: diet back to normal.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the saying "the filial son eats no meat for a hundred days" is common.
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: the funeral feast is called "white seat", mostly vegetarian; the filial son forbidden wine.
- **Jiangnan**: do the sevens with a "vegetarian seat", monks and Daoists chant and eat vegetarian.
- **Ethnic minorities**: dietary taboos mostly tie to faith (e.g. the Hui halal diet, the Tibetan no-eat rules), their basis their own faith, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Diet vessels**: vegetarian-seat tableware, vegetarian-meal vessels.
- **Documents**: the funeral-feast seat list, the vegetarian-meal list.
- **Literary basis**: *Liji* "Jianzhuan", the *Family Rituals*.
