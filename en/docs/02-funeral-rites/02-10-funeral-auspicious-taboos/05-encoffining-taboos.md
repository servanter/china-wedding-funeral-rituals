# Encoffining Taboos

## 1. Definition

Encoffining is the most taboo-dense link of the funeral, involving four sides: **tears, objects, persons, time**.

| Class | Taboo |
| --- | --- |
| **Tears** | Taboo on **tears falling on the corpse** ("tears on the corpse, the dead uneasy"); weep with the back or side turned |
| **Animals** | Taboo on **cats and dogs near the corpse** ("the cat startles the corpse", "borrows breath and rises"); taboo on a chicken flying into the encoffining room |
| **Clothes** | Taboo on **fur** ("fear rebirth as a beast"); taboo on **satin** (homophone "cut off"); taboo on **buttons** (use ties); taboo on an **even number** of pieces |
| **Persons** | Taboo on those **zodiac-clashing** being present (see "Sha-Clash and Zodiac Taboos"); taboo on the pregnant and the just-delivered at encoffining |
| **Time** | Taboo on encoffining on a **zhongshang day**; taboo on encoffining after sunset (varies by place) |
| **Objects** | Taboo on **iron, mirrors** in the coffin; taboo on leaving **metal, plastic** unrotted things in the coffin |
| **Speech** | Taboo on saying "come again", "seldom see" in the encoffining room; taboo on calling the living by name |

**Related care**: put "dog-beating cakes", "paper money", "five-grain bag" in the coffin; after burial taboo on "looking back".

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the *Yili* ("Shi Sang Li") details the great encoffining rite; the "xi" (adorning), "fan han" (rice-in-mouth) steps each have notes.
- **Han-Tang**: the encoffining rite and taboos grew recorded in the *Liji* commentaries and the gazetteers.
- **Ming-Qing**: encoffining taboos were heavily recorded in the gazetteers' "customs" sections and folk daily-use books.
- **Since the near age**: villages keep the taboos fairly complete; funeral homes follow hygiene rules, taboos reduced.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Clean the body, dress**: put on the grave clothes (see "Grave Clothes").
2. **Enter the coffin**: lift the body in, head inward, feet outward (or by place).
3. **Cover the encoffining (minor, great)**: cover with the quilt, add the coffin lid.
4. **Nail and seal the coffin**: after the kin's last view, nail the coffin.
5. **Avoid and act**: the clashing step aside, weep sideways, taboo words taboo objects.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the "cat near the corpse" saying is strongest; encoffining mostly at night or early morning.
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: encoffining stresses the hour, taboo on zodiac-clashing persons present.
- **Jiangnan**: taboo on tears on the corpse, stress the coffin lining and small grave goods.
- **Ethnic minorities**: each people's encoffining taboos differ (taboo on certain animals, certain persons present), their basis being the people's own faith, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **In-coffin goods**: five-grain bag, dog-beating cakes, paper money, small mingqi.
- **Grave clothes** (taboo on satin, fur).
- **Documents**: the encoffining sheet (listing the encoffining hour and taboo matters).
