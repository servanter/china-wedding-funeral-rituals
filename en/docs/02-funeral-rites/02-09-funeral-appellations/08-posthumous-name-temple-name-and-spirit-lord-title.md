# Posthumous Name, Temple Name and Spirit-Lord Title

## 1. Definition

| Term | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Posthumous name** (shi) | A praising-or-blaming title given after death by one's deeds | Wen, Wu, Zhong, Xian, Xiao; You, Li (blaming) |
| **Temple name** (miaohao) | The title of one enshrined in the Grand Temple | Taizu, Taizong, Gaozong (mostly emperors) |
| **Honorific name** (zunhao / huihao) | A respectful title given in life or after death | Mostly emperors and empresses |
| **Spirit-lord title** | The name written on the spirit-lord (wooden lord) | "Xiankao so-and-so, taboo so-and-so, the spirit-lord" |
| **Granted office / granted name** | Office and name granted by the court | Granted such office, named such |

**Writing rules** (spirit-lord, name banner, inscription):

- First put "Xiankao / Xianbi", then the office (e.g. "granted such grandee"), then "taboo so-and-so", ending with "the spirit-lord", "the coffin", "the tomb".
- The posthumous name is placed before or after the name, e.g. "so-and-so, taboo so-and-so, posthumously named Wenzhong".

**Ritual meaning**: the posthumous name is the institutionalised form of "**judging at the closing of the coffin**"; its praise-and-blame is closely tied to the political-ethical function of "encouraging and punishing".

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the *Yizhou Shu* ("Shifa Jie") is the earliest text on the posthumous-name law; the *Liji* ("Tan'gong") "the death posthumous-name is a Zhou way".
- **Qin-Han**: the emperor's and empress's posthumous names and the courtier's ran in parallel; the Han posthumous-name law tended to abuse.
- **Tang-Song**: the posthumous-name law was institutionalised, with an office of "discussing the posthumous name"; famous ministers mostly got fine posthumous names.
- **Ming-Qing**: the posthumous-name law was strictly controlled; only those of official rank and above, or with special merit, could get one; "Wenzheng" was the top civil-official posthumous name.
- **Modern**: the posthumous-name system was abolished; tombstones and spirit-lords keep the old "Xiankao" form.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Set the posthumous name**: the ritual official deliberates ("discussing the posthumous name", "requesting the posthumous name").
2. **Grant the posthumous name / grant the office**: the court issues it.
3. **Inscribe the lord**: the spirit-lord is titled by the posthumous name and office.
4. **Write the banner**: the name banner writes the office together (see "Name Banner and Soul-Recalling Banner").
5. **Inscription**: the epitaph and spirit-way stele put the office and posthumous name first.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Han areas**: the posthumous-name law was for emperors, officials and famous scholars (private posthumous names too, e.g. "village worthy" disciples privately naming their teacher).
- **Non-Han regimes**: the Liao, Jin, Yuan and Qing emperors and empresses also used the posthumous-name and temple-name systems, their lineage running parallel with and a system of its own alongside the Han, and must be recorded separately.
