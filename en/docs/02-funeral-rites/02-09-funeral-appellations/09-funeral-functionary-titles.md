# Funeral Functionary Titles

## 1. Definition

The funeral is a collectively co-operated ritual; its division of labour has a fixed appellation system.

| Title | Duty | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **General manager / overall overseer** (duguan / zongguan) | Overall charge of the funeral | Usually a clansman or locally respected person |
| **Funeral guardian** (husang) | Presides over the funeral rites (set by the *Family Rituals*) | Aide to the mourning master |
| **Scribe** (sishu) | Records the rites, registers guests and gifts | Set by the *Family Rituals* |
| **Steward of goods** (sihuo) | Manages wealth, income and outlay | Set by the *Family Rituals* |
| **Accountant** (zhangfang) | Manages the accounts | The near-age term |
| **Great guest / lord-inscriber** (dabin / tizhuguan) | Inscribes the lord, dots the lord | A notable is invited |
| **Rite-master / cantor** (lisheng / zanli) | Chants the rites, leads the ceremony | "tongzan" (general cantor) and "yinzan" (lead cantor) differ |
| **Master of ceremonies** (siyi) | Presides over the rite's order | Near-age term |
| **Yinyang master / geomancer** | Chooses the day, reads the fengshui | Folk call him "master" |
| **Bearers / coffin-carriers** | Carry the coffin, send the burial | Eight, sixteen, thirty-two and more |
| **Musicians / band** | Play the funeral music | Some areas invite monks and Daoists |
| **Cook / kitchen** | Runs the funeral feast | The "running table" |

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the *Yili* ("Shi Sang Li") has the office division of "zhu" (invoker), "shi" (scribe), "dianren" (groundsman).
- **Tang-Song**: the *Family Rituals* sets the three offices "funeral guardian, scribe, steward of goods", the model for later ages.
- **Ming-Qing**: folk formed the division "overall manager, accountant, rite-master, bearer-house"; the "bearer-house" and "shed-shop" were special trades.
- **Modern**: the funeral home replaces most folk division; "master of ceremonies" and "ritual specialist" are new titles.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Open the office**: set a "funeral affairs office" (some post a "funeral notice" listing the functionaries).
2. **Divide the work**: the overall manager in chief, the accountant records the gifts, the rite-master chants, the yinyang chooses the day.
3. **Receive condolences**: the scribe registers guests and gifts, the rite-master calls names and leads.
4. **Send the coffin**: the bearers carry the coffin, the musicians play, the yinyang fixes the orientation.
5. **Thank the functionaries**: after the funeral, thank the functionaries with a feast or gifts.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the bearer-house, shed-shop and musicians are special trades; the "great guest" dotting the lord is solemn.
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: the "master" (Daoist), the "master" (geomancer), the "filial-son troupe" have clear division.
- **Jiangnan**: the rite-master (cantor) is a man learned in the old rites, chanting in four-character lines.
- **Ethnic minorities**: the functionaries are mostly religious professionals (e.g. the Tibetan lama, the Dai "bozhang"), their titles and duties not the same system, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Funeral notice** (listing the functionaries), **funeral sheet**, **gift list** (registering gifts).
- **Functionary implements**: suona and gongs, bearing gear, mourning shed, account books.
- **Ritual basis**: *Zhu Xi's Family Rituals*, "Mourning".
