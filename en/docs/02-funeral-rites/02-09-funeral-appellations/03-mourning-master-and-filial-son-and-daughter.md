# The Mourning Master, the Filial Son and Daughter

## 1. Definition

| Term | Identity | Duty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mourning master** (sangzhu) | The one who presides; usually the eldest son (father's mourning) or the succession-bearing grandson | Presides, receives condolences, leads the rites |
| **Mistress** (zhufu) | The mourning master's wife (or the dead's daughter-in-law, daughter) | Presides over the kitchen, the inner affairs |
| **Funeral guardian** (husang) | A kinsman-friend who presides over the funeral affairs | Overall charge of the funeral (the *Family Rituals* sets one "husang") |
| **Filial son** (xiaozi) | The dead's son | Wears zhan cui, holds the staff, greets guests, keeps vigil |
| **Filial daughter** (xiaonü) | The dead's daughter | Wails, performs the rites |
| **Filial daughter-in-law** (xiaofu) | The dead's daughter-in-law | Presides within, performs the rites |
| **Filial grandson, filial nephew** | Grandchildren, nephews | Perform by the degree of mourning |

**Fixing the "mourning master"** (lineage principle): for the father's mourning the **eldest son** is master; if he is absent, the **eldest grandson (succession-bearing grandson)** is master — the meaning of "he who succeeds is as a son"; for the mother's mourning the **eldest son** is master, or the husband (if alive) is master.

**Related offices**: the *Family Rituals* sets three offices — "scribe" (records the rites), "steward of goods" (manages wealth), "funeral guardian" (overall) — each with a person in charge.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the *Yili* ("Shi Sang Li") details the mourning master's conduct; the *Liji* treats the meaning of "mourning master".
- **Tang-Song**: the *Kaiyuan Rites* and *Family Rituals* fix the offices of mourning master, funeral guardian, mistress.
- **Ming-Qing**: the mourning master's duty entered the clan's ritual law; the succession-grandson's recognition was tied to property succession.
- **Modern**: the mourning master is mostly the eldest son (or main provider), the duty simplified to "presiding over the funeral, thanking the guests".

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Set the mourning master**: at first death the family agrees on the mourning master (eldest son / succession-grandson).
2. **Preside**: the mourning master presides over assuming the dress, the morning-and-evening offering, the funeral affairs.
3. **Receive condolences**: the mourning master receives condolences before the spirit and returns the rite.
4. **Send the coffin**: the mourning master holds the banner, supports the coffin, presides over the burial.
5. **Thank the guests**: after burial send a thanks-note (thanking the condolences).

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the filial son "kneels in the shed" (kneels in the mourning shed to greet guests), the filial daughter-in-law "kneels at the spirit" and wails.
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: the mourning master is called "filial son", the mistress "filial daughter-in-law"; the succession-grandson's term is still used.
- **Jiangnan**: the mourning master is mostly the eldest son; the "funeral guardian" is a clan elder or functionary.
- **Ethnic minorities**: the mourning master is produced by the people's own kinship system (e.g. the Yi clan elder, the Tibetan lama presiding), not the same source as the Han lineage system, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Obituary signature**: "unfilial son so-and-so", "qi-fu grandson so-and-so", by the mourning-degree line.
- **Functionary documents**: the funeral sheet, the funeral notice.
- **Ritual basis**: *Yili* "Shi Sang Li", *Zhu Xi's Family Rituals*, "Mourning".
