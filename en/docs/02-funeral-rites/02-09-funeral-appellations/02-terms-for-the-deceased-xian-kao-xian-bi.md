# Terms for the Deceased (Xiankao, Xianbi)

## 1. Definition

Terms for the dead are distinguished by the characters "**xian / xian / wang / gu**", with strict **rank and near/far**:

| Character | Object | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Xian (先)** | A deceased elder | Xiankao (father), Xianbi (mother), Xianzukao, Xianzumu, Xianxiong, Xianshi |
| **Xian (显)** | A deceased elder (and with descendants, a head of a隆重 funeral) | Xiankao, Xianbi ("xian" has the sense of "illustrious") |
| **Wang (亡)** | A deceased peer or junior | Late wife (wangqi), late younger brother, late child |
| **Gu (故)** | An ordinary deceased one | Old friend (guyu), deceased person |
| **Fujun / Ru ren** | A respectful couple term | So-and-so Fujun, So-and-so Ru ren (female) |

**"Taboo-name" and "style"**: obituaries and inscriptions do not name directly, but call "hui mou" (hui, the dead's name), "zi mou"; e.g. "Xiankao so-and-so, taboo so-and-so, the Fujun".

**Occasions**: spirit tablet, name banner, obituary, spirit-lord, epitaph, elegiac couplet, life-account, genealogy biography.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the *Liji* ("Quli") "in life called father and mother, in death called kao and bi"; the *Erya* ("Shiqin") fixes kin terms.
- **Han-Tang**: name-taboo was strict; inscriptions mostly call "hui mou", "Fujun".
- **Song-Ming**: the *Family Rituals* rules the spirit-tablet and spirit-lord name format; the "Xiankao / Xianbi" distinction clarifies.
- **Ming-Qing**: the "Xiankao / Xiankao" usage was fixed (one with descendants called "xian", one without called "xian"; another says the dead as a head of the family is called "xian").
- **Modern**: obituaries and inscriptions keep the old-style appellations.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **First death**: at once rename the dead "Xiankao / Xianbi", the living calling themselves "unfilial son / daughter".
2. **Obituary**: begins with "Xiankao so-and-so, taboo so-and-so" (see "Mourning Self-Appellations and Obituary Formats").
3. **Make the banner, inscribe the lord**: the name banner and spirit-lord write "Xiankao so-and-so, taboo so-and-so, the spirit-lord".
4. **Inscription**: the epitaph and tombstone write by the fixed appellation.
5. **Genealogy**: the biography and lineage table record as "taboo so-and-so".

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Han, all regions**: folk speech uses "forebear", "old one", "the one above" loosely; writing keeps the old style.
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: obituaries use "Xiankao" and "Xianbi" together, with the hall-name and ancestral seat before the name (e.g. "the Yingchuan Chen clan").
- **Ethnic minorities**: mostly call the dead by the people's own kin terms, and often by taboo (not naming directly); not the same source as the Han "Xiankao / Xiankao", and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Carriers**: spirit tablet, name banner, spirit-lord, obituary, life-account, epitaph, tombstone, elegiac couplet.
- **Formulas**: "hui mou", "zi mou", "Fujun", "Ru ren", "Xiankao", "Xianbi".
- **Literature**: *Erya* "Shiqin", *Liji* "Quli", *Chengwei Lu*, *Family Rituals*.
