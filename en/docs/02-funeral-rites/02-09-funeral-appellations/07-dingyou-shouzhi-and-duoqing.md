# Dingyou, Shouzhi and Duoqing

## 1. Definition

**Dingyou** means an official, on the parents' death, must **leave office and return home to observe the mourning**; **Shouzhi** is to complete the three-year (actually 27 months) mourning; **Duoqing** (seizing the affection) is the court, for public need, ordering the official **to remain in office and observe the mourning, not leaving**.

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Dingyou / Dingjian** | Leaving office on the parents' death (father's mourning, mother's mourning) |
| **Shouzhi** | Observing the rites in mourning, 27 months |
| **Duoqing** | The court forbids leaving office, ordering in-office mourning ("seizing his affection") |
| **Qifu** | Returning to post after the term or the duoqing |
| **Zhongzhi** | Completing the term and removing the dress |

**System point**: during Dingyou one may not marry, make music, or go to feasts; violators were guilty of "unfilial". The Ming-Qing system: an official must report Dingyou, and after the term await a post ("qifu ji yong", "demoted qifu" with differences).

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the *Liji* ("Wangzhi") "**for the parents' mourning, no office for three years**" — the ritual source of Dingyou.
- **Han**: the Dingyou system gradually formed, with the fashion of "recommending the filial and incorrupt" and "over-observing the mourning".
- **Tang-Song**: Dingyou entered the law; the *Tang Code* clearly punishes "hearing of the parents' death and not mourning", "making music in mourning"; duoqing cases grew.
- **Ming-Qing**: the system was most complete; the official histories' biographies have many "left office on Dingyou", "qifu after duoqing" records.
- **Modern**: the Dingyou system was abolished with bureaucratic reform; "shouzhi", "duoqing" became historical terms.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Hearing of the death**: on receiving the obituary, lament and report (report Dingyou).
2. **Leave office**: leave the post, return home to observe.
3. **Shouzhi**: three years (27 months) of mourning, not going out.
4. **Duoqing (exception)**: the court, for his talent or post, forbids leaving, ordering in-office mourning (e.g. Zhang Juzheng of the Ming).
5. **Qifu**: after the term or the duoqing, go to the court and take up a post.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Han areas**: Dingyou is a civil-official system, running parallel with folk funerals.
- **Non-Han regimes**: the Liao, Jin, Yuan and Qing each had their own inheritance and adaptation of the Han officials' Dingyou system, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: the Dingyou report, the qifu document, the duoqing edict.
- **Literary basis**: *Liji* "Wangzhi", the *Tang Code*, and the dynastic codes and the official histories' "Treatise on Rites" and "Treatise on Officials".
