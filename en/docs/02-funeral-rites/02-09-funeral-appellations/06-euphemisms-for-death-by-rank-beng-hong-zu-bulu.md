# Euphemisms for Death by Rank (Beng, Hong, Zu, Bulu)

## 1. Definition

The ancients tabooed the word "death", replacing it with **graded taboo-terms**. The *Liji* ("Quli xia"): "**the Son of Heaven in death is called beng (collapse), the feudal lords hong, the great officers zu (end), the knights bulu (no more salary), the commoners si (death).**"

| Taboo-term | Applies to | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Beng** (collapse) | The Son of Heaven | Like a mountain collapsing, the term for the utmost honour |
| **Hong** | Feudal lords, those with a title | The next-honour term |
| **Zu** (end) | Great officers | "To end, to exhaust" |
| **Bulu** | Knights | "No longer receiving salary" |
| **Si** (death) | Commoners | The plain word |

**Folk euphemism system** (common since the near ages):

- **Lao**: "lao le" (grown old), "lao le ren" (an old one has gone).
- **Zou / qu le**: "zou le" (left), "qu le" (gone), "guo qu le" (passed).
- **Guoshi / qushi / cishi / shishi**: passed away / departed this world / left the world / deceased.
- **Mo / gu / wang**: literary euphemisms.
- **Xianshi / guixi / guitian / dengxia / kuahe**: with religious or well-wishing sense.
- **Bainian / shenghou**: "after a hundred years", "after-life matters".
- **Meile / bu zai le**: colloquial euphemisms.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the *Liji* ("Quli") fixes the graded taboo-terms; the *Gongyang Zhuan* and *Guliang Zhuan* also distinguish "beng, hong, zu".
- **Han-Tang**: histories strictly used the words by grade (the emperor "beng", the courtier "hong", "zu").
- **Song-Yuan-Ming-Qing**: the taboo-term system continued; folk euphemisms grew rich.
- **Modern**: the graded taboo-terms survive only in historiography and ritual study; folk euphemisms are common, "shishi" and "qushi" the common written forms.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Reporting the death**: tell kin and friends with a euphemism ("the old master passed away at such hour").
2. **Obituary phrasing**: literary obituaries use "zu", "died of illness at such place".
3. **Inscription terms**: choose by the dead's status (officials use "hong", "zu").
4. **In speech**: villages tell each other "lao le ren".
5. **Avoid the direct word**: face to face taboo the word "death" directly — a funeral-language taboo.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: "lao le", "lao le ren" are common euphemisms.
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: "guo shen" (passed the body), "fan qu" (returned), "wang sheng" (Buddhist influence).
- **Jiangnan**: "guo shi", "zou tuo" (passed the world, slipped away).
- **Ethnic minorities**: each people's death euphemism relates to its faith (e.g. "wang sheng", "gone to heaven"), often with a language taboo (not naming directly), and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Carriers**: obituary, elegiac statement, inscription, elegiac couplet.
- **Terms**: beng, hong, zu, bulu, mo, shi, xianshi, guixi.
- **Literary basis**: *Liji* "Quli", *Gongyang Zhuan*, *Chengwei Lu*.
