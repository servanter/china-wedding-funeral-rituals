# Mourning Self-Appellations and Obituary Formats

## 1. Definition

The obituary (*fuwen*) is the core of funeral documents; its appellation format takes the **mourning-degree line** as its skeleton — the direct trace of the five-degree system in documents.

**Basic structure of the obituary**:

1. **First line**: the dead's name and title — "Xiankao so-and-so, taboo so-and-so, the Fujun".
2. **Second line**: year, month, day and hour of birth and death, and age.
3. **Third line**: died where, buried where, when the sacrifice and condolence-open are set.
4. **Filial-kin line**: arranged by the mourning degree, near to far —
   - "Gu zi so-and-so, weeping blood and bowing the head to the ground"
   - "Qi-fu grandson so-and-so, wiping tears and bowing the head"
   - "Da-gong-fu nephew so-and-so, wiping tears and knocking the head"
   - "Si-fu grand-nephew so-and-so, wiping tears and bowing"
5. **Last line**: "hereby we announce the death", "sorrowfully we announce the death".

**Mourning-degree terms compared**:

| Degree | Self-appellation term | Rite term |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zhan cui (son) | Gu Ai zi | Weeping blood and bowing the head to the ground |
| Zi cui qi (grandson, nephew) | Qi-fu grandson / Qi-fu nephew | Wiping tears and bowing the head |
| Da gong | Da-gong-fu nephew | Wiping tears and knocking the head |
| Xiao gong | Xiao-gong-fu great-nephew | Wiping tears and knocking the head |
| Si ma | Si-fu great-great-nephew | Wiping tears and bowing |

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin to Tang-Song**: obituary appellations scattered in letters; the *Family Rituals* standardises funeral-document formats.
- **Ming-Qing**: the obituary format fixed, the "mourning-degree line" became the common rule; many Qing and Republican obituary objects survive.
- **Modern**: newspaper obituaries use new phrasing, but the signature still keeps the "mourning-degree line" trace.
- **Contemporary**: obituaries mostly sign "unfilial son / daughter", sometimes with "qi-fu grandson" and other old terms.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Draft the obituary**: the scribe drafts the filial-kin line by the degree.
2. **Send the obituary**: the obituary is sent to kin and friends ("reporting the death").
3. **Open and receive condolences**: the sacrifice and condolence-open are set by the obituary's time.
4. **Thanks-note**: after burial send a thanks-note (thanking the condolences), matching the obituary.
5. **Archive**: genealogies and family records often append the obituary and elegiac statement.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the obituary is called "fuwen"; the funeral notice (funeral list) is posted in large characters at the door.
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: the "fuwen" (white card) format is elegant; the "mourning-degree line" is kept complete.
- **Jiangnan**: the obituary and elegiac statement are finely written, often by a literatus.
- **Ethnic minorities**: mostly oral "reporting the death" replaces the written obituary; the written obituary's form differs from the Han, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Obituary (fuwen)**, **elegiac statement**, **thanks-note**, **funeral notice**, **life-account**.
- **Writing material**: white paper, white card, plain silk; blue or black ink.
- **Related documents**: elegiac-couplet book, funeral book (registering guests and gifts).
