# Condolence Phrases and Mourning-Visit Titles

## 1. Definition

The phrases in a mourning-visit context divide into "**mourning the dead**" and "**consoling the living**", with names for giving goods.

| Phrase | Meaning | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Diao** (mourn) | Mourn the dead | "mourning the funeral", "going to mourn" |
| **Yan** (console) | Console the living (the bereaved) | The *Guliang Zhuan*: "mourning a lost state is called yan"; later "yan" specially means consoling the bereaved |
| **Fu** (gift) | Give wealth to help the funeral | "funeral money", "funeral gift" |
| **Zeng** (present) | Present burial goods (mingqi, clothes) | The *Yili* ("Jixi li") has the "zeng" rite |
| **Sui** (clothe) | Give the dead clothes | A pre-Qin rite |
| **Dian** (offer) | Offer sacrificial goods | "presenting the offering", "sending the offering" |
| **Wan** (elegise) | Give elegiac couplets, hangings, wreaths | A modern form |

**Consoling formulas**: "restrain your grief and follow the change", "restrain your grief and take care", "be comforted and love yourself", "please take care", "take good care of yourself"; taboo: do not say "see you again", "come again" at the bereaved's.

**Thanking formulas**: the bereaved answer with "thanking the condolence", phrases like "weeping blood and bowing the head to the ground", "wiping tears and bowing the head", "dare not accept".

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the *Liji* ("Quli", "Tan'gong") details the mourning-visit rites and phrases (e.g. "he who knows the living mourns, he who knows the dead grieves").
- **Han-Tang**: the diao, yan, fu, zeng distinctions clarified.
- **Song-Ming**: the *Family Rituals* fixes the receiving-condolence and answering rites.
- **Ming-Qing**: fu-gifts and wan-gifts (elegiac couplets, hangings) flourished, forming a written mourning-visit phrase system.
- **Modern**: centred on "wan"; the fu-money is renamed "offering money" (dianyi), "white package" (baibao, Cantonese), "white envelope".

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Report the death**: the obituary is sent, kin and friends prepare to mourn.
2. **Go to mourn**: the guest goes before the spirit, lights incense, performs the rite; the mourning master accompanies in weeping and answers.
3. **Give the gift**: give the offering money (funeral gift); the scribe registers it.
4. **Console**: console the bereaved, "restrain your grief and follow the change".
5. **Thank the condolence**: after burial send a thanks-note or hold a feast to thank.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the guest "lights incense, knocks the head"; the mourning master "kneels in the shed" to accompany; the gift is called "offering money".
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: the offering money is called "white package" or "offering money"; the hanging is called "scroll"; the answer uses a "thanks-note".
- **Guangdong**: the gift is called "white gold" (bok kam), must be an odd sum; the bereaved return a "lucky packet" (with sugar, a red packet) to "dissolve" it.
- **Ethnic minorities**: mourning-visit ways vary (presenting the hada, presenting wine, singing the funeral song, dancing the funeral); the phrases differ from the Han formulas, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Mourning-visit documents**: obituary, thanks-note, elegiac couplet, hanging, condolence telegram, condolence letter.
- **Gift registration**: gift list, funeral book.
- **Related objects**: incense and candle, offering-money envelope, mourning ribbon.
