# Name Banner (Mingjing) and Soul-Recalling Banner

## 1. Definition

The **name banner** (*mingjing*, also "ming jing", "jing ming") is the flag in the funeral that writes the dead's name and office. The *Yili* ("Shi Sang Li"): "make the ming, each by his thing"; Zheng Xuan's commentary: "ming is the bright banner; because the dead cannot be told apart, mark him by his flag."

- **Name banner**: made of red or plain silk, writing "the coffin of so-and-so the official" or "the name banner of the late father so-and-so"; raised on a bamboo pole, set before the coffin or laid over it.
- **Soul-recalling banner** (*zhaohun fan*, also "yin hun fan", "hun fan", "lead-way fan"): the white long banner guiding the dead soul, mostly held by the filial son, walking at the head of the procession.

**The banner difference**: the name banner stresses **marking the dead's identity** (the legacy of the pre-Qin "fu" rite of calling the soul with a robe); the soul-recalling banner stresses **guiding the dead soul** (under Buddhist-Daoist influence); folk often use both and conflate the names.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the *Yili* ("Shi Sang Li") and *Liji* ("Sangfu Xiao Ji") record the "ming" system, size by rank (the Son of Heaven, feudal lords, great officers, knights each with measure).
- **Han-Tang**: the name banner continues; excavated e.g. Mawangdui Han tomb's "T-shaped silk painting" is a banner-like object used at encoffining; after the Tang the name banner writes office and posthumous gift.
- **Song**: the *Family Rituals* fixes the name banner writing "the coffin of so-and-so the official", set before the coffin.
- **Ming-Qing**: the name banner writes "the name banner of the late Qing, graced with …"; the soul-recalling banner spread with Buddhist-Daoist rites.
- **Modern**: villages still use the white-cloth fan, soul-recalling banner; cities replace the banner's function with wreaths and elegiac hangings.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Make the banner**: after first death make the name banner of red or white cloth, have someone write the dead's office and name.
2. **Raise the banner**: raise the name banner before the spirit (per the *Family Rituals*, "stretch it on a bamboo pole, set before the coffin").
3. **Hold the fan to lead**: at the procession the filial son holds the soul-recalling banner (lead-way banner) and goes first, "pointing the way" for the dead soul.
4. **Cover the banner into the grave**: at burial lay the name banner over the coffin or place it inside, entering the grave together.
5. **Burn the fan**: some areas burn the soul-recalling banner after burial, or at the "third-day return" or "fifth seven".

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the name banner writes "the name banner of the late father so-and-so"; the soul-recalling banner cut from white paper, held by the eldest son.
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: the fan divides into "soul fan" and "soul-recalling fan"; the filial son holds the "staff" and fan; a "name-banner team" leads the column.
- **Jiangnan**: the name banner mostly writes official title and gift, stressing calligraphy and banner-stand ornament.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples mark the dead soul with a specific fan, cloth or paper strip (e.g. the Manchu "fotuo", some southwestern peoples' "lead-way fan"), whose belief and cultural thread differ from the Han, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Name banner**: red silk (or white cloth), bamboo pole, banner stand.
- **Soul-recalling banner**: white paper, white cloth, bamboo pole; forms include "cut-flower fan", "long-strip fan", "canopy fan".
- **Writing format**: "the coffin of so-and-so the official", "the name banner of the late father so-and-so"; if posthumous name or gift, write together.
- **Documents**: the name-banner text must agree with the obituary, the life-account and the epitaph.
