# Grave Clothes (Shouyi)

## 1. Definition

The grave clothes (*shouyi*), also "lian yi", "old clothes", "seeing-off-old clothes", "dressing-old clothes", are the clothes the dead wear at encoffining. The old ritual calls changing the dead's clothes "xi" (the "attire"), and "lesser encoffining" and "great encoffining" (layering clothes); later "shouyi" means the prepared set of clothes for the dead.

**Core rule**: folk grave clothes follow "**odd numbers lucky, even numbers taboo**" (sets of three, five, seven, nine), and avoid fur, avoid satin (satin homophones "cut off"), avoid black buttons. Ritually it is ruled by "suited to the family's means" (*Liji* "Tan'gong": "the funeral gear suits the family's means"), not advocating excess.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the *Yili* ("Shi Sang Li") records the "xi" and the "lesser/great encoffining" clothes, counted by "cheng" (e.g. great encoffining "thirty cheng of clothes"), the number by rank; material usually silk, hemp, cloth.
- **Han-Tang**: strict grades on the number and material of encoffining clothes; excavated objects (Mawangdui, Tang tombs) show multi-layer silk.
- **Song-Yuan-Ming-Qing**: the *Family Rituals* advocates simplification by custom; Ming-Qing gazetteers record the number and material rules, folk forming the "five collars three waists", "seven collars five waists" set forms.
- **Modern**: grave clothes are commodified; shops specialise; material mostly cotton, silk; colour mostly blue, brown, grey, white.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Prepare**: make grave clothes in life ("in a leap year or leap month", taking "leap" as lucky), or buy a ready set.
2. **Change after washing**: the "xi", after washing and the grain-in-mouth, change the dead into grave clothes; folk mostly by the filial son (daughter) after the last breath.
3. **Layers**: by the "odd" rule, usually "five collars three waists" (five upper, three lower) or "seven collars five waists".
4. **Wearing details**: a grave cap on the head, grave shoes on the feet (lotus or cloud patterns on the sole), a belt at the waist; avoid exposure, avoid fur.
5. **Encoffin**: over the grave clothes add the quilt, then into the coffin.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: stresses "winter cotton, summer single"; grave clothes mostly cotton jacket and trousers, colour mostly blue, black, brown.
- **Jiangnan**: grave clothes mostly silk, favouring "wan"-character and "round-flower" auspicious patterns.
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: the number stresses set numbers like "nine above seven below"; the "grave shoes" have lotus on the sole.
- **Guangdong**: stresses "five collars three waists", avoid satin, avoid fur, avoid bright red.
- **Ethnic minorities**: mostly encoffined in the people's traditional dress (e.g. the Miao full dress, the Tibetan pulu robe); the dress itself marks identity, different from the Han grave-clothes custom, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **The grave-clothes set**: grave jacket, grave trousers, grave cap, grave shoes, belt, quilt, pillow.
- **Material**: cotton, silk (avoid satin, avoid fur).
- **Colour**: blue, brown, grey, white, purple (by place).
- **Documents**: the number and material of grave clothes often in the "funeral sheet" and genealogy/family-rite manuscripts.
