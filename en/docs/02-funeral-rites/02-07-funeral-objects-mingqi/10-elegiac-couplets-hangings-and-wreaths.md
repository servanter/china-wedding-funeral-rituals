# Elegiac Couplets, Hangings and Wreaths

## 1. Definition

Elegiac couplets (*wanlian*), hangings (*wanzhang*) and wreaths are the most common **mourning gifts and displays** since modern times, mostly given by kin, friends and units, forming the main decoration of the hall.

- **Elegiac couplet**: a couplet mourning the dead, hung on the hall's two sides or before the hanging; the upper line is "right", the lower "left".
- **Elegiac hanging** (*wanzhang*, also "hanging scroll", "sacrificial hanging"): a whole piece of silk, satin, cloth or paper, writing mourning words ("riding the crane west", "voice and face still here"), with the sender's name on the sides.
- **Wreath**: a bamboo-wood frame with paper or fresh flowers, displayed in a ring, with a mourning ribbon stating the upper and lower inscriptions.
- **Mourning ribbon** (*wandai*): the two paper strips on the wreath; the upper writes "mourning Mr so-and-so", the lower "so-and-so respectfully mourns".

**Display order**: wreaths are arranged inside and outside the hall by **near/far and honour/humility**; the hangings hang on the hall's two sides, the couplets in a prominent place.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Distant source**: "wan" (輓) originally means "pulling the cart"; the ancient rite of "holding the rope" to help the burial; the "dirge" (挽歌) has been funeral music since the Han-Wei (*Jielu*, *Haoli*).
- **Elegiac couplet**: developed from the couplet; Ming-Qing literati made elegiac couplets for others, especially the Qing, with special collections.
- **Elegiac hanging**: since the Ming-Qing, silk and cloth as mourning gifts ("zhang" also written "帐"), hangable and takeable, with the nature of a gift.
- **Wreath**: introduced from the West in modern times (or said to combine the "wreath" with the Chinese "paper flower"); spread with the new-style funeral in the late Qing and early Republic.
- **Contemporary**: elegiac couplets, hangings and wreaths, with black armband and white flower, are the main mourning forms of the contemporary funeral; cities mostly replace paper wreaths with fresh-flower wreaths.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Deliver**: kin, friends and units send the hanging and wreath to the hall, registered by the "rite-room" (the steward) in the "elegiac-couplet book" and "funeral book".
2. **Display**: arrange wreaths by near/far, generation, unit; hang the hanging and couplet.
3. **Upper and lower inscriptions**: the wreath ribbon writes the upper and lower inscriptions; the couplet and hanging write the sender's name.
4. **Mourn and bow**: kin and friends mourn and bow before the spirit.
5. **Remove**: after the procession the couplets and hangings are taken down (kept by the giver or burned); wreaths mostly sent to the grave or burned.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the hanging is mostly silk and satin; "sending a hanging" is an important social exchange.
- **Jiangnan**: the elegiac couplet stresses wording and calligraphy; literati gatherings have special collections handed down.
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: the hanging is called "scroll", mostly white, blue, black, avoiding bright red (except the "happy funeral").
- **Cities**: mostly fresh-flower wreaths and couplets.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples mourn with specific cloth or cloth-banner; their meaning differs from the Han elegiac gift, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Elegiac couplet**: rice paper, silk (mounted), writing the two lines and the sender.
- **Elegiac hanging**: silk, cloth, paper, writing mourning words and the sender; "single hang" and "double hang" differ.
- **Wreath**: bamboo-wood frame, paper flower, silk flower, fresh flower; the mourning ribbon is white paper or silk.
- **Documents**: the elegiac-couplet book, funeral book (registering couplets, hangings, wreaths, condolence money) — an important class of modern funeral documents.
