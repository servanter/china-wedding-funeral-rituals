# Grave Goods

## 1. Definition

Grave goods is the general name for **all real objects and mingqi buried in the grave** — a wider scope than the narrow "mingqi", including both mingqi made for the funeral and practical vessels (living vessels) buried. Its kinds vary by age and grade, and are the main basis for archaeological periodisation and ritual study.

**Main classes:**

| Class | Representative objects |
| --- | --- |
| **Pottery** | Pottery figurines, granaries, stoves, wells, houses, courtyards, sancai, pottery vessels |
| **Jade** | Jade bi, jade cong, jade gui, jade suit (gold-thread / silver-thread / bronze-thread) |
| **Bronze** | Bronze ritual vessels (ding, gui, zun, jue), bronze mirror, bronze money, bronze lamp |
| **Lacquer-wood** | Lacquer coffin, lacquer box, wooden figurines, wooden vessels |
| **Metal money** | Buried real money (yiqian), gold and silver objects |
| **Other** | Bone, glass, porcelain figurines, stone figurines |

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Neolithic**: buried pottery, stone, bone, jade (Liangzhu jade, Dawenkou pottery as representatives).
- **Shang-Zhou**: bronze ritual vessels buried as a system; the "**set-ding system**" marks grade by the number of ding; jade used to "wrap the corpse" (jade face-cover, jade cong).
- **Warring States, Qin-Han**: **pottery figurines and model mingqi flourished**; the jade-suit system was complete by the Han (the Zhongshan Jing prince Liu Sheng and wife's gold-thread jade suit); "buried money" with bronze and lacquer.
- **Wei-Jin, Northern and Southern Dynasties**: the thin-burial fashion (Cao Cao's "thin-burial edict") and thick burial coexisted; grave goods lessened; porcelain appeared.
- **Sui-Tang**: sancai mingqi and pottery figurines (horse-riding, foreigner, tomb-guard figurines) peaked; Tang edicts limited the mingqi number.
- **Song-Yuan-Ming-Qing**: grave goods simplified; porcelain and gold-silver the main; paper mingqi replaced real objects as mainstream (see "Paper Effigies", "Spirit Money").
- **Modern**: grave goods are mostly small personal items (jewellery, watch, glasses).

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Prepare objects**: by status and means prepare grave goods, registered in the **dispatch register** (*qiance*).
2. **Display the objects**: before the procession set by the coffin or in the court.
3. **Arrange in the grave**: place by position in the outer coffin or chamber (front box, side box, wall niche, waist pit).
4. **Seal the grave**: after arranging, seal the grave door and cover with earth.
5. **Sacrifice at the grave**: after burial sacrifice at the grave ("grave sacrifice"); later "fifth seven", "hundredth day" and the like sweep again.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Central plains**: successive ages' pottery figurines, bronze, jade tombs are concentrated, the archaeological sequence complete.
- **Jiangnan**: Six-Dynasties to Ming-Qing tombs have developed celadon and gold-silver.
- **North and Northwest**: Han-Tang tomb murals, pottery figurines and "sancai" are rich.
- **Ethnic minorities**: each people's burial tradition differs vastly — e.g. some bury horse gear, tools, religious instruments; their object system is not framed by the Han "mingqi — living vessel", and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Mingqi-system literature**: *Zhouli* "Chun'guan", *Houhanshu* "Yili zhi", *Tang Huiyao* mingqi articles.
- **Dispatch register** (*qiance*): the grave-goods list (Warring States, Han tombs).
- **Archaeological material**: Mawangdui Han tomb, Mancheng Han tomb, Xi'an Tang tomb, Song-Yuan-Ming tomb reports.
- **Documents**: underworld report, land deed (next entry), funeral-gift documents.
