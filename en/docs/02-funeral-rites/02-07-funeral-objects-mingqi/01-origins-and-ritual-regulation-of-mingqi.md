# Origins and Ritual Regulation of Mingqi

## 1. Definition

*Mingqi* (also "mingqi", "ghost vessels", "alliance vessels") are objects **made specially for the dead and buried in the grave**. Their core ritual meaning is in the *Liji* ("Tan'gong"): "they are called *mingqi* because one treats the dead as a spirit" — serving the dead as a spirit (ghost), so the vessels used should differ from the living's.

The ritual books sum up the feature of *mingqi* as "**prepared yet unusable**":

- Bamboo vessels "not finished for use", earthen vessels "not finished in flavour" (cannot hold food), wooden vessels "not finished in cutting" (no carving);
- the qin and se "strung but not tuned", the yu and sheng "prepared but not in harmony";
- there are bells and chimes but no frames, so they cannot truly be played.

**Name and reality:**

| Term | Nature | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Living vessels** | practical vessels | used by the living, not buried |
| **Mingqi** | symbolic vessels "prepared yet unusable" | buried in the grave, for the dead's use in the underworld |
| **Sacrificial vessels** | vessels for sacrifice | used in temple and grave sacrifice, mostly set separately |

The *Liji* ("Tan'gong") also has Confucius saying "he who makes *mingqi* knows the way of mourning", showing the pre-Qin ritualists' approval; but there is also the criticism "he who made the figurine — may he have no descendants" (against human figurines), marking the boundary between *mingqi* and "burying the living with the dead".

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Prehistory**: the Neolithic already buried pottery, bone and stone objects (e.g. Yangshao, Longshan cultures) — the distant source of *mingqi*.
- **Shang-Zhou**: burying bronze ritual vessels and jade became a system; from the Western Zhou the parallel of "mingqi" and "practical vessels" appeared, some bronzes thin and light, unusable — called by scholars "mingqi-isation".
- **Warring States, Qin-Han**: **pottery-model mingqi flourished** — pottery figurines (the Terracotta Army of the First Qin Emperor's mausoleum as representative), pottery granaries, stoves, wells, houses, courtyards, pigsties, forming a complete "underground life scene".
- **Han**: the *mingqi* system was extremely developed, and "dispatch registers" (*qiance*) (grave-goods lists) and "underworld reports" (*gaodice*) appeared.
- **Wei-Jin, Northern and Southern Dynasties**: war and the thin-burial advocacy ran in parallel; the number of *mingqi* once lessened, but model *mingqi* continued.
- **Tang**: sancai (three-colour) *mingqi* peaked; the court several times by edict limited the number by rank (above the third, above the fifth, above the ninth rank each with grades).
- **Song-Yuan-Ming-Qing**: after papermaking spread, **paper mingqi (paper effigies) gradually became mainstream**; the *Family Rituals* advocated simplification; Ming-Qing ritual books graded the *mingqi* kinds.
- **Modern**: Western-style funeral goods (wreaths, ashes caskets, elegiac couplets and hangings) entered, coexisting with paper effigies and spirit money.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Prepare objects**: around the great encoffining, kin prepare *mingqi* by ritual and means, or commission the paper-effigy shop.
2. **Display the objects**: the *Yili* ("Jixi li") records "display the *mingqi* west of the carriage", the *mingqi* displayed by the coffin or in the court before the procession for kin and friends to view.
3. **Write the dispatch**: register the *mingqi* and grave goods in the **dispatch register** (*qiance*), buried together at the funeral.
4. **Enter the grave**: after lowering the coffin, place the *mingqi* in fixed positions in the outer coffin or chamber (front box, side box, wall niche).
5. **Burn**: after the Song, paper *mingqi* are mostly burned before the grave or at the road junction, replacing real objects entering the grave.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: complete paper-effigy kinds (paper figures, paper horse, paper house, paper car), burned together on the procession day.
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: fine paper-effigy craft; the "paper house" (*zhi cuo*) is large, with special workshops.
- **Jiangnan**: since the Ming-Qing mostly tin-foil and paper ingots; little real *mingqi* enters the grave, "burning" more than "burying".
- **Ethnic minorities**: grave goods and the *mingqi* idea differ — e.g. some peoples bury tools, livestock or specific ritual objects, not the same system as the Han "prepared yet unusable" *mingqi*, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Mingqi kinds**: pottery figurines, pottery models (granary, stove, well, house, pen), ritual-vessel imitations, paper effigies, spirit money, paper ingots.
- **Dispatch register** (*qiance*): the grave-goods list, excavated from Warring States to Han tombs, direct material for studying the *mingqi* system.
- **Funeral-gift documents**: records of friends' gifts, in the *Yili* "Jixi li" system.
- **Ritual basis**: *Liji* "Tan'gong", *Yili* "Jixi li", *Zhouli* and the funeral edicts of each age.
