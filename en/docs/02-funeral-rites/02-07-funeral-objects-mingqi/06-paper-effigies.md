# Paper Effigies (Zhi Za)

![纸扎与纸钱等明器 / Paper effigies and spirit money](../../../../assets/images/04-paper-effigies.png)


## 1. Definition

Paper effigies (*zhiza*, also "zha cai", "paper work", "paper code", "pasted paper") are **paper mingqi** with a frame of bamboo splints (or sorghum stalks, wire) and coloured paper pasted outside — the most common mingqi form in folk funerals since the Song. They replaced the real-object mingqi of the Han and Tang, embodying the shift "**paper for objects, burning for burying**".

**Common kinds:**

| Class | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Figures | Paper figures (boy and girl attendants, servants, opera characters) |
| Beasts | Paper horse, paper ox, paper sheep, paper deer |
| Dwellings | Paper house (paper residence), paper tower, paper courtyard |
| Utensils | Paper sedan, paper car, paper boat, paper box, paper chest, paper TV, paper phone |

**Ritual meaning and dispute**: paper effigies are seen as an extension of "serving the dead as the living", but through the ages there is also criticism "burning paper effigies wastes wealth and harms custom" (e.g. Ming-Qing local-official bans); modern custom-change also takes banning paper-effigy burning as a key item.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Basis**: the spread of papermaking in the Eastern Han made paper mingqi possible; the Tang already had the custom of paper for money.
- **Tang-Song**: the *Dongjing Menghua Lu* and *Mengliang Lu* record that Bianjing and Lin'an funerals had a "paper-horse shop" and "paper-effigy" trade; burning paper figures, horses and houses was in vogue.
- **Song-Yuan-Ming-Qing**: paper-effigy became a special trade; the kinds expanded with the age (Ming-Qing had paper sedan, paper boat, paper house); the craft refined.
- **Modern**: paper effigies clash with urbanisation and funeral reform; cities mostly ban burning, villages (especially Min-Tai, North China, Jiangnan) still flourish.
- **Contemporary**: paper effigies show "modernised" kinds (paper car, paper villa, paper phone); Min-Tai "paper residence" craft is fine, listed in some local intangible-cultural-heritage catalogues.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Order or make**: the family orders from the paper-effigy shop, reporting the dead's sex, age and burial needs; the craftsman makes by the sample.
2. **Display**: the paper effigies are set outside the hall or in the court for kin and friends to view (called "setting out paper").
3. **Line up before the procession**: the paper effigies go with the column, paper figures and horses in front.
4. **Burn**: burned before the grave, at the road junction or a designated spot; some stress "three fires" or "burning at a chosen time".
5. **Return the spirit**: after burning, dispose of the ashes, completing the "sending paper" rite.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: the finest craft; the "paper residence" (*zhi cuo*) is large, dismantlable, with furniture and appliances; burning is "burning the residence".
- **North China**: paper effigies are mostly paper figures, horses, cars, burned together on the procession day.
- **Jiangnan**: relatively simple paper effigies, mostly paper boxes and chests, with tin-foil ingots.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples also burn paper or cut paper in funerals, but mostly in their own belief and craft system (e.g. paper-cutting to recall the soul), not the same source as the Han paper effigies, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Frame material**: bamboo splints, sorghum stalks, wire, wood strips.
- **Paste material**: coloured paper, glazed paper, gold-silver paper, paste.
- **Craft**: splitting splints, framing, pasting, painting, assembling.
- **Documents**: the paper-effigy list ("paper-effigy sheet") often in the funeral sheet, noting names and numbers.
