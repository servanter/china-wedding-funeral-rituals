# Funerary Ritual Instruments

## 1. Definition

Funerary ritual instruments are the **instruments and musical instruments used by Buddhism and Daoism in funeral rites** (the seven-day rites, releasing the *yamu* mouth, deliverance, welcoming the third, crossing the dead). Since the Tang and Song, folk funerals commonly invite monks and Daoists to chant and hold rites, and the instruments became an important class of funeral objects.

**Common instruments:**

| Instrument | Form | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hand-bell chime** (*yinqing*) | Small bronze chime with a long handle | Lead the assembly, begin and end the chanting; clear and far |
| **Wooden fish** (*muyu*) | Wooden, fish-shaped or round | Regulate the chanting beat, calm the mind |
| **Bell / hand bell** | Bronze bell, vajra bell | Awaken, invite; stressed in Esotericism |
| **Chime-cymbals** (*dangzi, hazi*) | Bronze percussion | Accompany the rite's band |
| **Drum, great chime** | Large percussion | Main play of the rite |
| **Ritual ruler, command tablet** | Wood, metal | Used by Daoism to issue orders in the rite |

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Buddhism**: instruments entered China with Buddhism's eastward spread (Han-Wei); the name "hand-bell chime" appears in Tang-Song Chan rules; the wooden fish was fixed by the Ming-Qing.
- **Daoism**: bell, chime, bell-drum are needed for the fast rites; Daoism's "ritual instrument" originally means the implement of performing the law.
- **Tang-Song**: folk funerals already commonly invited monks and Daoists; the "seven-day rites" and "ten-kings fast" joined the rites.
- **Ming-Qing**: Buddhist-Daoist rites deeply joined the funeral; the "releasing the *yamu* mouth", "giving food", "bowing penance" rites prevailed; the instrument kinds were complete.
- **Modern**: villages still invite monks and Daoists with hand-bell chime and wooden fish; city funeral homes replace with audio playback or a rite-master.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Begin the scripture**: set the scripture altar and Buddha image (or spirit seat) before the spirit; the instruments are placed.
2. **Chant and bow penance**: the hand-bell chime leads, the wooden fish beats, the bell, drum, chime-cymbals accompany.
3. **The seven-day rites**: a rite every seven days from death (first to seventh seven), the instruments used by the rite.
4. **Release the *yamu* mouth / give food**: the rite's climax, bell and chime together, giving food to deliver the lone souls.
5. **Procession music**: the instrument band goes first at the sending-off ("procession music").

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Jiangnan**: the monk-Daoist rite is elaborate; "wind-and-percussion" and "doing the altar" prevail; instruments with silk-and-bamboo.
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: Buddhism and Daoism run together; the "master" (Daoist) and monks both use hand-bell chime, wooden fish, instrument bands.
- **North China**: mostly "wind players" (suona); instruments with drum-and-wind music.
- **Ethnic minorities**: each people's religious funeral instruments differ (e.g. the Tibetan prayer-wheel and suona, the Dai bronze drum); their belief system differs from the Han Buddhist-Daoist funeral, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Instruments**: hand-bell chime, wooden fish, bell, chime-cymbals, drum, great chime, incense burner, candle stand.
- **Altar display**: sutras, Buddha image (spirit seat), rebirth tablet, scripture banner.
- **Rite texts**: *Chanmen Ritual Recitation*, *Yoga Yama Mouth*, Daoist deliverance rites, folk "scripture sheet", "petition".
- **Documents**: petition, notice, rebirth-tablet text (see the "Document Templates" section).
