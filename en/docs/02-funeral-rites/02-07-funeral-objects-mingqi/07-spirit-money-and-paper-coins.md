# Spirit Money and Paper Coins

## 1. Definition

Spirit money and paper money are **"currency" made of paper and burned for the dead's use in the underworld** — the most common mingqi in folk funerals since the Song. Many names: "paper money", "lodged money", "paper money", "money paper", "gold-silver paper", "ingot", "tin foil", "spirit coin".

**Relation to mingqi**: paper money is the most typical "prepared yet unusable" object — a fake coin, whose transformation from "paper" to "underworld wealth" is completed by the ritual act of **burning**; hence "burning" is the core of paper-money use.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Han-Wei**: the custom of burying real money (*yiqian*) prevailed; after the Wei-Jin there was talk of paper for money.
- **Tang**: **paper money flourished**. Feng Yan's *Fengshi Wenjian Ji* records "now at send-offs they cut paper money, piling it into mountains", and calls it "lodged money". Burning paper money was already common in the Tang.
- **Tang-Song**: the trades of "punched money" (beating coin patterns with a paper mould), "paper horse" and "paper-money shop" appeared; Bianjing and Lin'an had shops specialising in paper horse and paper money.
- **Song-Yuan-Ming-Qing**: paper money formed a complete system — "burning paper", "hanging paper", "pressing paper" (paper pressed on the grave), "scattering paper money" (road-buying on the procession) — many uses; tin-foil ingots were especially popular in the Ming-Qing.
- **Modern**: machine-printed "spirit coins" appeared, imitating real currency (even foreign notes).
- **Contemporary**: cities mostly ban burning paper money, replacing it with flowers; villages and some cities still burn.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Choose the kind**: white paper money (plain) for first death and announcing; yellow paper money, gold-silver paper (ingots, tin foil) for burning and offering.
2. **Hang and press paper**: after burial hang and press paper on the grave head, marking the sweep.
3. **Burn paper**: at each funeral node (encoffining, procession, after burial "third-day return", "fifth seven", "hundredth day", "anniversary", "Zhongyuan") burn before the grave or at the road junction.
4. **Scatter paper money** (road-buying money): scatter along the procession, folk explained as "buying the road" or "giving to the wandering souls".
5. **Burning care**: avoid stepping on it, avoid casually turning it; some areas stress "drawing a circle" with an opening, or first burn some "scattered money" to feed the lone souls.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: mostly yellow paper "punched money" (beating coin patterns with a copper-coin mould), burned before the grave.
- **Jiangnan**: tin-foil ingots developed; Suzhou and Hangzhou's "tin-foil trade" has a long history.
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: the finest classification of gold-silver paper (longevity gold, cut gold, silver paper, treasury money), different in use; different paper for different sacrificial objects.
- **Guangdong**: "stream money" and "gold-silver paper" used together, scattered along the procession.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples also burn paper to ancestors (under Han influence), but more peoples sacrifice by burning incense, offering animals, offering wine; their system differs from the Han paper money, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Paper-money types**: white paper money, yellow paper money, gold-silver paper, ingots, treasury money, spirit coins.
- **Craft**: punched money (coin mould), folding ingots, tin-foil pasting.
- **Implements**: coin mould, paper-money punch, money-burning furnace, paper-burning basin.
- **Documents**: the "treasury" document of "treasury money", the packet (writing the receiver's name, e.g. "received by the late so-and-so").
