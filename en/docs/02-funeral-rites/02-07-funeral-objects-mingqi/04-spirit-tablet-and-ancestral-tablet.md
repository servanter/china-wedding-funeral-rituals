# Spirit Tablet and Ancestral Tablet

## 1. Definition

The **spirit tablet** (*lingwei*, the dead's seat-board) represents the dead for sacrifice in the mourning period, mostly inscribed "the spirit seat of the late father so-and-so"; the **ancestral tablet** (*shenzhu*, wooden lord, tablet) is the tablet served long-term after entering the temple. The two are the earlier and later forms of one system:

| Term | Stage | Form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Spirit tablet (spirit board)** | from first death to removing mourning | Paper or wood, white paper black characters, "spirit seat" |
| **Ancestral tablet (wooden lord)** | after removing mourning, into the hall or home shrine | Wood, chestnut or cypress, "ancestral lord", with an "inner layer" |
| **Soul-silk** (*hunbo*) | the "setting the heavy" stage at first death | white silk tied into human shape, temporarily standing for the dead's "heavy" |

**"Dotting the lord"** (*dianzhu*), also "inscribing the lord" (*tizhu*): with a vermilion brush dotting the last stroke of "lord" (or dotting "king" into "lord") on the ancestral tablet — an extremely solemn part of the funeral, held to give the dead "a resting place" after dotting.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the *Yili* ("Shi Sang Li") has the "heavy" (wood, hung in the court) and "leaning on the spirit" system; the *Liji* ("Tan'gong") discusses the "heavy" and "lord" relation, the ritual basis of the ancestral-tablet system.
- **Han-Tang**: set the "ancestral tablet" in the ancestral temple, home shrine; the tablet form of official families had grades (e.g. Tang *Kaiyuan Rites*).
- **Song**: the *Family Rituals* details "making the lord", "inscribing the lord", "dotting the lord" — the basic model for later folk funerals.
- **Ming-Qing**: the home-shrine (hall) system matured; the tablet entering the hall ("attaching to the temple", "entering the lord") became a lineage affair; the dotting rite grew solemn in folk, inviting a "great guest" (gentry, notable) to dot.
- **Modern**: the hall system declined; home sacrifice uses photos and tablets; the "dotting" rite still survives in some lineage areas (Fujian-Guangdong, Jiangxi-Anhui).

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Set the spirit tablet**: after first death set the spirit tablet (or soul-silk) in the hall; morning and evening offering, incense, food all face the tablet.
2. **Make the lord**: before or after burial make the wooden lord per the *Family Rituals*; the inner layer (陷中) writes the dead's birth-death, name, order; the outer layer writes "the ancestral lord of the late father so-and-so".
3. **Inscribe and dot the lord**: invite a great guest to write the lord; at dotting use a vermilion brush (some mix cinnabar with chicken blood) to dot the last stroke of "lord".
4. **Serve**: the lord first served at home, later "attached to the temple" (into the hall) or the home shrine, receiving the seasonal sacrifices.
5. **Remove mourning**: after the mourning ends, burn the spirit tablet or change to the ancestral tablet, completing the passage from "spirit" to "lord".

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: the hall (ancestral hall) sacrifice is developed; the tablet entering the hall is "entering the lord", a solemn rite; "dotting" mostly invites a local notable.
- **Jiangnan**: the home shrine and hall tablets are arranged by zhao-mu; the genealogy details the tablet's position.
- **North China**: mostly set a spirit board in the hall; after burial the "tablet" accompanies the burial or is burned; the hall system is less developed than the south.
- **Ethnic minorities**: ancestor-worship forms differ (e.g. the Yi "ancestral spirit tube", the Miao "drum society"), their ancestral-spirit symbols not the same source as the Han tablet, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Spirit-board**: paper, wood (white ground black characters).
- **Ancestral tablet (wooden lord)**: wood, divided into "inner layer" (陷中) and "powder face" (outer).
- **Soul-silk, heavy**: white silk, wooden frame.
- **Lord-inscribing document**: the dead's name, style, order, birth-death, burial place, format in the *Family Rituals* and funeral models (see the "Document Templates" section).
