# Coffin, Outer Coffin and Coffin Furniture

## 1. Definition

The **coffin** (*guan*) directly holds the body; the **outer coffin** (*guo*) is the large container around the coffin (wooden or stone). The *Liji* ("Tan'gong"): "the Master instituted at Zhongdu a coffin four inches, an outer coffin five inches" — Zheng Xuan's commentary says the coffin is four inches thick, the outer five, and later ages joined "coffin-outer" as the core mark of funeral grade.

- **Coffin**: also "jiu" (the cofined coffin), "chen", "du" (the smaller).
- **Outer coffin**: also "guo chamber", "outer guo"; the Shang-Zhou great tombs' "guo chamber" is built of squared timber, divided into several rooms.
- **Coffin furniture** (*guanshi*): ornaments over or hung on the coffin at the vigil, including the veil, the awning, the canopy, the pool, the knobs, the skirt, the uplift, the fans (shades).

**Grade principle**: the **number of layers** (coffin-outer layers) and the **size** follow status — the Son of Heaven "four layers" (or "five"), the feudal lords three, the great officers two, the knights one, the commoner a single coffin with no outer (the ritual books differ slightly).

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the wooden-outer tomb is the aristocratic mainstream, with multiple coffin-outer layers and much coffin furniture; the *Liji* ("Sang Da Ji") details the coffin-furniture names and display positions.
- **Warring States, Qin-Han**: the wooden-outer tomb peaks (e.g. Mawangdui Han tomb, one outer and four coffins, gorgeous coffin furniture, painted silk on the inner coffin); after the Eastern Han, **brick-chamber and stone-chamber tombs** replace the wooden outer, the guo-chamber system fades, the coffin becomes the main container.
- **Wei-Jin to Sui-Tang**: the coffin system continues; the Tang has edict-rules on the coffin's size and material; coffin furniture (canopy, veil) simplifies.
- **Song-Yuan-Ming-Qing**: coffin material is best cypress, fir, pine; they stress "single-wood coffin" and "plank coffin"; the south favours thick "earth-lacquer coffin", the canopy made of embroidered silk.
- **Modern**: cities adopt cremation; the coffin recedes, the ashes casket replaces its containing function; some rural areas still keep coffin burial.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Choose and make the coffin**: mostly made in life or right after death, stressing material (cypress, fir best), plank thickness, single-wood or plank.
2. **Encoffin**: at the great encoffining the body enters the coffin, padded, with small *mingqi* buried.
3. **Close the coffin** (closing the verdict): after the kin's last view, close and nail the coffin.
4. **Ornament the coffin**: in the vigil add the canopy, veil, embroidered coffin-cover; at the procession the "coffin canopy" covers the coffin.
5. **Lower into the grave**: the "bian", the coffin enters the pit, the grave door sealed.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: thick coffins, stressing "single plank", "four singles"; at the procession add an embroidered coffin canopy.
- **Jiangnan**: heavy-lacquer coffin, mostly black or vermilion lacquer, often a coffin-cover outside.
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: the saying "cypress above, fir below"; the coffin canopy (veil) is finely embroidered; the procession distinguishes "coffin head, coffin tail".
- **Some southwestern areas**: the "boat coffin" or "single-wood coffin" hollowed from a whole tree, of unique shape, a local tradition, a different lineage from the Han coffin-outer system.
- **Ethnic minorities**: the containers differ vastly (tree, sky, urn, stone coffins), not the Han coffin-outer system, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Coffin wood**: cypress, fir, pine, nanmu; plank thickness "three-inch", "four-inch".
- **Coffin furniture**: coffin canopy, coffin veil, embroidered coffin-cover, fans (great shades), awning-veil.
- **Outer coffin**: wooden, stone (as excavated).
- **Documents**: coffin size and material appear in the funeral list ("funeral sheet", "funeral notice") and genealogy/family-rite manuscripts.
