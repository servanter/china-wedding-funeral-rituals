# Underworld Land Deeds and Notices

## 1. Definition

The **land deed** (*maidi quan*) and the **underworld notice** (*gaodice*) are **religious documents buried with the dead**, whose function is to "report" the dead's identity and burial place to the underground gods and ghosts, obtaining a "lawful dwelling" in the underworld — the material remains of the "underworld world" idea since the Han.

| Term | Nature | Format points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Underworld notice** | A "transfer document / pass" to the underground officials | Records the dead's name, native place, grave-goods list, asks the "underground lord" to accept (e.g. "dare to inform the lord so-and-so") |
| **Land deed** | A "land contract" buying underworld land by contract form | Records the buyer and seller, land price, the four boundaries, the money sum, the contract time and witnesses, "as the law commands" |

**Important note**: the transaction recorded in the land deed is mostly **virtual** (symbolically obtaining underworld land), not a real land-right proof; its text imitates the real land contract, hence of great value for studying folk contracts, land and money terms.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Han**: the underworld notice appeared in the early Western Han (e.g. Jiangling Fenghuangshan Han tomb), recording the dead's "transfer of name-count" to the underground; the Eastern Han already had the land deed.
- **Wei-Jin, Northern and Southern Dynasties**: the land deed and the town-beast text ran in parallel, strongly Daoist in colour ("as the law commands", "the Most High Lord Lao").
- **Tang-Song**: the land-deed form gradually fixed, mostly carved on brick, stone or wooden slip; the content formalised (buying land's four boundaries, price so-many cash).
- **Song-Yuan-Ming-Qing**: the land deed was commonly used in folk, often with the town-beast text and talisman; the Ming-Qing "deed-form" land deed text is highly stylised.
- **Modern**: the land-deed custom gradually faded; only some areas still write a "deed" on paper to bury with the dead.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Write the deed**: the family or a geomancer writes the land deed or underworld notice by a fixed format.
2. **Write and carve**: carve on brick, stone, wooden slip, lead deed, or write in vermilion or ink.
3. **Bury with**: before lowering the coffin place in the coffin, before the coffin or at the grave door.
4. **Report to the earth**: the master of ceremonies or geomancer reads the "report to the earth" words, notifying the underground gods.
5. **Seal the grave**: the rite done, seal the grave.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Jiang-Han area**: early Western Han underworld notices are concentrated (Jiangling Fenghuangshan, Yunmeng).
- **Jiangnan**: Six-Dynasties to Song-Ming land deeds are often excavated, mostly brick-carved.
- **North China**: Ming-Qing land deeds and town-beast texts merged, formalised.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples' funerals also have oral or written rites of "telling the path" or "delivering property" to the dead; their belief system differs from the Han underworld idea, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Deed material**: brick, stone, wooden slip, lead plate, jade plate; paper (modern).
- **Deed-text elements**: year-month-day, the dead's name and native place, the seller (receiver) of land, the four boundaries, the land price, the witness ("the one who knows"), "as the law commands".
- **Accompanying objects**: town-beast text, talisman, grave-goods list (dispatch register).
- **Records and study**: the epigraphy works of each age and land-deed study collections.
