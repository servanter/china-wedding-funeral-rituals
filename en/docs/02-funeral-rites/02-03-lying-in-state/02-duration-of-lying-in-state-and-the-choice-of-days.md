# Duration of Lying in State and the Choice of Days

## 1. Definition

The lying-in-state period is the time from encoffining to the procession. Its length rests on three bases: **ritual grade**, **auspicious selection**, and **practical conditions** (kin arriving, grave ready, weather).

## 2. Historical Origins

- The classic *bin* term by rank: the *Liji* ("Royal Regulations") — Son of Heaven seven days to bier, seven months to burial; lords five days, five months; great officers, gentlemen, commoners three days, three months.
- Pre-Qin *bin* was very long (seven months for the Son of Heaven), an expression of feudal rank.
- *Family Rituals* compressed it to "three months to burial" as the norm; folk mostly three, five to seven days.
- Ming-Qing length was shaped by the "seven rites": within the first seven, or after all seven (49 days); rich families to a hundred days or a year.
- The burial day must be auspicious, avoiding "double mourning", "three mournings".
- Under cremation, lying-in-state is mostly 3 or 5 days ("three, five, seven").

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Fix the bier term**: with elders and a geomancer — commonly 3, 5, 7 days; extend for kin, grave, auspicious day.
2. **Choose the burial day**: by almanac and *Xiejibianfang shu*, avoiding clashes.
3. **Schedule backward** from the burial: encoffining, open-mourning, family offering (three libations), burial, third-day rite, each seven.
4. **Issue the obituary**: the schedule goes in the obituary.
5. **Keep vigil**: day and night, morning/evening offerings.
6. **Adjust**: extend for weather or absent kin, re-selecting the day.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: mostly 3 or 5 days ("encoffin on three, bury on five").
- **Jiangnan**: 3 to 7 days; "burial must be auspicious, no double mourning".
- **Min-Tai**: shorter (1–3 days), "watch the day"; some bury after the sevens.
- **Guangdong**: 3 to 7 days; "fasting" (Zhai) during lying-in-state.
- **Ethnic minorities**: great difference. Muslims **swift burial** (same or next day, at most three), by law, vs the Han "several days"; Tibetans 3–5 days by the lama per the Tibetan calendar; Miao, Yi 1 to dozens of days with singing and rites. **Lying in state is not universal.**

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Obituary**: the public notice of schedule.
- **Almanac, date-book**: for the burial day.
- **Mourning schedule**: the family's internal table.
- **Filial lamp, ever-burning lamp**: never out during lying-in-state.
