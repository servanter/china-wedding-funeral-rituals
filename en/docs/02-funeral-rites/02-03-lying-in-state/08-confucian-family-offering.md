# Confucian Family Offering: the Three Libations

## 1. Definition

The family offering (*jia dian*) is the sacrifice during lying-in-state led by **family members** on Confucian rite, parallel to but not mixed with Buddhist/Daoist rites. Its core is the "three libations" (*san xian*): **initial, second, final**, three times offering wine and food with bows.

The three libations are the common Confucian sacrificial form, used in ancestor-worship, family offering, public sacrifice; in funerals the rite-master leads, the chief mourner presides.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The three libations derive from the *Yili* (three offerings of wine in the sacrifice); the "Shisangli" already has multiple wine-offerings.
- The *Liji* ("Ji Yi"): "sacrifice as if present, sacrifice to the spirit as if the spirit is present".
- *Family Rituals* regularised family sacrifice/offering with "three libations" and "reading the prayer", the later model.
- The *Ming/Da Qing Collective Rites* fixed the offering for officials and commoners.
- Modern "public sacrifice" keeps the three libations, for public figures and collective mourning; folk became "family offering", "hall sacrifice", "opening the offering".
- Chinese burials and intangible-heritage rites still keep it.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Set the seat**: offering table with incense, wine, dishes; chief, companion, participant seats by closeness.
2. **Drum and bell**: the rite-master sings "three drums, three bells".
3. **Take places**: chief mourner at chief seat; others in order.
4. **Welcome the spirit**: incense, kneel, symbolic invite.
5. **Initial libation**: chief offers wine, food, bows; "initial libation done".
6. **Second libation**: another kin (second son) offers, bows.
7. **Final libation**: a third kin offers, bows.
8. **Read the elegy**: recite, telling the life and virtues.
9. **Prostrate and wail**: chief prostrates; all grieve.
10. **Burn the prayer, watch the fire**: burn the elegy and paper.
11. **Send the spirit**: "send the spirit"; chief bows again; done.
12. **Remove the dishes**.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: "family offering", "hall sacrifice", by a rite-master, in the home hall.
- **Jiang-Zhe**: "opening the offering", "three libations, nine bows".
- **Min-Guang**: with "merit"; Guangdong has "hall sacrifice".
- **Taiwan**: before burial, by *Family Rituals* with localisation.
- **Public sacrifice**: for public figures, like the three libations but bows replace kneeling.
- **Ethnic minorities**: mostly no three-libations form, but their own offerings (Yi animal/wine by *bimo*; Miao animal and song-hall). The three libations are within the Confucian system, not to be extended.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Offering table, wine-vessel, dishes, censer, candles**: the setting.
- **Elegy (prayer)**: core document (see the document-template entry).
- **Kneeling mats, washing vessels**.
- **Drum/bell instruments**.
- **Brazier, prayer-burner**.
