# Morning and Evening Offerings, and the Serving of Meals

## 1. Definition

The morning-and-evening offering (*zhao xi dian*) is the twice-daily offering before the spirit during lying-in-state. **An offering, not a sacrifice** — food and wine are set out but not "tasted", for the dead, "not yet buried", have no "spirit" status (only after the reassurance sacrifice is it called "sacrifice").

The serving of meals (*gong fan*) is its folk form: three meals a day brought to the spirit, with incense, kneeling and a call to eat.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* ("Shisangli"): "morning and evening wail, no offering" — the classic prescribes wail, not a separate offering; the morning/evening offering appears in the *Liji* ("Tan'gong") and later ritualists.
- The classic offering sequence: first-death, minor-dressing, major-dressing, morning/evening, new-moon, fresh-tasting, coffin-moving, ancestral, sending.
- The *Liji* ("Tan'gong"): "the offering uses plain vessels, for the living have a heart of plain mourning".
- *Family Rituals* simplified to morning/evening and new-moon offerings, with set items (wine, fruit, dried meat, mince).
- Folk "serving meals" joins the offering with "serving the dead as the living" — three meals as usual, of the dead's favourite.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Morning offering**: at sunrise; wine, fruit, rice, dishes; host incense and kneel; hostess wails within.
2. **Evening offering**: at sunset; same.
3. **Serving meals**: three meals; bowls, chopsticks, rice, wine; call "X, it is time to eat"; later remove; some share with children ("longevity meal").
4. **New-moon offering**: the first of the month, grander, with meat and wine.
5. **Fresh-tasting offering**: seasonal new things (new grain, fruit) offered.
6. **Wail and leap**: after each offering the host wails, women wail and leap; also on visitors.
7. **Burn paper**: mostly after the offering.
8. **Remove**: some shared ("eat the offering"), some given to neighbours.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North**: "serving meals", three times, "no meal overnight".
- **Jiangnan**: hot food, paired chopsticks.
- **Min-Tai**: "holding the meal" to the spirit with a call; strong rite.
- **Guangdong**: "three meals unbroken", with tea and wine.
- **Ethnic minorities**: most serve food to the dead but by different mechanism — Yi by the *bimo* inviting the soul to eat; Miao with funeral songs; Tibetans with butter and *tsampa* before Buddha and spirit; **Muslims serve no meals** (law: the dead cannot eat, only good deeds avail). A doctrinal difference, not "frugality".

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Offering table, vessels**: wine, bowls, tray.
- **Offerings**: wine, fruit, dried meat, mince, meals.
- **Incense, censer, candles**.
- **Brazier, paper money**.
- **"Offering" tablet, spirit tablet**: the object.
