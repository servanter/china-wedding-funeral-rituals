# Incense before the Spirit Tablet and Rites of Condolence

## 1. Definition

This entry records the **operative details** of a visitor to the hall: how to enter, offer incense, how many bows, how to console, how the filial son answers.

**Division from "Announcing the Death and Obituary Notices"**: that records the system and meaning of condoling; this records the step-by-step execution in the hall.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* ("Shisangli") records "guest enters, ascends, bows" — the earliest norm.
- The *Liji* ("Quli"): "at a funeral, do not smile", "on the day of condoling, no merriment", "no wine or meat" — the guest's restraint.
- *Family Rituals* fixes "condoling": enter, incense, bow, console, the host answers.
- Ming-Qing gazetteers note "three bows", "four bows", "kneel at the spirit".
- The rite-master's role is to "sing the rite" and "lead the guest".

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

**The guest**

1. **Compose dress**: at the door, sober; no red, no heavy make-up, no smile.
2. **Enter**: led by the usher; the announcer may not enter, the guest may.
3. **Incense**: at the spirit, both hands raise the incense (or the son gives it), into the censer.
4. **Bow**: by local rule — **three bows** (North), **four bows** (Min-Tai), or "bow, re-bow, three-bow" (modern).
5. **Kneel at the spirit** (some): the guest kneels, the son accompanies.
6. **Console**: to the son, "restrain grief", "take care"; avoid "died" etc.
7. **Give**: wreath, banner, condolence money (white envelope), recorded.
8. **Withdraw**: led out.

**The son's answer**

1. **Kneel company**: son kneels at the spirit's side (men out, women in).
2. **Answer bow**: as the guest bows, the son answers by kneel/bow ("the son's kowtow undeserved", mostly a prostration).
3. **Give incense**: to the guest.
4. **Thank**: on withdrawal, give a kerchief etc. ("scatter the mourning").

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North**: incense, three bows, son kneels; "accompany the guest's kowtow".
- **Jiang-Zhe**: three bows, son prostrates, "undeserved".
- **Min-Tai**: four bows, by master/rite-master; son kneels at side.
- **Guangdong**: three bows, plus "gold-silver paper".
- **Ethnic minorities**: Muslims no incense/bow — silent grief, scripture, handshake; Tibetans with *khata*, butter-lamp, scripture; Yi with animal/wine offering. **The rite is set by faith; the Han "incense, three bows" is not universal.**

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Incense, censer, candles**: for offering.
- **Bow/kneel mats**.
- **Gift-book**: registers guest and gift.
- **Wreath, banner, couplet**: gifts.
- **Condolence money (white envelope)**: never red.
- **Mourning kerchief, towel**: the answer.
