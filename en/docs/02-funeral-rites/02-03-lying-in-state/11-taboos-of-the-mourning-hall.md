# Taboos of the Mourning Hall

## 1. Definition

The mourning-hall taboos restrict the living's conduct, speech and access during lying-in-state, in four groups: **conduct, speech, persons, objects**.

Their basis is "life and death differ" and "avoid clashing evil": the hall is the boundary; wrong conduct may "take the living" or "clash the evil god".

(Recorded as custom, **not endorsed**; some taboos have a hygienic/safety kernel.)

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Liji* ("Quli") already: "at a funeral, no smile", "see the coffin, no song", "enter to mourn, no flapping arms".
- The *Liji* ("Tan'gong"): "on the day of condoling, no merriment".
- Folk taboos accumulated; Ming-Qing gazetteers record much.
- Reasonable kernels: "no cats/dogs near the corpse" (fear of change, partly explained by rigor/static); "no pregnant/new mothers" (protect the vulnerable); "no children" (avoid fright); "no tears on the corpse" (preserve, avoid decay).
- Modern cities softened most; rural areas keep them.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

**Conduct**

1. No smile, merriment, wine-drinking games.
2. No loud noise, quarrel.
3. No sweeping outward in the hall (sweeps away fortune); sweep inward or after burial.
4. No breaking vessels (ill omen; cure with lucky words).
5. No sleeping, no sitting on the bier-bed.
6. No turning the back to the tablet, no stepping over offerings.
7. No dressing up, red/green before burial.

**Speech**

1. No saying "died"; use "gone", "aged", "passed".
2. No "come again", "see you" at parting; say "take care".
3. No speaking the dead's faults before the spirit.
4. No unlucky words ("part", "scatter", "done").

**Persons**

1. No zodiac-clashing at encoffining/burial.
2. No pregnant/new mothers in the hall.
3. No mourners visiting each other's homes.
4. No "four-eyed" (pregnant) seeing the encoffining.

**Objects**

1. No breaking the incense/lamp.
2. No cats/dogs/fowl in the hall.
3. No moving the coffin after encoffining.
4. No outsider eating the offerings first.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North**: "no sweeping, no spilling" in the hall; no clashing zodiac at encoffining.
- **Jiangnan**: no "rain on the coffin"; no umbrella in the hall.
- **Min-Tai**: no "mourning into another's home"; no "mourner before a mirror".
- **Guangdong**: no "white seen with red"; no red in the hall.
- Note: protective, not discriminatory; "clash", "cat-leaps" lack science — recorded, not endorsed.
- **Ethnic difference**: Muslims have no incense/hall, hence no such "incense taboos"; Tibetan taboos are religious (no earth-moving, no killing on some days). Each people's taboos recorded apart.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Censer, ever-burning lamp**: most tended.
- **"No X zodiac" notice**: at the door.
- **Broom, door-curtain**: related.
- **Gazetteers**: the main source.
