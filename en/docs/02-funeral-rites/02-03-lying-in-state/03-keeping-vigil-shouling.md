# Keeping Vigil (Shouling)

## 1. Definition

Keeping vigil (*shou ling*, "guarding the night", "keeping the dead company") is the kin's day-and-night attendance at the spirit during lying-in-state: **tending the lamp** (incense unbroken, lamp unextinguished), **wailing by rule**, **receiving visitors**, and **keeping cats/dogs from the coffin**.

It is the most direct expression of filial piety: as many days as the lying-in-state, so many days the kin keep watch, sleepless.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* ("Shisangli"): the host "dwells in the lean-to, sleeps on grass, rests on clods" — the ritual dwelling in mourning.
- The *Liji* ("Sangdaji"): during *bin* the host "does not loosen the bands", "does not eat" — vigil is the most bodily of these.
- The *Liji* ("Wen Sang"): "for three days no fire is lit; neighbours give congee" — linked with "not eating", bodily mourning.
- The Song regularised "morning/evening wail" and "lamps by night".
- Ming-Qing: "the filial son keeps vigil, never leaving day or night"; friends "keep company by night".
- Modern funeral homes have vigil halls, but actual time is much shortened.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Roster**: kin take shifts — children, daughters-in-law mainly; friends may join.
2. **Tend the lamp**: incense unbroken; the ever-burning lamp (filial, seven-star) day and night.
3. **Wail by rule**: at morning/evening offerings all wail; by night incense and paper at set times.
4. **Guard against cats/dogs**: a great taboo ("cat leaps the corpse") — a folk belief from static and rigor, no science.
5. **Receive visitors**: by night, lead them to the spirit and accompany the bow.
6. **Burn paper**: at set night times, calling the dead's name.
7. **Serve meals**: a "night meal" before the spirit.
8. **No merriment**: no entertainment, laughter, wine (some allow chess to stay awake — a concession).
9. **Relief**: others by day; men by night.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North**: "keep vigil", "keep company"; "incense unbroken, lamp unextinguished"; friends' night-company is valued.
- **Jiangnan**: night-vigil with chanting.
- **Min-Tai**: by the "master" chanting; kin follow; night "wailing at the spirit".
- **Guangdong**: "keep the night", the seven-star lamp unquenched.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some have collective night-vigils — Miao "funeral-song hall", Tujia "jumping the funeral" (drum), Yi "stamping" — all-night song and dance to comfort the dead. **Not a "no-merriment" vigil but a collective send-off by dance**, contrasting the Han strict ban; to be understood apart.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Ever-burning, filial, seven-star lamps**: never out.
- **Incense, censer**: the lamp of succession.
- **Paper money, brazier**: for burning.
- **Lean-to, grass mat, clod**: ancient mourning implements.
- **Served meals, night snacks**: before the spirit.
- **Vigil roster**: in great families.
