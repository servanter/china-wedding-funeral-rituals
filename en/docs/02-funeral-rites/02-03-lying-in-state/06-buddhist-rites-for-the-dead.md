# Buddhist Rites for the Dead

## 1. Definition

Buddhist rites for the dead are ceremonies where monks are invited to chant, repent and give food, to "save" (*chao du*) the deceased.

**Doctrinal basis**: Buddhism holds the dead enter the *antarabhava* (intermediate state) and are reborn by karma; kin's scripture, merit and almsgiving can transfer merit to aid a good rebirth. The *Ksitigarbha Sutra* is on this theme.

## 2. Historical Origins

- Buddhism entered in the Han; from Wei-Jin it joined funerals. The Tang "seven-seven fasts" tie to the "intermediate state" (rebirth within 49 days), hence a monk-chant every seven (see "The Seven-Day Rites").
- After Song, Buddhist rites spread in folk funerals; the *Qingbai leichao* records monk-chanting everywhere.
- Main rites: scripture-chanting (Amitabha, Diamond, Ksitigarbha); repentance (Liang Huang, Water); Yogacara Fire-Mouth (*Yamakou*); Mengshan food-bestowal; Buddha-seventh/nianfo (Pure Land, "assisting the nianfo" at death and early mourning); releasing Fire-Mouth/Mengshan with the sevens.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Invite monks**: to the temple or hall; set the sessions and schedule.
2. **Set the altar**: Buddha image, flowers, lamps, fruit, a petition.
3. **Announce the petition**: read the "petition" naming the dead, life-dates, the family's wish.
4. **Chant and repent**: by session; kin follow.
5. **Transfer merit**: dedicate the chanting to the dead.
6. **Fire-Mouth (if held)**: a platform, chanting, mudras, visualisation, at dusk, 3–5 hours.
7. **Nianfo assistance**: a team chanting the name day and night (some areas).
8. **Thank the monks**: feed and give; see them off.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: chanting and repentance, mostly at the sevens.
- **Jiang-Zhe**: set "sessions" ("five sevens", "seven sevens"), with Fire-Mouth.
- **Min-Tai**: Buddhist-Daoist blend, by "master" and monks; "merit" can last days.
- **Guangdong**: "fasting", "Zhai", both Buddhist and Daoist.
- **Ethnic minorities**: Tibetan Buddhism's saving (Phowa, *Bardo Thodol* reading) by lamas differs from Han; Dai by Theravada monks; Mongol by lamas. **Same religion, but sect, language and rite differ greatly.**

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Buddha image, altar, flowers, lamps**: the setting.
- **Sutras, repentance books, instruments** (chin, wood-fish, bell, drum).
- **Petition**: the document of the dead's information.
- **Transfer verse**: the text.
- **Feeding monks, Zhai**: the giving.
