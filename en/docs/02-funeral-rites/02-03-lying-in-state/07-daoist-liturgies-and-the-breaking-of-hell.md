# Daoist Liturgies and the Breaking of Hell

## 1. Definition

Daoist rites for the dead invite Daoists to perform "refinement" (*lian du*), "breaking hell" (*po yu*), "summoning the soul", "bathing", to "rescue the soul and raise it to the immortal land".

**Doctrinal basis**: Daoism holds the dead's soul may fall to hell ("nine darkness") and suffer; rites break hell, summon the soul, refine it by water-fire, to ascend.

## 2. Historical Origins

- Daoist saving rites formed a system by the Northern-Southern dynasties; the Tang "Yellow Register fast" was a state rite.
- After Tang-Song, folk widely invited Daoists for "merit", "rites-field".
- Main rites: summoning (with talisman); bathing (symbolic); breaking hell (sword, talisman, fierce); refinement (water-fire); crossing the bridge (Naihe); food-bestowal (*shi hu*); sending the dead (burning the "death memorial", "spirit house").
- Ming-Qing gazetteers abound in "breaking hell", "refinement", especially Min-Tai, Guangdong, Jiang-Zhe.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Invite Daoists** (Min-Tai "master"); set scale ("a day-night", "three day-nights").
2. **Set the altar**: Daoist images (Three Pure Ones, Taiyi), instruments, talisman-banners.
3. **Issue the talisman**: notify the "underworld officials" (city god, earth god).
4. **Summon the soul**: chant, burn talisman, call the soul.
5. **Bathe and change**: symbolic water and cloth; burn "robe".
6. **Break hell**: sword and talisman, at night, symbolic.
7. **Refine**: water-fire refinement by talisman-water and lamp.
8. **Cross bridge, give food**: lead the soul across; feed the hungry ghosts.
9. **Send and burn**: burn "death memorial", "spirit house", paper — send the soul up.
10. **Thank the master**: feed and thank.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Min-Tai**: strongest; "merit" with strict liturgy books, Zhengyi and Lingbao lines.
- **Guangdong**: "Zhai", "fasting", both Buddhist and Daoist.
- **Jiang-Zhe**: "rites-field", refinement and breaking hell core.
- **North China**: weaker Daoist colour, Buddhist-led, but rural "yin-yang masters" remain.
- **Ethnic minorities**: Tujia by "tima"; Yi by *bimo* "guiding the road" (similar "send the soul" but unrelated to Daoism); Naxi by *dongba* "spirit-road map". **These are the peoples' own religions, not Daoist "liturgy".**

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Deity images, Daoist altar**: the setting.
- **Instruments**: sword, seal, flag, tablet, ruler, bell, drum.
- **Talismans, banners, posters**: documents.
- **Death memorial, petition**: to the underworld officials.
- **Paper spirit-house, gold/silver bridge**: to burn.
- **Hu-food**: for feeding.
