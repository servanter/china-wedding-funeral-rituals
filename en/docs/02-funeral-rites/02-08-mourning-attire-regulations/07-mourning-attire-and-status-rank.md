# Mourning Attire and Status Rank

## 1. Definition

Besides kin relation, **political status (rank)** is also a factor fixing the mourning grades — the five-degree system joins the two principles of "kin-affection" (*qinqin*) and "**reverence**" (*zunzun*).

**Main status-based dress** (per the *Yili* "Sangfu"):

| Relation | Grade |
| --- | --- |
| Subject for the ruler | Zhan cui three years (pre-Qin) |
| Feudal lords for the Son of Heaven | Zhan cui three years |
| Son of Heaven for the three dukes, feudal lords for the great officers, etc. | By rank, with "tin cui", "si cui" systems |
| Commoner for the state ruler | Zi cui three months |
| Great officer for the former ruler | Zi cui three months |
| Knight for the great officer he served | Zi cui three months (with "not returning the weapon" clauses) |

**"Rank" and "reduction"**: the same kin relation, by the two parties' rank, may be **reduced** (e.g. "the great officer for a knight" is reduced one grade), hence the principle "**reduce by rank**". The higher the status, the more special the scope and grade of the dress.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: a society divided by feudal lords, great officers, knights and commoners, so the *Yili* "Sangfu" has many rank-based dress articles.
- **After Qin-Han**: as the bureaucratic system replaced the feudal, the actual scope of "subject for the ruler, zhan cui three years" shrank, mostly replaced by the "former ruler" dress.
- **Tang-Song**: the official ritual codes (*Kaiyuan Rites*) kept graded dress rules, while simplifying.
- **Ming-Qing**: the *Ming Code* and *Comprehensive Rituals* grade the official-funeral rites (posthumous name, stele, mingqi, grave size) by rank; the dress itself tended to unify.
- **Modern**: the status-graded dress vanished; only kin dress survives in folk form.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Fix the status**: by the dead's and the wearer's rank and office, fix the applicable grade.
2. **Fix the dress**: by the ritual-book articles choose the dress (including reduction, increase).
3. **Assume and perform**: assume the dress together with the kin dress.
4. **Special dress**: e.g. "tin cui" (the feudal lords for the great officers) and other special-material dresses.
5. **Remove**: change and remove by the corresponding period.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Han, all regions**: folk actually keep only kin dress; the status dress is no longer practised.
- **Lineage society**: the clan chief's and lineage-head's funeral is given a heavier dress and higher rite grade within the clan — the folk remnant of the "reverence" principle.
- **Ethnic minorities**: native officials (tusi), headmen and the like have their own status-funeral systems, not the same as the Han "reduce by rank", and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Graded mourning dress**: tin cui, si cui, yi cui (pre-Qin graded dresses).
- **Official-funeral rules**: the graded limits on posthumous name, stele, mingqi, grave size (the codes of each age).
- **Documents**: official-rank, honour-bestowal, posthumous-name documents (life-account, stele-epitaph).
