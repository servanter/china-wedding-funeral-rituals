# Changing and Removing the Mourning Dress (Bianchu)

## 1. Definition

Mourning is not "one dress to the end", but **step by step lightens from heavy to light with the passing of time, until removal**. Three key concepts:

| Concept | Meaning | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Receiving the dress** (shou fu) | Receive the dress of "finished cloth" (finer worked cloth), coarse to fine | After the third month, once buried |
| **Changing the dress** (bian fu) | Change crown and robe by the season (trained crown, trained robe) | Minor *xiang*, major *xiang* |
| **Removing the dress** (chu fu) | The mourning term ends, take off the mourning dress | After the *dan* sacrifice |

**The theory of change and removal**: the *Liji* ("Jianzhuan") explains it by "**the decreasing of grief**" — grief lessens with time, hence the mourning dress also goes coarse to fine, heavy to light, the "rite"'s tempering of "feeling".

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the *Yili* ("Sangfu") and *Liji* ("Jianzhuan", "Sannian Wen") fix the change-and-removal nodes.
- **Han-Wei**: the "twenty-five months" vs "twenty-seven months" dispute (Zheng Xuan held twenty-seven, Wang Su twenty-five).
- **Tang-Song**: the *Kaiyuan Rites* fixed the "twenty-seven months" system (including the *dan*), followed by later ages.
- **Ming-Qing**: ritual books detail the minor *xiang*, major *xiang*, *dan* change-and-removal rites.
- **Modern**: folk take "hundred days", "first anniversary", "three years" (joining the furnace, removing mourning) as the change-and-removal nodes.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Third month, once buried — receiving the dress**: change to finer cloth (e.g. *zhan cui*'s lower robe changed to finished cloth).
2. **Thirteenth month — minor *xiang***: trained crown, trained robe ("trained" means boiled-and-washed cloth), remove the coarse head-fillet.
3. **Twenty-fifth month — major *xiang***: wear "plain white-hemp robe", remove the fillet and staff, take off the heavy dress.
4. **Twenty-seventh month — *dan***: perform the *dan* sacrifice, remove the dress, wear the dark-and-light cap and court dress; the mourning system ends.
5. **Remove the dress**: after the *dan*, resume normal life and dress colour.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Han, all regions**: take "fifth seven", "hundred days", "anniversary", "three years" as the mourning-removal nodes; Min-Tai ends with "joining the furnace".
- **Ethnic minorities**: the mourning period and removal mark differ (removing the mourning cloth, washing the hair, feasting guests); not the change-and-removal system, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Trained crown, trained robe, plain white-hemp robe**.
- **Dan sacrifice furnishings**: offerings, prayer.
- **Documents**: the "mourning-degree line" in the obituary and thanks-note (e.g. "term-mourning grandson"), marking the status after change-and-removal.
