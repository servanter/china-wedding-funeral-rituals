# Mourning Attire for Women

## 1. Definition

Women's dress is divided by "**unmarried** (in the chamber)" and "**married out**", the most concentrated expression of the "derivative" (*cong*) and "reduced" (*jiang*) principles of the five degrees.

| Relation | Unmarried daughter | Married-out daughter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| For the father | Zhan cui three years | Zi cui no-staff (reduced) |
| For the mother | Zi cui three years (after the Ming, father living, also zhan cui) | Zi cui no-staff (reduced) |
| For brothers | Zi cui no-staff (father living) / Da gong | Da gong (reduced) |
| For grandparents | Zi cui no-staff | Da gong (reduced) |
| For the husband | — | **Zhan cui three years** |
| For the husband's parents (in-laws) | — | Zi cui no-staff |
| For the eldest son | Zi cui three years (Ming changed to no-staff) | Same |
| For the other sons | Zi cui no-staff | Same |
| Wife of the heir, concubine for the principal wife | Follows derivative-mourning cases | — |

**Two core principles**:
1. **"Unmarried, follow the father; once married, follow the husband"** — the married-out daughter enters the husband's dress system, "reduced one grade" in her natal line.
2. **"The husband is the wife's heaven"** — the wife wears the heaviest zhan cui three years for the husband, embodying the absolute rank between husband and wife.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the *Yili* ("Sangfu") fixes the unmarried and married-out women's dress; the clauses "women do not hold the staff" and "women also hold the staff" are disputed.
- **Tang**: "father living, for the mother" was once changed to zi cui three years, then reverted.
- **Ming**: "father living, for the mother" fixed as zhan cui three years (mother's mourning elevated); "mother for the eldest son" changed to zi cui no-staff.
- **Qing**: followed the Ming, with detailed women's-dress rules.
- **Modern**: the man-woman dress difference faded; only the folk idea "the daughter's mourning lighter than the son's" survives.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Fix unmarried / married-out**: first judge the woman's marital status to fix the grade.
2. **Assume the dress**: wear the corresponding dress by grade.
3. **Out-marrying and returning home**: a married-out woman returning to her natal funeral follows the reduced-mourning case.
4. **Change and remove**: change and remove by the husband's or natal line's period.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Han, all regions**: folk mostly keep the trace of the ritual by "the daughter's mourning lighter, the daughter-in-law's heavier"; Min-Tai's "filial daughter" and "filial daughter-in-law" differ in dress colour and belt.
- **Ethnic minorities**: women's mourning form and duty are mostly decided by each people's tradition and religion, different from the Han "unmarried / married-out" reduction principle, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Women's mourning dress**: cui, shang, crown (or "mourning head"), die, belt.
- **Documents**: the obituary's "mourning-degree line" distinguishes "filial son", "filial daughter", "filial daughter-in-law".
- **Ritual basis**: the *Yili* "Sangfu" women's-dress articles, the *Family Rituals*, the *Comprehensive Rituals*.
