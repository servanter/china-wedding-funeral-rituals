# Mourning Taboos and Living Restrictions

## 1. Definition

"Mourning" is not only dress, but a **whole set of life restrictions (the rites of dwelling in mourning)**. The ancients used "**the rites of dwelling in mourning**" to restrain body and desire, so grief was realised in outward conduct.

| Aspect | Restriction |
| --- | --- |
| **Diet** | Zhan cui "no food for three days"; "once coffined, eat congee"; "third month, once buried, eat plain food and water"; the *qi*, da gong each with decrease (*Liji* "Jianzhuan") |
| **Dwelling** | "Dwell in the lean-to, sleep on straw and a clod" — live in a grass shed, sleep on straw, pillow on a clod; "not plastered", "not curtained" |
| **Sleep** | "Sleep not untying the fillet and belt" (zhan cui); at the minor *xiang* begin "dwelling in the white-washed chamber", at the major *xiang* "return to the bed" |
| **Speech** | "In mourning, speak only of the mourning"; no joking with others, no music |
| **Dress** | No coloured dress, no ornaments, no bathing (at first) |
| **Marriage** | "**In the parents' mourning, no marriage for three years**"; within the period arrange no marriage for children, go to no feast, make no music |
| **Other** | Dwell not within (not with wife/concubine), go to no other's celebration, join no sacrifice ("the mourner sacrifices not") |

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the *Liji* ("Jianzhuan", "Sang Da Ji", "Quli") systematically records the dwelling restrictions and their decrease.
- **Han-Tang**: the dwelling rites entered the law — "**violating the dwelling rites**" could be a crime (e.g. the Han "unfilial" crime, the Tang "making music in mourning" law).
- **Song-Ming**: Neo-Confucianism strengthened the dwelling norms; the *Family Rituals* fixes the period's conduct.
- **Ming-Qing**: the law had clear penalties for marrying, making music, going to feasts in mourning.
- **Modern**: the legal articles abolished; folk keep some taboos (no happy events in mourning, no red couplets, no New-Year visiting).

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **After assuming the dress**: begin the mourning life, move into the "lean-to".
2. **Diet decrease**: no food three days → congee → plain food and water → after the minor *xiang* eat vegetables and fruit → after the major *xiang* resume normal.
3. **Dwelling move**: lean-to → white-washed chamber → return to the bed.
4. **Speech and intercourse**: speak only of the mourning, make no music, go to no feast.
5. **Remove the dress**: after the *dan*, resume normal dwelling, marriage allowed.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Han, all regions**: folk keep taboos like "no happy events in mourning", "no red couplets", "no New-Year visiting"; North China has "the filial son keeps vigil and eats no meat".
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: within the mourning period (the "filial period") forbid marriage and attending celebrations; the mourning family "holds no festivity".
- **Ethnic minorities**: each people's mourning taboos differ greatly (forbidding hunting, song-dance, marriage, certain foods); their basis is mostly the people's own belief, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Mourning furnishings**: the lean-to (grass shed), straw and clod, white-washed chamber.
- **Diet vessels**: the congee vessel, plain-food vessels.
- **Legal articles**: the "violating the dwelling rites" clauses in the codes of each age.
- **Ritual basis**: *Liji* "Jianzhuan", "Sang Da Ji", "Quli".
