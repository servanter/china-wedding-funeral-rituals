# The Cut and Construction of Mourning Dress

![传统白色丧服（孝服）/ Traditional white hemp mourning attire](../../../../assets/images/03-mourning-attire.png)


## 1. Definition

The cut and construction of mourning dress means **the appearance and tailoring of each part of the five grades of mourning dress**. A complete mourning dress consists of the following parts:

| Part | Note |
| --- | --- |
| **Cui (upper robe)** | The mourning upper robe, with a "cui" patch on the breast; *zhan cui* uses coarse hemp, hemmed ones called "qi" |
| **Shang (lower garment)** | The lower-body garment; *zhan cui* "three panels front, four back" |
| **Crown** (guan) | Mourning crown; *zhan cui* uses a rope-knot, *zi cui* a cloth-knot |
| **Die** (jíng) | Head-die (band on the head) and waist-die (band at the waist), of hemp |
| **Belt** (dài) | Twisted belt (*zhan cui* uses raw-hemp twist), cloth belt (*zi cui* down) |
| **Staff** (zhàng) | Raw-hemp staff (bamboo), cut staff (tong) |
| **Shoes** (jù) | Reed shoes, coarse shoes, rope shoes (of grass, hemp rope) |

The difference of "**zhan**" and "**qi**" lies in the cutting: "**zhan**" means **the hem is left unhemmed** (cut but not sewn), showing grief too extreme to adorn; "**qi**" means **the hem is hemmed** (sewn even), showing grief slightly lessened.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* ("Sangfu") systematically fixes the cut of the five grades and the differences of twenty-odd dresses; its text and the Zixia commentary are the source of all later mourning-dress systems.
- The *Liji* ("Jianzhuan") explains the ritual meaning behind the cut ("why *zhan cui* wears raw hemp", "why *zi cui* wears for the mother") and the principle of decreasing grief.
- Han-Tang: exegetes (Zheng Xuan, Jia Gongyan, Kong Yingda) detail every point of cut (size, thread-count, tailoring); the *Tang Kaiyuan Rites* put the dress system into the state code.
- Song-Yuan-Ming-Qing: the *Family Rituals*, *Ming Code*, *Comprehensive Rituals* preserve the cut by charts and tables.
- Modern: the material cut has left daily use, surviving only in lineage family-rite texts, opera and scholarly reconstruction.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Assuming the dress** (*chengfu*): the day after the great encoffining, kin change into the corresponding dress by grade.
2. **Wear the robe**: first the cui and shang, then the crown, die, belt, shoes.
3. **Hold the staff**: *zhan cui* and *zi cui* staff-period wearers hold the staff.
4. **Change the dress** (*bianfu*): at the minor and major *xiang* change by rite (see "Changing and Removing the Mourning Dress").
5. **Remove the dress** (*chufu*): at term remove it.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Han, all regions**: the ritual cut is more complete in lineage areas (Fujian-Guangdong, Huizhou) genealogies; common folk long simplified to "mourning dress".
- **Ethnic minorities**: each people's mourning dress differs (white cloth, hemp, reversed sheep-skin, cutting the hair); their garment system is not framed by "cui, shang, die, belt", and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Mourning-dress objects**: cui, shang, crown, die, belt, staff, shoes.
- **Dress charts**: the natal nine-degree chart, the mourning-dress chart (in the *Family Rituals* and *Comprehensive Rituals*).
- **Ritual books**: *Yili* "Sangfu" and its commentaries, the mourning-dress charts of each age.
