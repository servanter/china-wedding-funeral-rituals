# The Mourning Staff (Sangzhang)

## 1. Definition

The mourning staff, also "grief staff", "filial staff", "weeping staff", is the staff the filial son holds in heavy mourning. The *Liji* ("Wensang"): "**the filial son mourning kin weeps without count, serves diligently three years, his body ill and weak, and leans on the staff for his illness**" — the staff's original meaning is **to lean on illness**, not ornament.

| Term | Material | Use | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Raw-hemp staff** (ju zhang) | Bamboo | Zhan cui (father's mourning, etc.) | *Ju* means coarse and vile; "the raw-hemp staff is of bamboo" |
| **Cut staff** (xue zhang) | Tong (paulownia) | Zi cui staff-period (mother's mourning, etc.) | "the cut staff is of tong", pared tong wood |

**Carrying the staff**: the staff is held "**with the root down**" (the coarse end below), the hand at the upper end; ascending steps, taking position, weeping each have their way of holding; the staff's thickness and length also have grades (the Son of Heaven, feudal lords, great officers, knights each with measure).

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the *Liji* ("Wensang", "Sangfu Sizhi") and *Yili* ("Sangfu") fix the staff system.
- **Han-Tang**: exegetes fix the staff's size and carrying; Tang edicts put it in the mourning system.
- **Song-Ming**: the filial son's staff-carrying is in the *Family Rituals*.
- **Ming-Qing**: folk "weeping staff" mostly of willow, bamboo or sorghum stalk wrapped in white paper.
- **Modern**: North China and some southern areas still keep the "filial staff" custom.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Make the staff**: at assuming the dress prepare the staff — *zhan cui* bamboo, *zi cui* tong (or folk willow).
2. **Hold the staff**: the filial son holds the staff in coming and going, kneeling, receiving guests, sending the coffin.
3. **Cry on the staff**: at raising the coffin and burial "cry on the staff" (touch the ground with it).
4. **Set or burn the staff**: after burial set by the grave or burn it.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the filial son holds the "weeping staff", wrapped in white paper, set before the grave after burial.
- **Jiangnan**: holds the staff in mourning, mostly bamboo.
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: the "filial staff" and "hemp staff" differ; the staff-end wrapped in white cloth.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples mark the filial son's status with a wood or bamboo staff in the funeral; its symbolic meaning (exorcism, leading the way) differs from the Han "leaning on illness", and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Staff**: bamboo staff (raw-hemp), tong staff (cut), willow staff.
- **Wrap**: white paper, white cloth.
- **Ritual books and dress charts**: *Liji* "Wensang", *Yili* "Sangfu" mourning-dress charts.
