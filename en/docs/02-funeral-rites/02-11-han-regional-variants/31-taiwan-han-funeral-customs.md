# Taiwan Han Funeral Customs

## 1. Definition

Taiwan's Han are mainly "**Minnan (Zhang-Quan) and Hakka immigrant descendants**"; the funeral custom comes from the Fujian-Guangdong rites and is preserved very completely, a "**living specimen of the Han traditional funeral**". Main features:

1. **"Do the decade"** — take the "decade" (ten days) as the sacrifice term, first decade to seventh decade; the "**sixth decade**" is mostly offered by the daughter, hence "daughter's decade".
2. **"Master doing merit"** — invite Daoists (masters) for deliverance rites, grand.
3. **"Cry at the road-head"** — the daughter, hearing of death and returning home, cries a welcome at the road-head.
4. **"Nailing the coffin"** — at nailing, descendants shout together "hē" (will), showing the dead rest.
5. **"Bone-picking" and "entering the pagoda"** — after earth burial years, pick the bones into a gold urn; or place the ashes in a spirit-ash pagoda ("entering the pagoda").
6. **"Joining the furnace"** — at removing the dress hold the "joining the furnace" rite, writing the dead's name into the ancestral tablet; the end-point of mourning.
7. **Three-grade mourning dress** — hemp shirt (heavy), ramie shirt (next), white shirt (light), matching the five-degree "heavy, next, light" three grades.
8. **"Black umbrella shading the soul"** — at the procession a black umbrella shades the tablet (soul-silk); the sons bear it.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Ming-Qing**: Fujian-Guangdong immigrants crossed to Taiwan, bringing native customs; Qing gazetteers (Taiwan prefecture gazetteer, county gazetteers) record the rites.
- **Japanese period**: the custom was suppressed and reformed, yet the folk kept self-running.
- **After 1945**: custom continued; Buddhist groups pushed "Buddhist funeral" (cancelling paper-burning, simplifying).
- **Contemporary**: cremation and spirit-ash pagodas spread; "bone-picking" gradually less; professional funeral service; traditional rites continue in folk belief and clan activity.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **First death**: move the bed, cleanse, change (the robe), "cry at the road-head".
2. **Report death**: obituary to kin (white card).
3. **Encoffin**: minor and great encoffining, "nailing the coffin".
4. **Do the decade / do merit**: an offering every decade; invite the master for rites; "offer rice" morning and evening.
5. **Procession**: raise the coffin, road sacrifice, send; the mourning kin hold the mourning staff, bear the black umbrella.
6. **Bury and pick bones**: after earth burial pick the bones into the gold urn, or cremate.
7. **After burial**: "hundred days", "first year", "three years", "joining the furnace" to remove the dress; sweep at Qingming, death day.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **South (Tainan, Kaohsiung, Pingtung)**: custom best preserved; doing the decade, merit exquisite.
- **Central (Changhua, Taichung)**: both Zhang-Quan and Hakka custom.
- **North (Taipei, Keelung)**: urbanised earlier, simpler rites; Buddhist funeral popular.
- **Hakka (Taoyuan, Hsinchu, Miaoli, Meinong)**: secondary burial (bone-picking) and clan custom clear.
- **Other ethnicities**: Taiwan's indigenous peoples' funerals each form systems, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: coffin, gold urn (bone-picking), mourning dress (hemp/ramie/white shirt), mourning staff, black umbrella, paper house, treasury money, gold and silver paper.
- **Documents**: obituary (white card), thanks-note, gift list, ancestral tablet (joining-furnace inscription).
- **Facilities**: hall, spirit-ash pagoda.
