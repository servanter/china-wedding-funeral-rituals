# Beijing Funeral Customs

## 1. Definition

Beijing funeral customs are a blend of **capital ritual tradition and marketplace folk custom**; with banners and Han, officials and commoners living together, the rites stress "having a pattern and a style", and old Beijing especially valued "**propriety**" and "**show**". Main features:

1. **"Jie San" and "Song San"** — on the third day after death hold "Jie San" (receiving the third), and in the evening "Song San" (sending paper carriages and horses, burning the "bundle"), the most solemn node of old Beijing.
2. **Bearing-house and coffin canopy** — the procession is run by a professional "bearing-house"; the number of bearers (eight, sixteen, thirty-two) marks status.
3. **The "happy funeral" idea** — one who dies at a high age is mourned with bustle; wind music, opera and storytelling are all allowed.
4. **Staying the coffin in a temple** — old custom stayed and did Buddhist rites in temples (e.g. Jiaxing Temple, Xianliang Temple).

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Yuan-Ming**: Dadu and Beijing as capitals, the funeral rites followed the *Ming Code*, also absorbing immigrants' customs.
- **Qing**: banner and Han customs merged; the bearing-house, shed-shop and mingyi-shop (paper-effigy shop) became special trades, forming "the Beijing-school funeral custom".
- **Republic**: new and old coexisted; newspaper obituaries and public memorials arose.
- **Contemporary**: cremation spread; Babaoshan and the district funeral homes take the main funeral function; old customs partly remain in suburbs and old city.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **First death**: move the body, test the breath, bathe, mouth-fill, put on grave clothes.
2. **Report death**: notify kin and friends; paste white paper at the door ("funeral notice", "door report").
3. **Encoffin**: minor and great encoffining, small grave goods in the coffin.
4. **Jie San and Song San**: on the third day Jie San, in the evening Song San, burn paper carriages and horses and the bundle.
5. **Open the condolence, receive condolences**: set the mourning shed; the filial son kneels in the shed to receive; rich families invite a rite-master to chant.
6. **Procession (send forth)**: smash the basin, raise the poles, send the coffin under the canopy, scatter paper money along the way.
7. **Burial and after**: lower the grave, round the mound; offer at "fifth seven", "hundred days", "anniversary".

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Old city (east and west city)**: many rules, stressing show; bearing-house, shed-shop, mingyi-shop old trades concentrated.
- **Haidian, Chaoyang and near suburbs**: simpler rites, mostly by village custom.
- **Far suburbs (Mentougou, Fangshan, Miyun)**: keep old customs like "sending the travelling money", "running the directions".
- **Banner old custom**: traces of "Manchu rites" (e.g. mourning-dress form, sacrifice mode).
- **Other ethnicities**: Beijing also has the Hui (Islamic funeral), Manchu, Mongol and other funeral customs, not in the Han funeral system, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: coffin canopy, bearing gear, mingyi (paper effigies), bundle, mourning basin, mourning robe and cap.
- **Documents**: obituary (fuwen), funeral notice, gift list (registering gifts), elegiac-couplet book.
- **Old Beijing trade goods**: bearing-house poles, shed-shop mat sheds, mingyi-shop paper work.
