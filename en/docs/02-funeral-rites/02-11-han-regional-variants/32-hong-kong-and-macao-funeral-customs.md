# Hong Kong and Macao Funeral Customs

## 1. Definition

Hong Kong and Macao residents are mainly "**Cantonese (Cantonese-speech)**"; the funeral custom inherits the "**Cantonese tradition**", yet under modern urbanisation and Chinese-Western meeting formed a "**urban-type traditional funeral**". Main features:

1. **"Keep the night" and "great encoffining"** — set the mourning hall in the funeral home to keep the night; next day great encoffin, procession.
2. **The Daoist "breaking the hell"** — Hong Kong's most marked Daoist deliverance rite; the Daoist with "breaking the hell" symbolises freeing the dead from the nether path (listed as intangible heritage).
3. **"Hit the zhai"** — invite monks and Daoists to do the zhai, chant for the dead.
4. **"Return-of-soul night"** — by reckoning the night the soul returns home; the family prepare offerings, spread ash to read traces.
5. **"Cleanse-wine feast"** — after the procession set a vegetarian feast for guests, called "cleanse-wine feast" (like the tofu feast elsewhere).
6. **"White gold" and "lucky packet"** — the funeral gift is "white gold" (must be odd); the bereaved return a "lucky packet" (with sugar, a red packet) to dissolve.
7. **Cremation spread** — the ashes niche (public/private ash-placement) is the main resting.
8. **Macao Chinese-Western parallel** — the Catholic funeral (mass, holy water, cross) and the Chinese funeral coexist; funeral facilities follow the old style.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Ming-Qing**: Hong Kong, Macao belonged to Guangzhou prefecture, Xiangshan county; custom consistent with Cantonese.
- **After the opening**: under British Hong Kong and Portuguese Macao, Western funeral systems (cemetery, crematorium, funeral home) were introduced.
- **20th century**: funeral homes (e.g. Hong Kong Funeral Home, World Funeral Home) became the mourning centre; Daoist rites joined funeral service.
- **Contemporary**: cremation and ashes niches main; traditional rites hosted by professional "hall attendants" (rite-masters).

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **First death**: move to the mortuary (public mortuary); obituary to kin.
2. **Set the spirit (keep the night)**: the funeral home sets the hall; kin offer; Daoist "breaking the hell" or Buddhist chanting.
3. **Great encoffining**: into the coffin, kin view, close the lid.
4. **Procession**: bid farewell to the spirit, send to the cemetery or crematorium.
5. **Cremate and place**: place the ashes in the niche or choose a burial.
6. **"Cleanse-wine feast"**: after the procession set a vegetarian feast; the bereaved return the "lucky packet".
7. **After burial**: sweep at Qingming, Double Ninth, death day; set the offering on the return-of-soul night.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Hong Kong urban**: the funeral-home—crematorium—ashes-niche system complete; rites hosted by rite-masters.
- **New Territories (Yuen Long, Tai Po, Sha Tin)**: indigenous residents (Wai Tau, Hakka) keep clan funeral and earth-burial traces, with clan-ancestor rites like "the ancestral share".
- **Outlying islands (Cheung Chau, Lamma)**: island custom, simpler rites.
- **Macao**: Chinese and Catholic funerals parallel; facilities near Cantonese.
- **Other ethnicities**: Hong Kong-Macao also have South-Asian, Filipino funeral customs, not in the Han system, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: coffin, mourning dress (white robe, hemp belt), gold and silver paper, paper effigies, "breaking the hell" altar furnishings, lucky-packet envelope.
- **Documents**: obituary (often newspaper obituary), thanks-note, white-gold envelope, tombstone inscription.
- **Facilities**: funeral home, public mortuary, cemetery, ashes-niche place (ash-placement).
