# Guangdong Funeral Customs

## 1. Definition

Guangdong's three lineages (**Cantonese, Teochew, Hakka**) each have funeral customs; the common point is "**clan and rites equally stressed, stress on secondary burial**". Main features:

1. **"Buy water"** — Cantonese custom: the filial son (daughter-in-law) throws coins into the river to fetch water, to bathe the dead; the most locally marked rite.
2. **"Hit the zhai"** — invite monks and Daoists to do the zhai, release the hungry ghosts; the deliverance rite is solemn.
3. **"Do the sevens"** — first seven to seven sevens, with "hundred days", "anniversary".
4. **"Bone-picking (collecting bones) re-burial"** — a few years after first earth burial pick the bones into a gold pagoda (gold urn), re-bury elsewhere; Hakka and Teochew especially flourish.
5. **"White gold" and "lucky packet"** — the funeral gift is "white gold" (must be odd), the bereaved return a "lucky packet" (with sugar, a red packet) to dissolve.
6. **"Parade the street" procession (Teochew)** — the procession walks along the street, with solemn insignia.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Tang-Song**: central-plains and Fujian-Jiangxi immigrants went south; the Cantonese, Teochew, Hakka groups took shape in the Song-Ming.
- **Ming-Qing**: clan halls and clan property developed; gazetteers and genealogies record the rites in detail.
- **Modern**: close Guangdong-Hong Kong exchange; Hong Kong's "breaking the hell" and other Daoist rites flowed back.
- **Contemporary**: cremation main; "bone-picking" gradually less; clan villages still do the sevens, hit the zhai.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **First death**: move the bed, buy water to bathe, change, set the spirit.
2. **Report death**: obituary to kin (white card).
3. **Encoffin**: great encoffining, grave goods in the coffin.
4. **Hit the zhai and do the sevens**: invite monks and Daoists for the zhai; an offering every seven days.
5. **Procession**: raise the coffin, parade the street (Teochew), send.
6. **Bury and pick bones**: after earth burial, years later pick the bones, into the gold pagoda re-bury.
7. **After burial**: remove the dress, "hundred days", "anniversary"; sweep at Qingming, Double Ninth.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Cantonese (Guangzhou, Foshan, Dongguan)**: "buy water", hit the zhai, do the sevens as features.
- **Teochew (Shantou, Chaozhou, Jieyang)**: solemn procession insignia, fine paper effigies; "doing merit" flourishes.
- **Hakka (Meizhou, Huizhou, Heyuan)**: secondary burial (bone-picking) most typical; clan hall leads.
- **Leizhou peninsula, west Guangdong**: both Minnan and native colour.
- **Other ethnicities**: Guangdong's Yao, Zhuang, She funerals form their own systems, and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: coffin, gold pagoda (gold urn), mourning dress (white robe, hemp belt), paper effigies, white-gold envelope, lucky packet.
- **Documents**: obituary (white card), thanks-note, gift list, genealogy family-rite manuscript.
- **Clan goods**: hall, clan property, ancestral grave.
