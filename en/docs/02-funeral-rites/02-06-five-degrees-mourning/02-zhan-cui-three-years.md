# Zhan Cui (Three Years)

## 1. Definition

*Zhan cui* is the heaviest of the five degrees of mourning, its period three years (actually twenty-five or twenty-seven months). "*Zhan*" means the hem of the mourning dress is **left unhemmed, the cut exposed**, made of the coarsest raw hemp, signifying "grief so extreme there is no time to trim".

**Those who wear *zhan cui* for three years (per the *Yili*, "Sangfu"):**

1. The son for the father (including the heir son, concubine-born son, and unmarried daughter for the father);
2. The wife for the husband;
3. The subject for the ruler;
4. The father for the eldest son (*chengzhong*, bearing the succession);
5. The adopted heir (*weirenhou*) for the father he succeeds;
6. The feudal lords for the Son of Heaven;
7. The concubine for the husband (as ruler);
8. The unmarried daughter in the chamber for the father; the married daughter back in the chamber (repudiated or widowed, returned home) for the father;
9. The stepmother "as mother", the nurturing mother "as mother" (under set conditions).

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* ("Sangfu") opens with "the *zhan cui* robe, the strung *jiedie*, the staff, the twisted belt, the cord-tassel cap, the rush shoes" — marking the heaviest degree by the coarsest gear.
- The *Liji* ("Sannian Wen") argues the meaning of the "three-year" mourning, comparing it to "heaven's three years making one intercalary month", explaining why "three years" is the longest period.
- **The elevation of the mother's mourning**: before the Tang, "the father living, for the mother" was *zi cui* staff-period; under Wu Zetian it became *zi cui* three years; after Ming Taizu's statute, **"the son for the mother" is *zhan cui* three years whether the father lives or not** (mother's mourning elevated) — one of the most important changes in the history of the five degrees.
- **The adopted heir**: the heir wears *zhan cui* three years for the father he succeeds, but reduces (*jiang*) the mourning for his birth parents to *zi cui* no-staff period — called "reduced mourning" (*jiangfu*), showing the lineage-law "transfer of lineage" (移宗).
- The Qing *Comprehensive Rituals* follows the Ming; the scope of *zhan cui* three years is broadly stable.
- In modern times the degree system was abolished; only folk mourning dress survives (the filial son in coarse white cloth, with a hemp rope at the waist).

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Assuming the dress** (*chengfu*): after the great encoffining, the *zhan cui* wearer changes into the *zhan cui* dress (coarse raw-hemp robe, unhemmed), adds the strung *die* (hemp belt), cord-tassel cap, rush shoes.
2. **Mourning residence**: live in the "lean-to" (*yilu*), sleep on straw and a clod; no wine or meat, only after a hundred days may eat vegetables and fruit.
3. **Position of weeping**: keep vigil in the mourning period, weep morning and evening; at the three-libation rite is the chief sacrificer.
4. **Changing the dress**: at the first anniversary (*xiaoxiang*) change to the trained cap; at the second anniversary (*daxiang*) change to plain white-hemp dress.
5. **Removing mourning**: a month (or three) after the *daxiang*, perform the *dan* sacrifice and remove the dress.
6. **Restrictions within the period**: no marriage, no banquets, no music, no office (丁忧 *dingyou*), no inner chamber ("in mourning dwell not within").

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Han, all regions**: folk mostly simplify to "the filial son wears coarse white mourning dress, a mourning cap, a hemp rope, holds the mourning staff", the period shortened from three years to a hundred days, a year, or a symbolic "three years".
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: the filial son wears "coarse white mourning dress" ("hemp shirt", "mourning shirt"), to the "removing of mourning"; the "three years" mostly ends with the "joining the furnace" rite.
- **North China**: the filial son and daughter wear a "mourning gown", hemp rope, mourning cap (stiffened white cloth), mostly for a hundred days or a year.
- **Ethnic minorities**: the five degrees are a product of the Han lineage law. The Yi's clan (jiazhi) mourning and the Tibetan's religious mourning each have their rules, not the same system as *zhan cui* three years.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Zhan cui dress**: coarse raw-hemp robe, unhemmed.
- **Strung *die* (head and waist), twisted belt**.
- **Cord-tassel cap, rush shoes** (straw sandals).
- **Mourning staff** (the *judie*).
- **Lean-to, straw and clod**: mourning implements.
- **Ritual books and mourning diagrams**.
