# Da Gong (Nine Months)

## 1. Definition

*Da gong* is the third of the five degrees, its period nine months. "*Gong*" means the working of the hemp cloth (功 has the sense of "to labour"): the *da gong* dress uses **cooked hemp**, roughly worked, finer than *zi cui* and coarser than *xiao gong*, hence "great work".

*Da gong* is the degree of **same-hall (同堂) lineage kin**: brothers' children, cousin-brothers and the like are mostly *da gong*.

## 2. Scope of the Degree

**Per the *Yili* ("Sangfu"), the chief *da gong* nine-months relations:**

1. For the paternal cousins (堂兄弟, i.e. uncles' sons);
2. For the concubine-grandson (grandson other than the heir grandson);
3. For the married-out daughter (a daughter married out, reduced to *da gong*);
4. For the paternal aunts unmarried (unmarried paternal aunts and sisters);
5. For the husband's grandparents (wife for the husband's grandparents);
6. For the divorced mother for the son (the repudiated mother for the son);
7. For the stepmother for the concubine-son under set conditions;
8. For the husband's brother's son (wife for the husband's brother's son), etc.

**Note**: the scope and grades listed by the ritualists differ, and the ritual codes of each age adjusted them; the above follows the common *Yili* system.

## 3. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* ("Sangfu") fixes the *da gong* nine-month degree and scope; the exegesis (Zheng Xuan, Jia Gongyan) details the difference of "reduced" (*jiang*) and "regular" (*zheng*) mourning.
- The role of **reduced mourning** is clearest: a woman married out becomes "outer kin", and her mourning for her natal kin is reduced by one degree across the board — hence "for the married-out daughter" drops from *zi cui* to *da gong*, "for the married-out paternal aunt" drops from *da gong* to *xiao gong*.
- The Tang and Song followed; the Ming *Ming Code* adjusted (e.g. the married-out daughter's mourning for her natal house).
- The Qing *Comprehensive Rituals* details the *da gong* relations.
- Folk practice no longer distinguishes seven, nine, five and three months, mostly calling all lighter dress "work dress" (功服).
- **Contemporary**: the five-degree distinction in folk is basically simplified to "heavy dress (parents) / next dress (grandparents, uncles) / light dress (others)" three grades.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: the mourning dress's three grades "hemp shirt — ramie shirt — white shirt", where the "ramie shirt" roughly corresponds to the work dress (nine months).
- **North China**: the coarseness of the mourning cloth and the mourning-cap shape mark the degree; the *da gong* grade mostly shows as "finer white-cloth mourning dress".
- **Jiangnan**: stresses "degrees of mourning dress" but mostly no longer distinguishes finely.
- **Ethnic minorities**: the five degrees are a product of the Han lineage law; ethnic differences must be recorded separately (e.g. the Yi fix mourning by clan relation, the Tibetan by religious rule).

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Da gong dress**: cooked-hemp cloth robe.
- **Die, belt**: of the corresponding grade (below *da gong* no twisted belt, etc.).
- **Mourning diagram**: the lines marking *da gong* in the nine-degree diagram of the lineage.
- **Ritual books**: *Yili* "Sangfu", *Comprehensive Rituals of the Great Qing*.
