# Xiao Gong (Five Months)

## 1. Definition

*Xiao gong* is the fourth of the five degrees, its period five months. Its dress is made of **finer cooked hemp**, "small work" meaning the working is finer and the material lighter.

*Xiao gong* is the degree of **same-great-grandfather kin**: great-uncles, great-cousins and the like fall mostly in the *xiao gong* range.

## 2. Scope of the Degree

**Per the *Yili* ("Sangfu"), the chief *xiao gong* five-month relations:**

1. For the great-granduncles (the grandfather's brothers and their wives, i.e. "great-uncle/ great-aunt");
2. For the great-cousins (the father's cousins and their wives);
3. For the second-cousins (same great-grandfather's brothers, i.e. "from-hall brothers");
4. For the maternal grandparents (the mother's parents);
5. For the maternal aunts (the mother's sisters, i.e. the aunt);
6. For the husband's paternal aunts and sisters (wife for the husband's aunts and sisters);
7. For brothers' daughters (unmarried);
8. For the concubine-grandson's wife;
9. For the husband's brothers (wife for the husband's brothers), etc.

**The "outer-kin degree" is most concentrated at the *xiao gong* grade**: maternal grandparents and maternal aunts are both *xiao gong*, reflecting the principle "outer kin one degree below the natal line".

## 3. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* ("Sangfu") fixes the *xiao gong* degree and scope.
- The handling of **outer kin** is the core topic of this grade: ritually "outer kin" (the mother's, wife's and daughter's lines) are uniformly one degree below the natal line, hence maternal grandparents are only *xiao gong*; after the Tang some elevation (e.g. "for maternal grandparents" made heavier in some ritual books).
- The Tang and Song followed; the Ming *Ming Code* adjusted some relations.
- The Qing *Comprehensive Rituals* details the *xiao gong* scope.
- Folk practice mostly no longer distinguishes "five months" from "three months", calling lighter mourning "work dress" and "silk-hemp dress" (缌服).
- **Contemporary**: the fine distinctions of the five degrees basically no longer exist.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: within the "white shirt" grade (light dress) roughly includes the *xiao gong* and *si ma* forms.
- **North China**: mostly marks by the coarseness of the mourning cloth and the wearing time, not by grade.
- **Jiangnan**: in lineage-developed areas (Huizhou, Jiang-Zhe) old finer mourning records survive, in genealogies and family-rite manuscripts.
- **Ethnic minorities**: the five degrees are a product of the Han lineage law; other peoples have their own kin-mourning rules and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Xiao gong dress**: fine cooked-hemp cloth robe.
- **Die, belt** (corresponding grade).
- **Mourning diagram**: the lines marking *xiao gong* in the nine-degree diagram and the outer-kin diagram.
- **Ritual books**: *Yili* "Sangfu", *Liji* "Fuwen", *Comprehensive Rituals*.
