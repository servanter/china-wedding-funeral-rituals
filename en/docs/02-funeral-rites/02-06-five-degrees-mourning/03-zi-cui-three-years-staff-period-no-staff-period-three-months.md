# Zi Cui (Three Years, Staff Period, No-Staff Period, Three Months)

## 1. Definition

*Zi cui* (zī cuī) is the degree next below *zhan cui*. "*Zi*" means the hem is **hemmed** (the edge sewn even); the material is raw hemp but finer than *zhan cui*, hence below it.

*Zi cui* divides into **four grades**, the most internally varied of the five degrees:

| Grade | Period | Typical relations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Zi cui three years** | three years | Father dead, for the mother; mother for the eldest son (old system) |
| **Zi cui staff-period** | one year (with staff) | Father living, for the mother (before the Tang); husband for the wife |
| **Zi cui no-staff-period** | one year (no staff) | For the grandparents; for uncles (father's brothers) and their wives; for all sons; for brothers; for brothers' sons; the grandson for the grandparents; for the husband's parents (the in-laws) |
| **Zi cui three months** | three months | For the great-grandparents; for the high-grandparents (varying by ritualist); for the lineage head and the lineage head's mother and wife |

**The staff** (*zhang*): in the staff-period, the staff marks that grief has reached the point of needing support in walking — a marker of the degree's weight.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* ("Sangfu") sets the four grades of *zi cui* in the greatest detail; the exegetes of each age debated "who falls in which grade", with slight differences among the ritual schools.
- **The two greatest changes**:
  1. **"Father living, for the mother"**: before the Tang, *zi cui* staff-period; under Wu Zetian raised to *zi cui* three years; after Ming Taizu's statute made it *zhan cui* three years (mother's mourning elevated).
  2. **"Husband for the wife"**: old was *zi cui* staff-period; after the Ming still *zi cui* staff-period, but folk practice often made it heavier.
- **"Wife for the in-laws"**: old was *zi cui* no-staff-period; after the Tang raised to *zhan cui* three years (same as son for parents), embodying "the in-laws as parents".
- The Qing *Comprehensive Rituals* follows the Ming.
- The "for the lineage head" item in *zi cui* three months is the direct projection of the lineage system onto the mourning code.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Assuming the dress**: by one's grade wear the *zi cui* dress (hemmed raw-hemp robe), add the *die*, belt, cap.
2. **Staff or not**: the staff-period wearer holds the staff; the no-staff-period and three-month wearers do not.
3. **Mourning residence**: lighter than *zhan cui*, but still "dwell in the outer chamber", "no wine or meat" (initially).
4. **Changing and removing**: at the first anniversary change the dress; at term remove the mourning.
5. **Restrictions**: within the period no marriage, no music; the staff-period and three-year wearers most strictly limited.
6. **Zi cui three months**: a very short period, mostly symbolic mourning, "having the dress but not heavily".

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- Folk practice simplifies the dress to "degrees of mourning dress": the dress for grandparents and uncles (white cloth, mourning cap) differs clearly from that for parents (coarse white cloth, hemp rope) — the folk projection of the *zi cui* / *zhan cui* distinction.
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: the mourning dress divides into three grades "hemp shirt" (heavy), "ramie shirt" (next), "white shirt" (light), corresponding to the three-level simplification of the five degrees.
- **North China**: the "white-cloth mourning gown" is general; only "hemp rope or not" and "mourning-cap shape" mark the weight.
- **Ethnic minorities**: each has its own mourning period and marks (e.g. mourning cloth, cutting the hair, forbidding ornaments), unrelated to the *zi cui* grades.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Zi cui dress**: raw-hemp robe, hemmed.
- **Mourning staff**: held by staff-period wearers (the "cut staff" — branches pared, unlike the *zhan cui* "strung staff").
- **Die, belt, cap**.
- **Mourning diagrams and ritual books**.
