# Mourning for Maternal and Affinal Kin

## 1. Definition

"Outer kin" (*waiqin*) are kin outside the natal nine generations, of three classes:

- **Mother's line**: maternal grandparents, maternal uncle, maternal aunt, cousins by the uncle and aunt.
- **Wife's line**: the wife's parents (parents-in-law), the wife's brothers, the wife's sisters.
- **Daughter's line**: married-out paternal aunts, sisters, daughters and their children (maternal grandson, nephew).

The basic principle of outer-kin mourning is: **uniformly one degree below the natal line, and the degrees are generally light**. This is the lineage logic of "kinship centres on the natal line".

## 2. Degree Correspondence

| Outer kin | Relation to self | Degree (*Yili* system) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maternal grandparents | mother's parents | Xiao gong five months |
| Maternal uncle | mother's brother | Si ma three months (increased after the Tang) |
| Maternal aunt | mother's sister | Xiao gong five months |
| Cousins by uncle/aunt | children of mother's brother/sister | Si ma three months |
| Wife's parents (in-laws) | wife's parents | Si ma three months |
| Wife's brothers | affinal | no dress (increased later) |
| Son-in-law | daughter's husband | Si ma three months |
| Maternal grandson | daughter's son | Si ma three months |
| Mother-in-law | wife's mother | Si ma three months |

**Note**: outer-kin mourning is the most changed and most disputed part of the five degrees across the ages. After the Tang *Kaiyuan Rites* some outer kin were increased (e.g. "for the maternal uncle" up to *xiao gong*), reflecting the gradual breaking of "lineage" by "grace".

## 3. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* ("Sangfu") establishes the principle "outer kin one degree below the natal line", hence maternal grandparents only *xiao gong*, the maternal uncle only *si ma*.
- **Tang increases**: the Tang *Kaiyuan Rites* increased some outer kin (e.g. "for the maternal uncle" up to *xiao gong*) — an important change in outer-kin mourning.
- **Ming adjustments**: the Ming *Ming Code* elevated "for maternal grandparents", "for the maternal uncle", "for the maternal aunt", generally up one degree.
- The Qing *Comprehensive Rituals* follows the Ming with slight adjustment.
- **Important background**: the trend of outer-kin mourning is **"grace grows heavier"** — as the family form changed and the reality of "mother's-line affection" pressed, the lineage colour of the ritual was gradually weakened.
- After the modern abolition of the degree system, folk mourning for "maternal grandparents, maternal uncle, maternal aunt" has drawn close to that for "uncles", the opposite direction from the ritual.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: the mourning dress for outer kin (the mother's line) is not markedly lighter than the natal line in folk; especially at the maternal grandparents' death the maternal grandson must wear "ramie shirt".
- **North China**: outer kin mostly wear a "white mourning cap" or black armband, simple in form.
- **Contemporary**: the distinction of five-degree grades basically vanishes in folk; the difference between "outer kin" and "natal line" shows mainly in custom.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples (e.g. the Naxi, the Bai) give the mother's-line kin an important place; their mourning rules differ from the Han principle of "outer kin reduced", and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Outer-kin mourning chart**: a chart alongside the "natal nine-degree chart".
- **Genealogy**: outer kin generally do not enter the natal genealogy, so the degree is handed on by rhyme or chart.
- **Ritual books**: *Yili* "Sangfu", *Tang Kaiyuan Rites*, *Ming Code*, *Comprehensive Rituals*.
