# The Five Degrees and the Lineage System

## 1. Definition

The five-degree system is not an isolated funeral rule but **a side of the lineage system**. The lineage organises the clan by "great and small lineage" and "primogeniture"; the five degrees mark this organisational structure on the individual's mourning dress and duties. The two are two faces of one body:

- **The lineage fixes the "position"**: who is the great lineage, who the lineage head (*zongzi*), who holds the right of chief sacrifice.
- **The five degrees fix the "feeling" and "duty"**: who wears what for whom, for how long, and what mourning restrictions they bear.

## 2. The Correspondence of the Five Degrees and the Lineage

### 1. The right of chief sacrifice and the mourning for the "lineage head"

The *Yili* ("Sangfu") sets "for the lineage head" *zi cui* three months — even if the lineage head is distantly related, one still mourns him, because he is "the lineage continuing the ancestors", representing the clan's continuity. This is **institutional mourning**, unrelated to feeling.

### 2. The primogenitor and the "bearing of succession"

- The father for the **eldest son** wears *zhan cui* three years (the eldest son is "the proper body above", to continue the father's lineage).
- The heir grandson "bears the succession" (*chengzhong*): if the eldest son is dead, the heir grandson wears *zhan cui* three years for the grandfather (the "succession-bearing grandson"), because he is the heir of the ancestral sacrifice.
- This rule makes **the chief mourner (the funeral host) and the successor one person**, hence the folk saying "he who smashes the basin and holds the banner inherits" (see "Smashing the Mourning Basin").

### 3. "Becoming an heir" and reduced mourning

- One without a son adopts an heir ("becoming an heir"); the heir wears *zhan cui* three years for the father he succeeds, but reduced to *zi cui* no-staff for his birth parents.
- This is the mourning expression of **ancestral-lineage succession** (宗祧继承): once "the lineage is transferred", the blood tie is ritually rearranged.

### 4. The degree and property succession, and legal concomitance

- The degree is an important reference for the **order of succession** (close kin first).
- The degree is also the basis for **legal joint liability**: the Tang scope of "dwelling together", the Ming "penalty added or reduced for kin-offences", are mostly set by the degree of mourning ("punish by the five degrees").
- The *Jinshu* ("Xingfa zhi") records the principle "punish by the five degrees", marking the entry of the degree into the legal system.

## 3. Historical Origins

- The *Liji* ("Dazhuan") systematically treats the relation of lineage and degree ("the separate son is the ancestor, the one continuing the separate is the lineage"), the programmatic text on the question.
- After the Han, the bond of degree and law tightened ("punish by the five degrees").
- The Tang, Ming and Qing codes have many articles setting penalty by the mourning degree.
- The Song *Family Rituals* rebuilds the lineage organisation by the hall system; the five degrees become the clan's daily operating rule.
- In lineage-developed areas of the Ming and Qing (Fujian-Guangdong, Huizhou), the five degrees were tied to clan property (sacrifice lands), clan rules, clan schools and relief.
- After modern times, the legal effect of the degree was abolished (the Republican *Civil Code* replaces the degree with degrees of kinship), the lineage system collapsed, and the five degrees receded to folk custom.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Fujian-Guangdong, Huizhou**: the lineage organisation is tight; the five degrees are directly tied to clan property and clan rules — the most complete region of "degree — lineage" integration.
- **North China**: the lineage is looser; the "five degrees" are more a boundary of kin terms and visiting scope.
- **Contemporary**: the "five degrees" are still used in folk as a phrase for "near or far kin", but have wholly lost legal effect (current law uses "collateral blood relatives within three generations" and the like).
- **Ethnic minorities**: the Yi "clan" system and the Tibetan clan structure have their own kin organisation and mourning rules, different from the Han lineage — five-degree logic, and must not be confused.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Genealogy (lineage register)**: the core document of the lineage, recording branches, descent and degree relations.
- **Five-degree chart**: the diagram of the lineage.
- **Clan rules, hall rules**: the clan's operating rules.
- **Sacrifice-land contracts**: the clan-property proof.
- **Codes**: the Tang, Ming and Qing codes' articles punishing by the degree.
