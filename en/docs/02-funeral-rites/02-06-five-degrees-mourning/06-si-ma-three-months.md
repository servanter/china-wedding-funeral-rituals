# Si Ma (Three Months)

## 1. Definition

*Si ma* is the lightest of the five degrees, its period three months. "*Si*" is fine hemp cloth (a loose, fine cloth woven of fine hemp thread), hence the lightest material and shortest period.

**"Out of the five degrees" means beyond the *si ma* range**: kin with no mourning dress no longer have the duty of mourning, but still keep up visits in wedding and funeral rites (袒免 *tan mian* is a special mark, see the next entry).

## 2. Scope of the Degree

**Per the *Yili* ("Sangfu"), the chief *si ma* three-month relations:**

1. For the lineage great-grandparents (same high-grandfather's great-grandparent generation);
2. For the lineage uncles (same high-grandfather's uncle generation);
3. For the lineage brothers (same high-grandfather's brothers, i.e. "lineage brothers");
4. For the great-grandson, great-great-grandson (partly listed);
5. For the maternal grandson;
6. For the son-in-law;
7. For the wife's parents (the parents-in-law);
8. For the maternal uncle (the mother's brother);
9. For the maternal-aunt's children (cousins by the aunt);
10. For the nurse (under set conditions);
11. For the husband's paternal cousins (wife for the husband's cousins), etc.

**Note**: the scope and grades listed by ritualists differ; items like "for the maternal uncle" and "for the wife's parents" are handled variously in the ritual codes of each age; the above follows the common *Yili* system.

## 3. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* ("Sangfu") fixes the *si ma* degree and scope — the widest, most distant grade in the five-degree system.
- **"Outer kin" are most numerous at this grade**: maternal uncle, cousins by the aunt, wife's parents, maternal grandson, son-in-law are all *si ma*, showing the principle of uniformly reducing outer kin.
- **"Mourning for reburial is *si ma*"**: the *Yili* ("Sangfu") sets "for reburial, *si ma*" — at a moved burial one wears *si ma* three months, the most special item in *si ma* (because "already buried, the mourning is spent", only *si ma* is re-donned to exhaust the grief).
- The Tang and Song followed; the Ming *Ming Code* and Qing *Comprehensive Rituals* both adjusted.
- Folk practice replaces *si ma* three months with "wearing mourning one month or a few days".
- **Contemporary**: the name *si ma* mostly no longer exists, surviving only in lineage family-rite texts.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: within the "white shirt (light mourning)" grade roughly includes the *si ma* range; the period simplified mostly to "some days after the procession".
- **North China**: mostly marks light dress by wearing black armband or white flower for a few days to a month.
- **Jiangnan**: lineage family-rite manuscripts keep finer *si ma* records.
- **Ethnic minorities**: the five degrees are a product of the Han lineage law; other peoples have their own kin-mourning rules and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Si ma dress**: fine-hemp cloth robe.
- **Mourning diagram**: the lines marking *si ma* in the nine-degree diagram and the outer-kin diagram.
- **Ritual books**: *Yili* "Sangfu", *Liji* "Fuwen", *Comprehensive Rituals*.
- **Genealogy**: the basis for matching degree and kin.
