# The Nine-Generation Mourning-Degree Chart

![Nine-generation mourning-degree chart](../../../../assets/diagrams/本宗九族五服图.svg)

*Figure 8. The natal nine-generation mourning-degree chart (degree grades from the high ancestor to the great-great-grandson).*

## 1. Definition

The "natal nine-generation mourning-degree chart" maps the natal nine generations (nine generations from the high ancestor to the great-great-grandson) onto the five degrees, a basic tool of the traditional lineage society for understanding kinship.

**The "nine generations"** (from the high ancestor to the great-great-grandson, nine vertical generations):

```
High ancestor — Great ancestor — Grandfather — Father — Self — Son — Grandson — Great-grandson — Great-great-grandson
```

**The "five degrees"**: centred on "self", extending upward, downward and sideways, the degree decreasing step by step.

## 2. Degree Correspondence (centred on "self")

| Kin | Relation to self | Degree |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Father | up 1 | Zhan cui three years |
| Mother | up 1 | Zhan cui three years (after the Ming; before the Tang, father living, zi cui staff-period) |
| Grandparents | up 2 | Zi cui no-staff period |
| Great-grandparents | up 3 | Zi cui three months |
| High-grandparents | up 4 | Zi cui three months (varying by ritualist) |
| Son | down 1 | Zi cui no-staff period (eldest son zhan cui three years) |
| All grandsons (concubine-grandsons) | down 2 | Da gong nine months |
| Heir grandson | down 2 | Zi cui no-staff period (the bearer zhan cui) |
| Great-grandson | down 3 | Si ma three months |
| Great-great-grandson | down 4 | Si ma three months |
| Brothers | side 1 | Zi cui no-staff period |
| Brother's son (nephew) | side 2 | Zi cui no-staff period |
| Paternal cousins (堂兄弟) | side 3 | Da gong nine months |
| Great-cousins | side 4 | Xiao gong five months |
| Lineage brothers | side 5 | Si ma three months |
| Uncles and aunts | side 1 | Zi cui no-staff period |
| Great-cousins' parents | side 2 | Xiao gong five months |
| Lineage uncles | side 3 | Si ma three months |

(The above follows the common *Yili* system, with slight differences among ritualists; the full natal chart is in the *Yili* "Sangfu" and the ritual codes of each age.)

## 3. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* ("Sangfu") itself explains the degree by recursive kin-rank; though it drew no chart, its structure can already be charted.
- The Song *Family Rituals* simplifies the degree to the "four generations" (below the high ancestor) framework and appends a "natal five-degree chart" — the direct source of the later common "five-degree chart" (owing to the *Family Rituals*' influence, folk use the five-generation chart of "high, great, grandfather, father, self").
- Ming and Qing ritual books such as the *Comprehensive Rituals* detail the mourning charts.
- Folk printed the "five-degree chart" at the back of books or in genealogies, for clan members to consult at funerals.
- **Practical impact**: the five-degree chart is not only ritual content but the **actual basis for defining "close kin", "same lineage" and "order of succession"**.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Fujian-Guangdong, Huizhou**: genealogies often append a "five-degree chart"; the lineage handles funerals and fixes near/far by the chart.
- **North China**: mostly replaces the chart with the oral scope of "the family".
- **Ethnic minorities**: the five-degree chart is a product of the Han lineage law; peoples' kin structures differ (e.g. the Yi clan, the Tibetan clan), and the chart does not apply.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Five-degree chart (natal nine-generation chart, outer-kin chart)**: printed or hand-drawn.
- **Genealogy**: appends the five-degree chart, the practical tool for looking up kinship.
- **Ritual books**: *Yili* "Sangfu", *Family Rituals*, *Comprehensive Rituals*.
