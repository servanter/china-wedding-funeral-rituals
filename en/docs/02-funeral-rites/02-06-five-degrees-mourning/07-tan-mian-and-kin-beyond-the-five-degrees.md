# Tan Mian and Kin beyond the Five Degrees

## 1. Definition

- **Kin with no dress** (*wufu zhi qin*): kin beyond the *si ma* range, with no longer a duty of mourning — what is popularly called "out of the five degrees".
- **Tan mian**: "*tan*" is baring the left arm (removing the outer robe, baring the left arm); "*mian*" (wèn) is binding the hair with cloth (no cap, but a cloth band). *Tan mian* is the **marking dress of kin with no dress when attending the funeral** — wearing no mourning dress, but baring the arm and binding the hair to show "though with no dress, still kin".

The *Liji* ("Dazhuan"): "at four generations, *si ma* — the end of the dress; at five generations, *tan mian* — the diminishing of the same surname; at six generations, kinship is exhausted." This clearly outlines the three grades of thinning kinship: *si ma* (fourth generation) → *tan mian* (fifth generation) → kinship ends (sixth generation).

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Liji* ("Dazhuan") establishes the "decreasing mourning" framework, the ritual basis for understanding kin with no dress.
- The *Yili* ("Sangfu") sets the condolence dress for "friends" as *si ma*, to be read alongside *tan mian*: the ritual gives a mark both to "kin with no dress" and to "no kin but with duty".
- The exact *tan mian* dress is clearly fixed in the ritual codes of each age ("in *tan*, remove the ornaments; in *mian*, bind the hair with cloth").
- Ming and Qing folk: kin "out of the five degrees" still attend funerals and weddings, but need not wear mourning dress, mostly marking with a "white cloth strip" or "white paper flower".
- After the lineage system weakened, the boundary of the "five degrees" was replaced in practice by the notions of "same lineage" and "same clan": "out of the five degrees is still same clan; within the five degrees is close kin".

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Confirm the degree**: by the genealogy confirm one's degree to the dead, judging whether within the five degrees.
2. **Kin with no dress**: wear no mourning dress, but duties at the funeral include:
   - attending the condolence (need not kneel at the vigil);
   - attending the procession (need not hold the rope);
   - attending the post-burial family sacrifice.
3. **The tan mian rite (old system)**: kin of the fifth generation, attending the funeral, bare the left arm and bind the hair, showing "kin but no dress".
4. **Folk form**: folk use "white cloth strip at the waist", "white paper flower at the breast", "plain clothes" as the substitute mark of light dress.
5. **Duty of visiting**: kin with no dress must still visit at weddings, funerals and festivals — the real boundary of the "lineage community".
6. **Entry in the genealogy**: kin with no dress are still within the genealogy — clansmen, not "relatives".

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Fujian-Guangdong, Huizhou**: the lineage system is tight; the "out of the five degrees" boundary is clear (the genealogy records the branches), and the visiting duty of kin with no dress is explicit.
- **North China**: the lineage is looser; the "five degrees" are mostly judged by the oral scope of "the family".
- **Jiangnan**: the lineage joins the charitable-estate system; the mourning boundary often corresponds to the distribution of clan property.
- **Contemporary**: the "five degrees" are still used in folk as a saying for "near or far kin" (e.g. "out of the five degrees, not close kin"), with no direct correspondence to the law's "collateral blood relatives within three generations".
- **Ethnic minorities**: the five degrees are a product of the Han lineage law; each people has its own kin boundary (e.g. the Yi's clan, the Tibetan's clan), not to be confused.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Genealogy**: the sole basis for confirming the degree.
- **Tan mian dress (old system)**: cloth strip, hair-binding cloth.
- **White cloth strip, white paper flower, black armband** (folk light-dress marks).
- **Mourning diagram**: marking the five-degree boundary.
- **Ritual books**: *Liji* "Dazhuan", *Yili* "Sangfu", *Comprehensive Rituals*.
