# Laying Out the Coffin

## 1. Definition

*Zhuang lian* is the whole set of operations after the body enters the coffin: the padding, bedding, filling and placing of grave-goods inside. The care lies in two points: **posture** (the body steady and upright) and **order** (what goes where).

Its substance is "fixing the form with things": padding and filling steady the body, and grave-goods settle what the dead "need after death".

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* ("Shisangli") records the "filling" (*shi*): after entering, fill the gaps with clothes, shells, rice, so the body does not shift.
- The *Liji* ("Sangdaji"): the coffin lining, with detailed rules inside and out.
- Tombs show rich interior furnishing: Mawangdui's multilayer silk wrapping, Mancheng's jade suit, the "nine-orifice plugs" of later tombs.
- The Han jade suit (gold/silver/bronze thread) was the highest-grade laying-out.
- After Tang-Song, religious grave-goods grew: scripture-paper, bronze mirrors (against demons), grain-bags, dog-beating cakes.
- Ming-Qing folk fixed the set: **gold underneath, silver on top; a pillow under the head; ingots in the hands; a coin in the mouth; lotus under the feet**.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Bottom layer**: ash, charcoal (damp), rush, silk or "gold cloth" (yellow silk).
2. **Pillow**: under the head (a "cock-crow pillow", "ingot pillow"), raising it slightly.
3. **Place the body**: lift in, upright, face up, hands at sides (or folded on the belly).
4. **Fill**: clothes, paper, silk floss around the body and gaps.
5. **Cover**: a "silver cloth" (white silk) or shroud over the body.
6. **Opening-the-eyes items**: some place ingots, coins in the hands, "lotus" at the feet.
7. **Grave-goods**: by custom — grain-bag (not hungry), dog-beating stick/cake (against dogs), bronze mirror (against demons), beloved things (books, pen, pipe, comb, jewelry), mingqi (figurines, pottery, paper) set aside, not all in the coffin.
8. **Check**: an elder verifies the count.
9. **Close**: lid paused or set at once (see "Closing and Nailing the Coffin").

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: "gold underneath, silver on top", ingots in hands, lotus under feet; grain-bag, dog-cake inside.
- **Jiangnan**: "ingot pillow, lotus under feet"; mirror, scissors (against evil) inside.
- **Min-Guang**: gold/silver jewelry and paper mingqi, in pairs.
- **Hakka (secondary burial)**: first burial simpler; at bone-picking years later, a different set in the urn.
- **Ethnic minorities**: grave-goods reflect belief — Yi tools and silver, Miao silver and brocade, Tibetan sky burial none, Hui/Uyghur **none** (law holds grave-goods useless, only good deeds avail). The difference is doctrinal, not "frugality".

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Gold-under, silver-on**: yellow/white silk.
- **Cock-crow/ingot pillow**: head rest.
- **Grain-bag, dog-stick, dog-cake**: folk items.
- **Mirror, scissors, jade plugs**: against evil and to "stop the orifices".
- **Mingqi, figurines, paper**: grave-goods (see the objects section).
- **Grave-goods list**: some families keep it in the account book.
