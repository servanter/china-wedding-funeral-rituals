# Opening the Eyes (Kaiguang)

## 1. Definition

*Kaiguang* is the rite, at encoffining, of "opening" the dead person's senses: with a cotton swab (or brush, willow twig) dipped in clear water (or wine, tea), touch in turn the eyes, ears, nose and mouth (some add hands and feet), with an auspicious phrase at each touch.

The idea: at death the senses "close"; this rite lets the dead "see, hear, eat and walk" in the underworld. Commonly "opening the eyes", "opening the face".

## 2. Historical Origins

- *Kaiguang* is not in the ritual classics; it is a folk rite. Its "touching with a thing" resembles Buddhist eye-dotting, but **the funeral *kaiguang* "opens the dead's perception", different in object and meaning from the Buddhist "infuses spirit into an image" — the two should not be confused**.
- Ming-Qing gazetteers note *kaiguang* much, joined with encoffining, sometimes by a Daoist or monk.
- The formulae are its core: rhymed quatraons or octads — "open the eyes, see the four quarters", "open the ears, hear the eight directions", "open the nose, smell the flowers", "open the mouth, eat the offering".
- Some areas add "wash hands", "wash feet": "open the hands, take the money-grain", "open the feet, walk the four quarters".
- Still seen in earth-burial and restored rites.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Prepare**: a cotton swab (or brush, willow twig), a bowl of clear water (or wine, tea).
2. **Choose the doer**: the chief mourner (eldest son), a "full-fortune" person, or the presiding Daoist/monk.
3. **Touch in order**: eyes ("open the eyes, see the four quarters"), ears ("open the ears, hear the eight directions"), nose ("open the nose, smell the flowers"), mouth ("open the mouth, eat the offering"), hands ("open the hands, take the money-grain"), feet ("open the feet, walk the four quarters").
4. **Close the blessing**: "So-and-so, the opening is done, fare well on the road".
5. **Encoffin**: *kaiguang* is mostly before or during major dressing, then into the coffin.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: by the eldest son with a water swab, the formulae rhymed in sets.
- **Jiang-Zhe**: by a Daoist after chanting.
- **Min-Tai**: by the "master" (Daoist) or earth officer, formulae in Southern-Min rhyme.
- **Guangdong**: with wine or tea, formulae in Cantonese.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some have similar "opening the senses" but joined with religion — Tibetans by the lama chant and "guidance" (the *Bardo Thodol*, "liberation by hearing in the intermediate state"), teaching the dead to recognise the light — a different level from the Han "opening the five senses"; not to be conflated.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Cotton swab, brush, willow twig**: the touchers.
- **Clear water, wine, tea**: the dipped substance.
- **Formulae (kaiguang words)**: oral rhymes, some areas with written copies.
- **Incense, candles, offerings**: the setting.
