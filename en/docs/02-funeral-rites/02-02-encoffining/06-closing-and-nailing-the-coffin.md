# Closing and Nailing the Coffin

## 1. Definition

*Gai guan* is placing the lid; *ding guan* ("sealing the coffin", "fastening") is fixing lid to body with wooden wedges or iron nails.

Nailing is, in folk belief, the instant life and death are wholly parted: after it one can never see the loved face again, so the kin's farewell is fiercest. The saying "the lid sealed, the verdict fixed" (*gai guan lun ding*) comes from this — once nailed, a life's merits are settled.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* ("Shisangli") records "then cover" — the rite of placing the lid; the classic does not highlight "nailing".
- Han coffins used mortise-tenon and "waist" wedges; archaeology shows tenon-joining mainly, nails a later convenience.
- After Tang-Song, "nailing" appears, with "descendant nails" — the nailing nail left for the eldest (or sons) each to strike once, meaning "descendants flourish", "nail down the estate".
- Ming-Qing gazetteers note: "none of clashing zodiac may enter at nailing"; "the eldest son nails first, the others in turn".
- Some areas "seal with lacquer" after nailing — raw lacquer for damp-proofing, meaning "eternal firmness".
- Under cremation today, nailing survives only in earth-burial areas and restored rites.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Last farewell**: before nailing, kin view the face in turn and bid farewell.
2. **Clear the space**: zodiac-clashing, pregnant, new mothers withdraw (folk).
3. **Test**: lift the lid ajar, check posture and goods.
4. **Close**: officers together lift and seat the lid on the tenon (or holes).
5. **Drive descendant nails**: eldest son first, then each son in turn — "descendant nails"; some leave the last undriven ("leave a thread of life").
6. **Seal with lacquer**: raw lacquer and tung-oil ash on seams and holes.
7. **Cover with canopy**: the embroidered canopy hides seams and holes.
8. **Set the seat and wail**: after nailing, an offering; kin weep; enter the lying-in-state stage.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: "nailing", "fastening"; the descendant-nail custom is common.
- **Jiang-Zhe**: "sealing the coffin", varying on keeping a nail or not.
- **Min-Tai**: "sealing", mostly the day before or at dawn of the procession, by the earth officer.
- Some areas cry "So-and-so, dodge the nail!" to warn the soul — a vivid detail.
- **Ethnic minorities**: most have no nailing. Muslims do not nail (no coffin); Tibetans none (sky burial); Miao, Yi fix lids by their own custom, not necessarily nails. **Nailing is a specific Han coffin-burial rite, not to be extended to others.**

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Lid, coffin-nails (descendant nails), wedges**: for closing.
- **Raw lacquer, tung oil, ash**: for sealing.
- **Hammer, chisel**: tools.
- **Coffin canopy**: the embroidered cover.
- **Offering mat, incense, candles**: for the offering.
