# Major Dressing (Dalian)

![大殓（入殓）/ The major encoffining](../../../../assets/images/18-encoffining.png)


## 1. Definition

*Dalian* (major dressing), also "entering the coffin", "entering the wood", "encoffining", is the rite, on the day after minor dressing (for the gentleman), of lifting the corpse into the coffin and closing it into the bier.

Major dressing is the first great divide of the funeral: **after entering the coffin, the dead and living are forever parted; the kin's weeping is no longer before a "person" but before a "coffin"**. Hence the wailing and leaping are fiercest — the *Yili* says "the host wails and leaps without count".

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* ("Shisangli"): "at dawn, lay the clothes in the chamber… then the major dressing" — the day after minor dressing.
- The number of dresses by rank: gentleman 30, great officer 50, lord 100 (slightly varying by edition).
- The *Liji* ("Sangdaji"): at major dressing "lift the corpse to the coffin", "the coffin enters by the west steps", with rules for the "bier" (*bin*) position.
- After major dressing comes *bin* — resting the coffin awaiting burial; its term by rank: Son of Heaven seven months, lords five, great officers three, gentleman over a month (*Liji*, "Royal Regulations").
- Zhu Xi's *Family Rituals* compresses major dressing to the third day after death and joins *bin* with the "mourning hall", the direct source of the later "lying in state".
- Folk "encoffining" is major dressing, mostly fixed on the third day ("encoffining on the third day"), still current.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Lay out clothes**: the major-dressing clothes by rank in the chamber.
2. **Prepare the coffin**: line it (ash, charcoal, rush, silk cloth), place grave-goods.
3. **Lift the corpse**: the chief mourner, kin and officers lift the body from the bed into the coffin, head north (or by orientation).
4. **Add clothes and fill**: lay the major-dressing clothes over and pack around the body ("fill" the gaps) to steady it.
5. **Add ornaments**: cover with the inner quilt, add inner decorations.
6. **Wail and leap**: as the body enters, kin wail and leap ("without count").
7. **Close the lid**: not yet nailed, awaiting the final farewell (some keep it open until the procession).
8. **Set the offering**: the major-dressing offering.
9. **Take the dress**: after major dressing, kin formally take mourning dress by grade (see the mourning-attire entries).

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: "encoffining", "entering the wood", mostly the third day; those of clashing zodiac must avoid.
- **Jiangnan**: "encoffining", "receiving the corpse", stressing the "hour", with crackers and wailing.
- **Min-Tai**: "encoffining", "entering the wood", stressing "hour" and "orientation", by the "earth officer".
- Some areas forbid weeping at encoffining ("weeping disturbs the corpse") or demand loud weeping — two opposite beliefs, showing how local such taboos are.
- **Ethnic minorities**: most have no coffin system or differ. The Miao, Yi with coffins have their own hour and rite; Tibetan sky burial offers the body to vultures, no encoffining; Hui, Uyghur wrap in white and go straight to earth. **"Encoffining" is a product of the Han coffin-burial system, not a universal funeral stage.**

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Coffin**: the core vessel (see "Coffins and Outer Coffins").
- **Major-dressing clothes**: by rank.
- **Coffin lining**: ash, charcoal, rush, silk — for damp-proofing and preservation.
- **Grave-goods**: personal items, mingqi.
- **Offering mat, food and wine**; **mourning dress**.
