# Minor Dressing (Xiaolian)

## 1. Definition

*Xiaolian* (minor dressing) is the rite, on the day after death (for the gentleman, "the morrow of death"), of adding clothes to the dead, wrapping the corpse in a coverlet, setting an offering and wailing with stamping (*yong*).

With *dalian* (major dressing) it forms two grades: **minor dressing adds clothes upon the bed (the corpse still rests on the bed); major dressing lifts the body into the coffin**. Minor dressing is the preparation before the coffin; major dressing is the formal parting of life and death.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* ("Shisangli"): "at dawn, lay the clothes in the chamber, collars south, westernmost first" — the next morning's layout of minor-dressing clothes.
- The number of dresses by rank: the gentleman 19 sets, the great officer 50, the lord 100 ("set" = a suit).
- The *Liji* ("Sangdaji") fixes the time, place and orientation of minor dressing, with "minor dressing within the door" (done indoors).
- After minor dressing one "sets the offering" and "the host wails and leaps" — the stamp (*yong*, jumping) is the most violent expression of grief in the rites, paired with weeping.
- The Song *Shuyi* and *Family Rituals* simplified minor and major dressing but kept their order and meaning.
- Folk custom keeps the substance to this day: dressing the dead, changing to the shroud, kin wailing.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Choose the time**: the morrow of death (or by local habit), at dawn.
2. **Lay out clothes**: in the chamber, by rank in sets (gentleman 19), collars south, west to east.
3. **Move the corpse**: to the hall (or coffin-bed), ready to be dressed.
4. **Add clothes**: layer by layer; at each addition the kin wail and leap ("add a dress, wail and leap").
5. **Use the coverlet**: wrap the corpse in the quilt (*qin*), tie with bands (*jiao*) to hold the posture.
6. **Set the offering**: after minor dressing, set food and wine before the spirit.
7. **Wail and leap**: the host wails and leaps; the hostess wails within the curtain.
8. **Await major dressing**: choose the time for the coffin (major dressing).

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North**: "minor dressing", changing clothes, wrapping in white cloth, kin "wailing at the spirit".
- **Jiangnan**: "minor dressing", "receiving the corpse", stressing "by the eldest son's hand", "adding in order".
- **Min-Tai**: "minor dressing", linked with "moving the bed", done within 12–24 hours of death.
- Some areas cover the face with "face paper" or a shroud after minor dressing, not to be seen again.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples have no minor/major division. Muslim peoples, after washing, wrap at once in white cloth and hold the funeral, with no lying in state; Tibetans with sky/water burial dispose of the body wholly differently. **The division itself is a product of the Han earth-burial system**, not to be imposed on other modes.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Minor-dressing clothes**: by rank in sets (gentleman 19).
- **Coverlet (*qin*)**: for wrapping the corpse.
- **Tie-bands (*jiao*)**: for binding the body.
- **Bed, coffin-bed**: for resting the corpse.
- **Offering mat, food and wine**: for the offering.
