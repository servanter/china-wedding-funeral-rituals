# Coffins and Outer Coffins: Form and Materials

## 1. Definition

- **Coffin (*guan*)**: the vessel holding the corpse directly, also "jiu" (a coffin with the corpse in it).
- **Outer coffin (*guo*)**: a larger case around the coffin, like an outer box, protecting the wood and marking rank. Sons of Heaven and lords had it; gentlemen and commoners mostly not.

The form, layers, timber and lacquer of coffin and outer coffin are the most rank-conscious part of the burial system: "the thickness of coffin and outer coffin differs by noble rank".

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Liji* ("Tan'gong"): the Son of Heaven's coffin fourfold, lords threefold, great officers twofold, gentlemen onefold; with sizes for the system.
- The *Liji* ("Sangdaji"): timbers — the lord "pine", the great officer "cypress", the gentleman "mixed wood"; "cypress outer coffin" is the lords' rule.
- The *Yili* ("Shisangli") details the gentleman's coffin ("enters by the west steps").
- Archaeology: Shang-Zhou wooden outer-coffin tombs (the great wooden outer coffin of Marquis Yi), the Han "yellow-heart cypress" (*huangchang ticou*), later brick and stone-chambered tombs — all material evidence.
- Ming-Qing folk coffins of fir, cypress, nanmu, tung, with thickness specs "three-four-five" (three-inch bottom, four-inch sides, five-inch lid), called "four-five-six".
- Mid-20th-century funeral reform: earth-burial and coffins gradually replaced by cremation; coffins unused in most cities.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Choose timber**: fir most common (light, rot-resistant, workable), cypress best (dense), nanmu precious, tung/pine next; avoid willow/poplar (some areas), avoid worm-eaten or lightning-struck wood.
2. **Make the coffin**: by the coffin joiner, sawn, joined with mortise-tenon and wedges (the "descendant nail" is for nailing, different in meaning).
3. **Lacquer**: lacquer outside (black, red), with layers and gloss. Red for the aged, plain (white) for the young or special cases.
4. **Line**: ash, charcoal, rush, silk, cotton inside for damp-proofing.
5. **Encoffin**: lift the body in, add clothes, fill (see "Laying Out the Coffin").
6. **Close and nail**: add the lid, drive the "descendant nails" (see "Closing and Nailing the Coffin").
7. **Add the canopy**: at the procession, add the embroidered coffin-canopy (see "Coffin Canopy and Bearing").

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: cypress/fir, the "four-five-six" thickness; red coffins for the aged.
- **Jiangnan**: "twelve-piece", "eighteen-piece" (fewer pieces prized); a single plank best.
- **Min-Guang**: "dark wood" (ebony, nanmu), thick and heavily lacquered; "secondary-burial" areas use a "gold-bucket urn" not a coffin.
- **Hakka**: "bone-picking burial" — first burial in a coffin, years later bones to an urn.
- **Ethnic minorities**: great difference. The Miao, Yi, Dong have their own coffins (boat-coffin, log-coffin); Tibetans none (sky burial); Hui, Uyghur wrap in white, no coffin; the Yao use rock-coffins. **The coffin is not a universal vessel; its form binds to the people's mode of disposal.**

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Coffin (*guan/jiu*), outer coffin (*guo*)**: the core vessels.
- **Coffin ornaments**: coffin cloth, canopy, drape.
- **Descendant nails, wedges**: for closing.
- **Ash, charcoal, rush**: the preservative lining.
- **Lacquer, tung oil**: for finishing.
- **Coffin list**: some areas list the fittings.
