# Timing and Taboos for Encoffining

## 1. Definition

Choosing the time for encoffining is among the most valued date-selection steps in funeral custom. It covers the **day** (which day), the **hour** (which moment), the **orientation** (the coffin's direction) and the **persons to avoid** (which zodiac signs, which statuses).

The idea: encoffining is the instant the living and dead are "divided"; if the hour, orientation or persons are wrong, it may "clash with evil" (*chong sha*) and harm the living.

## 2. Historical Origins

- Ritual fixes a time for major dressing: the *Yili* sets it the day after minor dressing; the *Liji* ("Royal Regulations") fixes the *bin* term by rank.
- From Tang-Song, date-selection entered funerals. The *Xiejibianfang shu* (Qianlong-era imperial compilation) systematically organised auspicious-selection rules including funeral dating.
- Ming-Qing gazetteers record much: "must take an auspicious hour, avoid clash with the dead's zodiac"; "avoid pregnant women, new mothers, widows at encoffining".
- Folk speak of "double mourning" (*zhong sang*): certain days for encoffining/burial are held to bring "double mourning" (another death at home), neutralised by special means (inviting an extra Daoist at the seventh rites, placing a rooster on the coffin, pressing with a talisman).
- Note: these taboos are folk belief without scientific basis; recorded here as custom, not endorsed.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Invite a selector**: a geomancer or almanac fixes the day and hour.
2. **Fix orientation**: the coffin's resting direction and head-orientation (by hill, house, birth-data).
3. **List the avoiders**: those of clashing zodiac and statuses to avoid (pregnant/new mothers/menstruating women/clashing signs), posted at the door.
4. **Clear the space**: at start, clear by the list; avoiders leave.
5. **Await the hour**: at the fixed hour, fire crackers as signal, begin.
6. **Perform**: lift into coffin, add clothes, fill, close.
7. **Scatter charms**: some place a rooster, mirror, ruler, talisman on the coffin, or scatter the five grains.
8. **Resolve**: if a clash is thought to occur, a Daoist or monk performs a "resolving evil" rite.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North**: "encoffining before noon", avoid afternoon; avoid clashing zodiac present.
- **Jiang-Zhe**: avoid the "three evils" at the hour; rush and charcoal in the coffin.
- **Min-Tai**: "watch the hour" by earth officer and Daoist; a "zodiac-avoidance plaque".
- **Guangdong**: "not see daylight" (indoors), avoid the "double-mourning day".
- **Ethnic minorities**: the moment is mostly set by religious specialists — the Yi by the *bimo*'s divination, Tibetans by the lama per the Tibetan calendar, Muslims by swift burial (no timing). **The basis differs: Han from stem-branch destiny, these from religious calendar or law.**

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Almanac, date-book, compass**: for timing and orientation.
- **Zodiac-avoidance plaque / list**: posted at the door.
- **Rooster, mirror, ruler, talisman**: charms (folk).
- **Five grains, cinnabar**: scattered charms.
- **Firecrackers**: signal the hour.
