# Dressing the Dead (Xishi) and Changing Garments

## 1. Definition

*Xi* is the rite of clothing the dead. After washing and filling the mouth, the dead are dressed in new garments (*xi yi*, three sets for a gentleman), then successively add the *mao* (cloth shroud-cover), the *mi mu* (face cover), the *yan* (head wrap), the *fu jin* (hair cloth), the *wo shou* (hand binders) and the *kuang* (ear-stoppers), and finally bind the feet with *zu qi*, so the form is settled and the face unstartling.

*"Changing garments"* here has two senses: changing the **dead** into new clothes, and the **living** changing into plain mourning dress, removing ornaments, entering the mourning state. Both happen in the initial-mourning stage, hence "dressing the dead and changing garments".

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* ("Shisangli"): "then dress, three sets" — Zheng Xuan notes "three sets" as three suits, put on after moving the corpse to the bed.
- The *Yili* also records the *mao* ("cover the corpse and tie"), the *mi mu* ("face cover, of black, one foot two inches square, red lining, with ribbon ties"), the *wo shou* ("hand binders, of dark-red and dark, skin-next, hooked to the middle finger, tied at the wrist").
- The *Liji* ("Sangdaji") records rank in dressing: the lord's *mao* of brocade with axe-pattern, the great officer's of plain dark with axe-pattern, the gentleman's of black with red-pattern, with differing numbers of side-ties.
- After the Han, *xi yi* became "grave-clothes"; the *Ming Hui Dian* fixed commoners' grave-clothes at five pieces; Qing folk spoke of "five tops three waists", "seven tops five waists", "nine tops", favouring odd numbers.
- *Mi mu* and *wo shou* became the folk "face paper" and "handkerchief/gloves".

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Lay out clothes**: arrange the grave-clothes in order in the chamber.
2. **Dress**: put on the three suits (gentleman's rite) inner to outer, belt and tie.
3. **Add the *mao***: first the *zhi* (upper cover), then the *sha* (lower cover), tied at the waist.
4. **Set the face cover**: black square cloth (one foot two inches) over the face, red lining, ties at the back of the head.
5. **Add *yan* and *fu jin***: wrap the head with *yan*, bind the hair with *fu jin*.
6. **Stop the ears**: plug the ears with *kuang*, "that they hear no unseemly sound".
7. **Set the hand binders**: wrap both hands in dark-red/dark cloth, hook the tie to the middle finger, tie at the wrist.
8. **Bind the feet (*zu qi*)**: tie the feet so the body is straight.
9. **Living change garments**: kin simultaneously change to plain dress, remove ornaments; men "bind the hair", women "remove the pin".

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Number of pieces**: folk grave-clothes favour odd numbers (five, seven, nine), forbidding even.
- **Material**: cotton or silk, forbidding fur (lest one fall into the animal path) and satin (a pun on "cut off descendants").
- **Style**: no buttons, only ties (ties = "bring sons").
- **Colour**: deep blue, brown, date-red; forbidding pure black (some areas) and pure white (some areas).
- **North**: "five tops three waists"; **Jiangnan**: "nine tops"; **Min-Tai**: grave-clothes provided by the daughter, "seven above, five below".
- **Ethnic minorities**: Muslim peoples (Hui, Uyghur, Kazakh) wrap in white cloth (*kafan*), no sewn garments; Tibetans differ by mode of disposal (sky burial, water burial); Miao, Yi use their festive dress with silver — see ethnic entries.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Grave-clothes (*xi yi*)**: three to thirteen sets, by rank and means.
- ***Mao***: *zhi* (upper) and *sha* (lower), sewn cloth with ties.
- **Face cover (*mi mu*), head wrap (*yan*), hair cloth (*fu jin*)**.
- **Hand binders (*wo shou*)**: dark-red/dark cloth.
- **Ear-floss (*kuang*)**.
- **Foot tie (*zu qi*)**.
- **Face paper, white cloth, mourning kerchief**: folk substitutes.
