# Hastening to the Funeral (Bensang)

## 1. Definition

*Bensang* is hurrying back from afar on hearing of a kinsman's death. *Ben* takes the sense of speed — hearing the death, one sets out at once, without delay.

The *Liji* ("Bensang") is a chapter devoted to the rite, prescribing diet and lodging on the road, the wailing position on entry, the wailing manner and the order of taking mourning dress. Its core concern: **distance in space cannot be a reason to be absent from the funeral**.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Liji* ("Bensang"): "on first hearing of a parent's death, answer the messenger with wailing, to the full of grief; ask the cause, then wail again to the full" — the first reaction is wailing, not asking details.
- Same chapter: "one who hurries to the funeral, from the capping-mourning grade down, enters and goes left, wails facing north in the middle court, to the full of grief" — fixing the entry wail-position by grade.
- The *Liji* ("Tan'gong") records on the road "travel at star-rise, rest at star-rise", and "a hundred li a day" — urgency.
- On the road diet is limited (no wine or meat, no feasting or talk).
- Law also bound it: the *Tang Code* punished officials who on hearing of a parent's death did not hurry or mourn; the Ming added the crime of "concealing the mourning".
- With modern transport and communication, the details mostly vanished; "bensang" remains only "hurry back to bid farewell", but "return on hearing", "no feasting on the road" still appear.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Hear the death**: on the obituary, answer the messenger with wailing, then ask the cause and schedule.
2. **Set out at once**: choose no day, delay not; on the road no wine or meat, no entertainment.
3. **Travel by night**: "travel at star-rise, rest at star-rise", to arrive before dressing or burial.
4. **Wail at the door**: on seeing the house, wail; enter to the middle court, face north and wail fully.
5. **Take place and dress**: by grade receive the mourning dress, at the spirit "bind the hair", remove ornaments, join the chief mourner in wailing.
6. **Make up rites**: if one missed the minor or major dressing, the code prescribes a "supplementary offering" before the spirit.
7. **Join the rest**: with the chief mourner take part in morning/evening offerings, the procession and burial.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- Today *bensang* is mainly "hurrying back"; the details show in transport and funeral timing (whether to wait for distant kin before dressing/burial).
- Some areas: if kin truly cannot come, wait or delay the dressing, called "waiting for people", "waiting for all".
- **Ethnic difference**: Muslim peoples practise "swift burial" (same or next day), leaving almost no window for *bensang* and no "waiting for all"; some Tibetan areas have sky/water burial with religious timing.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Obituary / condolence notice**: the source of the news.
- **Mourning dress, filial kerchief**: worn by the hurrying one.
- **Luggage, offerings**: carried on the way.
- **Legal texts** (historical): the *Tang Code*, *Ming Code* on concealing or not hurrying to the mourning.
