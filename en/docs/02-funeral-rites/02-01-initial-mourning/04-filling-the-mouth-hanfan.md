# Filling the Mouth (Hanfan)

## 1. Definition

*Hanfan* places grain (*fan*) and pearls, jade or shells (*han*) into the dead person's mouth. *Fan* is rice put in the mouth; *han* is shells, jade or pearls. The ritual meaning is "unwilling to let the mouth be empty" — not letting the dead depart hungry — with also a primitive sense of "filling the mouth to suppress".

*Hanfan* is among the most rank-conscious of the initial-mourning rites: the Son of Heaven, lords, great officers, gentlemen and commoners each had prescribed contents.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Liji* ("Zaji xia") says "the Son of Heaven fills with nine shells, the lords with seven, the great officers with five, the gentleman with three" — fixing the rank of *fan*.
- The *Zhouli* ("Tianguan, Da Zai") says "at a great death, assist with the gift-jade and the mouth-jade" — mouth-jade for the Son of Heaven, by the Da Zai.
- The *Yili* ("Shisangli") records the gentleman's *hanfan*: "three shells, placed in the pin… one measure of rice, placed in the basket."
- Han emperors had pearls and jade; Han tombs often yield "jade fillers" (jade cicadas), taking the meaning of "cicada slough, rebirth".
- After Tang-Song the rite continued, but folk used "fill with coin, jade, pearl", alongside the classic shells and rice.
- Ming-Qing folk had "mouth-coin" (also "held-coin"), of copper or gold-silver, with the rule "male gold, female silver" or local custom.
- Today some areas still fill the mouth with coin, but most have replaced it with a symbolic placing.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Wedge the teeth**: with a horn spoon (*jiao si*) pry the teeth slightly apart to keep the mouth open.
2. **Bind the feet**: press the feet with a low table (*yan ji*) to keep the body straight.
3. **Fill (*fan*)**: put rice (or cooked grain) into the left and right of the mouth.
4. **Contain (*han*)**: place shells (three for a gentleman), jade, pearls or coin in the mouth.
5. **Fix**: with the spoon or cloth secure the contents against falling out.
6. **Remove implements**: take away the horn spoon and the foot-table.
7. **Proceed to dressing**: lay out the grave-clothes and dress the corpse.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: "mouth-coin", often copper coin or small gold-silver ingot, tied with a red thread (left outside the mouth, drawn out at the funeral, lest "the line of descendants be cut").
- **Jiangnan**: often jade or pearl, "fill with jade to suppress".
- **Min-Tai**: jade, silver, tea-leaf as filler; "fill with jade" is also called "fill with pearl".
- **Guangdong**: gold, silver, jade, in pairs.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some do not fill the mouth, or substitute rice, tea, *tsampa*; some Tibetan areas fill with butter and *tsampa*. Some peoples forbid metal in the mouth — to be treated separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Horn spoon (*jiao si*)**: for wedging the teeth (horn, smooth and not harming them).
- **Foot-table (*yan ji*)**: for binding the feet.
- **Shells, jade, pearls, coin**: the filler, by rank.
- **Rice, millet**: the grain.
- **Red thread, small pouch**: folk aids.
