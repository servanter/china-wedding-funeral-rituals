# Setting the Offerings and Curtaining the Hall

## 1. Definition

*Dian* (offering) and *ji* (sacrifice) are strictly distinguished: *dian* is for the just-dead, who are only presented with food and drink but "not tasted" — no actual eating; *ji* is the rite of "being entertained", with the sense of enjoyment. So from death to after burial, the rite is called *dian*; only after the "reassurance sacrifice" (*yu ji*) is it called *ji*.

*Wei tang* is hanging a curtain about the hall: it lets kin wail within, and shows that "spirits love the dark", separating inner from outer, the living from the dead.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* ("Shisangli"): "curtain the hall", "set the offering east of the corpse" — fixing the relation of curtain and offering.
- The *Yili* names many offerings: the "first dying offering" (dried meat, mince, sweet wine), the "minor-dressing offering", the "major-dressing offering", "morning and evening offerings", "new-moon offering", "fresh-tasting offering", "coffin-moving offering", "departure offering", "send-off offering" — a full sequence. Zheng Xuan notes the number matches rank.
- The *Liji* ("Tan'gong"): "the offering uses plain vessels, because the living have a heart of plain mourning" — plain vessels express grief.
- The Song *Shuyi* and *Family Rituals* simplified the offerings to "morning and evening" and "new-moon and full-moon", introducing the "spirit seat" and "soul-silk" into family rites.
- Later "mourning hall", curtains, mourning screens and offering tables are the long continuance of "curtaining the hall" and "setting the offering".

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Curtain the hall**: after dressing, hang a curtain (or mourning screen) in the hall, separating the spirit seat from the wailers.
2. **Set the offering**: east of the corpse (or spirit seat) set an offering mat, place dried meat, mince and sweet wine in the baskets and stands, with plain vessels.
3. **Take places and wail**: the host (chief mourner) and hostess take places and wail; men east, women west — the basic spatial order.
4. **Receive condolers**: a condoler arrives, the host bows and wails, the hostess wails within the curtain.
5. **Morning and evening offerings**: twice daily — "the morning offering at sunrise, the evening at sunset".
6. **New-moon offering**: the first of each month, grander than the daily.
7. **Fresh-tasting offering**: seasonal new things (new grain, new fruit) offered before the spirit.
8. **Serve meals**: folk custom becomes "serving meals" three times a day — incense, kneel, bow — the vulgarisation of morning and evening offerings.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Mourning-hall layout**: everywhere a portrait, spirit seat, curtain, incense, candles, fruit; the North calls it the "mourning shed", Min-Tai the "filial hall".
- **Soul-silk / soul-flag**: from the Song, silk bearing the name as the seat of the spirit.
- **Serving meals**: North and Jiangnan mostly "three servings a day"; Min-Tai set "filial lamps" and "soul-silk" and invite a "master" to chant.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some set no curtain but a separate "corpse-rest" or "spirit house" indoors or out; Tibetans, Mongols set the spirit seat by religious rite — quite different from the Han hall.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Curtain, mourning screen, drape**: the curtaining implements.
- **Offering mat, mat and table**: the offering place.
- **Sweet wine, dried meat, mince, baskets and stands**: the food and vessels, plain.
- **Spirit seat, soul-silk, portrait**: the core later furnishings.
- **Incense, candles, fruit, paper figures**: folk supplements.
