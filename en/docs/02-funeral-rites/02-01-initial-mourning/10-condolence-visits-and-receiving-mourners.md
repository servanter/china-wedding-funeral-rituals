# Condolence Visits and Receiving Mourners

## 1. Definition

- **Diao** (condole): mourning the dead, going to the spirit to grieve.
- **Yin** (console): comforting the mourners, the living.
- **Opening the mourning** (*kai diao*): the family chooses a day to receive condolers publicly (also "opening the funeral", "receiving mourners"). Before it, condolence is limited to kin; after, friends, colleagues, neighbours and the public may come.

Two gifts accompany condolence:
- **Sui** (grave-garment): clothes given the dead, for burial.
- **Fu zeng** (mourning gift): goods given the family, to help with the funeral.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Zhouli* ("Chun'guan, Da Zongbo") lists the mourning rites as "death, famine, condolence, relief, pity" — condolence among them, a rite between states, later sinking to folk social exchange.
- The *Yili* ("Shisangli") has special "gift" and "grave-garment" rites, fixing order and thanks.
- The *Liji* ("Quli"): "at a funeral, do not smile", "he who consoles, that day is not merry" — the condoler's restraint: no smile, no music, no feast.
- The *Liji* ("Tan'gong"): "on the day of condoling, no wine or meat".
- Tang-Song, the *Shuyi* and *Family Rituals* fixed "condoling-offering" and "gift"; Ming-Qing had "three days of open mourning", highly formalised.
- Modern: the "memorial meeting" and "body-farewell ceremony"; the gift becomes the "white envelope" and wreath — the core function (confirming social ties, public expression of grief) unchanged.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Usher in**: the condoler arrives, the usher leads into the hall.
2. **Incense and bow**: at the spirit, incense, kneel (or bow), silent grief.
3. **Read the condolence word / present the tribute**: read the message, or present couplets, banner, wreath.
4. **Filial son answers**: the filial son kneels at the spirit's side to answer (kneel-thanks, accompany-kneel); women wail within.
5. **Present the gift**: the condoler gives the mourning money (white envelope), banner, wreath, registered by the scribe.
6. **Distribute mourning**: the family returns a mourning kerchief, towel, candy (or mourning cloth).
7. **Entertain**: during open mourning, a feast for condolers (the "tofu meal"), some areas vegetarian.
8. **End receiving**: by the announced schedule, close and turn to burial prep.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North**: incense and three bows, filial son kneels; the feast mostly vegetarian (tofu, vermicelli, cabbage) — hence "tofu meal".
- **Jiang-Zhe**: a "filial hall" receiving all day, stressing "thanking the mourners"; condolers give "white envelopes".
- **Min-Tai**: incense and four bows, invite a "master" to chant; the family returns "mourning kerchief", "longevity bowl".
- Some areas oldly hired "wailing women" — a performative, commercial custom, a vulgarity to be noted critically.
- **Ethnic difference**: Muslim peoples' funeral at mosque or home, by the ahong, with washing, wrapping, the standing prayer (*janaza*) and swift burial — **no incense, no kneeling, no mourning-money gift**, wholly different in basis and form from the Han open mourning; Tibetans, Mongols each have their own rite.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Incense, censer**: for the spirit.
- **Gift-book**: registers condolers and gifts.
- **Couplets, banners, wreaths**: the condolers' gifts.
- **Mourning money (white envelope)**: to help, never a red envelope.
- **Mourning kerchief, cloth, longevity bowl**: the returned gift.
