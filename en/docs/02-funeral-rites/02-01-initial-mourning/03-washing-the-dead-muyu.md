# Washing the Dead (Muyu)

## 1. Definition

*Muyu* is the rite of cleansing the dead: washing the hair and body, cutting the nails (fingers and toes) and binding the hair. Its purpose is to send the dead off clean and dignified, to meet the ancestors; ritually it is the prerequisite for "dressing the corpse" (*xi*) and "filling the mouth" (*han*).

The *Liji* ("Sangdaji") says "in the mourning of a gentleman, for three days one does not slacken" — only after washing is the form clean, and then new clothes may be put on and the mouth filled.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* ("Shisangli") details preparation: dig a pit west of the steps, build a stove by the west wall, prepare new basin, tray, jar and vessels, all washed, made at the west steps — the procedure is elaborate.
- The wash-water and cut hair and nails were buried in the pit or kept in a pouch for burial, embodying the belief that "body, hair and skin" must not be lightly discarded.
- The *Liji* ("Sangdaji") records differences by rank in the implements and numbers of washers.
- After the Han the rite was simplified, but the substance of "washing and dressing in grave-clothes" was fully kept.
- After Buddhism entered, some areas added scripture-chanting around washing, forming the folk flow "assist-the-recitation — cleanse — change clothes".
- Note: modern funerals replace traditional washing with cleansing, disinfection and restoration of the remains — the function is the same, the form changed.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Prepare vessels**: build a stove by the west wall, heat water, prepare new basin, tray, jar; dig a pit west of the steps for waste water.
2. **Lay out the clothes**: set out the change of grave-clothes in the chamber.
3. **Wash the hair**: warm water, remove dirt, comb smooth.
4. **Wash the body**: wipe the whole body with warm water, front and back.
5. **Cut the nails**: cut finger- and toe-nails, keep in a pouch.
6. **Bind the hair**: comb and bind the hair with a pin, then wrap with a cloth.
7. **Dispose of water and nails**: pour waste into the pit; keep nails in the pouch or bury with the water.
8. **Proceed to dressing**: after washing, perform the dressing of the corpse.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North**: children or close kin do the wiping, with the saying "washed clean to meet the ancestors".
- **Jiang-Zhe**: "wiping the body", often by a specialist ("earth officer" or funeral worker).
- **Min-Tai**: the custom of "buying water" — the filial son takes a jar to the river, burns incense and paper to the water-god to "buy water", then washes the dead with it, clearly a primitive idea of "obtaining cleansing water from the spirits".
- Some areas forbid using the dead person's own water or towel; "new basin, new towel" is required.
- **Ethnic minorities**: the Dai, Miao and others boil herbs for washing; Muslim peoples (Hui, Uyghur) are washed ("washing the body", *xisong maoti*) by specialists per religious law, wrapped in white cloth (*kafan*), wholly different from the Han way — see the ethnic entries.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **New basin, tray, jar**: cleansing vessels, must be new or washed clean.
- **Abandoned *dun*, *chong li***: vessels recorded in the classics for heating and holding water.
- **Stove (*yi*), pit (*kan*)**: temporary hearth and waste pit.
- **Wash-cloths**: must be newly made.
- **Pouch**: for the nails.
- **Grave-clothes**: worn after washing.
