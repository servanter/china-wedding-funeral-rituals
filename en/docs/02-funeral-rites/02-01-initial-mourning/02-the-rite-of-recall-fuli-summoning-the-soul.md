# The Rite of Recall (Fuli): Summoning the Soul

![复礼（招魂）/ The rite of recalling the soul](../../../../assets/images/20-rite-of-recall.png)


## 1. Definition

*Fu* is "summoning the soul". When a person first dies, a kinsman climbs to the roof (or a height) holding the dead person's upper garment, faces north and calls the deceased's name three times, then drops the garment to cover the corpse, hoping the soul will return and the person revive.

*Fu* is the **first true procedure of the funeral rites**. The *Yili* ("Shisangli") opens with *fu* because, at the first moment of death, ritual does not yet admit the death: one must first "exhaust the way of love" — do everything possible to save — before entering the formal mourning. Only when *fu* fails to revive does the funeral begin.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* ("Shisangli") prescribes every motion of *fu*: the summoner, in ceremonial cap and robe, ascends from the eastern eave, reaches the roof-ridge, faces north, waves the garment and cries "Gao — [name] — return!", three times, then drops the garment, which is received below in a basket and laid upon the corpse.
- The *Liji* ("Tan'gong xia") explains its meaning: "*Fu* is the way of exhausting love, with the heart of prayer… facing north is to seek [the soul] in the dark." 
- The *Liji* ("Sangdaji") records rank differences: the Son of Heaven is recalled with coronal robes, and the lords, great officers and gentlemen each with their own garment, the rank shown by the robe used.
- The Chu region especially favoured soul-summoning; the *Chuci* ("Summoning the Soul", "Great Summons") are its literary form, corroborating the ritual.
- Later: rooftop summoning became "calling the soul"; the garment became the "soul-flag" (*zhao hun fan*); the cry "Gao [name] return" became a loud call by the dead person's name.
- Because rooftop summoning was difficult and dangerous, after the Song a ladder in the courtyard (or a "soul-silk", *hunbo*) was used instead.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Prepare the garment**: take the dead person's sacrificial robe, join upper and lower, fix with a pin.
2. **Hold the garment**: the summoner drapes it over the left shoulder, tucks the collar into the belt, for climbing and waving.
3. **Ascend**: climb from the eastern eave to the roof-ridge centre.
4. **Summon**: face north, wave the garment, cry "Gao — [name] — return!" three times.
5. **Drop the garment**: let it fall from the roof.
6. **Receive it**: below, a basket receives the garment.
7. **Cover the corpse**: ascend by the eastern steps and lay the garment upon the corpse.
8. **Decide**: if *fu* does not wake the dead, proceed to washing, filling the mouth and the rest.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North**: "calling the soul", the filial son climbs a ladder (or stands on a stool or table) facing north crying "XXX, come back!".
- **Jiang-Zhe**: "summoning/calling the soul", often with a Daoist or Buddhist monk chanting.
- **Min-Tai**: "calling at the foot of the bed", with a "soul-silk" and soul-flag; Daoist summoning rites are especially common.
- **Hunan-Hubei (Chu)**: custom echoes the *Chuci*; some areas still sing soul-summoning songs.
- **Ethnic minorities**: the Naxi have "summoning the soul" led by a *dongba*; the Miao, Yao and Yi each have their own ancient soul-summoning songs and a fixed "road-guiding" route sending the soul back to the ancestral land. This differs from the Han *fu*: the minorities "send back", the Han "recall" — two soul-concepts.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Recall garment**: the dead person's sacrificial robe, by rank.
- **Basket (*fei*)**: receives the dropped garment.
- **Soul-flag / guide-flag**: later substitutes, of paper or cloth, bearing name and dates.
- **Soul-silk (*hunbo*)**: from the Song, silk bearing the name as a seat for the spirit.
- **Ladder, stool**: folk means of reaching height.
