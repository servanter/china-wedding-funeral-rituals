# Removing Mourning and the Dan Sacrifice

## 1. Definition

- **Removing mourning** (*chufu*): the rite, when the mourning period ends, of taking off the mourning dress and returning to normal life.
- **Dan sacrifice** (*danji*): the sacrifice performed after the major *xiang*, the last sacrifice before removing mourning. The *Liji* explains *dan* as "the calm, peaceful feeling" — grief fades, the mind turns to peace.

The *dan* sacrifice and removing mourning are the endpoint of the "three-year mourning". After the rite, the mourner resumes ordinary dress and normal social intercourse (may marry, may feast, may cut the hair), and the funeral truly ends.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Liji* ("Jianzhuan") details the later changing and removing of mourning dress: "a year and the minor *xiang* … another year and the major *xiang* … the midst month and the *dan*" — "midst month" means an interval of one month, so the *dan* falls at the twenty-seventh month.
- The *Liji* ("Tan'gong"): "the three-year mourning is finished in twenty-five months." This runs parallel with the "twenty-seven month" saying, adopted variously by the ritual codes: the Han and Wei mostly used twenty-five months; after the Tang and Song the codes mostly used twenty-seven (e.g. the Tang *Kaiyuan Rites*, the Song *Zhenghe Rites*); Ming officials' mourning leave was twenty-seven months.
- The *Yili* ("Sangfu") clearly fixes the "removing" time for each mourning grade (e.g. "major gong nine months", "minor gong five months", "sima three months").
- Zhu Xi's *Family Rituals* fixes the *dan* rite, the paradigm folk followed afterward.
- Folk practice often simplified: completing removing mourning in the form of "removing mourning", "taking off mourning", "acknowledging thanks" at the hundredth day, anniversary or "three years", not necessarily strictly performing the *dan* sacrifice.
- Under contemporary cremation and urban life, removing mourning becomes "taking off mourning" — taking off the black armband, white flower and mourning band, resuming normal dress.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Choose the day**: one month after the major *xiang* ("midst month") choose an auspicious day for the *dan* sacrifice.
2. **Prepare the sacrifice**: prepare offerings, incense, candles, paper money, and a ritual master (if any).
3. **Set the sacrifice**: set an offering before the spirit seat (or shrine, or grave), with the three-libation rite.
4. **Read the prayer**: read the *dan* prayer, telling that the mourning code is about to end.
5. **Change the dress**: put on the *xian* (black-warp white-weft cloth) dress, or change to ordinary dress.
6. **Remove the spirit seat (removing the spirit)**: remove the spirit seat and soul-silk, change to the ancestor tablet, and attach it to the ancestral temple (or the family's ancestral tablets).
7. **Remove mourning**: take off the mourning dress, change to plain-coloured ordinary dress; men cut the hair, women remove ornaments.
8. **Feast guests (dan feast)**: hold a feast to thank kin and the funeral managers (the "*dan* feast").
9. **Report to the temple**: formally enter the dead's tablet into the family temple (or home ancestral seat); the sacrificial order returns to normal.
10. **Resume normal intercourse**: thereafter may marry, may feast, may join festivals.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: "removing mourning", "taking off mourning", mostly at the "three years" (or anniversary, hundredth day); stressing "after removing mourning may perform auspicious rites".
- **Jiangnan**: "removing the spirit", "taking off mourning", mostly at the "major *xiang*" or "three-year sacrifice".
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: "taking off mourning", mostly at the "three-year sacrifice" or "joining the furnace" — joining the dead's name into the "grandparents tablet" (ancestral tablet), a most important rite.
- **Guangdong**: "taking off mourning", mostly at the anniversary or three years, stressing "changing to red" (taking off white, putting on red or plain).
- **Contemporary**: cities mostly simplify to wearing a black armband or white flower for some days to the hundredth day, then removing.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples have a mourning period and removing-mourning system — the Yi mourning period is set by lineage branch; the Tibetan take the "anniversary" as the time to take off mourning. The length of mourning and the removing rite differ by each people's customary law.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Mourning dress (dan dress, xian dress)**.
- **Ancestor tablet (wood tablet)**: the tablet served after removing mourning.
- **Grandparents tablet (Min-Tai)**: the ancestral tablet used in joining the furnace.
- **Dan prayer**: document.
- **Offerings, incense, candles, paper money**.
- **Lineage register**: formally recorded after removing mourning.
