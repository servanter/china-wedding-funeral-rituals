# Modern Memorial Services and Online Grave Sweeping

## 1. Definition

Contemporary times see three new forms running parallel with traditional sacrifice:

1. **Memorial meeting** (*zhuisi hui*): a commemorative activity replacing religious or ritual ceremony with a gathering, mostly for public figures, teachers, colleagues; time may be the death day, birthday or death anniversary.
2. **Online grave sweeping** (*wangshang jisao*): creating a memorial hall on an internet platform, with flowers, messages and candles as virtual sacrifice.
3. **Ecological burial** (*shengtai anzang*): land-saving burials such as tree, flower, lawn, sea and wall burial, with matching memorial forms (memorial wall, memorial grove).

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Memorial meeting**: from the modern "memorial gathering" (*zhuidao hui*), widely used on the mainland from the 1950s as the main form of farewell (replacing the home offering and Buddhist-Daoist rites). Recently "memorial meeting" is milder in wording than "memorial gathering", often used for anniversaries.
- **Online grave sweeping**: appeared with internet普及 in the 2000s; after **2008 Qingming became a statutory holiday**, platforms multiplied; around 2020, due to the pandemic, "proxy sweeping" and "cloud sweeping" were promoted officially.
- **Ecological burial**: strongly advocated by the Ministry of Civil Affairs since the 2010s; the *Guiding Opinions on Promoting Land-Saving Ecological Burial* (2016) clearly encourages it; many places issued reward policies.
- **Civilised sweeping**: everywhere advocates flowers, tree-planting and the internet to replace burning paper money and firecrackers, chiefly for fire-prevention and the environment.
- **Policy background**: "rear nourishment, thin burial" and "simple funerals" are written into the advocacy documents of changing customs.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

### Memorial meeting

1. **Prepare**: fix time, place, host; contact participants.
2. **Arrange**: set the portrait (or photo wall), flowers, memorial book; play the deceased's life footage.
3. **Open**: the host announces the start, all observe silence.
4. **Reminiscence speeches**: kin, colleagues, students speak in turn, telling stories with the deceased (unlike the memorial gathering's "eulogy", the memorial meeting stresses many participants and a gentle mood).
5. **Play memorial footage / music**.
6. **Lay flowers**: participants lay flowers in turn.
7. **End and converse**: after, a tea may be set; family and participants converse.

### Online grave sweeping

1. **Create the memorial hall**: register on the platform, fill the deceased's information, upload photos and biography.
2. **Lay flowers, light candles, burn incense**: complete the sacrificial symbols with virtual acts.
3. **Leave a message**: write a remembrance message.
4. **Share and invite**: invite kin and friends to sweep together (forming a virtual "joint sacrifice").
5. **Anniversary reminder**: the platform reminds at the death day, Qingming, etc.
6. **Physical pairing (optional)**: some platforms offer "proxy flower-laying", "proxy gravestone-wiping" and other offline services.

### Ecological burial and matching memorial

1. **Choose the method**: tree, flower, lawn, sea, wall burial, etc.
2. **Handle formalities**: apply to the cemetery or civil-affairs department, sign an agreement.
3. **Burial rite**: a brief farewell (flowers, silence, scattering or placing the ashes).
4. **Set the memorial**: engrave the name on a memorial wall, plant a memorial grove, hang a memorial plaque on a tree.
5. **Later sweeping**: lay flowers at the memorial site, or use the online memorial hall.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Cities (mainly Han)**: ecological burial, memorial meetings and online sweeping are more accepted, especially in first-tier and coastal cities.
- **Rural (mainly Han)**: traditional sacrifice (burning paper, grave-visiting, burning packets) is still mainstream; ecological burial's acceptance is affected by the "rest in peace" idea and "face" factors.
- **Ethnic minorities**: special policies protect them. The earth burial of the Hui and other Muslim peoples, the Tibetan sky-burial and the like are protected by law; the promotion of ecological burial must respect each people's funeral custom, with no "one-size-fits-all".
- **Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan**: cremation rates are high, while fairly complete traditional sacrifice is kept (e.g. Taiwan's "seven-day rites", "grave sweeping", "universal deliverance"), and online worship platforms have developed.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Memorial meeting**: portrait, flowers, memorial book, footage player, guest book.
- **Online sweeping**: platform account, virtual memorial hall, message board; note the personal-information protection and digital-heritage issues.
- **Ecological burial**: degradable ashes casket, memorial plaque, memorial tree.
- **Documents**: ecological-burial agreement, grave-site certificate, ashes-placement certificate.
- **Advocacy documents**: the Ministry of Civil Affairs' guiding opinions on land-saving ecological burial and civilised sweeping.
