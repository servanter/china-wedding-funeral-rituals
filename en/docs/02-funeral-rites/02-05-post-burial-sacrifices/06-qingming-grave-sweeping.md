# Qingming Grave Sweeping

![清明扫墓 / Qingming grave sweeping](../../../../assets/images/11-qingming-grave-sweeping.png)


## 1. Definition

Qingming grave sweeping (*shangfen*, *baishan*, *jisa*) is the activity, at the Qingming season, of going to the ancestors' graves to weed, add earth, set offerings and burn paper.

It is the most widespread of the seasonal ancestor-sacrifice system, and has a public character: **not limited to one clan or family, it becomes a society-wide act of sweeping**. In 2006 Qingming was listed in the first batch of the national intangible-cultural-heritage catalogue; from 2008 it has been a statutory holiday.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **The merging of Cold Food and Qingming**: the Cold Food Festival falls 105 days after the winter solstice, with the old custom of no fire and cold food, said to commemorate Jie Zitui (recorded in the *Jingchu Suishi Ji* and others). Qingming is one of the twenty-four solar terms, fifteen days after the spring equinox. The Tang already had Cold Food grave-sweeping as custom (the *Tang Huiyao* records the Kaiyuan-era edict on "Cold Food grave-visiting", which the court recognised once folk sweeping became customary). Because Cold Food and Qingming are close in date, after the Tang and Song they gradually merged, and "Qingming grave sweeping" became the common name.
- **Grave sacrifice existed in high antiquity**: the *Zhouli* ("Chun'guan", "Zhongren") records "sacrificing at the grave with a personator", showing the grave sacrifice's early origin.
- **"Hanging paper on the tree" and "adding earth"**: the two basic acts of sweeping — "hanging paper" (pressing white paper on the grave head) and "adding earth" (adding earth to repair the grave) — are widely recorded in Ming and Qing gazetteers.
- **Local difference**: the south mostly sweeps on Qingming day ("sweeping the true Qingming"), some areas within ten days before or after, or on the Shangsi (third day of third month); some areas of Guangdong and Guangxi sweep at the Chongyang (ninth day of ninth month); Minnan at the "third-month festival".
- Contemporary sweeping is varied: going in person, commissioning a proxy, online sweeping.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Choose the day**: Qingming day or a few days around (rules differ by place).
2. **Prepare objects**: incense, candles, offerings (wine, fruit, cakes; some with "three kinds of meat"), paper money, white paper (for hanging paper), weeding tools.
3. **Reach the grave**: the whole family (or clan) goes to the grave.
4. **Weed and add earth**: clear weeds on the grave, add earth to repair the head and ring.
5. **Hang paper**: press white paper (or five-colour paper) on the grave head and ring, called "hanging paper", "pressing paper", "marking the grave". This is **the most identifying act of sweeping**, showing the grave has descendants who sweep it.
6. **Set the offerings**: set offerings before the grave, set cups.
7. **Incense and kneel**: by seniority and age, in turn light incense and kneel (or bow).
8. **Pour libation**: pour wine on the ground.
9. **Burn paper**: burn paper money and paper effigies (today flowers are promoted to replace paper money, for fire-prevention and the environment).
10. **Tidy the stele**: wipe the inscription, retouch the characters.
11. **Return**: gather the offerings, some left before the grave.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: "going to the grave", insisting "Qingming grave-going not past noon", offerings mostly cakes and fruit.
- **Jiangnan**: "sweeping the grave", "going to the grave", insisting the "green dumpling" (wormwood cake) is a necessary offering.
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: "worshipping the mountain", "sweeping", insisting on "hanging paper", with "spring rolls" as offering; before and after Qingming "tending the grave".
- **Guangdong**: "worshipping the mountain", mostly within ten days of Qingming or at Chongyang; offerings include roast pig, insisting on "the grandfather shares out the pork" (clan-property sacrifice then divides the meat).
- **Some Guangxi areas**: sweep on the third of third month (Shangsi).
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples also sacrifice at Qingming, but more have their own ancestor days (see "Ethnic-Minority Funeral Customs"). Qingming sweeping is mainly the Han and Han-influenced peoples.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **White paper (hanging paper), five-colour paper**: marking the sweep.
- **Incense, candles, offerings, wine cups**.
- **Paper money, paper effigies**.
- **Weeding tools** (hoe, sickle, basket).
- **Lineage register, grave map**: to find the grave.
- **Ancestor-sacrifice prayer**: read in some areas.
