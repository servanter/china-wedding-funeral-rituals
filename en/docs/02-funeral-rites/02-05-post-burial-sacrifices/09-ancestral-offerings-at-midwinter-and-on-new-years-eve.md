# Ancestral Offerings at Midwinter and on New Year's Eve

## 1. Definition

- **Winter-solstice sacrifice**: the winter solstice is the "second new year" (*yasui*, lesser than the spring festival); antiquity had "the winter solstice is as great as the year". Sacrificing at the winter solstice is the first node of year-end ancestor sacrifice. The Zhou once took the winter solstice as the year's start, so the winter-solstice rites of heaven and ancestors are very ancient.
- **New-Year's-Eve sacrifice**: sacrificing on the lunar year's end (eve) is the highest node of seasonal ancestor sacrifice. On this day one "invites the ancestors home for the new year", serving them until the third of the first month or after the lantern, then "sees the ancestors off".

Both are **home sacrifices** (held before the clan hall or home tablet), different in space and form from the **grave sacrifices** of Qingming, Zhongyuan and Cold-Clothes (see "Grave Offerings and Hall Offerings Compared").

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Zhouli* ("Chun'guan") records "on the winter solstice, summon the heavenly spirits and human ghosts" — the ritual source of the winter-solstice sacrifice reaches back to the Zhou.
- After the Han the winter solstice was an important festival; officials and commoners alike sacrificed and celebrated. The *Houhanshu* ("Yili zhi") records the winter-solstice rite.
- From the Tang and Song the New-Year's-Eve sacrifice took shape: set offerings, light incense, kneel, "invite the ancestors for the new year"; and on a chosen day in the first month "see the ancestors off".
- Zhu Xi's *Family Rituals* fixes the four-season sacrifices ("the sacrifices of the four seasons") and "winter-solstice sacrifice to the first ancestor", "beginning-of-spring sacrifice to the earlier ancestors", the Song family-rite framework of ancestor sacrifice.
- Ming and Qing gazetteers record "at the winter solstice set a feast to sacrifice ancestors", "on New Year's Eve set the ancestors' seat, serve wine and dishes", "on the first day pay respects to the ancestors" as the common system.
- Contemporary: the New-Year's-Eve sacrifice is still the most widespread ancestor sacrifice in Han areas; the winter solstice is especially valued in the south (especially Min-Tai, Guangdong, Jiang-Zhe), with the saying "winter is greater than the new year".

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

### Winter-solstice sacrifice

1. **Sweep and set the seat**: sweep the ancestral tablet (or hall), set the incense table and offering table.
2. **Prepare offerings**: wine, dishes, rice, fruit, cakes; some with "three kinds of meat" (south), glutinous balls (south), dumplings (north).
3. **Light incense**: the head of household (or eldest son) lights incense, reports to the ancestors.
4. **Kneel**: the whole family by seniority and age in turn kneels (or bows thrice).
5. **Pour libation**: pour wine on the ground.
6. **Burn paper**: burn paper money.
7. **Share the meal**: the family eats together (the "winter-solstice feast"), some "eat the winter-solstice balls".

### New-Year's-Eve sacrifice

1. **Invite the ancestors**: on the eve afternoon the head of household (or eldest son) goes to the door (or road junction), burns incense and paper, calling the ancestors home for the new year ("inviting the year", "receiving the ancestors").
2. **Set the seat and offerings**: in the main hall set the ancestral tablet (or portrait), the offering table, with the new-year's-eve dishes, wine, rice, chopsticks.
3. **Incense and kneel**: the family in turn kneels.
4. **Keep the incense**: through the eve the incense never stops; serve until the second or third of the month.
5. **See the ancestors off**: on a chosen day (third, fifth of the first month, or after the lantern) burn paper money and paper clothes, seeing the ancestors back (the "seeing-off of the year").
6. **Remove offerings**: take away the offerings, return to normal.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North**: the New-Year's-Eve sacrifice is solemn, the "inviting the year" and "seeing-off the year" customs widespread; the winter solstice eats dumplings, the sacrifice simple.
- **Jiangnan**: the winter solstice "as great as the year", the sacrifice solemn, with "winter-solstice balls"; the eve also sacrifices.
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: the winter-solstice sacrifice is extremely important (the "winter festival"), with the saying "not returning at the winter festival, no ancestors" — one must go home to sacrifice at the winter solstice, else counted as without ancestors; before the eve "seeing off the year" and "round stove" one must first sacrifice.
- **Guangdong**: the winter solstice "making winter" sacrifice, "winter greater than the new year"; the eve also sacrifices.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples have their own new-year ancestor sacrifice (the Yi "Yi new year", the Miao "Miao year", the Dong "Dong year", the Sui "Duan festival"), whose time and content each follow the people's calendar, not the same system as the Han winter-solstice and eve sacrifices.
