# Grave Offerings and Hall Offerings Compared

## 1. Definition

The Han ancestor sacrifice has two great forms:

- **Grave offering** (*muji*): sacrificing at the gravesite (before the grave), the object being **a concrete single ancestor or a single grave's ancestors**.
- **Hall offering** (*ciji*, home offering): sacrificing before the ancestral tablet in the hall or at home, the object being **the abstract whole of "the generations of ancestors"**.

The two differ systematically in space, time, participants, offerings and rite. Understanding this pair of differences is the key to understanding the traditional Chinese ancestor-sacrifice system.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The pre-Qin ritual code took **temple sacrifice as proper** and **grave sacrifice as "wild sacrifice"**. The *Liji* ("Jiyi"): "sacrificing at the grave is wild" — showing that in the Confucian ritual hierarchy the grave sacrifice ranked below the temple sacrifice.
- But folk grave sacrifice never ceased. The *Zhouli* ("Chun'guan", "Zhongren") has the duty of "sacrificing at the grave with a personator"; the Han had the fashion of "visiting the grave" (the *Hanshu* often records the "visiting the grave" rite).
- The Tang formally recognised folk Cold-Food grave-visiting (the *Tang Huiyao*), establishing the grave sacrifice's status.
- The Song Zhu Xi's *Family Rituals* established the "lineage-hall — home-offering" framework, running in parallel with folk grave sacrifice.
- The Ming and Qing formed the pattern where the "four-season sacrifices" (hall) and the "three great ghost festivals" (grave) ran in parallel: the hall serves the tablet, the grave visits the mound-head, with a clear division of labour.
- Contemporary: hall sacrifice declines (halls mostly abandoned), and grave sacrifice and "worshipping at the ashes-keeping place" become the mainstream.

## 3. Rite Comparison

| Item | Grave offering | Hall offering (home offering) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Space** | Graveyard (open, wild) | Lineage hall or home main chamber (indoor) |
| **Object** | The concrete grave lord (one person or a couple) | The whole of the generations of ancestors (first ancestor and each generation) |
| **Time** | Three ghost festivals (Qingming, Zhongyuan, Cold-Clothes), death day, post-burial nodes | Four-season sacrifices (spring/autumn equinox, winter solstice, New Year's Eve), new/full moon, death day, hall-sacrifice day |
| **Participants** | Whole family or whole lineage, scale variable | Lineage collective (whole lineage) or family members |
| **Presider** | Chief mourner / head of household; great lineages by the chief | The lineage son (ritual eldest) or lineage chief |
| **Offerings** | Wine, fruit, cakes, three kinds of meat (south); roast pig (Guangdong) | Three kinds of meat, gui, bian, dou and other vessels; stressing "great" or "lesser"牲 grades |
| **Core acts** | Weeding, adding earth, hanging paper, burning paper, pouring libation | Welcoming the spirits, three-libation rite, reading the prayer, distributing meat |
| **Core objects** | Hanging paper (white/five-colour), paper money, grave head | Spirit seat (ancestral tablet), incense burner, vessels, lineage register |
| **Rite nature** | Stressed "serve the dead as the living" (food, sending by burning) | Stressed "ritual order" (zhao-mu arrangement, distributing meat by rank) |
| **Religious colour** | Strong (paper money, buying the road, fengshui, cremation) | Weak (Confucian ritual code) |
| **Textual basis** | *Tang Huiyao*, gazetteers' sacrificial records | *Liji* "Wangzhi", Zhu Xi's *Family Rituals* |

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Fujian-Guangdong**: hall sacrifice developed, grave sacrifice also stressed; the two run in parallel, both solemn.
- **North China**: few halls; home offering (before the hall tablet) and grave offering run in parallel, plain in form.
- **Jiangnan**: hall sacrifice joined with the charitable estate; grave sacrifice stresses "hanging paper" and "grave-going not past noon".
- **Contemporary cities**: hall sacrifice basically gone; grave sacrifice turns into "cemetery sweeping" or "ashes-hall worshipping", simplified.
- **Ethnic minorities**: mostly no "grave — hall" opposition structure. The Yi ancestor sacrifice centres on the "ancestral spirit tube" (like hall sacrifice but no hall); the Tibetan centre on the Buddha niche and monastery; Muslim peoples have no ancestor-sacrifice rite (by doctrine they do not sacrifice to the dead, only pray for the dead's forgiveness). Applying the "grave / hall" dichotomy hides the differences among peoples' sacrifice systems.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Grave use**: hanging paper, paper money, offerings, weeding tools.
- **Hall use**: spirit seat, incense burner, vessels (gui, bian, dou), lineage register, prayer.
- **Hall-land deeds, lineage rules**: the institutional support of hall sacrifice.
- **Lineage-register's sacrifice-land, hall map, grave map**: the record载体 of the relation of the two.
