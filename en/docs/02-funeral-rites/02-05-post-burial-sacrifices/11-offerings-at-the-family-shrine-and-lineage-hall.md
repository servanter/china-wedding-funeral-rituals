# Offerings at the Family Shrine and Lineage Hall

![家庙祠堂祭祖 / Ancestral worship at the family shrine](../../../../assets/images/05-ancestral-worship.png)


## 1. Definition

- **Family shrine** (*jiamiao*): the dedicated place for sacrificing to ancestors, established by the ritual code, its scale and grade tied to rank (see "Temple Regulations and Ancestral Sacrifice").
- **Lineage hall** (*citang*): the ancestor-sacrifice site built by the folk (lineage) since the Ming and Qing, its scale and grade depending on the lineage's strength; the material carrier and activity centre of the lineage organisation.

The difference: the family shrine is **ritual-code** (with graded rules on the number and lords of temples), the lineage hall is **folk** (built jointly by the lineage, serving the first ancestor and the generations of ancestors).

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Liji* ("Wangzhi") fixes the temple system: "the Son of Heaven seven temples, the feudal lords five, the great officers three, the knights one, the commoner sacrifices in the chamber" — the commoner has no temple and can only sacrifice at home (the "chamber"), the ritual starting point of the family-shrine system.
- The Song Zhu Xi's *Family Rituals* broke the "commoner has no temple" limit, first creating the "lineage hall" (*citang*) system: build a hall east of the main chamber, serving the four generations of ancestors from the high ancestor down, the "four niches" system. This is the direct source of the lineage-hall system.
- The Ming allowed the folk to build halls for ancestor sacrifice (after the Jiajing "Great Rites Debate", folk hall-building flourished); the lineage hall spread rapidly in the south (Fujian-Guangdong, Huizhou, Jiang-Zhe), becoming the core building of the lineage.
- The Qing's developed lineages made the hall the **centre of lineage-power operation**: not only sacrifice, but also managing lineage property (hall lands, sacrifice lands), lineage rules, lineage schools, dispute mediation, relief distribution.
- Since modern times the lineage system weakened; many halls were put to other use (schools, stores, dwellings); today some areas restore halls as village-history and cultural-activity spaces.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

### Composition of the lineage hall

1. **Main hall** (*xiangtang*): the main hall serving the ancestral tablets, with the shrine and the spirit seats (ancestral tablets).
2. **Niche seats**: the first ancestor in the centre, the generations of ancestors arranged by the zhao-mu order.
3. **Sacrificial vessels**: incense burner, candle stand, offering table, gui, bian, dou, etc. (by regulation).
4. **Two wings**: keep the lineage register, steles, lineage rules.
5. **Hall lands** (sacrifice lands): the income of the lands funds sacrifice and lineage affairs.

### Hall sacrifice (lineage sacrifice)

1. **Choose the day**: held at the two seasonal sacrifices (spring equinox, autumn equinox) or winter solstice, Qingming, the ancestors' death days (by lineage rule).
2. **Sweep and arrange**: ahead sweep the hall, set the vessels, hang lanterns.
3. **Set the offerings**: prepare three kinds of meat, wine, fruit, cakes on the offering table.
4. **Beat the drum, ring the bell**: signal the start.
5. **Take positions**: the lineage chief (or lineage son) presides; the branches' sons stand by generation.
6. **Welcome the spirits**: light incense, kneel, welcome the ancestors' spirits.
7. **Three-libation rite**: first, second, final libation (see "Confucian Family Offering: the Three Libations").
8. **Read the prayer**: read the lineage-sacrifice prayer, recounting the forebears' virtue and declaring the lineage rules.
9. **Distribute the meat** (*banzuo*, dividing the meat): after the sacrifice divide the sacrificial meat among the lineage (the "distribution of meat"), by merit, generation and age — the material expression of lineage order.
10. **Call the names, discuss lineage affairs**: some lineages at the hall sacrifice read the register, admonish the rules, handle affairs (e.g. "hall deliberation", "beating the drum to accuse").
11. **Remove the feast, burn the prayer**: the rite is done.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Fujian-Guangdong**: the lineage-hall system most developed, one village one clan one hall, even one village many halls; the hall sacrifice pairs with the "grandfather shares out the pork" custom.
- **Huizhou**: tight lineage organisation, high-grade halls (three tiers "clan hall", "branch hall", "family hall").
- **Jiang-Zhe**: halls mostly joined with the charitable estate (*yizhuang*, the lineage-property relief institution).
- **North China**: halls relatively rare; mostly sacrifice before the home ancestral tablet.
- **Ethnic minorities**: most peoples have no "lineage hall" system. The Yi have the "ancestral spirit tube" (sacred vessel holding the ancestor spirit tablets) and a specific sacrifice site; the Dong's "drum tower" and "sa altar" have ethnic ceremonial function; the Tibetan have the "Buddha niche" and home shrine. These are the peoples' own belief spaces, not the same system as the Han lineage hall.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Shrine, spirit seats (ancestral tablets), grandparents tablet**.
- **Incense burner, candle stand, offering table, vessels (gui, bian, dou)**.
- **Hall-land (sacrifice-land) deeds**.
- **Lineage register (family register, clan register)**.
- **Lineage rules, hall rules, steles**.
- **Lineage-sacrifice prayer**.
- **Lanterns, bell, drum**.
