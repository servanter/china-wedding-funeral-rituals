# The Hundred-Day Offering

## 1. Definition

The hundred-day offering (*zuobairi*, *baiqi*) is the sacrifice on the hundredth day after death. It follows the "seven sevens" (forty-nine days) and precedes the "anniversary" (minor *xiang*), the third important node in the post-burial sequence.

Its function is **"transition"**: the seven sevens mark the end of the intermediate state, the anniversary is an important node of the mourning code; the hundredth day sits between, letting the sacrificial rhythm continue and avoiding a long blank.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The ritual basis of "hundred days" is not prominent in the pre-Qin classics. The *Liji* ("Wangzhi") has the system of "buried in three months", and the hundred days roughly equals the "three months" period.
- Buddhism's intermediate state has the "seven sevens" saying and also a "hundred days" saying. Buddhist texts' "hundred days" means the dead are reborn within a hundred days, hence the "hundredth-day" sacrifice. Joined with the Han "buried in three months" tradition, this formed the hundred-day offering.
- Zhu Xi's *Family Rituals* does not list a special "hundred-day offering", but from the Song, family-rite books mostly put the "hundredth day" into the mourning schedule.
- Ming and Qing gazetteers and notebooks widely record "doing the hundred days", mostly before the spirit seat (if not yet removed) or before the grave.
- The hundred-day offering is still widely kept today, simplified to visiting the grave or setting an offering at home.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Count the days**: from the day of death, the hundredth day.
2. **Prepare the sacrifice**: offerings (wine, dishes, fruit, cakes; some areas with meat), incense, candles, paper money.
3. **Set the offering**: if the spirit seat is not removed, sacrifice before it at home, with incense and kneeling; if removed, sacrifice at the grave (or ashes-placement site).
4. **Chant sutras** (if a rite is held): invite monks or the family chants and dedicates merit.
5. **Wail**: kin wail, speaking words of remembrance.
6. **Burn paper**: burn paper money and paper effigies.
7. **Await the anniversary**: between here and the anniversary (minor *xiang*) there is no fixed sacrifice.
8. **Some areas**: perform "removing the spirit" and "removing mourning" on the hundredth day (see "Removing Mourning and the Dan Sacrifice").

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: "doing the hundred days", mostly at the grave, with simpler offerings.
- **Jiangnan**: "the hundred days", if the spirit seat is not removed sacrifice at home, stressing "no haircut within the hundred days".
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: "doing the hundred days", mostly invite the *sigong* to chant; some places join it with "removing mourning".
- **Guangdong**: "the hundred days", stressing "do the hundred days, remove the mourning dress" (some areas).
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples have their own "hundred-day"-like node — the Yi "hundred-day offering" exists in some branches; but more peoples take the "anniversary" or "three-year sacrifice" as the main node, or arrange sacrifice by the religious calendar (the Tibetan by the Tibetan calendar, the Islamic by the lunar feast). The "hundredth day" is not a node common to all peoples.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Offerings**: wine, dishes, fruit, cakes.
- **Incense, candles, paper money**.
- **Sutras, ritual instruments** (if a rite is held).
- **Elegy**: read at the hundred-day offering.
- **Funeral schedule record**: records the seven periods, hundredth day, anniversary, etc.
