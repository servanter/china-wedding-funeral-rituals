# Anniversary Offerings: Xiaoxiang and Daxiang

## 1. Definition

The anniversary offering is the "auspicious sacrifice" (*xiangji*), in two grades:

- **Minor *xiang*** (*xiaoxiang*): the sacrifice on the first full year after death. "*Xiang*" means "auspicious", i.e. "approaching the auspicious".
- **Major *xiang*** (*daxiang*): the sacrifice on the second full year after death.
- **Dan sacrifice** (*danji*): one month (or three) after the major *xiang*, the rite of removing mourning (see "Removing Mourning and the Dan Sacrifice").

The three form the staged nodes of the "three-year mourning". The "three-year mourning" is actually twenty-seven months (or twenty-five), divided by the "year" unit.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* ("Shiyu li") records the "attaching sacrifice" (*fuji*) after the repose sacrifice, i.e. the newly dead's principal "attaching" to the ancestral temple, the prelude to the *xiang* sacrifice.
- The *Liji* ("Jianzhuan") details the later "changing and removing" of mourning dress: at the minor *xiang* the "training cap" (*lianguan*) — the cap changed to trained (white boiled-silk) making, hence minor *xiang* is also "training"; at the major *xiang* "plain white hemp clothing"; at the *dan* the "dark-and-light" (*xian*) cloth.
- The *Liji* ("Tan'gong"): "the three-year mourning is finished in twenty-five months." After the Han there were the "twenty-five month" and "twenty-seven month" sayings, adopted variously by the ritual codes.
- Zhu Xi's *Family Rituals* fixes the rites of "minor *xiang*", "major *xiang*" and "*dan*", the paradigm later folk followed.
- The Ming and Qing ritual codes adjusted the "three-year mourning" (e.g. officials' mourning leave twenty-seven months), while folk mostly sacrificed on the first and second anniversaries, not necessarily fulfilling the full dress.
- Contemporary: the death-day anniversary ("doing the anniversary") is still widely sacrificed, but the names "minor *xiang*" "major *xiang*" and the dress changes of "training" and "plain white" mostly no longer exist.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

### Minor xiang (first year)

1. **Choose the day**: the day of the first full year after death (mostly by the lunar calendar).
2. **Prepare the sacrifice**: offerings, incense, candles, paper money.
3. **Set the offering**: before the spirit seat (or grave), with the three-libation rite (if a ritual master presides).
4. **Read the elegy**: read the minor-*xiang* elegy.
5. **Change the dress**: **put on the "training cap"** — from the coarse hemp of *zhan cui* to the trained white boiled-silk cap, marking that grief is slightly lessened, "approaching the auspicious".
6. **Feast guests** (some areas): at the minor *xiang* "make a modest spread to thank guests", the folk "anniversary thanks to kin and friends".

### Major xiang (second year)

1. **Choose the day**: the day of the second full year.
2. **Prepare and set the sacrifice**: the rite as the minor *xiang*.
3. **Read the elegy**.
4. **Change the dress**: put on the "plain white hemp clothing" (or change to finer mourning).
5. **Remove the spirit seat**: some areas remove the spirit seat at the major *xiang* ("removing the spirit").
6. **Report to the temple**: formally attach the dead's "tablet" to the ancestral temple (if the attaching-tablet rite is performed).

### Dan sacrifice

- One month (or three) after the major *xiang*, the *dan* sacrifice is performed, removing the mourning; the mourning code ends (see "Removing Mourning and the Dan Sacrifice").

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: "doing the anniversary", "first year", sacrifice at the grave or home; some places do not hold the "second year" ("do not go up to the second year"), by local custom.
- **Jiangnan**: "minor *xiang*", "major *xiang*", more of the traditional code kept, stressing "changing the dress".
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: "doing the anniversary", joined with "removing mourning"; the "three-year sacrifice" (the full-three-years idea) is also an important node.
- **Guangdong**: "doing the anniversary", stressing the three times "first year, second year, third year".
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples have their own anniversary sacrifice — the Yi "anniversary sacrifice" mostly done within the "making the fast" rite; the Naxi have a "three-year sacrifice" (led by the Dongba); the Tibetan take the anniversary sutra-chanting as the main sacrifice. "Minor *xiang*" and "major *xiang*" are special names of the Confucian ritual code and should not be extended to other peoples.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Offerings, incense, candles, paper money**.
- **Training cap, plain white hemp clothing**: the changed dress of minor and major *xiang*.
- **Elegy (minor-*xiang* elegy, major-*xiang* elegy)**: document, format in "Document Templates / Elegy Model".
- **Spirit tablet** (if the attaching-tablet rite is performed).
- **Lineage register**: after the *xiang* sacrifice may be recorded in it.
