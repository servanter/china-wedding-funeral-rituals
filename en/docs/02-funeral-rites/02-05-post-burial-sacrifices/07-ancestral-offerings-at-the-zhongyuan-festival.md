# Ancestral Offerings at the Zhongyuan Festival

![中元节放水灯（河灯）/ Zhongyuan Festival river lanterns](../../../../assets/images/12-zhongyuan-river-lanterns.png)


## 1. Definition

The Zhongyuan Festival, the fifteenth day of the seventh lunar month, popularly "the half of the seventh" (*qiyueban*), "ghost festival" (*guijie*), "Ullambana Festival" (*Yulanpen jie*). On this day ancestors are sacrificed to and the dead souls are delivered; the custom fuses three sources:

1. **The Daoist Zhongyuan**: Daoism takes the fifteenth of the first month as the Upper Prime (the heavenly official bestows blessing), the fifteenth of the seventh as the Middle Prime (the earthly official pardons sins), the fifteenth of the tenth as the Lower Prime (the water official relieves peril). The Middle Prime's earthly official pardoning sins is exactly the festival of delivering the dead.
2. **The Buddhist Ullambana assembly**: the *Ullambana Sutra* tells of Maudgalyayana saving his mother; the Buddha bid that on the fifteenth of the seventh, with food of a hundred flavours, one nourish the monks of the ten directions, to save the peril of seven generations of parents. Hence the fifteenth of the seventh is the "Ullambana Festival".
3. **Folk ancestor sacrifice**: ranked with Cold Food, Qingming and the first of the tenth as the Han's annual grave-sacrifice nodes (one of the "three great ghost festivals", with the Shangsi).

## 2. Historical Origins

- The Zhongyuan Festival formed between the Northern and Southern Dynasties and the Tang, the result of the fusion of Buddhism, Daoism and folk sacrifice.
- The Tang and Song saw it at its height: folk sacrificed to ancestors, Buddhist temples held Ullambana assemblies, Daoist temples held Zhongyuan fasts; at night "floating river-lanterns" delivered the water-dead.
- Ming and Qing gazetteers record Zhongyuan "every home prepares wine and dishes to sacrifice ancestors, burns underworld clothes and paper money" and "holds Ullambana assemblies, floats river-lanterns".
- Folk have the saying "on the fifteenth of the seventh the ghost gate opens", holding the dead souls may return home; hence also the custom of "burning the packet" (burning the paper packet inscribed with the ancestor's name, containing paper money).
- Contemporary Zhongyuan's religious colour has lessened; ancestor sacrifice, burning packets and floating lanterns survive in some areas, while everywhere promotes "civilised sweeping" with flowers and online sacrifice replacing burning.
- Concerning folk belief, this corpus records it as folklore.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Prepare objects**: incense, candles, offerings (wine, dishes, rice, fruit), paper money, "packets" (paper packets inscribed with ancestors' names).
2. **Home sacrifice**: before the ancestral tablet (or portrait) set offerings, light incense, kneel.
3. **Write the packet (burn the packet)**: on the paper packet write the ancestor's name and "received by so-and-so", put in paper money.
4. **Burn**: at the door (or designated spot) burn the packet, paper money, underworld clothes. Some draw a circle in the ashes to keep "wild ghosts from grabbing the money".
5. **Float river-lanterns**: set paper lanterns (or lotus-leaf lanterns) on the river to deliver the water-dead. Mostly in river-lake shore areas.
6. **Ullambana assembly (temple)**: the temple holds an Ullambana rite, chanting sutras and giving food.
7. **Zhongyuan fast (Daoist temple)**: the Daoist temple holds a Zhongyuan rite to deliver the dead.
8. **Grave sacrifice (some areas)**: Zhongyuan also visits the grave, especially for the newly dead (the local rule "new grave not past the she, old grave not past Qingming" varies by place).

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: "the fifteenth of the seventh", "ghost festival", insisting on "burning the packet"; some places float lanterns by the river.
- **Jiangnan**: "the half of the seventh", insisting on "floating river-lanterns" and "Ullambana assembly", with "eggplant cakes" as offering.
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: Zhongyuan is a festival second only to the Spring Festival; villages in turn hold the "universal deliverance" (*pudu*), feasting guests on a grand scale; "grabbing the lonely" (*qianggu*) is a distinctive custom.
- **Guangdong**: "the fourteenth of the seventh", insisting on "burning clothes" (burning paper clothes) and "giving to the lonely" (feeding the lone souls).
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples have their own "ghost festival" or ancestor day (e.g. the Yi sacrifice ancestors during the Torch Festival, the Zhuang at Zhongyuan), but these must not be conflated with the Buddhist-Daoist origin of the Han Zhongyuan.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Paper packet (burden)**: inscribed with the ancestor's name, the core object of "burning the packet".
- **Paper money, underworld clothes, paper effigies**.
- **River-lantern (lotus-leaf lantern)**.
- **Incense, candles, offerings**.
- **Ullambana offering**: used in temples.
- **Rite petition**: used in Daoist temples.
