# The Third-Day Rite (Fusan) and Rounding the Grave

## 1. Definition

The third-day rite (*fusan*, also *yuanfen*, *fushan*, *sanchao*, *nuanmu*) is the rite, on the third day after burial, of kin returning to the grave, tidying the grave head, setting an offering and wailing.

The name *fusan* has two explanations: one that *fu* means "to go again" (going to the grave a third time — actually returning three days after burial); one that takes the number "three" (three days). Its substance is: **adding earth and rounding the grave (making the head round and tidy) + setting an offering and wailing + burning paper money**.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* ("Jixi li") records the "returning wail" and "repose sacrifice" after burial, but the name *fusan* is absent from the ritual classics.
- Zhu Xi's *Family Rituals* sets the schedule frame of "the repose sacrifice on the third day after burial", the ritual basis for the "third day" node.
- The custom of *fusan* is widely recorded in Ming and Qing gazetteers, with names varying by place: the north mostly "fusan" or "rounding the grave", Jiangnan "returning to the mountain" or "third morning", Min-Tai "patrolling the mountain" or "rounding the grave".
- The actual function of *fusan* is **to turn the "new grave" into a "finished grave"**: the earth-covering on the burial day is mostly rough, and three days later they come to shape it — the last step of grave-building.
- Some areas also have the "meeting the third" or "welcoming the third" idea (holding the soul returns home on the third day), coinciding in time with *fusan* but different in idea.
- Contemporary earth-burial areas still widely practise *fusan*; cremation areas simplify it to "sweeping on the third day after ashes placement".

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Choose the time**: the third day after burial (some count the burial day in the three, some count from the next day — follow local rule).
2. **Prepare objects**: incense, candles, offerings (wine, fruit, cakes), paper money, paper effigies, and grave tools (spade, basket).
3. **Reach the grave**: kin in mourning attire go to the grave; men arrive first, women follow.
4. **Round the grave**: add earth, tidy the grave head into a regular round; tidy the grave ring, clear weeds.
5. **Set the offering**: set offerings before the grave, light incense, kneel and bow.
6. **Wail**: kin wail, telling the dead "you are settled here".
7. **Burn paper**: burn paper money and paper effigies.
8. **Return**: on return do not turn back (some areas).
9. **Afterward**: enter the periodic sacrifice of the "seven-day rites".

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: "fusan" or "rounding the grave", insisting "round the grave on the third day, the head round and true".
- **Jiangnan**: "returning to the mountain" or "third morning", insisting on wine and dishes before the grave to "keep the dead company at the meal".
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: "patrolling the mountain" or "rounding the grave", some places must visit the grave three days running.
- **Guangdong**: "returning to the grave", insisting on "visiting and rounding the grave, erecting the stele on the third morning".
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples have a post-burial sacrifice — the Yi "making the fast" and "sending the spirit" on a chosen day after burial, far larger than the Han *fusan*; the Miao "burning the spirit" only years after burial; the Tibetan take the sutra-chanting in the "seven sevens" as the main sacrifice. *Fusan* is a specific custom of the Han (and some Han-influenced peoples).

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Offerings**: wine, fruit, cakes.
- **Incense, candles, paper money, paper effigies**.
- **Grave tools**: spade, basket, hoe.
- **Mourning attire, mourning kerchief**.
- **Grave map, grave-site deed**: records of the grave position.
