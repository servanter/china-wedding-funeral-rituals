# Offerings, Paper Effigies and Spirit Money

## 1. Definition

This entry gathers the two great classes of objects used in ancestor sacrifice:

- **Offerings (food class)**: what can be set out, eaten, and shared after the rite.
- **Paper effigies and spirit money** (burning class): not edible, made only for burning.

Their common logic is "**serve the dead as the living**": imagining the dead's needs from the living's. Offerings answer "eating"; paper effigies and spirit money answer "use" and "spending".

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Ritual basis of offerings**: the *Yili* and *Liji* record sacrificial offerings chiefly of animal (ox, sheep, pig), grain (millet), ceremonial wine, and the contents of the *bian* and *dou*, stressing grade (great and lesser 牲).
- **Origin of paper money**: Tang documents (the *Fengshi Wenjian Ji*) record "paper money" as a thing since the Wei and Jin; "punched money" (paper holed into coin shape) was common in the Tang and Song.
- **Rise of paper effigies** (paper mingqi): after the Song, paper objects replaced real objects for burial and offering — low cost, easy to burn, rapidly popular (see "Spirit Money and Paper Coins", "Paper Effigies").
- **Formation of "burning the packet"**: after the Ming and Qing, paper money and paper clothes were put into a paper packet inscribed with the ancestor's name and burned together, forming the "burning the packet" / "burden" system.
- **Religious fusion**: Buddhism's food-giving, Daoism's gold-and-silver bridge, and the folk "underworld bank" idea together enriched the kinds of paper effigies.
- Contemporary: everywhere promotes civilised sweeping, replacing burning with flowers, tree-planting and online sacrifice; the paper-effigy trade contracts in cities, survives in villages.

## 3. Rite Comparison

### Offerings (by festival)

| Festival | Common offerings |
| --- | --- |
| Qingming | Green dumpling (Jiangnan), spring rolls (Min-Tai), fruit, cakes |
| Zhongyuan | Three kinds of meat, rice and dishes, fruit, eggplant cake (Jiangnan), rice cake |
| Cold-Clothes | Dumplings (north), glutinous cake, seasonal fruit |
| Winter solstice | Dumplings (north), glutinous balls / winter-solstice balls (south) |
| New Year's Eve | New-year's-eve dishes, wine, rice, chopsticks (invite the ancestors to "share the new-year meal") |
| Death day | The food the deceased liked in life |
| Hall sacrifice | Three kinds of meat (pig, sheep, chicken), wine, fruit, cakes |

### Paper effigies and spirit money (burning class)

| Kind | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Paper money (punched, underworld coins) | Paper coin shapes, or printed "Underworld Bank" |
| Silver paper / gold paper / treasury money (Min-Tai) | Silver paper to the dead, gold paper to the gods, treasury money to repay underworld debt |
| Paper clothes (cold clothes) | Five-colour paper cut into clothes |
| Paper effigy goods | Paper house (spirit house), paper chest, paper furniture, paper appliances, paper car |
| Paper figures and horses | Attendants and mounts |
| Burden (paper packet) | Inscribed with the ancestor's name, holding paper money and clothes |
| Gold and silver bridge | Used by Daoism to deliver the dead |

### Burning order

1. Home or grave sacrifice: set offerings, light incense, kneel.
2. First burn "paper for the gods" (e.g. gold paper), then "paper for the dead" (silver paper).
3. When burning the "burden", write the name and recite the deceased's appellation.
4. When burning, poke with a stick to let it breathe (folk: "let the dead receive well").
5. After burning, pour wine, kneel, gather.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: paper money mostly "punched money" (round-holed scraps), insisting "burn paper not empty".
- **Jiangnan**: fine paper effigies; offerings stress the season (green dumpling, eggplant cake, dumplings).
- **Min-Tai (Fujian-Taiwan)**: the finest classification of paper money (gold, silver, treasury, stream, etc.), strictly divided in use; large-scale paper effigies (spirit house).
- **Guangdong**: "gold-silver-clothing paper", complete "burning clothes"; offerings stress "good omen".
- **Ethnic difference**: paper effigies and spirit money are Han products; **Muslim peoples do not use paper money** (the law deems it useless), the Tibetan take butter lamps, prayer flags and offerings as the main form, the Dai take "giving" and flowers. These object systems must be recorded by people.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Offering vessels**: offering table, incense burner, candle stand, wine cups, bowls and chopsticks.
- **Paper effigies and spirit money**: see the tables above.
- **Burden paper, brush and ink**: to write the ancestor's name.
- **Brazeros, incineration furnace**.
- **Contemporary substitutes**: flowers, memorial trees, online-sweeping platforms.
