# The Relation between Ritual and Custom

## 1. Definition

**Ritual** (*li*) is a body of norms drawn up by the upper levels of society and grounded in the classics; **custom** (*su*) is the habit of life handed down over long periods among the people and marked by local colour. The two are not sharply separated. Their relation can be reduced to four modes:

| Relation | Explanation | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ritual transforms custom** | Ritual norms reshape popular practice | Dynastic prohibitions of “joyous marriage to the dying” and of ghost marriage |
| **Custom enters ritual** | Popular usage is taken up into the ritual codes | “Bowing in the hall” and “roughhousing the bridal chamber” enter the wedding of commoners |
| **Ritual and custom run side by side** | Each follows its own course | Official funeral rites and popular rites of deliverance coexist |
| **Where ritual is lost, custom preserves it** | Ritual decays while usage survives | Details of ancient rite survive only in folk practice |

## 2. Historical Origins

- The “Royal Regulations” chapter of the *Liji*: “Cultivate their instruction without changing their customs; unify their government without altering what suits them” — a policy of teaching according to local custom.
- The Han and Wei practised “transforming custom by ritual”, guiding the people through village covenants and family instructions.
- The Tang and Song saw a turn towards “ritual descending to the commoners”: Sima Guang’s *Book of Ceremonies* and Zhu Xi’s *Family Rituals* drew up ritual for ordinary people.
- In the Ming and Qing family rituals spread widely, and ritual and custom tended to merge: popular weddings and funerals added or omitted within the framework of the ritual codes.
- In modern times the ritual order dissolved while custom survived in greater measure, and the two gradually drew apart.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Making the ritual**: drawing up a ceremonial agenda on the basis of the classics.
2. **Promulgation**: carrying it downwards through ritual codes, village covenants and family instructions.
3. **Gentlemen as models**: the households of the gentry set the example, and those below follow.
4. **Popular reception**: the people add or omit according to their own circumstances, producing regional variants.
5. **Feeding back**: usage long established is recorded in the ritual codes or family rituals, completing the cycle from custom to ritual.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Han Chinese areas**: ritual and custom are closely interwoven, and weddings and funerals are largely “half ritual, half custom”.
- **Frontier and ethnic-minority areas**: the influence of the ritual codes is weaker, the people’s own customs predominate, and Chinese nomenclature is sometimes borrowed.
- **Record separately**: the categories of Chinese ritual must not be imposed upon ethnic custom.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: village covenants, family instructions, family rituals, local gazetteers and their sections on custom.
- **Objects**: shrine rules, tablets bearing lineage rules, tablets bearing village covenants.
