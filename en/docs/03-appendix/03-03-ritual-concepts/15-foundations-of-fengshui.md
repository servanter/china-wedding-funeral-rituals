# Foundations of Fengshui

## 1. Definition

*Kanyu* is the art of reading the land; its original sense was “to survey heaven and to survey earth”. In later use it refers specifically to siting a dwelling and to siting a grave: reading a dwelling is called the **yang dwelling**, reading a grave the **yin dwelling** (also called “geomancy”). There are two principal schools:

| School | Other names | Emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Landscape school** | Jiangxi school, *luantou* school | Reading the shape of mountains and the movement of water, attending to “dragon, sand, water and apex” |
| **Cosmological school** | Fujian school | Determining good and ill from direction, the trigrams and the stems and branches |

## 2. Historical Origins

- The pre-Qin period already knew the custom of reading the land to choose a dwelling.
- The Han and Wei produced works on siting such as the *Kanyu Golden Casket*.
- Guo Pu’s *Book of Burial* of the Jin was honoured as the ancestor of burial method and advanced the doctrine of “riding the vital breath”.
- In the Tang and Song, Yang Yunsong and Lai Wenjun transmitted the landscape school in Jiangxi, and it became a major tradition.
- In the Ming and Qing works such as the *Five Secrets of Geomancy* circulated, and the art of siting entered deeply into popular burial practice.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

**Reading a grave (yin dwelling)**: seeking the dragon — observing the sand — watching the water — fixing the apex — orienting the grave — setting the compass divisions.
**Reading a dwelling (yang dwelling)**: reading the form — fixing the orientation — laying out the positions of door, stove and bed.

In burial custom the coffin may be lowered and the mound raised only after the orientation has been set and the compass divisions fixed.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Han Chinese areas**: in Jiangxi, Fujian and Guangdong the practice of reading the land is especially strong, and grave sites are mostly chosen geomanctically.
- **Ethnic-minority areas**: most peoples have their own ways of choosing a burial place (reading the mountain, throwing an egg, divination), not of the same origin as fengshui, and these must be recorded separately.
- **Treat critically**: claims about good and ill fortune in geomancy and burial should be recorded as historical and folk phenomena, never advocated.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: books of burial, land deeds, land-purchase deeds, compass diagrams.
- **Objects**: the geomantic compass, the water level, the carpenter’s rule, contracts and bonds.
