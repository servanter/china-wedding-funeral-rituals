# The Three Ritual Classics: Yili, Liji and Zhouli

## 1. Definition

The “Three Rituals” are the three ritual books — the *Yili*, the *Liji* and the *Zhouli* — the corpus of traditional ritual literature. Their structure and function differ:

| Book | Alternative names | Contents | Character |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Yili** | *Rituals of the Knights*, *Classic of Rites* | The ceremonial of capping, marriage, mourning, sacrifice, archery, village drinking, court audience and embassy | A book of ceremonial |
| **Liji** | *Small Dai Record of Rites* | Discourses on ritual meaning and explanations of institutions | A book of ritual meaning |
| **Zhouli** | *Offices of Zhou* | The functions of the six ministries and the system of ritual | A book of offices and institutions |

The three books corroborate one another: the *Yili* shows the ceremonial, the *Liji* makes the meaning clear, and the *Zhouli* reveals the institutions.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The books were formed in the pre-Qin period; early in the Han the *Yili* was established among the official scholarly disciplines and called the “Classic of Rites”.
- At the end of the Han, Zheng Xuan commented on all three, and the name “Three Rituals” was established.
- In the Tang, Kong Yingda and others compiled the *Correct Meaning of the Liji*, and subcommentaries were made to the *Yili* and *Zhouli* as well.
- The Qing *Commentaries and Subcommentaries to the Thirteen Classics* brought this work to completion, and it remains the fundamental corpus for the study of ritual institutions.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

The three rituals may be studied and applied in the following order:

1. **Mastering the ceremonial**: read the *Yili* first, to grasp the steps, objects and formulas of each rite.
2. **Understanding the meaning**: then read the *Liji*, to understand the meaning and institutions behind the ceremonial.
3. **Knowing the offices**: read the *Zhouli*, to learn the place of ritual within the bureaucracy and the bodies that carried it out.
4. **Comparing with the codes**: use the dynastic ritual codes (*Great Tang Code of the Kaiyuan Reign*, *Comprehensive Rituals of the Great Qing*) to see what was added and removed.
5. **Corroborating with custom**: use what fieldwork records to confirm the survival and transformation of ancient rite.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Han Chinese areas**: the ceremonial recorded in the Three Rituals survives and is transformed in many forms of folk custom.
- **Ethnic-minority areas**: each people has its own ceremonial system, to which the Three Rituals do not apply; where Chinese institutions have been borrowed, what was taken and what was left must be distinguished and recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: annotated editions of the Three Rituals, dynastic ritual codes, ritual diagrams (the *Diagrams of the Three Rituals*).
- **Objects**: ritual vessels, sacrificial vessels, musical instruments, reconstructed from the forms recorded in the ritual books.
