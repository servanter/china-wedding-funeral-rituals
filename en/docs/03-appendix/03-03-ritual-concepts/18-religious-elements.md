# Religious Elements in Wedding and Funeral Custom: Confucian, Buddhist, Daoist and Folk

## 1. Definition

Traditional Chinese wedding and funeral custom is the product of several overlapping beliefs: Confucianism governs ritual meaning and lineage law, Buddhism governs deliverance and karmic retribution, Daoism governs liturgy and the driving out of evil, and folk belief governs spirits and ghosts, taboo and the pursuit of good fortune.

| Belief | Its expression in weddings and funerals |
| --- | --- |
| **Confucian** | Family rituals, the Six Rites, the five degrees of mourning, ancestral sacrifice, the three libations |
| **Buddhist** | Deliverance, the flame-mouth rite, sutra chanting, vegetarian fare, the tying of thread (combined with Theravada custom) |
| **Daoist** | Breaking hell, refining the soul, summoning the spirit, talismanic writing, choosing days |
| **Folk belief** | Choosing auspicious days, warding off evil (peach branches, cinnabar), paper offerings, “joyous” marriage to the dying, bridal lament |

## 2. Historical Origins

- Under the Han and Wei, Confucian ritual was the basis of funeral practice, and ritual order stood together with lineage law.
- In the Northern and Southern Dynasties Buddhism spread, and rites of deliverance gradually entered the funeral.
- In the Tang and Song, Daoist liturgy entered popular funerals and proceeded alongside Buddhist rites.
- In the Ming and Qing the three teachings merged: popular funerals commonly set “Confucian family offering + Buddhist rite + Daoist liturgy” side by side, and wedding custom likewise adopted the choice of days and the warding off of evil.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Confucian family offering**: the three libations and the reading of the elegy, the backbone of the funeral.
2. **Buddhist rite**: monks are invited to chant sutras, perform the flame-mouth rite and conduct the seven-day cycle, delivering the dead.
3. **Daoist liturgy**: Daoist priests perform rites of breaking hell and refining the soul.
4. **Folk apotropaics**: peach branches, talismanic writing and cinnabar are used to seek good fortune and avoid ill.
5. **Choice of days throughout**: from encoffining and the funeral procession to every stage of the wedding, days and hours are chosen.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Han Chinese areas**: Buddhist and Daoist rites are used together, with different emphases by region (Fujian and Taiwan favour Buddhist rites, Hunan the Daoist liturgy).
- **Ethnic-minority areas**: the people’s own religion predominates (shamanism, Bön, Theravada Buddhism, indigenous religion) and partly overlaps with Chinese ceremonial; these must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: elegies, sutras, liturgical texts, talismans, invocations.
- **Objects**: ritual instruments (hand-bell, wooden fish, bell), paper offerings, incense and candles, peach branches, cinnabar.
