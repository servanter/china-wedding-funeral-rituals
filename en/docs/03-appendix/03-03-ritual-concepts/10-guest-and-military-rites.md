# The Guest Rites and the Military Rites: An Outline

## 1. Definition

The **guest rites** are those by which “the states are brought close”, governing dealings among states: **audience** (a feudal lord attending the sovereign), **embassy** (sending envoys on a visit), **conference** (sworn meetings) and **assembly** (joint gatherings). The **military rites** are those by which “the states are overawed”, concerning war and mobilisation: the **great levy** (sending out troops), the **great equalisation** (levelling taxes), the **great hunt**, the **great labour** (corvée) and the **great investiture** (fixing boundaries).

The guest and military rites appear to have nothing to do with weddings and funerals, yet institutions such as **marriage alliance** (heqin) and **tribute marriage** profoundly influenced marriage custom, and are therefore noted here.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The “Office of the Great Ancestor” chapter of the *Rites of Zhou* established the names of the guest and military rites.
- Under the Han and Tang the guest rites consisted chiefly of tribute, investiture and marriage alliance, while the military rites were regularly those of reviewing troops, hunting and swearing oaths before battle.
- Between the Song, Liao, Jin and Yuan, the rites of envoy exchange proceeded alongside the institution of marriage alliance.
- In the Ming and Qing the guest rites were devoted to tribute and foreign envoys, while the military rites developed into reviews and grand inspections.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

- **Guest rites**: receiving the guest — the meeting (bows and salutes) — presenting offerings (silks and gifts) — the banquet — seeing the guest off.
- **Military rites**: swearing the oath — marching — the triumphal return — presenting captives — the victory feast.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **The institution of marriage alliance**: under the Han and Tang, princesses (or daughters of the imperial clan) were married to frontier powers, which turned cross-ethnic marriage into a matter of ritual regulation with long-term effects on the marriage custom of the peoples concerned.
- **Tribute marriage**: intermarriage between the Ming and Qing and neighbouring powers also carried the colour of guest ritual.
- **Record separately**: the marriage custom of each people has its own system, and the categories of guest ritual must not be imposed upon it.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: tallies and credentials, the ceremonial staff, memorials, tribute lists, covenants.
- **Objects**: the staff of authority, seals and tallies, tribute goods, musical instruments.
