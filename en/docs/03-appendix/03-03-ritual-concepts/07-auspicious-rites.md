# The Auspicious Rites (Jili): An Outline

## 1. Definition

The auspicious rites are those by which “the spirits and deities of the state are served”, that is, the rites of sacrifice, and they stand first among the five categories. Their objects fall into three classes:

| Object | Contents | Examples of the manner of sacrifice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Heavenly spirits** | The Lord on High, sun, moon, stars, wind and rain | Suburban sacrifice, *yin* sacrifice, the burning of offerings |
| **Earthly deities** | The altars of soil and grain, the five sacred peaks, mountains and rivers, the four quarters | Blood sacrifice, burial of offerings |
| **Human ghosts** | Ancestors, former sages and worthies | The four seasonal sacrifices in the ancestral temple, the attached sacrifice |

The meaning of sacrifice lies in “requiting the root and returning to the beginning” — requiting the virtue of heaven that covers and earth that bears, and tracing the source of ancestral and clan merit.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The “Office of the Great Ancestor” chapter of the *Rites of Zhou* lists twelve items of auspicious rite, the beginning of the classification.
- Under the Han and Tang the suburban sacrifices and the great *fengshan* ceremonies made the institutions of sacrifice gradually complete.
- In the Song, Ming and Qing sacrifice was reshaped by neo-Confucian learning, and household and lineage sacrifice spread among the people.
- Today popular seasonal sacrifices (Qingming, Zhongyuan, midwinter) proceed alongside household sacrifice.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Divining the day**: choosing the day of sacrifice.
2. **Fasting and abstinence**: the chief sacrificer fasts and bathes, purifying body and mind.
3. **Setting out the offerings**: victims and sweet wine, grain, jades and silks are set out before the spirits.
4. **Welcoming the spirits**: burning incense and playing music to welcome them.
5. **The three libations**: the first, second and third libations, each with wine and food.
6. **Drinking the blessing and receiving the meat**: the chief sacrificer drinks the cup of blessing and receives the sacrificial meat, a sign of divine bestowal.
7. **Sending off the spirits and burning the invocation**: the farewell completed, the invocation text and paper money are burned.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Han Chinese areas**: household sacrifice, lineage-hall sacrifice and grave sacrifice predominate; the suburban sacrifices have lapsed.
- **Ethnic-minority areas**: the sacrifice to heaven, to mountains and to ancestors each form their own system (the *obo* sacrifice of the Mongolians, the heaven sacrifice of the Naxi), and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: invocation texts, sacrificial registers, ritual codes.
- **Objects**: victims and sweet wine, grain, jades and silks, incense stands, sacrificial vessels, musical instruments.
