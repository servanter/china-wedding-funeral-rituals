# Lineage Law

![宗法大宗小宗图 / Lineage law: greater and lesser branches](../../../../assets/diagrams/宗法大宗小宗图.svg)

*Figure 12. The greater and lesser branches of lineage law, and succession by the eldest son of the principal wife.*

## 1. Definition

Lineage law takes patrilineal blood ties as its bond and distinguishes **the greater line** (*dazong*) from **the lesser lines** (*xiaozong*) according to legitimacy and seniority: the eldest son of the principal wife carries the sacrifices generation after generation as the lineage heir, and is called the greater line; the remaining sons establish separate branches, called lesser lines. Its core is **succession by the eldest legitimate son**, **sacrifice presided over by the lineage heir**, and **the clan dwelling together**.

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Greater line** | The line of eldest sons of the principal wife, generation after generation the lineage, presiding over the sacrifices of the ancestral temple |
| **Lesser line** | Branches established by sons of concubines or by other sons, shifted every five generations |
| **Lineage heir** | Head of the lineage, presiding over sacrifice and managing lineage affairs |
| **Separated son** | One who separates from another lineage or branch, the starting point of lineage law |

## 2. Historical Origins

- The Zhou established lineage law, whose meaning is discussed at length in the “Great Traditions” and “Small Records of Mourning Dress” chapters of the *Liji*.
- After the Warring States its political significance declined while its social significance continued.
- The great houses of the Wei and Jin valued genealogies, and clans took pride in the standing of their line.
- After the Song lineages were rebuilt: Fan Zhongyan founded charitable estates, and the *Family Rituals* established the system of the ancestral hall, carrying lineage law down among the people.
- In the Ming and Qing clans became organised, with genealogies, ancestral halls, lineage lands and lineage rules all in place.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

The daily working of a lineage proceeds as follows:

1. **Installing the lineage heir**: the eldest son of the principal wife is chosen as lineage heir, to preside over sacrifice and affairs.
2. **Building the ancestral hall**: a hall is built east of the main chamber, with ancestral tablets installed.
3. **Compiling the genealogy**: recording descent, order of birth, taboo names and migrations.
4. **Providing sacrificial land and charitable estates**: sacrificial land supports the offerings, charitable estates support members of the lineage.
5. **Performing the four rites**: capping, marriage, mourning and sacrifice are carried out according to lineage rules and must leave a trace in the genealogy and the ancestral hall.
6. **Deliberating lineage affairs and applying lineage rules**: disputes among members are discussed and settled by the lineage heir and the elders.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: lineage organisation is relatively loose and the authority of the lineage heir is weak.
- **South China (Fujian and Guangdong, Huizhou)**: lineages are strong, ancestral halls stand everywhere, and lineage rules are strict.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples have comparable organisations (the village elders of the Hani, the *kasang* of the Derung, the *zhuoba* of the Jino), but their character differs from Han lineage law and they must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: genealogies, lineage rules, sacrificial registers, charters of charitable estates.
- **Objects**: ancestral halls, ancestral tablets, deeds to sacrificial land, lineage seals.
