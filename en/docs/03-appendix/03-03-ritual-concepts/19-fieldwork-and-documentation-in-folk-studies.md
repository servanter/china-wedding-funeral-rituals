# Fieldwork and Documentation in Folk Studies

## 1. Definition

Fieldwork is the basic means of gathering living folk material. It comprises five methods: **participant observation**, **in-depth interviewing**, **oral history**, **photographic and video recording**, and **the collection of objects**. For this corpus its purpose is to corroborate texts, fill in detail, and record regional and ethnic variants.

**Basic principles**: respect the wishes of those interviewed; record faithfully without inference; note the time, place and narrator; anonymise sensitive material.

## 2. Historical Origins

- Early surveys of custom are represented by compendia such as the *Gazetteer of Chinese Customs*, which relied largely on texts and hearsay.
- In the middle of the twentieth century large-scale surveys of ethnic society and history were carried out, accumulating a great deal of material on weddings and funerals.
- Contemporary folklore studies and anthropology have established standards for fieldwork, emphasising long residence and ethnographic writing.
- In the past twenty years audio recording, video and digital means have come into wide use in documenting custom.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Choosing the site**: selecting a representative village or community according to the research question.
2. **Reporting and making contact**: explaining the purpose to the local authorities and to the families concerned, and obtaining consent.
3. **Participant observation**: following the whole wedding or funeral, recording the sequence, the participants, the objects and the formulas.
4. **Interviewing**: interviewing at different levels (elders, officiants, the parties concerned, women and young people), noting divergent accounts.
5. **Recording**: notes, audio, video, measured drawings of objects and transcriptions of documents in parallel.
6. **Organising and checking**: arranging the material into a survey record, and revisiting to verify what remains doubtful.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Ethnic areas**: attend to language barriers and the accuracy of translation, recording terms and ritual texts in the original language wherever possible, with transcription and gloss.
- **Urban and rural difference**: urban custom changes quickly, and both the old and the new practice must be recorded.
- **Ethical requirements**: photographing at a funeral must be discreet, and the progress of the rite must not be disturbed.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: survey forms, interview outlines, field diaries, oral transcripts, transcriptions of model documents.
- **Objects**: audio recorders, cameras, measuring tools, storage for audio and video.
