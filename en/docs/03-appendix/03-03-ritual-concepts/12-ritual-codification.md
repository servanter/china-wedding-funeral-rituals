# Ritual Codification through the Dynasties

![历代礼制沿革时间轴 / Timeline of ritual codification](../../../../assets/diagrams/历代礼制沿革时间轴.svg)

*Figure 16. Timeline of ritual codification: Zhou, Han, Tang, Song, Ming, Qing.*

## 1. Definition

The ritual order took shape in the Zhou and was revised and reformed in every dynasty since. Its general tendency is from “ritual does not descend to the commoners” to “ritual descends to the commoners”, and from “state ceremony” to “daily use among the people”.

| Period | Principal codes and institutions | Key points for wedding and funeral |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zhou | *Rites of Zhou*, *Yili* | Establishing the Six Rites and the five degrees of mourning |
| Han | Shusun Tong’s ritual; *Baihu Tongyi* | Confucianisation of ritual; mourning regulation entering the statutes |
| Tang | *Great Tang Code of the Kaiyuan Reign* | Systematisation of wedding and funeral regulation for officials |
| Song | Sima Guang’s *Book of Ceremonies*; Zhu Xi’s *Family Rituals* | “Ritual descends to the commoners”; the rise of family rituals |
| Ming | *Collected Rites of the Ming* | Ritual for commoners and for lineage organisation |
| Qing | *Comprehensive Rituals of the Great Qing* | Fusion of Manchu and Han ritual; summation of the ritual order |

## 2. Historical Origins

- In the Han, Shusun Tong drew up court ceremonial for the dynasty, and the *Baihu Tongyi* fixed ritual institutions by the meaning of the classics.
- In the Wei, Jin and Northern and Southern Dynasties the ritual of the great houses proceeded alongside a mixing of Xianbei and Han practice.
- The Tang *Great Tang Code of the Kaiyuan Reign* is the earliest surviving systematic official ritual code, with fixed regulation for the weddings and funerals of ranked officials.
- In the Song the ritual order descended: the *Book of Ceremonies* and the *Family Rituals* replaced the official codes as the model for daily use among the people.
- In the Ming and Qing the ritual codes were brought together; the *Comprehensive Rituals of the Great Qing* is the summation of the traditional ritual order. In modern times the ritual order disintegrated along with the imperial system.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Making the ritual**: a decree commissioned Confucian officials to draft it on the meaning of the classics.
2. **Compiling the code**: it was gathered into an officially compiled ritual book and promulgated throughout the realm.
3. **Promulgation and promotion**: it was carried down level by level through government offices, schools and village covenants.
4. **Simplification in family rituals**: the gentry drew up family rituals on the model of the official code, reducing complexity so that it could be practised among the people.
5. **Popular reception**: popular ritual added and omitted within the framework of the codes, producing regional variants.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Han Chinese areas**: the ritual codes reached deeply into popular weddings and funerals.
- **Ethnic-minority areas**: practice follows the people’s own custom and tradition, and the influence of the Chinese codes varies from place to place; these must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: the dynastic ritual codes, family rituals, village covenants, and the ritual and custom sections of local gazetteers.
- **Objects**: ritual vessels, sacrificial vessels, caps and robes.
