# The Mourning Rites (Xiongli): An Outline

## 1. Definition

The mourning rites are those by which “the sorrows of the state are grieved”. Their content falls into five classes:

| Class | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| **Mourning (sang)** | Funeral rites, grieving death (**funerals belong here**) |
| **Famine (huang)** | Relieving disaster and famine, grieving calamity and epidemic |
| **Condolence (diao)** | Offering condolence for disaster and misfortune |
| **Exorcism (gui)** | Praying for blessing and averting calamity, driving out misfortune |
| **Relief (xu)** | Succouring the poor and the distressed |

Among the five, **the funeral rites** have the most elaborate ceremonial and the widest reach; they are the basis of the funeral book of this corpus.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Rites of Zhou* established the five classes of mourning rite, and the *Yili* preserves the three chapters “Mourning of a Knight”, “The Evening of the Burial” and “Sacrifice of a Knight”, in which the funeral ceremonial is set out in detail.
- The chapters “Great Records of Mourning”, “Interior Traditions” and “Questions on Mourning” in the *Liji* are devoted to funeral rite and mourning regulation.
- Dynastic ritual codes contain a section on mourning rites, as in the *Great Tang Code of the Kaiyuan Reign* and the *Comprehensive Rituals of the Great Qing*.
- The five degrees of mourning established in the “Mourning” chapter of the *Yili* became the fundamental basis of later mourning regulation.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

The chain of the funeral rites runs as follows:

1. **Initial mourning**: testing the breath, summoning the soul, washing the body, filling the mouth, dressing the dead.
2. **Encoffining**: minor dressing, major dressing, closing the coffin.
3. **Lying in state**: arranging the mourning hall, keeping vigil, morning and evening offerings, Buddhist or Daoist rites.
4. **Procession and burial**: raising the coffin, the procession, roadside offerings, lowering the coffin, raising the mound.
5. **Post-burial sacrifices**: the third day, the seven-day cycle, the hundred days, the anniversaries, the removal of mourning.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Han Chinese areas**: the details of funeral custom differ markedly from place to place; see “Han Regional Variants of Funeral Custom”.
- **Ethnic-minority areas**: modes of disposal are varied (earth, fire, sky, water, tree, stupa and cliff burial), and their funeral systems are not of the same origin as Han practice; see “Funeral Customs of the Ethnic Minorities”.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: obituaries, elegies, mourning announcements, epitaphs, mourning couplets.
- **Objects**: coffins and outer coffins, mingqi, paper offerings, name banners, mourning attire, mourning staves.
