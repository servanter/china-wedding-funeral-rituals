# Model Grave Inscription and Epitaph

## 1. Definition

The **grave inscription** (*mubeiwen*) stands before the grave, bearing the deceased's surname, taboo name, birth-death, age attained and the descendants who set the stone — brief in wording. The **epitaph** (*muzhiming*) is buried in the grave (or placed at its side); it has two parts — the "account" (prose, telling the lineage, life, death and burial) and the "inscription" (rhymed, grieving and praising virtue) — anciently called the "buried inscription".

**Format**: the inscription's name is centred on one line; both sides record birth-death and the stone-setters; the epitaph opens with the taboo name and lineage, then the life, ending with the inscription.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The Han saw the great rise of stone-setting; the inscription was often written and brushed by a master.
- Wei–Jin forbade lavish burial and stone-setting, hence the custom of burying the account in the grave, and the epitaph flourished.
- Tang–Song epitaphs peaked; Han Yu, Ouyang Xiu and others wrote them, the form settling.
- Ming–Qing grave inscriptions and epitaphs followed the old form; shape and layout regularised.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Write the account**: the family or a commissioned hand writes the epitaph and inscription.
2. **Brush the stone**: ask a good writer to brush the characters in vermilion on the stone.
3. **Carve**: the carver cuts following the brush.
4. **Choose a day to set the stone**: by the almanac, set the stone and cover with earth.
5. **Sacrifice and announce**: after setting, arrange viands and announce.
6. **Keep the rubbing**: keep a rubbing, into the genealogy or family collection.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the inscription is brief, valuing birth-death and descendants.
- **Jiangnan**: the epitaph is long, elegant in wording.
- **Fujian, Guangdong**: the stone adds "illustrious father/mother" and the ancestral place.
- **Ethnic minorities**: have stone-heaps, notched wood, hanging banners as markers, and some borrow the Han stone form; record separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: grave inscription, epitaph, life-account, mourning announcement.
- **Objects**: monument stone, epitaph cover, epitaph stone, vermilion ink, rubbing pad, rubbing paper.

**Model example (grave inscription)**:

```text
            The grave of the late father, the honoured ○○, styled ○○

  The gentleman was born ○ month ○ day, ○ year, died ○ month ○ day, ○ year,
  aged ○○ years.
  Buried on the south slope of ○○ mountain, facing ○, sitting ○.

        Filial son ○○   Filial daughter ○○
        Filial grandson ○○        set the stone

              ○ year ○ month ○ day
```

**Model example (epitaph)**:

```text
[Account]
  The gentleman styled ○○, of ○○. Great-grandfather styled ○, grandfather styled ○, father styled ○.
  The gentleman was of pure nature, diligent in keeping the house, praised by the village.
  He married the ○ clan; had two sons and one daughter.
  He died ○ month ○ day, ○ year, aged ○○, buried at ○○.

[Inscription]
  High the mountain, long the river; the virtuous voice not forgotten;
  engraved on the stone in the dark chamber, to show the later generation forever.
```
