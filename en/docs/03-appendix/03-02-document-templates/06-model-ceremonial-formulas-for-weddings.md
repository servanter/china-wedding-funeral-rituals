# Model Ceremonial Formulas for Weddings

## 1. Definition

The ceremonial formula (*zanlici*), also "master-of-ceremony words" or "ritualist's chant", is the words chanted by the master of ceremonies (the general chanter, the leading chanter, the ritualist) at each stage of the wedding. Mostly in four-character lines and parallel couplets, it values a clear rhythm and auspicious phrasing, and both directs the rite's progress and builds the mood.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* "Ceremony of a Scholar's Wedding" sets the office of "chanter" (*zan*), assisting the host to perform the rite and relay the words — the origin of the ritualist.
- From the Tang and Song, the family rites fixed the division of "general chanter" and "leading chanter", chanting the name at each stage.
- In the Ming and Qing, the village rites and ancestral-hall rites valued the ritualist's chant; encyclopaedias carried the formulas.
- In modern times the master of ceremonies inherits the ritualist's office; the formulas turn vernacular but keep set phrases like "bow to heaven and earth".

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

The formulas are chanted in the wedding's temporal order:

1. **Open the door, welcome the bride**: "Auspicious day and fine hour, joy fills the gate; pray the new couple enter the hall."
2. **Bow in the hall**: "First bow to heaven and earth, again to the elders, the couple bow to each other; the rite is done."
3. **Join the cups**: "Eat of the same stall, drink of the joined cups; the couple are one, forever of one heart."
4. **Scatter the bed**: "Scatter the bed east, scatter the bed west; soon a noble son, five boys and two girls."
5. **Lift the veil**: "The joy-weigh lifts the veil, just as the heart wishes."
6. **Rite done, see off guests**: "The rite is done! Pray the honoured guests be seated."

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the formulas are brief, valuing the "calling of the rite".
- **Jiangnan**: the formulas are elegant, mostly four-six prose.
- **Fujian–Taiwan**: the master chants in dialect, mixing auspicious folk sayings.
- **Ethnic minorities**: mostly use song-contest, recitative and sacrificial words in place of ceremonial formulas (e.g., the Miao "bride-receiving song", the Hezhen *Imanakan*); record separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: the ceremonial order, the officers' list, the gift list.
- **Objects**: the joy-weigh, the joined-cups, the bed-scattering fruits.

**Model example**:

```text
[Entering the hall]
  Auspicious day and fine hour, joy fills the gate;
  the new couple enter the hall, a hundred blessings gather.

[Bowing in the hall]
  First bow to heaven and earth, grateful for their covering and bearing;
  again bow to the elders, thanking the parents' toil;
  the couple bow to each other, binding a hundred years' good union.

[Joining the cups]
  Eat of the same stall, drink of the joined cups;
  the couple are one, forever of one heart.

[Rite done]
  The rite is done! Respectfully pray the honoured guests be seated.
```
