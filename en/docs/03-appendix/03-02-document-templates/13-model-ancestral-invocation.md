# Model Ancestral Invocation

## 1. Definition

The **ancestral invocation** (*jizu zhuwen*, short "invocation") is the text read when sacrificing to the ancestors, used at the seasonal sacrifice (the four seasons of spring, summer, autumn, winter), the death-day sacrifice, the grave sacrifice and the temple report (reporting a wedding or funeral to the ancestors). Its essence is to announce the season, set out the offerings, tell the reason and pray for protection.

**Format**: first "on ○ year ○ month ○ day", then the principal sacrificer's "filial grandson"/"filial son" and the offerings ("seasonal viands, clear wine"), then the reason of sacrifice, ending with "may you enjoy".

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* "Single Victim at the Feast of the Living" and "Lesser Victim at the Feast" carry the ancestral sacrificial words — the source of the invocation.
- The *Liji* "Record of Sacrifice" expounds the meaning of sacrificing to ancestors — "repay the root, turn back to the beginning".
- Zhu Xi's *Family Rites* fixed the four-season sacrifice rite and invocation format, the basis of later family sacrifice.
- Ming–Qing family rites and instructions followed the form; the seasonal invocation format regularised.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Set the viands**: by season, arrange the牲醴 grain and humble viands.
2. **Take position**: the principal sacrificer and co-sacrificers take position by generation.
3. **Offer incense**: the principal sacrificer offers incense and kneels in obeisance.
4. **Kneel and read the invocation**: the principal sacrificer kneels and reads the invocation.
5. **Present wine**: after the three presentations, pour wine on the ground.
6. **Burn the invocation to dismiss the spirit**: burn the invocation, perform the dismissing-spirit rite.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the invocation is brief, valuing the spring and autumn sacrifices.
- **Jiangnan**: the ancestral-hall spring and autumn sacrifices are solemn; the invocation is written by a clansman.
- **Fujian, Guangdong**: the hall sacrifice is frequent; the invocation is read in dialect.
- **Ethnic minorities**: mostly have ancestral words chanted in their own language (e.g., the Zhuang "singing the ancestors", the Jino "big-drum sacrifice words"), a different source from the Han invocation; record separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: invocation, family genealogy, sacrifice book.
- **Objects**: incense altar, candle stands,牲醴, grain vessels, wine vessels, the invocation-burning basin.

**Model example (Qingming grave invocation)**:

```text
On ○ year ○ month ○ day, the filial grandson ○○ respectfully with
the seasonal viands and a libation of clear wine
sacrifices before the grave of
    the successive ancestors of the ○ clan, saying:

    Now it is Qingming; the air is clear, the scene bright.
    Recalling the ancestors' virtue, our longing grows deeper.
    We humbly present this poor offering; pray you look down and accept.

    May you enjoy!
```

**Model example (New-Year family invocation)**:

```text
On ○ year ○ month ○ day, the filial son ○○ respectfully with
clear wine and humble viands
dares to declare before the spirits of
    the illustrious father ○○ and the illustrious mother, née ○○:

    The year's order renews; we chase the far and repay the root.
    Respectfully we offer this sacrifice; pray you come and enjoy.

    May you enjoy!
```

## 6. Sources

- *Liji*, "Record of Sacrifice"
- Song, Zhu Xi: *Family Rites* (the *Wengong Family Rites*)
- Folk fieldwork materials
