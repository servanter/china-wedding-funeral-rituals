# Model Elegy

## 1. Definition

The elegy (*jiwen*) is the text read when sacrificing to the dead or to the spirits. By occasion it divides into family elegy, public elegy, seasonal elegy and death-day elegy; in style mostly prose, or four-character, *sao*-style or parallel prose. Its essence is to state the virtue, tell the grief and present the offering.

**Format**: first "on ○ year ○ month ○ day", then the principal sacrificer (filial son / so-and-so) and the offering ("a libation of clear wine and humble viands"), then the deceased's name and the grief, ending with "may you enjoy".

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* "Ceremony of a Scholar's Mourning" and "The Next Day" have the "invocation" words and the rites of "reading the gift" and "reading the sent" — the ancestor of the elegy.
- Han–Wei favoured "lamentations" and "elegies"; Pan Yue's *Lament for the Far Journey* is famous.
- Tang–Song Han Yu's *Sacrifice to the Twelfth Nephew* and Ouyang Xiu's *Sacrifice to Shi Manying* are models of prose elegy.
- Zhu Xi's *Family Rites* fixed the family-sacrifice "three-presentation rite" with reading the elegy; the Ming and Qing followed.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Make the text**: prepare the elegy beforehand, copied fair in regular script.
2. **Set the viands**: arrange the offerings, incense and candles.
3. **Offer incense**: the principal sacrificer offers incense and kneels in obeisance.
4. **Three presentations**: first, second and final presentation, each takes position.
5. **Kneel and read the elegy**: the principal sacrificer kneels and reads; the kin raise their grief.
6. **Burn the text**: after reading, burn it, or burn with the paper money.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: mostly four-character, brief.
- **Jiangnan**: long prose, with detailed narrative.
- **Fujian, Guangdong**: keeps parallel prose, with dialect rhymes.
- **Ethnic minorities**: mostly chant in their own language (e.g., the Yi "road-guiding scripture", the Jingpo "soul-sending words"), a different system from the Han elegy; record separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: elegy, mourning announcement, life-account, mourning couplet.
- **Objects**: incense altar, offering vessels, paper money, the text-burning basin.

**Model example (family elegy)**:

```text
On ○ year ○ month ○ day, the filial son ○○ respectfully with
a libation of clear wine and humble viands
dares to declare before the spirit of
    the late father, the honoured ○○, styled ○○:

    Alas! The late father, departing our care, suddenly ○○ days gone.
    Recalling his life: thrifty in keeping the house, filial and friendly to neighbours,
    teaching his children; the grace hard to forget.
    Now before the spirit we present this offering, fully stating our grief;
    pray you look down and accept.

    May you enjoy!
```
