# Direct and Collateral Terms Compared

## 1. Definition

**Direct** (*zhixi*) kin are blood relatives in one unbroken line (grandfather—father—ego—son—grandson); **collateral** (*pangxi*) kin share a source but not a branch (uncles, brothers, hall-nephews, etc.). The same relation has different terms in the two directions "upward from below" and "downward from above", and must be grasped by comparison.

| Relation | Ego calls them (below to above) | They call ego (above to below) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Father's elder brother | Paternal uncle elder (*bofu*) | Nephew (*zhi*) |
| Father's younger brother | Paternal uncle younger (*shufu*) | Nephew (*zhi*) |
| Brother's son | Nephew (*zhizi*) | Uncle elder/younger (*bo*/*shu*) |
| Father's sister | Paternal aunt (*gumu*) | Nephew (*zhi*) |
| Mother's brother | Maternal uncle (*jiefu*) | Outer nephew (*waisheng*) |
| Husband's father | Father-in-law (*gonggong*, also *jiu*) | Daughter-in-law (*xi*) |
| Wife's father | Father-in-law (*yuefu*) | Son-in-law (*xu*) |

The line between direct and collateral directly decides the five-degree grading: direct kin bear heavier mourning, collateral lighter, and lighter still the more distant.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Yili* "Mourning Garments" distinguishes direct and collateral mourning grades by the three principles of "cut upward, cut downward, cut sideways".
- The *Erya* "Explaining Kin" already shows directional terms such as "nephew" (*zhi*) and "nephew-by-sister" (*sheng*).
- The Ming–Qing family rites systematically prescribed the "above-to-below" and "below-to-above" terms.
- Today the directional terms (nephew, outer nephew, daughter-in-law, son-in-law) are still fully used.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Tell direct from collateral**: first settle whether direct or collateral.
2. **Fix the direction**: separate "below to above" (honorific) from "above to below" (humble).
3. **Put into documents**: the self-reference in invitations and obituaries must observe direction (e.g., an obituary signed "orphan son").
4. **Order the seats**: seating and ritual order — direct before collateral, elder before junior.
5. **Upgrade the term**: after a son is born, the elder generation's term for ego upgrades (e.g., "brother" rises to "uncle").

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: collateral is clearly split into "hall" and "table".
- **South China**: directional terms often add "a" (a-uncle, a-nephew).
- **Ethnic areas**: the uncle–nephew and aunt–nephew relations tie terms to rights (maternal-uncle authority, paternal-aunt authority); record separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: the nine-generation five-degree chart, the descent table, invitations, obituaries.
- **Objects**: spirit tablets (whose inscriptions must distinguish direct/collateral and elder/junior).
