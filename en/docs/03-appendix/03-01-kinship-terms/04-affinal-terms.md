# Affinal Terms: the Wife's Kin

## 1. Definition

Wife's kin (*qiqin*, affinal kin) are relatives created by the marriage tie, covering both the wife's family and the husband's family.

| Relation | Term |
| --- | --- |
| Wife's father | Father-in-law (*yuefu*, also *taishan*, *zhangren*) |
| Wife's mother | Mother-in-law (*yuemu*, also *taishui*, *zhangmuniang*) |
| Wife's elder/younger brother | Elder/younger brother-in-law (*neixiong*/*neidi*, also *dajiuzi*, *xiaojiuzi*) |
| Wife's elder/younger sister | Elder/younger sister-in-law (*dayizi*/*xiaoyizi*, also *yijie*, *yimei*) |
| Elder brother's wife / younger brother's wife | Sister-in-law / brother's wife (*sao*/*difu*, calling each other *zhouliao*) |
| Sister's husband | Brother-in-law (*jiefu*/*meifu*, also *lianjin*, colloquially "one-shoulder-pole") |
| Daughter's husband | Son-in-law (*nüxu*, called "half a son") |
| Son's wife | Daughter-in-law (*xifu*, also *erxi*) |
| Parents of two affines | Co-parents-in-law (*qinjia*, *qinjiagong*, *qin jiamu*) |

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Erya*, in its "Wife's Party" and "Marriage" chapters of "Explaining Kin", records wife's-family terms (outer father-in-law, outer mother-in-law, etc.).
- In the Han and Tang the proper terms for the wife's parents were "outer father-in-law" (*waijiu*) and "outer mother-in-law" (*waigu*); from the Song and Yuan "father-in-law" (*yuefu*) and "mother-in-law" (*yuemu*) gradually spread.
- The term "Mount Tai" comes from the Tang Duan Chengshi's *Youyang Zazu*, in the story of Emperor Xuanzong's fengshan sacrifice.
- After the Ming and Qing, "elder gentleman" (*zhangren*) and "elder lady" (*zhangmu*) became colloquial; today "dad" and "mum" are often used as well.
- Contemporary affinal terms tend to simplify; "co-parents-in-law" (*qinjia*) remains the common mutual term.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Negotiate the match**: the go-between shuttles between the families; the two sets of parents call each other "co-parents-in-law".
2. **Perform rites**: at betrothal and bride-pickup, affines exchange the "affinal gift".
3. **Change the address**: after marriage the bride calls the husband's parents "dad" and "mum", and the son-in-law does likewise.
4. **Acknowledge kin**: after marriage, "acknowledge kin" and "visit kin" — affines pay calls in order.
5. **Keep up visits**: at New Year, birth and funeral, affines visit by fixed rules — "affinal kin are not abandoned".

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the son-in-law is called "young gentleman" (*guye*), treated as half a son.
- **Jiangnan**: affinal visiting follows the "visit-kin" seasonal custom.
- **Fujian, Guangdong**: the mother's family (*waijia*) is weighty; the wedding feast sets a "mother's-family seat".
- **Ethnic minorities**: in uxorilocal marriage (Monba, Blang, De'ang, etc.) the husband lives in the wife's home, and the wife's-family terms dominate; record separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: marriage contract, gift list, invitation (signed "affinal younger brother", "kinsman younger brother", etc.).
- **Objects**: betrothal gifts and dowry (the physical proof of affinal exchange).
