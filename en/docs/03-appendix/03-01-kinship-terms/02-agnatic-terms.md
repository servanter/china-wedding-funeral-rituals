# Agnatic Terms: the Paternal Nine Generations

## 1. Definition

Agnatic kin (*zongqin*) are patrilineal relatives of the same ancestry and lineage. Centred on "ego" (*jishen*), four generations upward and four downward make nine generations in all — the so-called "nine generations" (*jiuzu*).

| Generation | Term | Relation to ego | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4th up | High grandfather (*gaozu wangfu*) | father's father's father's father | "gaozu" |
| 3rd up | Great-grandfather (*zengzu wangfu*) | father's father's father | "zengzu" |
| 2nd up | Grandfather (*zufu*) | father's father | "wangfu" |
| 1st up | Father (*fu*) | — | "yanqin" |
| Ego | — | — | — |
| 1st down | Son (*zi*) | — | the eldest son is the "lineage heir" (*zongzi*) |
| 2nd down | Grandson (*sun*) | — | — |
| 3rd down | Great-grandson (*zengsun*) | — | — |
| 4th down | Great-great-grandson (*xuansun*) | — | the end of the nine generations |

**Collateral kin**: father's elder brother is *bofu*, father's younger brother *shufu*; those of the same grandfather are "tang brothers" (*congfu xiongdi*), of the same great-grandfather "again-tang brothers" (*zaicong xiongdi*), of the same high-grandfather "third-tang brothers" (*sancong xiongdi*). Father's sister is *gu* (aunt) and belongs to the agnatic kin; her children are "aunt's-table cousins" (*gubiao*), whose mourning grade falls under maternal kin.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Erya*, in its "Clan" chapter of "Explaining Kin", lists the generational terms from high grandfather down to great-great-grandson.
- The *Yili* "Mourning Garments" fixes the five degrees by the nine generations, and agnatic terms correspond one-to-one with the mourning grades.
- From the Han and Tang onward there were two accounts of the "nine generations": "the nine generations of one lineage" and "father's four, mother's three, wife's two".
- In the Ming and Qing, genealogies flourished, and agnatic terms solidified with clan organisation.
- After the modern dissolution of the clans, most people know only down to the great-grandfather; above that they generally say "old ancestor" (*laozu*).

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Order the generations**: determine rank by the genealogy's generation characters; the term follows (which branch, which generation).
2. **Distinguish elder and junior**: *bo* (father's elder brother), *shu* (father's younger brother), tang/cousin brothers, in turn called *tang*, *cong*, *zu*.
3. **Perform the four rites**: at capping, marriage, mourning and sacrifice, observe the mourning grades and seating by the five degrees, and write them into invitations and obituaries.
4. **Name the spirit tablet**: ancestral tablets are inscribed by generation and taboo name.
5. **Tend graves through the year**: at Qingming, Zhongyuan and the Winter Solstice, order the sacrifices by generation.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China, Shanxi**: the *bo*/*shu* ranking is clear; "eldest uncle", "second uncle" are common forms.
- **Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Huizhou**: the clan organisation is strict; branch and hall names are complete.
- **Fujian, Guangdong, Hakka**: archaic terms such as *agong* (grandfather), *ashu* (uncle) survive.
- **Ethnic minorities**: those influenced by the Han system (e.g., Zhuang, Manchu) have both agnatic terms and lineage-specific terms; record them separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: genealogy (descent chart, rank order, taboo characters), spirit tablets, ancestral-hall notices.
- **Charts**: the lineage's nine-generation five-degree chart, the branch chart.
- **Objects**: ancestral-hall sacrificial vessels, the genealogy chest.
