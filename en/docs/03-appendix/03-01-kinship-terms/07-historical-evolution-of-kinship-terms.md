# Historical Evolution of Kinship Terms

## 1. Definition

Kinship terms change with family structure, social system and language shift: some refined, some vulgarised, some merged, some died out.

| Today | Antiquity | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Father | *kao*, *yanqin*, *daren* | "die/ba" replaced the written "kao" |
| Mother | *bi*, *ciqin* | "niang/ma" became current |
| Father-in-law | *waijiu* | after Song–Yuan "yuefu" replaced it |
| Wife's mother | *waigu* | "yuemu" replaced it |
| Husband's elder brother | *xionggong* | "dabaizi" became colloquial |
| Hall brothers | *congyi xiongdi* | "tang" replaced "cong" |
| Table brothers | *wai xiongdi* | "biao" unified the term |
| Daughter-in-law | *zifu* | "xifu" became current |

**Two great trends**: first, written elegant terms are replaced by spoken ones; second, generational terms contract as the clan dissolves — today people commonly know only down to the great-grandfather.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the *Erya* "Explaining Kin" established the classical term system.
- **Han–Tang**: "outer father-in-law", "outer mother-in-law", "husband's elder brother" were the formal terms.
- **Song–Yuan**: market-place spoken terms (elder gentleman, father-in-law, husband) rose to common use.
- **Ming–Qing**: family rites and genealogies ran side by side; terms appeared as two systems, elegant and vulgar.
- **Modern**: clan dissolution, Mandarin promotion and one-child families greatly simplified the terms.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Tell old from new**: choose ancient or modern terms by the document's nature (obituaries use the elegant ancient terms, daily life the modern).
2. **Fix the level**: clan occasions use generational terms, daily life the spoken ones.
3. **Record the oral**: fieldwork must record term differences across age groups.
4. **Mark the shift**: when a genealogy is continued, note the correspondence of past and present terms.
5. **Keep the ancient**: halls and sacrifice occasions keep the ancient terms (e.g., "illustrious father", "illustrious mother").

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Cities**: mostly "ba", "ma", "father-in-law", "mother-in-law".
- **Countryside**: keeps old terms like "die", "niang", "elder gentleman".
- **Fujian, Guangdong, Hakka**: keeps many ancient terms (elder sister, younger brother, granny).
- **Ethnic minorities**: their own terms used alongside Chinese ones, with clear generational differences; record separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: genealogies, obituaries (using ancient terms like "illustrious father", "late mother"), elegies.
- **Objects**: spirit tablets (using ancient characters).
