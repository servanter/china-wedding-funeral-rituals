# Kinship Terms: An Overview

![亲属称谓三大系统图 / The three kinship term systems](../../../../assets/diagrams/亲属称谓三大系统图.svg)

*Figure 9. The three systems of kinship terms: agnatic kin, maternal kin and afinal kin.*

## 1. Definition

Traditional Chinese kinship terms take three systems as their skeleton — **agnatic kin** (*zongqin*, kin of the same patrilineal descent group), **maternal kin** (*waiqin*, kin of the mother’s family) and **afinal kin** (*qiqin*, kin of the wife’s family) — and then cut across them along the dimensions of direct or collateral, senior or junior, living or deceased, and consanguineal or affinal, producing a tightly layered network of address.

| System | Range | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Agnatic kin** | The paternal nine generations (from great-great-grandfather to great-great-grandson) | Father’s brothers, brothers, male cousins |
| **Maternal kin** | The mother’s family (maternal grandparents, maternal uncles and aunts, and their children) | Maternal grandfather, maternal uncle, maternal aunt’s son |
| **Afinal kin** | The wife’s family (parents-in-law, wife’s brothers) | Father-in-law, wife’s elder brother, co-brother-in-law |

The *Erya*, in its “Explaining Kin” chapter, already lists four classes — clan, mother’s party, wife’s party and marriage relations — which is the origin of the traditional classification.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The “Explaining Kin” chapter of the *Erya* divides kin into clan, mother’s party, wife’s party and marriage relations, establishing the framework of classification.
- The *Shiming*, in its “Explaining Kin” chapter, explains how each term arose by sound-gloss.
- The *Baihu Tongyi* interprets kin relations through lineage law, stressing “affection for kin, reverence for the honoured”.
- From the Tang and Song onwards, family rituals, family instructions and reference compendia (the *Comprehensive Institutions*, the *Taiping Imperial Reader*) continued to add to the system.
- Zheng Zhen’s *Record of Kinship* and Liang Zhangju’s *Record of Appellations*, both of the Qing, brought the system to its fullest statement; modern scholars have reorganised it with anthropological methods.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

The use of a term is determined by three conditions together — occasion, addressee and status — in the following order:

1. **Establish the relation**: first determine whether the person is agnatic, maternal or afinal kin, then whether direct or collateral, senior or junior.
2. **Choose the term**: address to a person’s face and reference in speaking of them to others differ, and written usage employs further honorifics and self-deprecating forms.
3. **Write it into documents**: the terms used in horoscope cards, marriage contracts, obituaries and elegies must follow the conventions of layout and of mourning grade; in an obituary the bereaved designate themselves “gu zi”, “ai zi” and so on.
4. **Revise with status**: marriage, childbirth, bereavement, adoption out of the family or uxorilocal marriage all bring consequential adjustments to terms and to mourning obligations.
5. **Change address in time**: the change of address after marriage, from “uncle” to “father”, is a popular re-confirmation of the relationship.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North and north-west China**: agnatic terms are strictly ordered, and the ranking of paternal uncles is clear (eldest uncle, second uncle).
- **Jiangsu, Zhejiang and Huizhou**: lineages are strong, terms carry the colour of lineage law, and generational rank in the ancestral hall is strictly observed.
- **Fujian, Guangdong and Hakka areas**: more archaic terms survive, such as *agong* (grandfather), *apo* (grandmother), *jiajie* (elder sister), *xilao* (younger brother).
- **Ethnic-minority areas of the south-west**: the authority of the maternal uncle is pronounced, and the term for him rises accordingly (see “Maternal Kin Terms”).

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: horoscope cards, marriage contracts, obituaries, elegies and genealogies, whose backbone is the terminology of kin and descent.
- **Diagrams**: the chart of the nine generations and the five degrees of mourning; descent charts.
- **Objects**: ancestral tablets, whose inscriptions follow descent, term of address and posthumous title.
