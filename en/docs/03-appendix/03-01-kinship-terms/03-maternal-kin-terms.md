# Maternal Kin Terms

## 1. Definition

Maternal kin (*waiqin*) are the mother's family (her parents, brothers, sisters and their children) and the kin reached through a married sister's husband's family. Their terms mostly carry the characters "outer" (*wai*) or "table" (*biao*) to distinguish them from agnatic kin.

| Kin | Term | Ego's term for them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mother's father | Outer grandfather (*waizufu*, also *waigong*, *laoye*) | outer grandson |
| Mother's mother | Outer grandmother (*waizumu*, also *waipo*, *laolao*) | outer grandson |
| Mother's brother | Maternal uncle (*jiefu*, also *jiujiu*) | outer nephew |
| Mother's sister | Maternal aunt (*yimu*, also *yima*) | aunt's nephew |
| Maternal uncle's children | Cousins via maternal uncle (*jiubiao*) | table cousins |
| Maternal aunt's children | Cousins via maternal aunt (*yibiao*) | table cousins |
| Sister's children | Outer nephew, outer niece (*waisheng*, *waishengnü*) | uncle, aunt |

**The difference between "table" and "hall"**: those of the same grandfather are "hall" (*tang*) kin, those not of the same grandfather but connected are "table" (*biao*); hence cousins via maternal uncle, maternal aunt and paternal aunt are all called "table kin" (*biaoqin*).

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Erya*, in its "Mother's Party" chapter of "Explaining Kin", records maternal-kin terms (outer grandfather, outer grandmother, maternal uncle, aunt-by-marriage, etc.).
- In the five degrees, maternal kin bear the lightest mourning: small-gong for maternal grandparents, *simao* for maternal uncle and aunt; the Tang *Kaiyuan Rites* slightly elevated them.
- After the Ming and Qing the maternal-kin mourning grew even lighter, yet folk custom valued their rites because of "maternal-uncle authority".
- Today, in one-child families, the maternal-grandparent terms (*waigong*, *laolao*) are used frequently and their real standing has risen.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Acknowledge kin**: on first meeting, address by the mother's family order — maternal uncle before maternal aunt.
2. **Perform rites**: at weddings and funerals maternal kin send the "outer gift", seated after agnatic kin.
3. **Separate "table" and "hall"**: wedding seating and invitation signatures must distinguish "hall" from "table".
4. **Shifting terms**: after a table cousin marries, their spouse is called "cousin's wife" (*biaosao*), "cousin's younger brother's wife" (*bidifu*).
5. **Maternal-uncle occasions**: in the south-west, the maternal uncle presides at weddings and funerals (see "Regional and Ethnic Variants").

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: "table" is common; "cousins via paternal aunt" are most valued.
- **South-western peoples (Yi, Naxi, Pumi, Nu, Achang)**: maternal-uncle authority is pronounced; the uncle holds the main seat at weddings and funerals, and a nephew's marriage needs the uncle's consent.
- **Fujian, Guangdong**: the mother's family (*waijia*) has greater say in matchmaking.
- **Record separately**: the maternal-kin systems of the peoples, centred on maternal-uncle authority, are not the same source as the Han system.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: invitations, gift lists (separating "hall" and "table").
- **Objects**: the gift-carrying pole and joyous banners sent by the mother's family.
