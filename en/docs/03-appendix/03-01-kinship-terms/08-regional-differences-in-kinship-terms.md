# Regional Differences in Kinship Terms

## 1. Definition

Chinese kinship terms differ markedly across dialect regions in pronunciation, word-formation and character choice; many are same in meaning but different in form.

| Object | Mandarin (Beijing) | Wu | Cantonese | Southern Min | Hakka |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Grandfather | yeye | adie/gonggong | aye | agong | agong |
| Grandmother | nainai | anai/qinniang | amah | amah | apuo |
| Father | baba | aba/ye | laodou/aba | laoba | aba |
| Mother | mama | mma/niang | laomu/ama | laomu | amu |
| Maternal grandmother | laolao | waipo | popo/waipo | waima | jiepo |
| Wife | xifu/laopo | niangzi/laopo | laopo/taotai | qianshou | laopo |
| Daughter | guinü | nonong | nü | chamoujian | meizai |

## 2. Historical Origins

- Archaic-Chinese terms split by dialect, forming distinct term layers.
- From the Tang and Song, migration waves (the gentry's move south, Fujian–Guangdong emigration) stranded ancient terms in the southern dialects.
- In the Ming and Qing, the *Yueyan* and various dialect works began systematic record.
- In modern times, dialectology has systematically surveyed and mapped kinship terms.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Fix the survey point**: choose representative points by dialect region.
2. **Fix the lexicon**: build a basic word list from core terms (grandparents, parents, wife, children).
3. **Note the sound**: transcribe with IPA, noting the original or colloquial character.
4. **Note the usage**: distinguish face-address from reference-address, elder from younger.
5. **Note the shift**: mark the old and new readings produced by Mandarin influence.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Mandarin area**: terms converge on Mandarin.
- **Wu area**: many "a-" prefixes; terms are soft.
- **Cantonese area**: keeps ancient terms like "laodou", "aye".
- **Fujian–Taiwan**: terms tie to clan and hall name.
- **Hakka**: keeps the most Central-Plain ancient terms.
- **Ethnic-minority areas**: Chinese dialect terms and ethnic-language terms interact; record separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: dialect dictionaries, local gazetteers' dialect sections, kinship-term survey sheets.
- **Objects**: recording equipment, survey notebooks (for fieldwork).

## 6. Sources

- Qing, Fan Yin: *Yueyan*
- Works on each dialect (*Hakka Dialect*, etc.)
- *Language Atlas of China* (phonology volume, lexicon volume)
- Provincial gazetteers' dialect sections
- Folk fieldwork materials
