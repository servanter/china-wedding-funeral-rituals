# Choosing an Auspicious Wedding Date (Yellow-Road Days and the Zhoutang Chart)

## 1. Definition

Choosing an auspicious wedding date (“looking at days”, “choosing a day”, “picking a day”) takes the **yellow-road auspicious days** as its principle, and must avoid days clashing with the birth data of the couple.

**The principal methods**:

| Item | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **Yellow road / black road** | The twelve day-officers (establish, remove, full, level, fix, hold, break, danger, complete, receive, open, close) and the six yellow-road spirits (Green Dragon, Bright Hall, Golden Coffer, Heavenly Virtue, Jade Hall, Keeper of Destinies) |
| **The zhoutang chart** | Proceeding day by day through the eight trigram positions “husband, mother-in-law, hall, father-in-law, brother, servant, wife, kitchen”; falling on a good position (“husband”, “hall”, “brother”) is suitable, on “father-in-law”, “mother-in-law”, “wife” or “servant” to be avoided |
| **General rules for good days** | Days marked “suitable for marriage” in the almanac; “monthly destruction”, “annual destruction”, the “four separations and four terminations” and “Lord Yang’s prohibitions” are avoided |
| **The hour** | The reception is usually set in the chen or si hours (morning); the afternoon is avoided |
| **Days matched to the “three letters and six rites”** | An auspicious day is chosen for nacai, nazheng, qingqi and qinying separately |

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Han**: the art of choosing days appears in the *Day Books*; the suitability and unsuitability of weddings was taking shape.
- **Tang and Song**: almanacs and calendars spread, and date selection became a general practice of wedding custom.
- **Qing**: the *Xieji bianfang shu* was the official basis for choosing days; the *Yuxia ji* was the almanac commonly used among the people.
- **Modern times**: almanacs (the yellow calendar) are still widely used.
- **Today**: town and country mostly consult the yellow calendar for a day “suitable for marriage”, or choose a holiday or anniversary; wedding companies and “date-choosing” services run side by side.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Reporting the birth data**: the couple give their dates and hours of birth to the date-chooser.
2. **Reckoning the day**: the almanac and the zhoutang chart are used, avoiding clashes and destructions.
3. **Issuing the date slip**: the “date card” is sent to the bride’s house.
4. **Performing the rites on time**: each ceremony proceeds on the appointed day.
5. **Accommodation**: where a date cannot be avoided, methods of “counteraction” are used (pasting charms, choosing an hour that avoids the clash).

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the *Yuxia ji* system is mostly used, with insistence on the “zhoutang chart”.
- **Jiangnan**: the yellow calendar is consulted for “suitable for marriage”, and a “yellow-road auspicious hour” chosen.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: “looking at days” is done through a date shop, combining the “zhoutang” and the “marriage almanac”.
- **Ethnic minorities**: each people chooses days on different grounds (its own calendar, divination, the phases of the moon); these do not belong to the Han almanac system and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Basis for choosing**: the *Xieji bianfang shu*, the *Yuxia ji*, the yellow almanac, the zhoutang chart.
- **Documents**: the date card (the card of the wedding date), the day slip.
- **Objects**: the calendar, the compass (for choosing directions).
