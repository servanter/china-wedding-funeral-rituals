# Wedding Meeting Wedding, and Clashing with Another Bridal Sedan

## 1. Definition

“**Joy clashing with joy**” is the taboo idea that two “joys” colliding turn to ill fortune. It has two main manifestations:

1. **Two bridal chairs (wedding cars) meeting** — two reception parties meeting on the road, popularly “clashing sedans”, held to produce “one joy pressing down another”, which must be **counteracted**:
   - the two brides exchange “joy gifts” (handkerchiefs, flowers, wedding sweets);
   - or their attendants throw wedding fruit at one another and exchange good wishes;
   - or one party goes round by another way.
2. **A pregnant woman clashing with the joy** — a pregnant woman (the “four-eyed person”) meeting the bride, touching the dowry or sitting on the bridal bed is held to “clash with the joy” and bring misfortune, and must withdraw.

**The underlying idea**: “joy” is conceived as an **energy that can be transmitted and can also clash**; hence weddings insist on “not colliding” — to collide is inauspicious, and must be reconciled by exchange, withdrawal or counteraction.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Tang and Song**: the idea of “avoiding clashes” in wedding custom appears in the notebooks.
- **Ming and Qing**: the gazetteers commonly record the custom that “when bridal chairs meet, joy gifts are exchanged”.
- **Modern times**: bridal chairs gave way to carts, carriages and cars, and “clashing sedans” became “wedding cars meeting”; the exchange of joy gifts (wedding sweets, red flowers) survives in some regions.
- **Today**: city weddings largely ignore it; the countryside still practises “exchanging joy gifts”.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Foreknowledge**: before the reception it is established whether another wedding is taking place in the same village or street.
2. **Counteracting a meeting**: if the chairs (or cars) meet, both stop and exchange joy gifts and good wishes.
3. **Making way**: one party goes round, or the two set out at different times.
4. **Avoiding pregnant women**: a pregnant woman withdraws from the bride, does not touch the dowry and does not sit on the bridal bed.
5. **Counteracting afterwards**: if a clash occurs by accident, wedding fruit is scattered and firecrackers set off to undo it.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: when bridal chairs meet, “handkerchiefs” and “joy flowers” are exchanged.
- **Jiangnan**: wedding sweets and fruit are exchanged, with good wishes.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: when reception arrays meet, there are commonly the rites of “exchanging flowers” and “firing crackers”.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples have comparable “rites of meeting” (as the exchange of tokens); these must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: joy gifts (handkerchiefs, joy flowers, wedding sweets, wedding fruit), firecrackers.
- **Documents**: the reception route sheet, the day slip.
- **Textual basis**: the “Customs” sections of the provincial gazetteers; the *Yuxia ji*.
