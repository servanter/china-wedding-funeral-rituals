# Behavioural Taboos at Weddings

## 1. Definition

Taboos on behaviour at weddings rest on **homophony** and **symbol**, forming a whole set of rules for “seeking the good and avoiding the ill”.

| Taboo | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| **Do not fan oneself** | *Shan* “fan” sounds like *san* “to scatter”, which is avoided |
| **Do not use an umbrella** | *San* “umbrella” likewise sounds like “to scatter”; also “blocking out heaven” is avoided |
| **Do not break objects** | A smashed bowl or dish is a “break” — “a broken mirror”, “a broken house” are avoided |
| **Do not say “scatter”, “part”, “divide”, “finished”, “gone”** | Verbal taboo |
| **Do not set the chair down midway** | “The chair does not touch the ground”; to touch it is inauspicious |
| **The bride must not look back** | “Looking back” betokens “calling off the marriage” |
| **Do not tread on the threshold** | The threshold is the “limit of the door”; “being unable to pass” is avoided |
| **Do not leave the bridal chamber empty** | On the wedding night the bridal chamber must not be without people (“no empty room”) |
| **Pregnant women must not sit on the bridal bed; mourners must not enter the chamber** | “To clash with the joy” is avoided |
| **Do not blow out the wedding candles** | Blowing them out is “breaking off” |
| **Do not use odd numbers** | An odd number is “alone”; hence gifts and items are in pairs |

**Counteraction**: if an object is broken by accident, one must say “peace year after year” (a pun on “shattered”) to undo it; if a forbidden word is spoken, one says “*pei pei*” to undo it.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Tang and Song**: verbal taboos and homophonic prohibitions at weddings appear in the notebooks.
- **Ming and Qing**: the “Customs” sections of the gazetteers record wedding taboos in detail, forming a system.
- **Modern times**: some taboos faded, but “no fans”, “no umbrellas” and “no breaking” remained general.
- **Today**: city weddings keep the verbal taboos and the insistence on pairs; other taboos are largely unobserved.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Dressing**: fans and umbrellas are avoided, and medicines used with care (in some regions the word “medicine” is avoided).
2. **Mounting the chair or car**: treading on the threshold and looking back are avoided.
3. **On the road**: setting the chair down and “going back the way one came” are avoided.
4. **Entering the house**: the brazier is crossed; the threshold must not be trodden.
5. **In the bridal chamber**: no empty room, no blowing out the candles, no inauspicious words.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the taboos on fans and umbrellas are strongest, and verbal taboos are general.
- **Jiangnan**: the sound “scatter” is avoided, and gifts are in pairs.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: the fourth month (“the month of death”) and umbrellas are avoided.
- **Ethnic minorities**: each people’s wedding taboos differ (particular foods forbidden, particular persons barred); these must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Related objects**: the brazier, red felt, wedding candles.
- **Documents**: wedding cards, gift lists (gifts recorded in even numbers).
- **Textual basis**: the “Customs” sections of the provincial gazetteers; the *Yuxia ji*.
