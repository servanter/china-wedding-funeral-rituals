# Eight-Character Matching and Fate-Based Prohibitions

## 1. Definition

“Matching a marriage”, also “matching the eight characters” or “matching the birth data”, is **the practice of judging a match’s good or ill fortune from the birth data of the man and woman (the four pillars of year, month, day and hour)**; it is the core content of the “asking of the name” stage in traditional wedding custom.

**The principal methods**:

| Item | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **The eight characters / four pillars** | Year, month, day and hour each with a stem and branch: eight characters in all |
| **Nayin** | The sixty-day cycle assigned the nayin of the five agents, discussed by mutual production and overcoming |
| **The six harmonies and three harmonies of the zodiac animals** | Six harmonies (zi-chou, yin-hai and so on) and three harmonies (shen-zi-chen and so on) are auspicious |
| **Spirits and demons** | Spirits such as “peach blossom”, “the lone star and solitary lodging”, “sheep blade” are used to judge suitability |
| **The “matching card”** | The birth data of both are written on horoscope cards and given to a master to assess |
| **“Exchanging horoscope cards”** | The two sides exchange them — the procedure of “asking the name” |

**Popular proverbs**: “a man fears being born in the year of the cock, a woman in the year of the sheep” and the like are folk sayings without any unified basis.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Tang**: the doctrine of fate (Li Xuzhong, taking the year as principal) was gradually completed, and the custom of matching marriages grew with it.
- **Song**: the theory of the eight characters based on the “Ziping method” (Xu Ziping, taking the day as principal) matured.
- **Ming and Qing**: matching became an obligatory stage of the wedding, and almanacs and books of fate were printed in quantity.
- **Modern times**: books of fate and the trade of “reading the eight characters” continued.
- **Today**: some families still match marriages, mostly for reference; others treat the zodiac and constellation as a game.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Asking the name**: the matchmaker seeks the birth data from the bride’s family.
2. **Exchanging horoscope cards**: the cards are exchanged (or the bride’s family replies with one).
3. **Matching the eight characters**: a master sets out the four pillars and discusses the nayin, spirits and demons.
4. **The verdict**: a conclusion of “match” or “no match”; for a “no match” there are methods of “counteraction”.
5. **Returning the cards**: if they match, the cards are “settled” (the engagement); if not, they are “returned”.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: “matching by zodiac animal” is emphasised, and the “six clashes” are avoided.
- **Jiangnan**: attention is paid to the four pillars and to the nayin combination.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: “matching a marriage” is done through a fate hall that reads the “eight characters”, with insistence on the “three harmonies” and “six harmonies”.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples judge matches by divination, by animal signs or by their own calendar; the systems differ and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: the horoscope card (dragon-and-phoenix card), the match record, the marriage letter.
- **Textual basis**: the *Xieji bianfang shu*, the *Yuxia ji*, books of fate (of the Ziping school).
- **Objects**: the calendar, the compass.
