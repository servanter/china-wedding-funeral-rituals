# Monthly Prohibitions, Years Without Spring and “Widow Years”

## 1. Definition

Monthly prohibitions are the doctrines that judge what is suitable for a wedding by **the combination of year, month and solar term**.

| Item | Content | Popular saying |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Year without spring (“blind year”, “widow year”)** | A lunar year with no “beginning of spring” solar term | Marriage avoided, said to be unfavourable |
| **Double-spring year** | A lunar year with two beginnings of spring | Marriage suitable: “double spring, double joy” |
| **One’s own zodiac year (the “root year”)** | The year of one’s own zodiac animal | Marriage avoided, or “tying red” to counteract |
| **Forbidden months** | The third lunar month (Qingming), the seventh (ghost month), the ninth and others | Marriage avoided in some regions |
| **The “four separations and four terminations”** | The day before and after the two equinoxes and two solstices | Marriage avoided |
| **“Sanniang’s baleful days”** | The third, seventh, thirteenth, eighteenth, twenty-second and twenty-seventh of each month (accounts differ) | Marriage avoided |

**On the “widow year”**: the difference between the lunar and tropical years leaves some years without a beginning of spring; popular usage has dubbed these “widow years”. It is a **calendar phenomenon with a folk interpretation attached, and has no scientific basis**.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Han onwards**: calendars and the art of choosing days combined, and monthly suitability and prohibition appear in the *Day Books* and the “Monthly Ordinances” tradition.
- **Tang, Song, Ming and Qing**: the almanacs brought the “year without spring” and “Sanniang’s baleful days” into the system of date selection.
- **Modern times**: the press repeatedly discussed the lack of foundation for the saying that a “widow year” is unfit for marriage.
- **Today**: some families still avoid holding a wedding in a “year without spring”; others take a “double-spring year” as auspicious.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Consulting the calendar**: whether the lunar year contains a beginning of spring.
2. **Choosing the year**: a “year without spring” is avoided, or a “double-spring year” chosen.
3. **Choosing the month**: Qingming month, the seventh month and other forbidden months are avoided.
4. **Choosing the day**: “Sanniang’s baleful days” and the “four separations and four terminations” are avoided.
5. **Counteraction**: where avoidance is inconvenient, “tying red” or “choosing an auspicious hour” is used.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the “year without spring” is most strongly held, weddings often being moved to the following year.
- **Jiangnan**: “forbidden months” and solar terms are emphasised; the doctrine of the springless year has less influence.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: the “solitary phoenix year” (the springless year) is avoided, and weddings are generally moved.
- **Ethnic minorities**: each people fixes its wedding dates by its own calendar and agricultural seasons; the Han monthly prohibitions do not apply, and they must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Basis for choosing**: the yellow almanac, the *Xieji bianfang shu*, the *Yuxia ji*.
- **Documents**: the date card, the date-choosing slip.
