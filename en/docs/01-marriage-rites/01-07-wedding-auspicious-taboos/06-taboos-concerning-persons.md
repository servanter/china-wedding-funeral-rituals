# Taboos Concerning Persons

## 1. Definition

At a wedding certain persons are held, because of their **status, condition or zodiac animal**, to be unfit to take part in particular stages, and must withdraw or be rearranged.

| Person | Stage excluded, and reason |
| --- | --- |
| **Pregnant woman (the “four-eyed person”)** | Must not touch the bride’s dowry, sit on the bridal bed or enter the bridal chamber (“joy clashing with joy”) |
| **Widow** | Must not serve as the “woman of full fortune” (dressing the bed, sewing the quilt, supporting the couple) |
| **One whose zodiac animal clashes** | Withdraws when the bride mounts and alights and enters the chamber (see the entry “Zodiac Clashes”) |
| **One in mourning** | Must not attend a wedding during mourning (“the inauspicious clashing with the joy”) |
| **The “unclean” (the unwell, menstruating women)** | Excluded in some regions, on varying old grounds |
| **Boys and girls** | On the contrary, auspicious: pressing the bed, carrying the descendants bucket, rolling on the bed |
| **The “woman of full fortune”** | Must have living parents, a harmonious marriage and both sons and daughters |

**The requirement for the “woman of full fortune”** is the positive form of the taboo: someone in a “perfect state of life” is chosen to carry out the rites, so that the “blessing” of that state may be lent to the couple.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **From the pre-Qin onwards**: the choice of wedding assistants was regulated by custom (the *Yili* has the offices of the “elder” and the “governess”).
- **Ming and Qing**: the gazetteers commonly record the customs of “avoiding the four-eyed person” and “engaging a woman of full fortune”.
- **Modern times**: with the spread of medical ideas the taboo on pregnant women faded.
- **Today**: customs such as keeping pregnant women from sitting on the bridal bed persist in the countryside; the cities largely ignore them.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Choosing the woman of full fortune**: before the wedding she is engaged to sew the quilt, dress the bed and support the couple.
2. **Avoiding clashes**: those whose animals clash withdraw when the bride mounts and alights and enters.
3. **Avoiding the four-eyed**: pregnant women do not touch the dowry or enter the bridal chamber (in some regions).
4. **Avoiding mourners**: those in mourning do not attend the wedding.
5. **Using children**: boys and girls are asked to “press the bed”, “roll on the bed” and “carry the descendants bucket”.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: “avoiding the four-eyed person” and “engaging a woman of full fortune” are emphasised.
- **Jiangnan**: dressing the bed requires a woman of full fortune; pregnant women must not sit on the bed.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: those whose animals clash withdraw; a boy is used to “press the bed”.
- **Ethnic minorities**: each people has its own rules about who should not take part (particular kin withdrawing); these must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Related objects**: the bridal bed, the dowry, the descendants bucket.
- **Documents**: marriage letters, gift lists, duty lists (naming the “woman of full fortune” and others).
