# Meeting the Prospect (Inspecting the Household)

## 1. Definition

Meeting the prospect — in popular speech “looking at one another”, “looking at the person”, “looking at the household” — is the stage of the marriage negotiation at which the two parties (or their families) meet and inspect one another.

It comprises two kinds of inspection: **of the person** (appearance, bearing, health) and **of the household** (house, land, means, family tone). In former times it was usually the parents and the matchmaker who did the looking, the parties themselves often not taking part directly — the greatest difference from the modern “blind date”.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The custom existed by the Tang and Song. The Song *Dongjing menghua lu*, “Taking a Wife”, records “meeting the prospect”: the man’s family would “choose a day, prepare wine and gifts and go to the woman’s house”, or the two families would meet; if the woman’s side was satisfied she would “insert a gold hairpin into the hair on the crown, called inserting the hairpin”; if not satisfied, she “sent two bolts of coloured satin, called calming the shock”.
- The Yuan, Ming and Qing continued the practice, and in some places the “inserting the hairpin” and “calming the shock” rites long survived (see *Qing bai lei chao*).
- “Inspecting the household” was especially important in an agrarian society: land, house, water supply and relations with neighbours were all part of the “household”.
- In modern times the inspection has gradually become a meeting of the parties themselves (“meeting once”, “catching one another’s eye”).
- Today the forms are plural — introduction by relatives and friends, the “matchmaking corner” (parents setting up stalls in parks) and dating platforms — but the underlying logic of “inspecting the household” persists.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Fixing the day**: the matchmaker agrees the day of the meeting.
2. **Preparing gifts**: the man’s family prepares wine and cakes; the woman’s family prepares tea (in some regions “drinking tea” is the form).
3. **Meeting**: the parents (and the parties) meet and observe character, bearing and health.
4. **Inspecting the household**: the woman’s side (or the man’s) visits the other house to look at the building, the land and the means.
5. **Signalling**: satisfaction is shown by “inserting the hairpin” or by oral consent; dissatisfaction is declined through “calming the shock” (the sending of satin or a gift), without loss of face.
6. **Proceeding**: if the meeting satisfies, the matter moves on to the exchange of horoscope cards and match-reading.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: called “xiangkan” or “kan ren”; usually held at the woman’s house, the man bringing gifts.
- **Jiangsu and Zhejiang**: called “kan renjia” or “xiangqin”; the rule is “first look at the household, then at the person”.
- **Fujian and Guangdong**: called “xiangkan” or “kan jia”; the matchmaker usually leads the man’s family to the woman’s house, which provides tea; “drinking tea” is an important courtesy.
- **Large cities today**: the “matchmaking corner” has appeared, where parents set up stalls and exchange particulars — in essence the publicising and standardising of “inspecting the household”.
- **Ethnic minorities**: the Miao “youfang”, the Dong “singing songs and sitting in the moonlight”, the Zhuang “song fair” and the Dai “visiting the girl” are all occasions on which young people choose freely in collective village activity (festivals, market days), so that the man and woman may come to know and love one another directly — the reverse of the Han order in which the parents do the looking. The social basis of such customs is the open oversight of the village community, not private arrangement between families.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Wine and cakes**: provided by the man’s family.
- **Tea**: offered by the woman’s side; “drinking tea” symbolises consent in Fujian and Guangdong.
- **Gold hairpin**: the token of satisfaction (the Song “inserting the hairpin”).
- **Coloured satin**: the “calming the shock” gift given on declining (Song practice).
- **Photographs and curricula vitae**: the standardised materials of the modern matchmaking corner.
