# Betrothal Contracts and Engagement Documents

## 1. Definition

The betrothal contract is the agreement between the two families to marry; the engagement documents are its written record, collectively called “marriage writings”, and include the horoscope card, the engagement card, the betrothal letter and the marriage contract (dragon-and-phoenix card).

A traditional betrothal contract had a **double force**: in custom it was binding through village covenant and lineage, and in law, during parts of the period from the Tang to the Qing, it was enforceable (the breaking of the promise being a punishable offence). After the modern period the legal enforceability of the betrothal contract was abolished, leaving only the constraints of custom and morality.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Zhouli*, “Offices of Earth: the Matchmaker”: “For all who take a divorced wife or bring in a child, he records it.” Marriage had to be registered and written down — the beginning of the marriage writing.
- The *Tang Code with Subcommentary*, “Households and Marriage”: “In all cases where a daughter has been promised in marriage, if a marriage letter has been sent or a private agreement made and the promise is then broken, sixty blows with the rod. If she is promised to another, a hundred blows.” Both marriage letter and private agreement had to be performed, and breach was punished.
- The *Song Criminal Code* followed the Tang system, and had the institution of the “engagement card”. The Song *Dongjing menghua lu* records that “when the betrothal was placed, next the engagement card was placed”, each side having its own card setting out three generations, land and houses.
- Zhu Xi’s *Family Rituals* lays down that the “nacai” section must use a “writing” — that is, a written marriage contract.
- In the Ming and Qing the “dragon-and-phoenix card” was the popular form of marriage writing: the man’s card marked “qianzao” and the woman’s “kunzao”, each in a fixed format.
- The Republican *Civil Code* established freedom of marriage, and a betrothal contract could not be enforced (Article 975: no action may be brought to compel performance of a betrothal contract).
- Today: the betrothal contract has no legal enforceability; property disputes over an engagement are handled under the rules for the return of the bride-price.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Agreement**: the two sides reach agreement on the terms (betrothal gift, wedding date, trousseau).
2. **Writing the cards**: someone is engaged to write the marriage contract, engagement card and horoscope card, on red paper in regular script, without corrections.
3. **Signing**: the names of three generations of each side are written, with ancestral home, birth data, the principal in the marriage (parent or lineage head) and the matchmaker (who must sign as witness).
4. **Exchange**: the matchmaker exchanges them, each side keeping one.
5. **Feasting**: the two families hold a feast (the engagement wine), with close kin and the matchmaker invited as witnesses.
6. **Keeping the record**: the documents are kept in each household as evidence in any later dispute.
7. **Repudiation**: if one side breaks the promise, the betrothal gift must by custom be returned or compensation made, with the matchmaker and lineage elders mediating; in former times it might be taken to the magistrate.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: two stages, the “exchange of minor cards” (simple) and the “exchange of major cards” (the formal contract).
- **Jiangsu and Zhejiang**: attention is paid to the form of the marriage writing, with a tradition of parallel prose in four-and-six rhythm.
- **Fujian and Guangdong**: the marriage writing uses the “dragon-and-phoenix card”, in sets of several pieces, with attention to mounting.
- **Hakka areas**: called “writing the horoscope document”, written by the lineage clerk.
- **Ethnic minorities**: among some peoples the betrothal is concluded by a religious ceremony, as with the Uyghur *nikah* (a religious marriage contract performed by an imam), which has both religious and legal force; Tibetan betrothal is connected with tribal customary law. **The force of these “contracts” derives not from a written document but from religion or customary law**, and they differ in nature from the Han marriage writing.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **The horoscope card**: recording the birth data.
- **The engagement card**: the Song institution, recording three generations, land and houses.
- **The marriage contract (dragon-and-phoenix card)**: the formal betrothal, one copy for each side.
- **The betrothal letter**: the writing from the groom’s family to the bride’s.
- **The reply card**: the writing by which the bride’s family answers.
- **The matchmaker’s attestation**: the matchmaker’s signature as witness.
- **Model forms**: see “Document Templates / Model Marriage Contract (Dragon-and-Phoenix Card)”.
