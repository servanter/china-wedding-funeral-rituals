# Setting and Dressing the Bridal Bed

## 1. Definition

Setting the bed is the rite of installing the bridal bed on an auspicious day before the wedding; dressing the bed is the custom of laying out bedding and scattering auspicious objects on the new bed, with a “woman of full fortune” reciting blessings. The two are connected and usually performed within one to three days before the reception.

Their core concern is **the prayer for offspring and for good fortune**: through the orientation and position of the bed, the number of layers of bedding, the kinds of fruit scattered and the content of the blessings, children and harmony are sought for the couple.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Shihunli* records a rite of “setting the bed” before the wedding: “the head of the house takes off his shoes in the chamber”, “the accompanying concubine receives them” — the furnishings of the chamber are the precondition of the wedding ritual.
- The Song *Dongjing menghua lu*, “Taking a Wife”, records that before the reception “the couple’s chamber was furnished with bed and bedding”, with a custom of “sitting in the empty tent”.
- In the Ming and Qing the customs of “setting the bed” and “dressing the bed” flourished, with attention to:
  - **orientation**: the head of the bed must stand against a wall; directly facing a door, a window or a mirror is avoided (a reflection being “double illumination”, which portends quarrels); a beam pressing on the bed is avoided.
  - **choice of day**: an auspicious day must be chosen, and in some places the groom sleeps alone on the bed for a night on the “bed-setting day” (“keeping the bed”).
  - **dressing by the woman of full fortune**: the bed is dressed by a woman whose parents are both living, whose marriage is harmonious and who has children (the “woman of full fortune”).
- Contemporary weddings generally keep the setting and dressing of the bed, with stages such as the “bed-dressing song” and the “scattering on the bed”.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Choosing the day**: an auspicious day is chosen for setting the bed (usually one to three days before the reception).
2. **Choosing the position**: the position of the bed is fixed, avoiding a pressing beam, a facing mirror and a confronting door; in former times the orientation was set by compass.
3. **Setting the bed**: the bed is placed and made firm; others (especially pregnant women and widows) are forbidden to sit or lie on the new bed.
4. **Dressing the bed**: a “woman of full fortune” lays the mat and the quilt and arranges the pillows; the bedding must be entirely new and in pairs.
5. **Scattering on the bed**: dates, chestnuts, peanuts, longans and lotus seeds are scattered on the bed while the bed-dressing song is sung, as in “dress the bed, dress the bed, may sons and grandsons fill the hall; a noble son first, a daughter after”.
6. **Lying on the bed**: a boy (the “bed-pressing child”) is arranged to sleep on the new bed for a night, in the sense of “an early noble son”.
7. **Sealing the room**: after the bed is dressed a double-happiness character is pasted on the door of the room, and outsiders are not to enter.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: called “anchuang” or “puchuang”; the “bed-dressing song” and the “pressing of the bed” are insisted upon, often performed jointly by the mother-in-law and the woman of full fortune.
- **Jiangsu and Zhejiang**: called “puchuang” or “anchuang”; the bedding must be in an even number of layers, and no pregnant woman may be present.
- **Fujian and Guangdong**: called “anchuang” or “puchuang”; a “bucket of descendants” is placed before the bed and a red packet hidden in the quilt.
- **Guangdong**: “setting the bed greatly auspicious” is insisted upon, and a *da jin jie* presides and sings the blessings.
- **Ethnic minorities**: most have no separate ceremony of “setting the bed”, the arrangements for living being folded into the wedding day itself — as with the Miao “sitting on the bed”, the Yi “entering the new house” and the Mongolian “setting up the new yurt”. Their forms of dwelling (yurt, stilt house, tower house) differ, and the ideas attached to the position of the bed differ with them.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **The new bed**: the central object, with attention to the timber (elm, camphor and nanmu commonly used).
- **Wedding quilt, wedding pillow and sheets**: entirely new, embroidered with dragons and phoenixes, mandarin ducks and pomegranates.
- **Dates, chestnuts, peanuts, longans and lotus seeds**: for scattering on the bed.
- **The bed-pressing child (a boy)**: a custom of some regions.
- **The bed-dressing song**: orally transmitted verses.
- **Red paper double-happiness characters**: for decorating the door.
