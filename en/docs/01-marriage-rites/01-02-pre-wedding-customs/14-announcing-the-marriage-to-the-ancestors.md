# Announcing the Marriage to the Ancestors

## 1. Definition

Announcing to the ancestors (*gaomiao*, also “informing the ancestors”) is the rite of reporting the coming marriage to the ancestors before the wedding (and on the wedding day) and asking their blessing.

With the post-wedding “presentation at the temple” it forms a pair: **announcement before, presentation after**. The announcement before the wedding is a “request for instruction”, the presentation after it an “introduction” — by which the new wife becomes a participant in the ancestral sacrifices.

## 2. Historical Origins

- Each of the Six Rites in the *Shihunli* is performed in the name of the “head of the house” (the groom’s family), the premise being that the head represents the lineage and is qualified to communicate with the ancestors.
- The *Liji*, “The Meaning of Marriage”: “The wedding ritual is that by which the good of two surnames is joined, so that above one may serve the ancestral temple and below continue the generations.” The purpose of marriage points directly to “serving the ancestral temple”, so that announcing to the ancestors before the wedding is of the essence.
- Zhu Xi’s *Family Rituals* provides that the chief stages of a wedding must be preceded by “announcing in the ancestral hall”: nacai, nabing and qinying alike. This is the Song codification of the announcement.
- In the Ming and Qing the popular announcement (“bowing to the ancestors”, “informing the ancestors”) was universal, the ceremony ranging from simple to elaborate: at its simplest, incense and bowing; at its most solemn, the three offerings and the reading of a written invocation.
- Contemporary Chinese-style weddings generally keep the “informing of the ancestors”.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Choosing the time**: the ancestors are informed at an auspicious hour before each key point — the conclusion of the negotiation, the engagement, the sending of the date, the reception.
2. **Sweeping the hall**: the ancestral hall (or the place of the ancestral tablets in the house) is swept and the incense table set out.
3. **Setting out the offerings**: wine, tea, fruit, cakes and the three sacrificial meats (according to means); incense and candles are lit.
4. **The head of the house offers incense**: the head of the family (or the groom) offers incense and kneels.
5. **Reading the invocation**: the written invocation to the ancestors is read, to the effect that so-and-so, son (or grandson) of the house, is now negotiating (or has concluded, or is receiving) a marriage with a woman of such-and-such a surname, and that this is respectfully announced to all the ancestors.
6. **Bowing**: three kneelings and nine bows, or the three bows.
7. **Burning the invocation**: the text is burnt and firecrackers set off (in some regions).
8. **Removing the offerings**: the ceremony ends and the offerings are taken away (and eaten, popularly called “eating the blessing”).

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: called “bowing to the ancestors” or “informing the ancestors”; the ceremony is performed before the ancestral tablets in the main hall and is simple.
- **Jiangsu and Zhejiang**: called “gaomiao” or “offering incense to inform the ancestors”; where the lineage has an ancestral hall, the ceremony is held in it.
- **Fujian and Guangdong**: the system of ancestral-hall sacrifice is developed, and the announcement is usually made in the hall with great solemnity, an elder of the lineage presiding.
- **Taiwan**: called “bowing to the ancestors”, the announcement being combined with the reception and the entry into the house.
- **Ethnic minorities**: most peoples have no system of “ancestral hall and tablets”; their announcements are made to the household god, the fire god, the mountain god or the guardian deity of the group. Thus Mongolian weddings worship fire (the hearth), Tibetan weddings bow to the Buddha and the household gods, the Yi sacrifice at the “ancestral spirit tube”, the Dai worship at the temple and the household gods. **Their systems of belief differ from Han ancestor worship**, and must be recorded separately rather than lumped together as “announcing to the ancestors”.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Incense table, incense and candles**: the appointments of the announcement.
- **Offerings**: wine, tea, fruit, cakes and the three sacrificial meats.
- **Ancestral tablets and the ancestral hall**: the place of the announcement.
- **The written invocation**: for its form and model text see “Document Templates / Model Ancestral Invocation”.
- **Kneeling mat**: the implement for bowing.
