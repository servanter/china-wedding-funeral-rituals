# Coming-of-Age Coiffure and Face Threading

## 1. Definition

- **Coming-of-age coiffure (*shangtou*)**: the change of a marrying woman’s hair from the braid of a girl to the coiled bun of an adult, with the adding of a hairpin and ornaments. Also called “adding the hairpin”, “dressing the hair”, “capping and pinning”.
- **Face threading (*kailian*, also “twisting the face”)**: the removal of the down on the face with doubled fine thread, and the trimming of the temples and the shape of the brows — the rite of “refurbishing the face” before a woman leaves home.

Both are performed on the day before the wedding (or on the morning of the day) and presided over by a “woman of full fortune” (whose parents are living, whose marriage is harmonious and who has children). Their ritual background is the **ancient capping ceremony for girls**: a girl took the hairpin at fifteen, marking adulthood, and marriage was a landmark node of “becoming adult”.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Shihunli*: “The mother ties her sash and arranges her kerchief, saying: ‘Strive, be reverent; morning and night do not disobey the commands of your parents-in-law.’” The mother’s tying of the sash and kerchief for a daughter about to marry is the ritual archetype of “shangtou”.
- In antiquity a girl of fifteen underwent the “hairpin ceremony”, binding up her hair and taking the hairpin to mark adulthood; at marriage the change of coiffure and the adding of ornaments was a re-enactment of that ceremony in the context of marriage.
- Records of “face threading” are later, occurring chiefly in Ming and Qing gazetteers and notebooks: “when a girl is to marry, her face is first twisted with thread, called opening the face”; “only after the face is opened does she become a wife”.
- In popular custom face threading carried the sense of a **threshold**: before it one was a “girl”, after it a “wife”. Hence an unmarried woman might never have her face threaded (in some regions), and a widow remarrying had to have it done again.
- Today the custom survives chiefly in the countryside and in re-enactments of traditional weddings.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

### The coming-of-age coiffure (usually on the night before the wedding)

1. **Choosing the hour**: usually on the night before the reception (the “night of shangtou”), at an auspicious hour (such as the hour of zi).
2. **Setting the place**: a table is set in the main hall, incense and candles lit, fruit offered, and comb, fine comb, hairpin, clasp and mirror laid out.
3. **Announcing to the ancestors**: the parents or the elder sister-in-law burn incense and inform the ancestors.
4. **Changing the coiffure**: the woman of full fortune unties the bride’s braid, combs it out, coils it into a bun, and adds the hairpin and ornaments.
5. **Reciting blessings**: while combing she recites blessings such as “first combing, comb to the end; second combing, white hair on matched brows; third combing, sons and grandsons filling the ground”.
6. **Bowing**: the bride bows to her parents and ancestors in gratitude for their raising her.
7. **Taking the hair**: in some regions the bride cuts a lock of hair for her mother to keep, or ties it with the groom’s (see the entry “Tying the Hair”).

### Face threading (the day before or on the day)

1. **Preparing the thread**: doubled cotton thread and talcum powder (or rouge) are prepared.
2. **Twisting the face**: the woman of full fortune crosses and twists the thread, removing the down from the forehead and cheeks to the chin, the order being “from top to bottom, from left to right”.
3. **Trimming the brows**: the shape of the brows is adjusted, in some places into the fine curved “new moon” brow.
4. **Applying powder**: when the twisting is done, powder and rouge are applied.
5. **Red packet**: the bride or her family gives the woman a red packet in thanks.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: called “shangtou” and “kailian”; the rules are that “no needlework is done on the day of shangtou” and “the face is opened on a double day”.
- **Jiangsu and Zhejiang**: called “shangtou” and “jiaomian”; in some places “no lamp is lit on the night of shangtou”.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: called “shangtou” and “kaimian”, presided over by a “woman of good fate” (the woman of full fortune), who must recite a full set of auspicious phrases.
- **Guangdong**: called “shangtou”, held on the night before the wedding together with “putting on the wedding dress”, presided over by the mother or a *da jin jie*.
- **Ethnic minorities**: a change of coiffure and of adornment is found among all peoples, but with different meanings. The Yi “combing of the hair”, for instance, marks ethnic identity; the Yao “coiling of the bun” marks the coiffure of a married woman; the Miao silver headdress is the counterpart of “shangtou”, but its ritual meaning differs from the Han “adding the hairpin”, being bound up chiefly with ethnic identity and marital status.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Comb and fine comb**: the implements of hair-dressing.
- **Hairpin, clasp and flowers**: for adding the pin and ornaments.
- **Mirror**: for looking at oneself.
- **Doubled cotton thread and talcum powder**: for face threading.
- **Incense, candles and offerings**: for informing the ancestors.
- **Blessings (the shangtou song, the face-opening song)**: orally transmitted verses.
- **Red packet**: for the woman who threads the face.
