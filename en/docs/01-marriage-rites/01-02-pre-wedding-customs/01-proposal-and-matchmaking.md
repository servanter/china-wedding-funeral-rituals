# Proposal and Matchmaking

## 1. Definition

Proposal and matchmaking are the first act of negotiating a marriage: either the groom’s family commissions a matchmaker to declare its wish to the bride’s family, or the matchmaker takes the initiative in bringing the two together.

The subject of “matchmaking” (*shuomei*) is the matchmaker; the subject of “proposing” (*tiqin*) is the groom’s family. In the ritual order this step corresponds to nacai, the first of the Six Rites, but in custom it is far more flexible — it may be repeated many times, or dropped halfway, and it is the only stage from which one may withdraw “at no cost”.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Shijing*, “Bin Odes: Cutting the Axe Handle”: “How does one cut an axe handle? Without an axe one cannot. How does one take a wife? Without a matchmaker one cannot.” The axe needed for the handle figures the matchmaker needed for the wife.
- The *Zhouli*, “Offices of Earth: the Matchmaker”, established an official matchmaker with charge of the marriages of the people, “in the month of mid-spring causing men and women to meet” — an early form of official intervention in marriage.
- From the Tang and Song the matchmaker became a professional; the Song *Dongjing menghua lu*, “Taking a Wife”, records the matchmaker’s shuttle diplomacy in detail.
- In the Ming and Qing “arranging a match” was a common trade, with a distinction between “official matchmakers” and “private matchmakers”, and also “reverse matchmakers” who sought husbands for daughters on the bride’s family’s behalf.
- In modern times the matchmaker has gradually been replaced by the marriage agency and the dating platform, though in the negotiation of the bride-price and in communication between the two households the role remains a practical one.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Forming the intention**: the groom’s family (or the bride’s) considers a marriage and first weighs whether family standing, age and means are comparable.
2. **Commissioning the matchmaker**: a matchmaker is engaged and the conditions set out (age, appearance, property, education).
3. **Conveying the word**: the matchmaker calls at the other house, sets out the particulars and sounds out the intention.
4. **The answer**: the other party says it “may be considered”, or declines politely — usually in face-saving terms such as “we could not presume to look so high” or “our daughter is still young”.
5. **Carrying messages**: the matchmaker shuttles the conditions of each side back and forth; this stage may take months and be repeated many times.
6. **Preliminary consent**: the two parties agree in principle and arrange a meeting (see the entry “Meeting the Prospect”).
7. **Thanking the matchmaker**: even if the marriage comes to nothing, a matchmaker who has taken trouble must be given a gift in thanks — a popular rule.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: called “tiqin” or “shuo xifu”; the matchmaker is usually a middle-aged woman from among relatives or neighbours, and the introduction is chiefly oral.
- **Jiangsu and Zhejiang**: called “timei” or “yigin”; matching family standing is insisted upon, and the matchmaker is often a relative common to both families, to increase trust.
- **Fujian and Guangdong**: called “shuoqin”; the matchmaker must bring the “fourfold gift” (sugar, cakes, tea, wine) when she calls.
- **Hakka areas**: called “jiangqin”; the rule is “first look at the household, then at the young man”.
- **Ethnic minorities**: among the Miao, courtship through “youfang” and “tiaoyue” precedes the sending of a matchmaker; the Yi have a rite of “shuomei” requiring three calls by the matchmaker, with a custom of “splashing water to welcome the matchmaker” (the woman’s side splashes water in jest); the Dong have a stage of “shuoqin” following “singing songs and sitting in the moonlight”. Among these peoples the starting point of the negotiation is usually the free association of the young people themselves, the matchmaker’s role being “to supply what the ritual order requires” — a different sequence from the Han, where the matchmaker opens the negotiation.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Proposal gifts**: wine, sugar, cakes and pastries, in pairs.
- **The fourfold gift**: sugar, cakes, tea and wine (Fujian and Guangdong).
- **The matchmaker’s note**: in some places a brief card bearing the particulars of both parties.
- **Thank-offering**: a pig’s head, shoes and socks, a red packet (when the marriage is accomplished).
