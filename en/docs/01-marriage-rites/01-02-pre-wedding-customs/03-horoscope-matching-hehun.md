# Horoscope Matching (Hehun)

## 1. Definition

Horoscope matching is the popular practice of judging the auspiciousness of a marriage by setting the birth characters of the man and the woman against one another. Its stages are “exchanging horoscope cards” and “matching the eight characters”.

In the ritual order horoscope matching corresponds to **wenming and naji** among the Six Rites: wenming is the ritual act of obtaining the woman’s birth data, and naji the ritual procedure that follows a favourable omen. Popular custom compresses the two into a single “casting of the eight characters”.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The “asking of the name” and “announcing the favourable divination” of the Six Rites are the ritual parent of horoscope matching: in the *Shihunli* the divination in the temple follows wenming, and is itself a form of divination.
- From the Tang and Song the court maintained a Directorate of Astronomy to issue the calendar, and the people used almanacs and books of fate; horoscope matching became a specialised craft.
- The Song *Dongjing menghua lu*, “Taking a Wife”, records that after “asking the name”, “the birth data of the man and woman were at once given to a master of yin and yang to choose a day”.
- In the Ming and Qing horoscope matching flourished, with a settled set of techniques:
  - **The eight characters**: the stems and branches of the four pillars of year, month, day and hour.
  - **The mutual attraction and clash of the zodiac animals**: the six harmonies (as zi with chou), the three harmonies (shen-zi-chen), the six clashes (zi with wu), the six harms.
  - **Nayin**: the mutual production and overcoming of the five agents assigned to the sixty-day cycle (such as “metal in the sea”, “water in the ravine”).
  - **Spirits and demons**: such as the “lone star”, the “solitary lodging”, “peach blossom” and “sheep blade”.
- The *Xieji bianfang shu* (compiled by imperial order in the Qianlong reign of the Qing) is the official compendium of date selection, and includes principles for choosing a wedding day in horoscope matching.
- Contemporary horoscope matching is mostly symbolic consultation, and is often combined with the choice of the wedding date.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Exchange of cards**: through the matchmaker the two families exchange horoscope cards (the dragon-and-phoenix cards), on which the eight birth characters are written.
2. **Consultation**: the man’s family (or both) engages a master of yin and yang (a fate reader) to set out the eight characters and match the horoscopes.
3. **The zodiac animals**: first the animals are compared for clash or harm, as in the sayings “the white horse fears the black ox” and “the cock and the monkey do not reach the end”.
4. **Nayin and spirits**: then the mutual production and overcoming of the nayin elements, and the good or ill of the spirits and demons.
5. **The verdict**: a conclusion of “greatly auspicious”, “usable” or “unsuitable”.
6. **Counteraction**: where inauspicious, popular custom has many “counteractions” (such as calling the parties “nominal kin”, marrying on a particular day, or wearing an object “to pass the gate”). Such practices belong to folk belief and have no scientific basis.
7. **Reporting back**: the matchmaker reports the result to both sides; if favourable, the engagement proceeds, if not, the matter is set aside.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: attention is paid to whether the zodiac animals agree and whether the nayin elements agree, the woman’s family especially to whether the bride will “bring prosperity to her husband”.
- **Jiangsu and Zhejiang**: mutual production of the eight characters is insisted upon, and the fortune-teller issues a conclusion in the form of a “marriage letter”.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: horoscope matching is treated with great solemnity; the card is placed on the altar for three days (“three days of inspection”), and only if no ill omen occurs (such as the breaking of an object) is it reckoned favourable.
- **Guangdong**: there is a custom of “clipping the eight characters”, the man’s family engaging a fortune-teller to assess them.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples have divination systems of their own, such as the Yi “notching wood” and “chicken divination”, divination by lamas among the Tibetans, and by shamans among the Manchu. These systems have their own religious backgrounds and do not belong to the same order as Han stems-and-branches fate reading; they must not be conflated.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Horoscope cards (dragon-and-phoenix cards)**: red paper, the man’s card marked “qianzao” and the woman’s “kunzao”.
- **Almanacs, books of fate and perpetual calendars**: the fortune-teller’s tools.
- **The marriage letter (horoscope conclusion)**: issued in some regions.
- **Tables of the eight characters and of the spirits and demons**: used in the reckoning.
