# Minor Betrothal (Xiaoding)

## 1. Definition

Minor betrothal, also called “passing the minor gift”, “minor betrothal gift” or “placing the minor betrothal”, is the stage at which the groom’s family sends a small betrothal gift to the bride’s family and the engagement is preliminarily confirmed.

Minor betrothal and “major betrothal” (the major gift) make up a **two-stage engagement**: the minor betrothal comes first, with light gifts, and the major betrothal afterwards, with heavy ones. This division is general in wedding custom from the Ming and Qing, and its practical function is to leave both sides **room to withdraw** — after the minor betrothal matters may still turn, but after the major betrothal they are settled.

## 2. Historical Origins

- In the ritual order the minor betrothal falls between naji and nazheng: after naji the marriage is settled, but the gifts have not yet been presented. Popular custom split the “settlement” into two steps, the minor betrothal being the popularised form of naji.
- The Song *Family Rituals* of Zhu Xi had already reduced the Six Rites to three, the single section “nacai” combining the negotiation and the settlement of the marriage; the minor betrothal is one of its subdivisions.
- In the Ming and Qing “minor betrothal” and “major betrothal” were named together, as in *Qing bai lei chao* and the provincial gazetteers.
- In some regions (such as Shanxi and Hebei) there is also a two-stage documentary procedure of “exchanging the minor card — exchanging the major card”, matching the minor and major betrothals.
- The modern “engagement” corresponds roughly to the two merged.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Fixing the day**: the matchmaker agrees the day for the minor gift.
2. **Preparing the gift**: the groom’s family prepares the gift — usually jewellery (rings, bracelets), cloth, sweets, wine and cakes, in pairs.
3. **Sending the card**: the “minor card” is brought, a brief note recording three generations of the groom’s family and the matters agreed.
4. **Receiving the gift**: the bride’s family receives it and returns customary things (shoes, hats, stationery, kerchiefs).
5. **Drinking wine**: the bride’s family holds an “engagement wine”, with close kin invited as company.
6. **Confirming the engagement**: both sides confirm that the engagement stands, though ritually it may still be discussed (in some places the rule is that after the minor betrothal the woman’s side may still withdraw, on returning the gifts).
7. **Proceeding to the major betrothal**: a date is agreed for the formal presentation.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: called “guo xiaoli”, “xiaoding” or “huan xiaotie”; the gifts are chiefly jewellery and cloth, insisted upon in “four colours” or “six colours”.
- **Jiangsu and Zhejiang**: called “xiaoding”, “fangding” or “chuanhong”; the “returning of the tray” (the woman’s return gift) is insisted upon.
- **Fujian and Guangdong**: called “guo xiaoli” or “song xiaoding”; the woman’s family entertains with tea, and “having drunk the tea” signals consent.
- **Hunan and Jiangxi**: called “dinggeng” or “guocha”.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples have a “minor betrothal — major betrothal” division, as with the Manchu “placing the minor betrothal” (sending *ruyi* and jewellery) and “placing the major betrothal” (sending the bride-price); the Uyghur have a many-staged procedure from “choosing the bride” and “speaking of the bride” to “betrothal”, with something of the character of a trial engagement. The stages and the religious significance of the gifts differ from people to people.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Jewellery**: rings, bracelets and earrings (mostly gold and silver).
- **Cloth**: silk and ordinary fabric.
- **Sweets, cakes and wine**: the gifts, in pairs.
- **The minor card**: a simple document of engagement.
- **Return gifts**: kerchiefs, shoes and hats, stationery.
