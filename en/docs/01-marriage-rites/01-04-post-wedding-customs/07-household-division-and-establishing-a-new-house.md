# Household Division and Establishing a New House

## 1. Definition

“Household division”, also “separation of residence” or “partition of property”, means **the married sons setting up their own households and dividing the family property**; “establishing a house” means becoming an independent household in the household register and in the lineage. The completion of a marriage was often marked by “establishing a house”.

**Essentials**:
- **Equal division among the sons** is the general principle (the eldest son or eldest grandson enjoying a “eldest-grandson’s share” of preference, varying from place to place).
- **“Division with witnesses”**: elders of the lineage and the maternal uncle’s family are invited as “witnesses” and a **deed of division** is drawn up.
- **Support in old age and continuation of the line**: special arrangements are made for the parents’ maintenance (the “maintenance share”), the ancestral house and the sacrificial land (the “sacrificial fields”).
- On the day of division a family banquet is usually held and the ancestors informed.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **From the pre-Qin onwards**: Shang Yang’s reforms, taxing double any household in which two adult men did not separate, promoted the system of divided households; in later ages “equal division among the sons” became the mainstream.
- **Tang and Song**: the statutes and family instructions laid down detailed rules for division (as with the “separate registration and separate property” article of the Tang code).
- **Ming and Qing**: deeds of division and deeds by lot survive in quantity, showing the union of custom and law.
- **Today**: the succession part of the Civil Code lays down the rules of statutory succession, while the traditional custom of division survives in the countryside in the form of “drawing up a deed and inviting witnesses”.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Discussion of the division**: if the parents are living, they preside; if not, an elder brother or a lineage elder presides.
2. **Inviting witnesses**: lineage elders, the maternal uncle’s family and the village head are invited as witnesses.
3. **Partition**: the fields, houses and goods are divided on the principle of “equal division among the sons”, with provision for an “eldest-grandson’s share” and a “maintenance share”.
4. **Drawing up the deed**: a **deed of division (or deed by lot)** is written and signed by the witnesses.
5. **Informing the ancestors**: a sacrifice is made to inform the ancestors and proclaim the separation.
6. **Establishing the households**: the division is reported to the authorities for registration, and each sets up its own household.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: division is generally by “drawing lots”, whence the deed is also called the “deed by lot”.
- **Jiangnan**: provision for “maintenance land” and “sacrificial land” is relatively common.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: the “ancestral house” and the sacrificial fields are much valued, and a “common field” is often reserved on division.
- **Guangdong**: the lineage’s “ancestral field” (lineage property) is distinct from family property, and division does not touch the lineage property.
- **Ethnic minorities**: the rules of inheritance and division differ (as with ultimogeniture, the intervention of the maternal uncle’s authority, and matrilineal inheritance), and do not belong to the Han system of “equal division among the sons”; they must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **The deed of division (or deed by lot)**: setting out the property, the witnesses and the signatures.
- **The lots**: by which the division is decided by drawing.
- **Related documents**: minutes of the kindred assembly, documents of household registration, title deeds to land and houses.
