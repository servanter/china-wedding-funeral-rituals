# The Joint Return and the Month-Long Stay

## 1. Definition

The “**joint return**” is the return of the newly married couple **together** to the bride’s parents’ house; the “**month-long stay**” is the bride’s return to her parents’ house after the wedding **for a full month** (the “matching month” being the term of a full month). The two are extensions and variants of the return visit.

**Essentials**:
- The “joint return” emphasises the **couple’s joint appearance**, and is the ceremony by which the groom “acknowledges kin” on his wife’s side.
- The “month-long stay” is **the lengthening of the homecoming**, common where the wife’s family is near and the husband’s family generous; some regions distinguish two grades, “staying seven days” and “staying the month”.
- The month-long stay ends with the husband’s family fetching her back, called “fetching the month”.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **From the Tang and Song**: the custom of “gui ning” became more detailed, the gazetteers recording “staying the month” and “staying the full month”.
- **Ming and Qing**: the records of the “joint return” and the “month-long stay” are common in the gazetteers of north and south China.
- **Modern times**: with the growth of city dwelling and small households, the month-long stay became less common.
- **Today**: in the countryside one may still see “a stay of ten days or a month after the return visit”; in the cities the return is mostly made and concluded on the same day.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **The joint return**: a day is chosen after the wedding (usually the third or the sixth) for the couple to go together to the bride’s house with gifts.
2. **Acknowledging kin**: the groom greets his parents-in-law and the elders of his wife’s family, receiving the “gifts of acknowledgement”.
3. **The month-long stay**: the bride remains at her parents’ house, for seven days or up to a month.
4. **Fetching her home**: the husband’s family chooses a day and brings gifts to “fetch the month” and take the bride back.
5. **Gifts and thanks**: the two sides exchange gifts, the dealings recorded on a “gift list”.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the custom of the “month-long stay” (a full month) exists, common in Shandong and Hebei.
- **Jiangnan**: mostly short homecomings, the month-long stay being rare.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: called “being a guest” or “staying at the outer house”, the duration settled between the two families.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples have the custom of “not taking up residence in the husband’s house” (living long at the parents’ house after marriage, moving to the husband’s only after a child is born or after several years), which differs in nature from the Han month-long stay and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Gifts**: cakes, wine and meat, sweets, cloth.
- **Gifts of acknowledgement**: red packets, clothing, shoes and hats.
- **Related documents**: gift lists, invitations, records of the dealings.
