# Acknowledging and Visiting the New Kin

## 1. Definition

“Acknowledging kin” is the custom by which, after the wedding, **the kin of both sides come to know one another and the afinal relation is formally established**; “visiting kin” is the **seasonal exchange of visits** that develops from it. An afinal relation is truly established only after both “acknowledging” and “visiting”.

**Essentials**:
- **Acknowledging kin**: the new wife (or new son-in-law) greets the elders household by household and receives “gifts of meeting”; the elders “acknowledge” the forms of address (such as “son-in-law”, “daughter-in-law”).
- **Visiting kin**: once the connection is established, gifts are exchanged in accordance with the seasons. The general rule is the “**four-colour gift**” (four kinds in pairs); odd numbers are avoided.
- **New affines give weighty gifts**: the dealings of the first year after the wedding are the most ceremonious (the “first year”), being simplified year by year thereafter.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **From the pre-Qin onwards**: afinal appellations appear in the *Erya*, “Explaining Kin”; the idea of “joining the good of two surnames” established the standing of affines.
- **Tang and Song**: the rites of dealings between affines appear in notebooks and family instructions.
- **Ming and Qing**: the “Customs” sections of the gazetteers record in detail the customs of “acknowledging kin”, “visiting kin” and the “four-colour gift”.
- **Today**: the countryside still keeps the etiquette of “acknowledging” and “visiting”; the cities have simplified it into New Year calls and the exchange of gifts.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Acknowledging kin**: a day is chosen after the wedding for the couple to greet the elders of both sides household by household, offering the gifts of meeting and receiving red packets.
2. **Fixing the names**: the forms of address and the relation of dealings are established (“this connection is now in motion”).
3. **Visiting kin in the first year**: at the New Year, the Dragon Boat Festival and the Mid-Autumn Festival the two sides exchange “four-colour gifts”.
4. **Return gifts**: the receiving side returns gifts of food and cloth, “returning empty-handed” being avoided.
5. **Dealings in ordinary years**: each side takes part in the other’s weddings, funerals, births and birthday celebrations.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the “four-colour gift” is insisted upon, and the gifts of the first year’s visiting are the weightiest (cakes, wine, meat, fruit).
- **Jiangnan**: visiting gifts are local produce — cakes, tea, ham.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: afinal dealings are called “walking the kin”, seasonal gifts insisting on pairs.
- **Guangdong**: afinal dealings are accompanied by “lucky money”, and a “return gift” is indispensable.
- **Ethnic minorities**: the gifts and seasons of afinal dealings differ; some peoples have particular afinal obligations (such as the authority of the maternal uncle), which must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Gifts**: the four-colour gift (cakes, wine, meat, fruit), cloth, tea, local produce.
- **Red packets**: the “gift of meeting”, the “lucky money”.
- **Related documents**: gift lists, invitations, records of the afinal dealings.
