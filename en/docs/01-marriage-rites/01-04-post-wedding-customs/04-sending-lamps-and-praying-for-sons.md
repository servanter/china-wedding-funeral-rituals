# Sending Lamps and Praying for Sons

## 1. Definition

“Sending lamps” is the custom of **the wife’s parents’ house (or relatives and friends) sending lamps to the newly married household** in prayer for offspring. “Lamp” (*deng*) is homophonous with “person” (*ding*, a member of the household), so that sending lamps signifies “**adding a member**” (obtaining a son).

**Essentials**:
- The time is usually the **Lantern Festival of the first year after the wedding** (the fifteenth of the first month), or the first New Year after the wedding.
- The lamps take many forms: lotus lamps, dragon-and-phoenix lamps, *qilin* lamps (“the *qilin* brings a son”), Guanyin lamps.
- Besides the lamps there is the custom of “**sending a child**”: clay figurines or dough figures of “babies” are given, and placed at the head of the bed or among the bedding.
- In some regions dates and chestnuts (homophonous with “an early standing son”) are placed in the lamps, a general practice of objects of prayer for offspring.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Tang and Song**: the custom of hanging lanterns at the Lantern Festival flourished, and the idea of praying for offspring through the homophony of “lamp” and “household member” is recorded by the Song.
- **Ming and Qing**: gazetteers commonly record the customs of “sending lamps”, “sending lotus lamps” and “the *qilin* brings a son”, especially in north and south China.
- **Modern times**: the Lantern Festival prayer for offspring continued, combined with the cult of “Guanyin who sends sons”.
- **Today**: in the cities it is mostly simplified into the giving of “celebration lamps” and the hanging of red lanterns; the sense of praying for offspring has faded and the sense of blessing increased.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Choosing the day**: usually before the Lantern Festival or at the first New Year after the wedding.
2. **Preparing the lamps**: the wife’s parents prepare the lamps (usually lotus or *qilin* lamps), with dates, chestnuts, longans and peanuts.
3. **Delivery**: they are taken to the couple’s room and hung up or placed at the head of the bed.
4. **Auspicious words**: phrases such as “an early noble son”, “adding a member to the household”.
5. **Return gifts**: the new household thanks them with sweets and red packets.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the custom of sending lamps at the Lantern Festival is at its most developed, with “sending lamps to seek a son” and “stealing a lamp”.
- **Jiangnan**: the “*qilin* brings a son” lamp and clay figurines of “sending a child”.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: the Lantern Festival has the customs of “begging the turtle” and “seeking a son”; the lamps are mostly of “Guanyin sending a son”.
- **Guangdong**: the Lantern Festival “lighting of lamps” (lamps hung at the Lantern Festival after a boy is born, called “raising the lamp”).
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples have rites of praying for a child after the wedding (such as sacrifices to the “goddess who sends sons”); their systems of belief differ and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Lamps**: lotus lamps, dragon-and-phoenix lamps, *qilin* lamps, Guanyin lamps.
- **Objects of prayer for offspring**: dates, chestnuts, longans, peanuts, clay figurines of babies, dough figures.
- **Documents**: cards of auspicious words, gift lists.
