# First Month, Hundredth Day and First Birthday

## 1. Definition

A child born after the wedding is followed by a series of **child-rearing rites**, the ceremonial confirmation of the marriage’s function of “continuing the line”.

| Ceremony | Time | Chief content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Third-day washing** | The third day after birth | The baby is bathed in water infused with mugwort and sophora twigs; relatives and friends offer congratulations |
| **Full month** | One month after birth | Shaving the birth hair (“shaving the full-month head”), “moving the nest” (carrying the baby to the maternal grandmother’s house), feasting |
| **Hundredth day** | A hundred days after birth | Photographs (“hundred-day portrait”), feasting, the gift of a “long-life lock” |
| **First birthday (grasping)** | One year after birth | The “grasping ceremony” (testing the child): books, brush and ink, abacus, steelyard, food and toys are set out, and the child’s choice is read as an omen of its bent |
| **Naming and entry in the genealogy** | According to the lineage | A name is given and the child entered in the genealogy by generation |

**The core ideas**: “long life to a hundred years”, “many sons and much blessing”; the “grasping ceremony” is the most symbolically charged of the stages.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Northern and Southern Dynasties**: the *Family Instructions of Master Yan*, “Manners”, already records the “testing of the child” (the grasping ceremony), one of the earliest documents of it.
- **Tang and Song**: the customs of the third-day washing, the full month and the first birthday became general, and notebooks and gazetteers record them more and more.
- **Ming and Qing**: the child-rearing rites settled, the “Customs” sections of the gazetteers recording in detail the “grasping ceremony”, “head-shaving” and the “gift of the long-life lock”.
- **Today**: the full-month wine, the hundred-day portrait, the first-birthday banquet and the grasping ceremony are generally kept in town and country alike.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Third-day washing**: on the third day after birth the baby is bathed in medicated water, and relatives and friends offer congratulations.
2. **Full month**: the birth hair is shaved, the baby shown to visitors and a banquet held (the “full-month wine”); the maternal grandmother’s family sends “full-month gifts”.
3. **Hundredth day**: a banquet, photographs, and the gift of the “long-life lock” and bracelets.
4. **First-birthday grasping**: various objects are laid on a tray for the baby to grasp, with the company looking on and offering congratulations.
5. **Entry in the genealogy**: a name is given according to the lineage rules and the child entered in the genealogy.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the “full-month wine” and the “hundred days” are insisted upon, the maternal grandmother’s family sending the “hundred-family garment” and the “long-life lock”.
- **Jiangnan**: the “rite of head-shaving” and the “first-birthday banquet”; at the grasping ceremony books, brush and ink are reckoned auspicious.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: “making the full month” and “making the first birthday”, with a further custom of “wiping the saliva” (at four months).
- **Guangdong**: the rites of “lighting the lamp” (for a boy), the “hundred days” and the “first year”.
- **Ethnic minorities**: the forms of child-rearing rites differ (naming rites, hair-cutting rites, rites of entry into the group) and do not belong to the Han ritual system; they must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: the long-life lock, bracelets, the hundred-family garment, the grasping tray and the objects on it (book, brush, abacus, steelyard, seal, food).
- **Related documents**: the “card announcing the addition of a member” that follows the horoscope card, invitations (for the full-month wine and the first-birthday banquet), and the record of entry in the genealogy.
