# Thanking the Matchmaker

## 1. Definition

“Thanking the matchmaker”, also “thanking the go-between” or “thanking the Old Man of the Moon”, is **the custom by which, once the wedding is accomplished, both families express their thanks to the matchmaker**. The matchmaker held the key position of “joining the good of two surnames” in traditional marriage, so that on the completion of the match thanks were due.

**Essentials**:
- The thank-offerings insist on **pairs**: wine, meat, cakes, shoes, cloth and red packets, all auspicious in even numbers.
- Popular custom has the saying “**eighteen hocks for the matchmaker**” (in Jiangnan), meaning that the hock (pig’s trotter) was the weightiest item of the thanks.
- The thanking is usually performed **after the return visit** or on the wedding day, sometimes deferred to the full month or a festival.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the *Shihunli* has the office of the “matchmaker”; the *Shijing*’s “without a matchmaker one cannot” reflects the intermediary position of the go-between.
- **Tang and Song**: the matchmaker became institutionalised, and the custom of thanking her on the completion of a marriage appears in notebooks and gazetteers.
- **Ming and Qing**: the etiquette of the thanks settled, and gazetteers and proverbs commonly record the customs of “thanking the matchmaker” and the “hock”.
- **Today**: the cities mostly thank the introducer with red packets, wedding sweets and a banquet; the countryside still keeps the rule of paired gifts.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Preparing the gifts**: the two families separately prepare the thank-offerings (wine, meat, hocks, cakes, cloth, red packets).
2. **Conveying thanks**: a day is chosen after the wedding to call and give thanks, or thanks are given in person at the banquet.
3. **The banquet**: a table is laid for the matchmaker, commonly called the “wine of thanks to the matchmaker”.
4. **Return gifts**: the matchmaker returns auspicious words and a small gift (mostly symbolic).
5. **Later dealings**: thereafter the matchmaker is often honoured as the “old matchmaker” and invited to family celebrations.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Jiangnan**: the hock is the weighty gift, and the saying “eighteen hocks for the matchmaker” circulates.
- **North China**: the thanks are chiefly wine and meat, cakes and cloth, insisting on the “four-colour gift”.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: the thanks are called the “matchmaker’s gift”, usually including red packets and cakes.
- **Guangdong**: chiefly red packets (“the matchmaker’s lucky money”) with ceremonial cakes.
- **Ethnic minorities**: the intermediaries of marriage among some peoples (such as the “male matchmaker”, the “matchmaker woman” or a village elder) receive thanks in various forms, which must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Thank-offerings**: wine, meat (hocks), cakes, sweets, cloth, shoes, red packets.
- **The banquet**: the table of the “wine of thanks to the matchmaker”.
- **Related documents**: gift lists, invitations, the marriage contract (bearing the matchmaker’s signature).
