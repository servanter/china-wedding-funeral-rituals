# Tujia Marriage Customs

## 1. Definition

The Tujia are concentrated in western Hunan, western Hubei, south-eastern Chongqing and north-eastern Guizhou. The marriage custom is most marked by **“bridal lament” and “keeping the ten sisters company”**.

**Core elements**:

1. **“Bridal lament”** — the bride sings bridal-lament songs before marriage (a marker custom of the Tujia), thanking her parents and parting from kin and friends.
2. **“Keeping the ten sisters company” / “keeping the ten brothers company”** — before the wedding the bride and groom are each kept company by friends, singing antiphonally through the night.
3. **“Proposal” and “betrothal”** — a matchmaker is sent to propose, and the bride price sent (wine, meat, cloth, rice).
4. **“Blocking the door” and “antiphonal song”** — at the fetching the bride’s family sets the door-blocking rite.
5. **“Bowing in the hall” and “sitting in the curtain”** — the sequence is complete.
6. **“Return visit”** — the third-day homecoming.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the Tujia forebears (the Ba people) esteemed song and dance, in the lineage of the shamanistic air of the *Chuci* and Ba-Chu.
- **Tang and Song**: the Ba song-custom is recorded; the “bridal lament” tradition is gestated in the song-custom.
- **Ming and Qing**: the “Customs” sections of the gazetteers (the Yongshun prefectural gazetteer, the Shinan prefectural gazetteer) record the wedding rite and the bridal-lament custom.
- **Today**: the “bridal-lament songs” and the “*saye’erhe*” dance have entered intangible-heritage protection; the traditional custom survives in the Qingjiang and Youshui river basins.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. Proposal and matching the marriage.
2. Betrothal: sending the bride price and fixing the wedding day.
3. Bridal lament, keeping the ten sisters company (before the wedding).
4. Fetching the bride: blocking the door with antiphonal song, receiving the bride.
5. Bowing in the hall, sitting in the curtain, feasting guests.
6. Return visit.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **The Qingjiang basin (Changyang, Wufeng)**: the bridal-lament and “jump-funeral” customs are completely preserved.
- **Western Hunan (Longshan, Yongshun)**: living among the Miao, with Miao customs added.
- **South-eastern Chongqing (Youyang, Xiushan)**: close to Chongqing, customs like those of Sichuan-Chongqing.
- **North-eastern Guizhou (Tongren)**: living among the Dong and Miao, customs interpenetrate.
- **Relation to other peoples**: Tujia marriage custom is not同源 with the Han “Six Rites” and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: the bride price (wine, meat, cloth, rice), gongs and drums, song-hall ware.
- **Dress**: Tujia dress (the *xilankapu* brocade).
- **Documents**: the bride-price list, manuscripts of bridal-lament songs, the marriage contract.
