# Daur Marriage Customs

## 1. Definition

The Daur are concentrated in Hulunbuir of Inner Mongolia (Molidawa and others), Qiqihar of Heilongjiang and Tacheng of Xinjiang. Marriage practises **exogamy beyond the clan**; the wedding is marked by **the “*cha’ente*” (bride price), the “blocking-the-door antiphonal song”**.

**Core elements**:

1. **“Proposal” and “betrothal”** — a matchmaker is sent to propose; the “*cha’ente*” (bride price, including horses, cattle, pigs and wine) is sent.
2. **“Blocking-the-door antiphonal song”** — at the fetching the bride’s family sets the blocking-the-door rite; the groom’s side enters only by antiphonal song.
3. **“Bowing to heaven and earth and worshipping the ancestors”** — the obeisances are performed and the ancestors reported to.
4. **“Shaman blessing” (old custom)** — a shaman was invited to bless; later, under Buddhist influence, a lama might also be asked to chant.
5. **“Return visit”** — returning home on the third day.
6. **“Uxorilocal residence”** — families without sons often take in a son-in-law, whose status is clearly defined.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Ming and Qing**: the Daur (“Dahur”) were distributed in the Heilongjiang basin; the custom is recorded in ethnography.
- **Qing dynasty**: they moved south to the Nenjiang basin; mixing with the Manchu, Han and Ewenki, the customs influenced one another.
- **The Xinjiang Daur (Tacheng)**: they moved west to garrison the frontier in the Qianlong reign; the custom is adjacent to that of the local peoples.
- **Today**: the traditional custom survives in the concentrated areas.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. Proposal and betrothal (sending the *cha’ente*).
2. Fixing the wedding day.
3. Fetching the bride: the blocking-the-door antiphonal song.
4. The wedding: bowing to heaven and earth, worshipping the ancestors, feasting guests.
5. Return visit: returning home on the third day.
6. Uxorilocal residence (for families without sons).

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Molidawa of Inner Mongolia, Qiqihar of Heilongjiang**: concentrated areas, with more of the old custom preserved.
- **Tacheng of Xinjiang**: a branch that moved west, with custom adjacent to the Kazakh and Mongol.
- **Relation to other peoples**: Daur marriage custom is not同源 with the Han “Six Rites” and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: the bride price (horses, cattle, pigs, wine), the big-wheeled cart, horse gear.
- **Dress**: Daur dress (the fur robe, embroidered).
- **Documents**: the bride-price list, the marriage contract (the marriage certificate).
