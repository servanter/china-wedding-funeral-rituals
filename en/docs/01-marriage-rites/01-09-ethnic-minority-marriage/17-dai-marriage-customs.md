# Dai Marriage Customs

## 1. Definition

The Dai are distributed in Xishuangbanna, Dehong, Pu’er and Lincang of Yunnan, and believe in **Southern Buddhism**. The marriage custom is marked by **choosing a mate by “throwing the pouch”, “uxorilocal residence” (the son-in-law entering the wife’s home) and the thread-tying blessing**.

**Core elements**:

1. **“Throwing the pouch” and “going to the village”** — young men and women make friends by “throwing the pouch” and antiphonal song.
2. **“Proposal” and “betrothal”** — the groom’s family sends a matchmaker to propose; on the bride’s assent the bride price is discussed.
3. **“Uxorilocal residence” (entering the wife’s home)** — after the wedding the man first lives in the wife’s home (one to several years), then takes his wife back to his own home or stays long-term uxorilocal (most common in Xishuangbanna).
4. **The “thread-tying” blessing** — at the wedding an elder ties white thread to the wrists of bride and groom and recites a blessing; the core rite.
5. **“Pouring water” in dedication** — at the wedding there is also “water-pouring” prayer for blessing.
6. **“Sacrificing to the village god”** — the wedding is tied to the village sacrifice.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Tang and Song**: Southern Buddhism entered via South-east Asia and combined with the Dai native faith.
- **Yuan, Ming and Qing**: under the tusi system the village community and the monastery jointly took part in weddings; the local gazetteers record the custom.
- **Modern times**: the “uxorilocal residence” and “thread-tying” customs continue.
- **Today**: town weddings are simplified; the “thread-tying” and the Water-splashing Festival run side by side as festive weddings.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. Throwing the pouch and antiphonal song to make friends.
2. Proposal and betrothal (discussing the bride price).
3. The wedding: the thread-tying blessing, water-pouring prayer.
4. Uxorilocal residence: the man first enters the wife’s home (as agreed).
5. Wedding banquet: the village joins in congratulation.
6. Moving to the husband’s home or long-term uxorilocal residence.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Xishuangbanna (the Dai Lü)**: the “uxorilocal residence” and thread-tying are complete, with a strong Buddhist colour.
- **Dehong (the Dai Na)**: living among the Jingpo and Achang, customs interpenetrate.
- **Pu’er, Lincang**: the branches differ in custom.
- **Relation to other peoples**: Dai marriage custom is not同源 with the Han “Six Rites” and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: white thread (for tying), silver ornaments, the bride price (cloth, wine, wax tapers), the water-pouring vessel.
- **Dress**: the Dai skirt, the silver belt.
- **Documents**: the bride-price list, the marriage contract (the marriage certificate).
