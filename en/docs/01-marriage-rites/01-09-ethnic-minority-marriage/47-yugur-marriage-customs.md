# Yugur Marriage Customs

## 1. Definition

The Yugur are concentrated in the Sunan Yugur Autonomous County of Zhangye, Gansu, and are also distributed in Huangnibao of Jiuquan. The marriage custom is marked by **"putting on the head-ornament in the tent", antiphonal song with toasts and Buddhist blessing**.

**Core elements**:

1. **"Putting on the head-ornament in the tent"** — between the ages of fifteen and seventeen a girl undergoes the "head-ornament-donning" rite, after which free socialising is permitted; it is an important prelude to marriage.
2. **"Proposal and betrothal"** — a matchmaker proposes; wine, tea and a khata are sent to seal the betrothal.
3. **"Antiphonal song with toasts"** — at the wedding the singers of the two sides sing in response and offer toasts, often through the night.
4. **"Seeing off and fetching the bride"** — the bride's party rides to the groom's home, singing along the way.
5. **"Worshipping the Buddha and praying"** — before and after the wedding they worship the Buddha and invite lamas to chant scriptures for blessing.
6. **"Return visit"** — after the wedding the bride returns to her natal home to pay respects.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Yuan and Ming**: the Yugur (the "Sariq Uyghur") appear in the *Ming shi* and the Hexi gazetteers.
- **Modern period**: the "head-ornament-donning in the tent" and such are recorded in ethnic-survey materials.
- **Contemporary**: the traditional Sunan Yugur wedding is valued under intangible-heritage protection.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. Put on the head-ornament in the tent (coming-of-age rite).
2. Propose and betroth.
3. Send the bride-price and choose the date.
4. See off and fetch the bride (riding and singing).
5. Worship the Buddha, banquet toasts.
6. Return visit.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Sunan (Kangle, Huangcheng)**: the Yugur core area, where the custom is best preserved.
- **Huangnibao of Jiuquan**: intermingled with the Han and Mongol; customs blend.
- **Relation to other peoples**: Yugur marriage custom does not share a common origin with the Han "Six Rites" and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: the head-ornament, khata, wine, tea, horses.
- **Dress**: Yugur high-collared long robes, head-ornament, boots and caps.
- **Documents**: the marriage contract, the gift list.
