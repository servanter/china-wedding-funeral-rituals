# Pumi Marriage Customs

## 1. Definition

The Pumi are distributed in Ninglang, Lanping, Lijiang and Weixi of Yunnan and in Muli and Yanyuan of Sichuan. The marriage custom is marked by **cross-cousin-preference marriage, carrying the bride across the hearth and the “*sulima*” wine rite**.

**Core elements**:

1. **“Cross-cousin-preference marriage”** — the son of the maternal uncle has the first right to marry the daughter of the paternal aunt; if he does not, an “uncle’s gift” must be sent in compensation.
2. **“The betrothal-wine rite”** — the groom’s family takes wine, tea and preserved-pork fat to the bride’s home to fix the betrothal.
3. **“Carrying the bride”** — the bride is carried out of the door onto a horse by her brother or uncle, “not touching the ground with her feet”.
4. **“Crossing the hearth and honouring the *guozhuang*”** — on entering the husband’s home the bride crosses the hearth and salutes the *guozhuang* (the house god); the core rite of the wedding.
5. **The “*sulima*” and preserved-pork fat** — at the wedding banquet they drink “*sulima*” (yellow wine) and eat preserved-pork fat.
6. **“Remnants of the visiting marriage”** — part of the Ninglang Pumi, adjacent to the Mosuo, keep remnants of the visiting form of marriage.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Yuan, Ming and Qing**: the Pumi (the “Xifan”, “Ba”) marriage custom is recorded in the *Yuan shi* and the north-western-Yunnan local gazetteers.
- **Modern times**: the cross-cousin marriage and hearth worship are recorded in ethnic-survey materials.
- **Today**: the traditional custom is preserved in Lanping and Ninglang.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. Proposal and matching the marriage.
2. Betrothal: sending wine, tea and preserved-pork fat.
3. Fetching the bride: the fetching party goes to the bride’s home to sing antiphonally.
4. Carrying the bride onto the horse, not touching the ground.
5. Crossing the hearth, saluting the *guozhuang*.
6. Wedding banquet (*sulima*), return visit.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Lanping, Weixi**: Pumi concentrated areas, with the custom fairly completely preserved.
- **Ninglang (around Lugu Lake)**: adjacent to the Mosuo, with remnants of the visiting marriage.
- **Muli, Yanyuan of Sichuan**: living among the Tibetan, Yi and Naxi, customs interpenetrate.
- **Relation to other peoples**: Pumi marriage custom is not同源 with the Han “Six Rites” and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: the wine rite (*sulima*), preserved-pork fat, tea, horse gear, hearth offerings.
- **Dress**: the Pumi sheep-skin cape, silver ornaments, head-wrap.
- **Documents**: the marriage contract, the gift list.
