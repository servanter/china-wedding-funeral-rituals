# Bai Marriage Customs

## 1. Definition

The Bai are concentrated in the Dali Bai Autonomous Prefecture of Yunnan. The marriage custom is **multi-influenced by the Han ritual system, Buddhism (the Azhali sect) and the native faith**, and the observances are elaborate.

**Core elements**:

1. **“Proposal” and “betrothal”** — a matchmaker is sent to propose, and the bride price sent (tea, wine, cloth, silver ornaments); close to Han usage yet with its own standards.
2. **“Bridal lament”** — the bride laments before marriage.
3. **“Fetching the bride” and “bowing in the hall”** — fetched by bridal chair, bowing to heaven and earth and to the “*ben zhu*” (the village patron god).
4. **“Reporting to the *ben zhu*”** — at the wedding the village “*ben zhu*” (the protective god) is sacrificed to and reported to.
5. **“The three-course tea” for guests** — the wedding banquet treats guests with the “three-course tea” of “first bitter, then sweet, then aftertaste”.
6. **“Return visit”** — the third-day homecoming.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **The Nanzhao and Dali-kingdom periods**: Buddhism (the Esoteric Azhali sect) flourished, and the wedding took on a religious colour.
- **Yuan, Ming and Qing**: influenced by Han culture and Daoism, the ritual system grew complete; the gazetteers record the wedding rite and the “*ben zhu*” sacrifice.
- **Today**: town weddings predominate; the villages keep the *ben zhu* sacrifice and the three-course tea.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. Proposal and matching the marriage.
2. Betrothal: sending the bride price and fixing the wedding day.
3. Bridal lament: the bride laments before marriage.
4. Fetching the bride: fetched by bridal chair; bowing in the hall and reporting to the *ben zhu*.
5. Wedding banquet: the three-course tea, feasting guests.
6. Return visit: the third-day homecoming.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **The Dali flatlands**: regular observances, with the *ben zhu* faith combined with the wedding.
- **Eryuan and Jianchuan**: living among the Han and Yi, customs interpenetrate.
- **The Nujiang and Baoshan Bai (the Lemu, Nama)**: the branch customs are more archaic.
- **Relation to other peoples**: Bai custom blends with the Han, but the procedure and the faith-basis differ, and it must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: the bridal chair, the bride price (tea, wine, cloth), the three-course-tea ware, the *ben zhu* offerings.
- **Dress**: Bai dress (the “wind-flower-snow-moon” headdress).
- **Documents**: the horoscope card, the bride-price list, the marriage contract.
