# Uyghur Marriage Customs

## 1. Definition

The Uyghur wedding combines **Islamic marriage norms** with the people’s own song-and-dance tradition; its core is **the ahong reciting the “Nikah” and the couple sharing salt-water naan**.

**Core elements**:

1. **The “Nikah” ceremony** — presided over by the ahong, who recites scripture and asks both parties whether they consent; the couple share naan dipped in salt water (the “salt” symbolising what cannot be broken, the “naan” being the food of life) — the most symbolic rite.
2. **“Proposal” and “betrothal”** — the groom sends a matchmaker to propose and gives the bride price (cloth, jewellery, sheep).
3. **The “Meshrep”** — a song-and-dance evening at the wedding, with kin and friends playing and dancing, a warm atmosphere.
4. **The wedding banquet (“*toy*”)** — a feast for guests, centred on pilaf, roast naan and mutton.
5. **The “three-day rite”** — the “return visit” is performed three days after the wedding.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **The Kara-Khanid period**: Islam entered, and the marriage norms fused with the local custom.
- **Ming and Qing**: under the Yarkent Khanate and others, the wedding’s song-dance tradition (the Twelve Muqam) flourished.
- **Modern times**: city weddings simplified; the religious rite and the song-dance celebration go side by side.
- **Today**: the “Nikah” and civil registration go side by side; the Meshrep is on the intangible-heritage list.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Proposal**: the groom sends a matchmaker to propose; on the bride’s “assent” the bride price is discussed.
2. **Betrothal (fixing the engagement)**: the bride price is sent, the engagement made, the wedding day fixed.
3. **Nikah**: the ahong recites the Nikah; the couple share the salt-water naan.
4. **Meshrep**: a song-and-dance evening, with kin and friends congratulating.
5. **Wedding banquet**: a feast for guests (pilaf, mutton, naan).
6. **Three-day rite / return visit**: the homecoming three days after the wedding.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Southern Xinjiang (Kashgar, Hotan, Aksu)**: intense religious life, solemn Meshrep.
- **Northern Xinjiang (Urumqi, Ili)**: town weddings simplified, song and dance still flourishing.
- **Relation to other peoples**: the Islamic-wedding peoples differ in detail; not同源 with the Han “Six Rites”, and it must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: the salt-water naan, pilaf, the bride price (cloth, jewellery, sheep), instruments (dutar, rawap, frame drum).
- **Dress**: the *Atlas* silk skirt, the small cap (*doppa*).
- **Documents**: the bride-price list, the marriage certificate.
