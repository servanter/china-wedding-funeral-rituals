# Miao Marriage Customs

## 1. Definition

The Miao are widely distributed (Guizhou, Yunnan, Hunan, Guangxi, Chongqing, Sichuan and others) with many branches and great differences in custom. The shared features are **choosing a mate by song, treating guests with wine, and “not settling in the husband’s home” among some branches**.

**Core elements**:

1. **“*Youfang*”, “flower dancing”, “autumn fair”** — young men and women choose mates by antiphonal song (the “*youfang*”), a form of pre-marital socialising.
2. **“Proposal” and “betrothal”** — a matchmaker is sent to propose, and the bride price sent (wine, meat, silver ornaments, clothing).
3. **“The door-blocking wine”** — at the fetching the bride’s family sets the “door-blocking wine”; the fetchers must drink and sing antiphonally to enter.
4. **“Not settling in the husband’s home”** — in some branches the new wife still lives at her mother’s home after the wedding, moving to the husband’s permanently after bearing children or after some years.
5. **“Sisters’ songs” and “bridal lament”** — girl friends keep the bride company singing sisters’ songs, and the bride laments.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin to Tang-Song**: the Miao forebears (“San Miao”, “Southern Man”) have their marriage custom recorded in histories.
- **Ming and Qing**: the “Miao customs” sections of the gazetteers (the Guizhou and Hunan general gazetteers) record the wedding rite and the “*youfang*” custom.
- **Modern times**: Christianity entered some Miao areas, and a few families hold church weddings.
- **Today**: the traditional custom survives in south-eastern Guizhou and western Hunan; the “*youfang*” and the “Sisters’ Festival” have entered the intangible-heritage lists.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Choosing a mate by *youfang***: young men and women meet and fall in love by antiphonal song.
2. **Proposal**: the groom sends a matchmaker to propose.
3. **Betrothal**: the bride price is discussed and the wedding day fixed.
4. **Fetching the bride**: the fetching party comes to the bride’s home, drinks the “door-blocking wine” and sings antiphonally.
5. **The wedding**: meeting the elders, feasting guests (playing the *lusheng*, singing).
6. **“Not settling” (some branches) → settling in the husband’s home**: moving into the husband’s home as children are born or by agreed time.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **South-eastern Guizhou (Taijiang, Leishan, Jianhe)**: a strong “*youfang*” and “Sisters’ Festival” tradition.
- **Western Hunan (the Xiangxi prefecture)**: living among the Tujia and Han, the custom blends.
- **North-western Guizhou and north-eastern Yunnan (Bijie, Zhaotong)**: close to the Yi, with mutual influence.
- **Southern Yunnan and northern Guangxi**: living among the Zhuang, Yao and Bouyei.
- **Relation to other peoples**: Miao marriage custom is not同源 with the Han “Six Rites” and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: the *lusheng* (reed pipe), silver ornaments, rice wine, glutinous rice, the bride price (wine, meat, cloth).
- **Dress**: the Miao embroidered finery, the silver crown and ornaments.
- **Documents**: the bride-price list, the marriage contract (today the marriage certificate).
