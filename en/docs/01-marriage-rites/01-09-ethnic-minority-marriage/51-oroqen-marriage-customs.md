# Oroqen Marriage Customs

## 1. Definition

The Oroqen are distributed across the Oroqen Autonomous Banner of Hulunbuir, Inner Mongolia, and Tahe, Huma and Heihe of Heilongjiang. The marriage custom is marked by **clan-exogamous marriage, hunting horses as bride-price and bonfire song-and-dance**.

**Core elements**:

1. **Clan-exogamous marriage** — no marriage within the same clan; a matchmaker proposes and brokers the match.
2. **"Hunting horses as bride-price"** — the bride-price is mainly hunting horses and game.
3. **"Proposal and betrothal"** — the groom brings wine and game to the bride's home to propose.
4. **"Bonfire and song-dance"** — the wedding is held around a bonfire with singing and dancing.
5. **"Survivals of bride-capture"** — the old custom included a symbolic "bride-capture" at the bride-fetching.
6. **"Shamanic blessing"** — in the old custom a shaman was invited to bless the couple.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Qing dynasty**: the Oroqen (the "Erlunchun") appear in the *Heilongjiang waixi* and the gazetteers.
- **Modern period**: the hunter custom is recorded in ethnic-survey materials.
- **Contemporary**: the traditional wedding of the Oroqen Autonomous Banner is presented in cultural activities and intangible-heritage protection.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. Matchmaker's proposal.
2. Betroth and discuss the bride-price (hunting horses).
3. Fetch the bride.
4. Bonfire rite, shamanic blessing.
5. Banquet song-and-dance.
6. Return visit.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Oroqen Autonomous Banner**: the Oroqen core area, where the custom is best preserved.
- **Tahe, Huma and Heihe of Heilongjiang**: intermingled with the Han and Manchu; customs blend.
- **Relation to other peoples**: Oroqen marriage custom does not share a common origin with the Han "Six Rites" and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: hunting horses, hunting guns, birch-bark vessels, animal hides.
- **Dress**: Oroqen roe-deer-skin robes, leather boots, head ornaments.
- **Documents**: the marriage contract, the gift list (in old times mainly an oral agreement).
