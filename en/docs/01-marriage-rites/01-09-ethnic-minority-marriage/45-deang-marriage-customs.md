# De'ang Marriage Customs

## 1. Definition

The De'ang are mainly distributed across Dehong of Yunnan (Mangshi, Ruili, Longchuan) and Zhenkang and Gengma of Lincang. The marriage custom is marked by **"visiting the girls", the Theravada-Buddhist thread-tying blessing and post-wedding "uxorilocal residence"**.

**Core elements**:

1. **"Visiting the girls"** — young men go at night to the girls' bamboo houses to "visit the girls" and sing in antiphonal song.
2. **"Tea as the gift"** — the proposal and betrothal send tea and tobacco; "sour tea" is a De'ang speciality.
3. **"Thread-tying blessing"** — under Theravada Buddhist influence, at the wedding elders tie cotton thread to bless the couple.
4. **"Uxorilocal residence"** — after the wedding the groom lives in the bride's home (for a number of years) in the old custom.
5. **"Worshipping the Buddha and splashing water"** — before and after the wedding they worship the Buddha; the "Water-splashing Festival" is a node for mate choice.
6. **"Sour tea and areca to entertain guests"** — the banquet serves sour tea and areca (betel) nut to guests.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Ming and Qing**: the De'ang (old name "Benglong") appear in the *Dianlüe* and the west-Yunnan gazetteers.
- **Modern period**: "visiting the girls" and "uxorilocal residence" are recorded in ethnic-survey materials.
- **Contemporary**: the traditional custom is preserved in places such as Santai Mountain of Mangshi.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. Visit the girls and sing in antiphonal song.
2. Propose and send tea to pledge the betrothal.
3. Betroth and discuss the bride-price.
4. Fetch the bride, thread-tying blessing.
5. Uxorilocal residence (living with the wife's family).
6. Banquet and worship of the Buddha.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Santai Mountain of Mangshi**: a De'ang core area, where the custom is best preserved.
- **Zhenkang and Gengma of Lincang**: intermingled with the Dai and Wa; customs blend.
- **Relation to other peoples**: De'ang marriage custom does not share a common origin with the Han "Six Rites" and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: sour tea, areca (betel) nut, tobacco, Buddhist-offering vessels.
- **Dress**: De'ang tube skirts, silver ornaments, head wraps.
- **Documents**: the marriage contract, the gift list.
