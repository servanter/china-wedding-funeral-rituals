# Korean Marriage Customs

## 1. Definition

The Koreans are distributed mainly in Jilin, Heilongjiang and Liaoning; their marriage custom is **the most deeply influenced by Zhu Xi’s *Family Rituals***, and is the one among the ethnic groups closest to the Confucian wedding system.

**Core elements**:

1. **“Discussion” and “*nacai*”** — a matchmaker is sent to discuss the match; the groom’s family sends the “*nacai*” (the four-pillar card and the wedding-request letter).
2. **The “groom’s wedding” is held at the bride’s home** — the groom first goes to the bride’s home to hold the wedding (the “personal reception” is completed there).
3. **The “wild-goose rite” (*jeon an*)** — the groom offers a wooden goose to the bride’s family (the “goose offering”), carrying the vestige of the ancient rite.
4. **“Mutual bowing” and “*hejin*”** — the couple bow to each other and share the cup.
5. **The “*bibeok* rite”** — the bride offers gifts to her parents-in-law (the “*bibeok*”), meeting the elders.
6. **“Returning on the third day” and “the second rite”** — three days after the wedding the groom returns first, then the bride is fetched to the husband’s home (“returning on the third day”).

## 2. Historical Origins

- **The Joseon period**: Zhu Xi’s *Family Rituals* was the state ritual model, and the wedding was highly institutionalised, transplanted to China’s north-east with the migrants.
- **Ming, Qing and modern times**: the Koreans moved into the north-east, bringing the wedding together with the *Family Rituals* text.
- **Today**: the custom is much simplified, mostly held in halls or hotels; forms such as the “*bibeok* rite” and “returning on the third day” survive in traditional weddings.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Discussion**: a matchmaker discusses the match and the horoscopes are compared (reading the “*gonghap*”).
2. **Nacai**: the groom’s family sends the wedding-request letter and the four-pillar card.
3. **Personal reception (the groom’s wedding)**: the groom goes to the bride’s home for the goose offering, mutual bowing and the shared cup.
4. **Bibeok rite**: the bride offers gifts to her parents-in-law.
5. **Returning on the third day**: three days after the wedding the groom returns first, then the bride is fetched to the husband’s home.
6. **Sacrifice and return visit**: reporting to the ancestors, and the homecoming visit.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **The Yanbian Korean Autonomous Prefecture**: a concentrated community, with more of the traditional custom preserved.
- **The Heilongjiang and Liaoning communities**: living among the Han, the ceremony is simplified.
- **Urban Koreans**: hotel weddings predominate, with the family custom continued.
- **Relation to other peoples**: Koreans and Han both belong to the Confucian wedding tradition, but the procedure and terminology differ, and it must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: the wooden goose (for the goose offering), the shared-cup cups, the bride price, the Korean wedding dress.
- **Dress**: the bride’s “green coat and red skirt”, the groom’s ceremonial dress.
- **Documents**: the wedding-request letter, the four-pillar card, the marriage letter, the genealogy.
