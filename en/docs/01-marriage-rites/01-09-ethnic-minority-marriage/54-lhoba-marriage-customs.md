# Lhoba Marriage Customs

## 1. Definition

The Lhoba are distributed across Mainling, Medog, Lhünzê and Zayü of Tibet. The marriage custom is marked by **the old form of bride-price marriage, shaman presiding and endogamy within the estate rank**.

**Core elements**:

1. **Old "bride-price marriage" custom** — in old times the bride-price was cattle, knives and bead ornaments, and the amount fixed the marriage (an old custom, to be critically noted).
2. **"Endogamy within the estate rank"** — in the old custom the upper and commoner estates did not intermarry (an old custom, not to be encouraged).
3. **"Shaman presiding"** — the wedding is presided over by a "niubu" or "miji" (shaman) who offers sacrifice to the gods.
4. **"Slaughtering beasts to entertain guests"** — at the wedding pigs and oxen are slaughtered and shared by the whole village.
5. **"Survivals of bride-capture"** — the old custom included a symbolic "bride-capture" at the bride-fetching.
6. **"Survivals of polygyny"** — in the old custom wealthy households practised polygyny.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Qing dynasty**: the Lhoba (the "Loyu" and others) appear in Tibetan-language documents and the *Xizang zhi*.
- **Modern period**: bride-price marriage and endogamy within the estate rank are recorded in ethnic-survey materials.
- **Contemporary**: the traditional Lhoba custom is documented under intangible-heritage protection and ethnic surveys.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. Propose and discuss the bride-price.
2. Betroth and send the bride-price (cattle, knives, bead ornaments).
3. Choose the date and prepare the gifts.
4. Fetch the bride (including a symbolic "bride-capture").
5. Shaman presides over the god-offering, beasts are slaughtered.
6. Feast, song and dance.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Nanyi of Mainling**: a Lhoba core area, where the custom is best preserved.
- **Medog, Lhünzê, Zayü**: intermingled with the Monba and Tibetan; customs blend.
- **Relation to other peoples**: Lhoba marriage custom does not share a common origin with the Han "Six Rites" and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: cattle, Lhoba knives, bead ornaments ("bajiao"), god-offering vessels.
- **Dress**: Lhoba pulu long robes, rattan hats, bamboo ornaments.
- **Documents**: the marriage contract, the gift list (no writing in old times, secured by oral and material tokens).
