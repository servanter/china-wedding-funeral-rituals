# Yi Marriage Customs

## 1. Definition

The Yi are distributed mainly in Liangshan of Sichuan, and in Yunnan, Guizhou and Guangxi. Traditional marriage practises **exogamy beyond the clan** (no marriage within the same clan); the ceremony mingles “bridal lament”, “splashing water” and “sword dance”.

**Core elements**:

1. **“Exogamy beyond the clan”** — marriage must be contracted across clans; the offender is sanctioned by clan rules.
2. **“Proposal” and “betrothal”** — a matchmaker is sent to propose; the bride price centres on cattle, sheep and silver ornaments, and is heavy.
3. **“Bridal lament” and “splashing water”** — the bride laments; the fetchers are “splashed with water” at the bride’s home (a playful rite).
4. **“Carrying the bride”** — the bride is carried out of the door by a brother or a bearer.
5. **“Sword dance” / “left-foot dance”** — at the wedding all join in song and dance (the “*dage*” of the western-Yunnan Yi).
6. **“Not settling in the husband’s home” (some regions)** — the new wife stays at her mother’s home for a time, then settles in the husband’s.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Han and Tang**: the Yi forebears (“Sou”, “Cuan” and others) have their marriage custom recorded in histories.
- **Yuan, Ming and Qing**: the tusi system and the clan system ran side by side; the custom is recorded in the gazetteers’ “barbarian customs” sections.
- **Modern times**: in Liangshan the clan system and marriage were closely bound, and marriage had the meaning of a clan alliance.
- **Today**: the Liangshan custom is preserved in cultural protection, and autonomy in marriage has increased.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Speaking the match (proposal)**: a matchmaker is sent; it must be exogamy beyond the clan.
2. **Betrothal**: the bride price (cattle, sheep, silver ornaments, cloth) is discussed and the wedding day fixed.
3. **Bridal lament**: before the wedding the bride laments and sings antiphonally with her girl friends.
4. **Fetching the bride**: the fetching party comes to the bride’s home and receives the “splashing water”; the bride is carried out.
5. **The wedding**: meeting the elders, feasting guests, song and dance (*dage*).
6. **Return visit / not settling**: the homecoming or a temporary stay at the mother’s home.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Sichuan Liangshan**: the clan system and bride-price rules are most typical.
- **Yunnan (Chuxiong, Honghe, Dali)**: mutual influence with the Bai and Hani customs; the “*dage*” flourishes.
- **Guizhou (Bijie, Liupanshui)**: close to the Han, with traces of Han custom.
- **Guangxi and the Yunnan border**: living among the Zhuang and Miao, customs interpenetrate.
- **Relation to other peoples**: the Yi clan marriage system is not同源 with the Han “Six Rites” and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: the bride price (cattle, sheep, silver ornaments), the wool cape, wine, the hearth (the wedding venue).
- **Dress**: Yi dress (the *cha’erwa* cape, the head-wrap).
- **Documents**: the bride-price list, the marriage contract (today the marriage certificate).
