# Mongolian Marriage Customs

## 1. Definition

The Mongols are distributed mainly in the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region, with others in the north-east and north-west provinces. Traditional marriage follows the principle of **exogamy beyond the clan and the tribe**; the ceremony lays stress on “offering the *hada*, toasting mare’s-milk wine, and sacrificing to the fire”.

**Core elements**:

1. **The *hada* and mare’s-milk wine as gifts** — the proposal, betrothal and bride-fetching all use the *hada*, mare’s-milk wine and a whole sheep as gifts.
2. **“Blocking the door” and the vestige of “bridal capture”** — at the fetching the bride’s family “shuts the door” to make difficulty, and the two sides answer with song and play; the wedding has symbolic “capture” games such as “snatching the *hada*” and “seizing the hat”.
3. **“Worshipping the fire” (the fire sacrifice)** — after the bride enters her husband’s home she circles the fire three times and offers sacrifice to the fire god; the core rite of the wedding.
4. **The “whole-sheep banquet” (*ucha*)** — the wedding feast treats guests to a whole sheep, with milk foods and milk tea.
5. **Fetching the bride on horseback** — the groom leads groomsmen on horseback to the bride’s home, the procession following tribal rules.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **The Mongol-Yuan period**: the steppe marriage custom is recorded in envoys’ travel accounts and histories; the custom of “offering the *hada*” spread with Tibetan Buddhism.
- **Ming and Qing**: the marriage customs of the various leagues and banners gradually took shape, with marked tribal differences (Ordos, Khorchin and Bargut weddings each have their features).
- **Modern times**: settlement and changes in pastoral life simplified weddings.
- **Today**: the traditional pastoral wedding is revived in cultural preservation; city weddings are mostly a Chinese-Western blend.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Proposal**: the groom’s family sends a matchmaker with a *hada* and mare’s-milk wine to propose.
2. **Betrothal**: on the bride’s family’s assent, a *hada* is offered and the bride price discussed (horses, cattle, sheep, cloth).
3. **Fetching the bride**: the groom leads groomsmen on horseback; the bride’s family “blocks the door” with song.
4. **Worshipping the fire and heaven**: the bride circles the fire three times, sacrifices to fire and heaven, and makes obeisance.
5. **Wedding banquet**: the “whole-sheep banquet” is laid, with song and dance (long songs, the *andai* dance).
6. **Return visit**: on a chosen day after the wedding, the homecoming visit.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Khorchin (east)**: deeply influenced by Manchu and Han, with some Han observances.
- **Ordos**: the wedding keeps ancient customs such as “asking the name and the birth data” and “offering the sheep and toasting”.
- **Bargut and Buryat (Hulunbuir)**: marked tribal features, archaic ceremony.
- **Qinghai and Gansu Mongols**: deeper influence of Tibetan Buddhism, with a Tibetan colouring to the wedding.
- **Relation to other peoples**: Mongolian marriage custom is not同源 with the Han “Six Rites” and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: the *hada*, mare’s-milk wine, the whole sheep (*ucha*), horse gear, the yurt (the wedding venue).
- **Dress**: the Mongolian robe, sash and headdress (the bride’s “*tou dai*”).
- **Documents**: the bride-price list, the marriage contract (today the marriage certificate).
