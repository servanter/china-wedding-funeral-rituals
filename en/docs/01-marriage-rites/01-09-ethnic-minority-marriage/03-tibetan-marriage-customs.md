# Tibetan Marriage Customs

## 1. Definition

The Tibetans are distributed mainly in Tibet and the Tibetan areas of Qinghai, Sichuan, Gansu and Yunnan. The forms of marriage were historically diverse (mainly **monogamy**, with polyandry and polygyny in some regions); the core custom is **offering the *hada*, inviting a monk to choose an auspicious day and to chant scripture**.

**Core elements**:

1. **Proposal with the *hada*** — the groom’s family sends a matchmaker with a *hada* and barley wine to propose.
2. **Choosing the day and chanting scripture** — a monk is invited to choose an auspicious day and chant for blessing.
3. **“Blocking the door” at the fetching** — the bride’s family sets the “blocking the door” rite; the fetchers answer with wine and song.
4. **“Singing the proposal song” and “antiphonal song”** — song and dance at the wedding (the *guozhuang* and others).
5. **Toasting and offering the *hada*** — the whole wedding uses barley wine, butter tea and the *hada* as gifts.
6. **“Residence with the wife’s family” and “residence with the husband’s family”** — as the family arranges (uxorilocal residence is fairly common in Tibetan areas, and the status of the live-in son-in-law is not low).

## 2. Historical Origins

- **The Tibetan imperial period**: Tibetan documents record the rites of royal and aristocratic alliance by marriage, with equal weight on oath and gift.
- **After the later spread of the doctrine**: Tibetan Buddhism penetrated society deeply, and weddings often invite monks to chant.
- **Ming, Qing and modern times**: under the local theocratic system the marriage forms were diverse.
- **Today**: monogamy is general; the traditional custom is preserved in farming and pastoral areas.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Proposal**: the matchmaker offers the *hada* and barley wine to propose.
2. **Betrothal**: the bride price (cattle, horses, *pulu* woollen, silver ornaments) and the wedding day are agreed.
3. **Fetching the bride**: the groom forms a fetching party; the bride’s family “blocks the door” and gives a feast.
4. **The wedding**: scripture is chanted for blessing, the *hada* offered, wine toasted, song and dance (the *guozhuang*).
5. **Entering the husband’s home**: the bride enters and meets the elders and the Buddhist niche.
6. **Return visit**: the homecoming after the wedding.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Ü-Tsang (Lhasa, Shigatse)**: regular observances, equal weight on monk and lay.
- **Amdo (Qinghai, Gansu, north-western Sichuan)**: pastoral-area weddings, with solemn song-dance and gift-giving.
- **Kham (Ganzi in Sichuan, Diqing in Yunnan)**: weddings with Kham features (*guozhuang*, *xianzi*).
- **Relation to other peoples**: Tibetan marriage custom is not同源 with the Han “Six Rites” and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: the *hada*, barley wine, butter tea, *pulu* woollen, silver ornaments, the Buddhist niche.
- **Dress**: the Tibetan robe, the *bangdian* (apron), headdress (coral, turquoise).
- **Documents**: the marriage contract, the bride-price list, the marriage certificate.
