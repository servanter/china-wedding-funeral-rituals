# Uzbek Marriage Customs

## 1. Definition

The Uzbek are distributed across Yining, Urumqi, Kashgar and Shache of Xinjiang. The marriage custom is marked by **the Islamic "Nikah", the lifting of the bridal veil and the "meshrep" song-dance gathering**.

**Core elements**:

1. **The "Nikah"** — an ahong presides and recites the "Nikah", which is the essential condition for the marriage to be established.
2. **"Proposal and betrothal"** — the groom's mother brings gifts to the bride's home to propose; after betrothal the bride-price is sent.
3. **"Lifting the veil"** — at the wedding the "veil-lifting song" is sung and the groom lifts the bride's veil.
4. **The "meshrep"** — a song-and-dance gathering is held at the banquet, accompanied by the frame drum and the rawap.
5. **"Pilaf banquet"** — guests are served "poron" (pilaf), naan and milk tea.
6. **"Three-day rite"** — the wedding includes the bride-fetching, the veil-lifting and the return visit.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Ming and Qing**: Uzbek merchants entered Xinjiang via the Silk Road; the custom is recorded in the *Xiyu wenjian lu* and such works.
- **Modern period**: concentrated in Yining and Kashgar, weddings followed Islamic law.
- **Contemporary**: urban Uzbek weddings are mostly simplified, while traditional rites survive in family transmission.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. Proposal (the groom's mother leads relatives with tea and sugar).
2. Betrothal and discussion of the bride-price.
3. Nikah (presided over by the ahong).
4. Fetching the bride, wedding banquet (meshrep).
5. Lifting the veil.
6. Return visit.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Yining**: an Uzbek core area, where the custom is best preserved.
- **Kashgar, Shache**: close intercourse with the Uyghur; customs are similar.
- **Urumqi**: urban weddings tend to be simplified.
- **Relation to other peoples**: Uzbek marriage custom does not share a common origin with the Han "Six Rites" and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: the frame drum, the rawap, pilaf (poron), naan, ornaments.
- **Dress**: embroidered caps, long skirts, waistcoats.
- **Documents**: the religious marriage contract (Nikah document), the gift list.
