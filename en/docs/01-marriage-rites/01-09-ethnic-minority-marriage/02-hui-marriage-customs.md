# Hui Marriage Customs

## 1. Definition

The Hui wedding is grounded in **Islamic marriage norms**; its core is **the ahong officiating, the free consent of both parties, and the reciting of the “Nikah”**.

**Core elements**:

1. **The “Nikah”** — the witnessing ceremony presided over by the ahong, who recites the wedding scripture and asks both parties whether they consent freely.
2. **The bride price (*mahr*)** — the groom must deliver a bride price to the bride, who accepts it of her own will.
3. **“Sending the great gift” and “sending the betrothal”** — the bride price is sent before the wedding (tea, sugar, clothing, jewellery).
4. **The wedding banquet (“*nie tie*”)** — a feast for guests; wine is forbidden (strictly in some families).
5. **Taboos** — no bowing to heaven and earth, no burning incense or worshipping idols, no geomancy, no choosing an “auspicious day” (convenience is the rule); no tobacco or wine at the wedding, no offerings laid out (varying by strictness of observance).

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Tang and Song**: Muslims (“foreign guests”, “Huihui”) entered China, and the religious wedding came with them.
- **Yuan and Ming**: the Hui national community took shape; the mosque and the “imam” presided over weddings, and the system of the “Nikah” was established.
- **Ming, Qing and Republican**: the Hui community was highly autonomous; the wedding norms were presided over by the ahong and the mosque quarter.
- **Today**: the religious rite and civil registration go side by side; city weddings are simplified, but “reciting the Nikah” and “inviting the ahong” remain common.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Proposal**: the groom’s parents send a matchmaker or kin to arrange the match.
2. **Betrothal (fixing the engagement)**: the bride price is sent and the engagement made (mostly “sending the great gift”).
3. **The wedding (Nikah)**: the ahong recites the “Nikah”; both parties declare free consent; the ahong blesses them.
4. **Wedding banquet**: a feast for guests, with kin and friends offering congratulations.
5. **“Acknowledging the kin”**: the bride meets her parents-in-law and elders (“ranking by generation”).
6. **Return visit**: the homecoming on a chosen day after the wedding.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **The north-west (Ningxia, Gansu, Qinghai, Xinjiang)**: strict religious rites, high community participation.
- **The central plains and north China**: living among the Han, they keep the religious norms strictly and do not adopt Han customs.
- **The south-east (Fujian, Guangdong, Hainan)**: coastal Hui communities, simpler weddings.
- **Relation to other peoples**: the Uyghur, Kazakh, Dongxiang, Salar, Bonan, Kyrgyz, Uzbek, Tajik and Tatar likewise follow the Islamic wedding, with differing details; they must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: the bride price (tea, sugar, cloth, jewellery), wedding sweets, banquetware.
- **Religious objects**: the *Quran*, the mosque.
- **Documents**: the marriage contract (the record made at the Nikah), the marriage certificate, the bride-price list.
