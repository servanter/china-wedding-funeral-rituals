# The Bridal Sedan Chair and Its Regulations

## 1. Definition

The bridal sedan (the wedding chair) is the purpose-made chair in which the bride is fetched. Its construction, decoration, number of bearers and the rites of mounting and alighting form a complete set of regulations.

Its place in the traditional wedding is very high: the saying “a grown girl in a bridal sedan — once in a lifetime” shows that the chair was a ceremonial object used once and not repeatable, its symbolic meaning far outweighing its function as transport.

## 2. Historical Origins

- Sedan chairs arose in the Tang and Song. By the Southern Song weddings already used the “flowered carrying-pole” (an ornately decorated carrying chair), as the *Mengliang lu* records.
- In the Ming and Qing the bridal sedan flourished, “fetching the bride by sedan” becoming the standard arrangement, and giving rise to the trade of the “carrying-house” — firms that let out sedans, insignia and bearers; old Beijing was famous for them.
- The construction and decoration of the chair differed by rank and wealth: the rich used the “eight-bearer chair” with embroidered covers and gilt finials and tassels; the poor a “two-bearer chair” or went on foot.
- In modern times rickshaws and decorated cars gradually replaced the chair, which had largely disappeared by the middle of the twentieth century; contemporary weddings use it as a prop for a “carrying the bridal chair” stage.
- In the Jiangnan water country the “flower boat” took the place of the chair, the function identical and the medium water.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Preparing the chair**: the carrying-house supplies it, decorated with red silk, coloured rosettes and embroidered covers, the roof tied with coloured cloth.
2. **Setting out**: drums and music lead, the bearers follow, and the chair is set down before the bride’s gate.
3. **Mounting**: the bride is “carried onto the chair” by an elder brother or uncle (her feet not touching the ground, so as “not to take away the soil of her mother’s house”), or helped by the matron of honour.
4. **Sealing the chair**: once she is seated the door of the chair is sealed (with a paper strip and red cord) to guard against “baleful influences” and against a substitution on the way (a precaution against the fraudulent marriages of former times).
5. **Lament**: as she mounts, the bride laments, mother and daughter weeping together, as custom permits.
6. **Raising the chair**: firecrackers are set off and the chair raised with a burst of music, and the return does not follow the same road.
7. **Setting down**: the chair is set down before the groom’s gate. In some regions the groom “kicks the chair door” three times, or the bride “steps on the chair stool” or “crosses the brazier”.
8. **Alighting**: the matron of honour helps her out of the chair and into the hall for the bowing.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the bridal chair is at its most elaborate, with insistence on the “eight-bearer” and “sixteen-bearer” chair, supplied by the carrying-houses.
- **Jiangnan**: in the water country the “flower boat” is used, its prow hung with red and gongs beating the way; there is also the combined use of “chair and boat” (chair by land, boat by water).
- **Fujian and Guangdong**: the chair is elaborately decorated, with “coloured flags” and “lanterns” before it; in modern times decorated cars are more usual.
- **Hakka areas**: usually called the “newcomer’s chair”, set down before the ancestral house.
- **Ethnic minorities**: most peoples have no institution of the chair. The Mongolian fetch the bride on horseback, the Tibetan with a troop of horsemen, and the mountain peoples of the south-west mostly on foot — their functional equivalent being the “escort party” rather than a vehicle. The symbolic structure of the chair in Han custom — “closure, transfer, opening” — is not found among them.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **The bridal chair**: a wooden cabin with embroidered covers and a coloured roof with tassels.
- **Carrying poles and ropes**: the implements of carriage.
- **Sealing strips and red cord**: for sealing the chair.
- **Chair stool and brazier**: used when the chair is set down.
- **Carrying-house letting documents**: the trade documents of former times.
