# Presentation at the Ancestral Temple (Miaojian)

## 1. Definition

The “presentation to the parents-in-law” is the rite in which the new wife is presented to her husband’s father and mother; the “presentation at the temple” is the rite in which she accompanies her husband to pay homage to the ancestors in the family shrine. The two are connected, and their order is clear:

- **Presentation to the parents-in-law** on the **day after** the wedding (the *Yili*: “at daybreak the assistant presents the wife to the parents-in-law”).
- **Presentation at the temple** three **months** after the wedding (the *Liji*, “Zengzi’s Questions”: “after three months she is presented at the temple, and is then called a wife who has come”).

The ritual lays down that **to die before the presentation at the temple is not to be established as a wife** — the woman is not reckoned a full member of the husband’s household, and both burial and sacrifice differ in specification. The presentation at the temple is thus the legal ceremony of “becoming a wife”, of higher standing than any of the ceremonies of the wedding day itself.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Shihunli*: “Rising early, the wife bathes, binds her hair with a pin, puts on plain garments and awaits the presentation. At daybreak the assistant presents the wife to the parents-in-law.” The new wife offered dates and chestnuts to the father-in-law, and dried meat seasoned with ginger and cinnamon to the mother-in-law.
- The *Liji*, “Zengzi’s Questions”, records Zengzi’s question on the meaning of the three-month presentation, and Confucius’s answer that “after three months she is presented at the temple, and is then called a wife who has come” — making plain that the presentation is the precondition of being “called a wife”.
- Zhu Xi’s *Family Rituals* changed the presentation to the third day after the wedding, on the ground that “three months is too long”, a reflection of the compression of the wedding calendar after the Song.
- *Qing bai lei chao* records the popular forms of these rites: “bowing to the ancestors”, “meeting the parents-in-law”, “ranking by generation”.
- In contemporary weddings “bowing to the ancestors” and “offering tea and changing address” are the survivals of this rite.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

### Presentation to the parents-in-law (the next day)

1. **The wife bathes**: on the morning after the wedding the new wife rises early, bathes, binds up her hair with a pin and puts on plain garments (“night garments”).
2. **The assistant leads her in**: an assistant leads the new wife to the parents-in-law.
3. **Presentation of gifts**: dates and chestnuts to the father-in-law, dried seasoned meat to the mother-in-law (both being gifts of presentation).
4. **The parents-in-law receive her**: they entertain the new wife with sweet wine and dishes (“sweetening the wife”, “feasting the wife”).
5. **The wife serves them food**: the new wife herself serves food to the parents-in-law, signifying her assumption of the duties of a wife.
6. **The parents-in-law feast her**: they return the courtesy with wine and food, and the ceremony is complete.

### Presentation at the temple (after three months, or three days)

1. **Choosing the day**: an auspicious day is chosen (the *Family Rituals* fixes the third day).
2. **Setting out the offerings**: offerings, incense and candles are set out in the family shrine (or ancestral hall).
3. **Informing the ancestors**: the new wife enters the shrine with her husband, bows to the ancestral tablets, and the words informing the ancestors are read, so that she is formally “called a wife who has come”.
4. **Meeting the kin**: she greets the elders of the lineage in turn (see the entry “Meeting the Relatives and Ranking by Generation”).
5. **Entering the lineage**: from this point the new wife is enrolled as a member of the husband’s lineage genealogy.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: called “acknowledging the kin”, “ranking by generation” or “bowing to the three generations”, performed on the day of the wedding or the day after.
- **Jiangsu and Zhejiang**: called “meeting the kin” or “meeting the generations”, with insistence on “offering tea and changing address”.
- **Fujian and Guangdong**: called “bowing to the ancestors” or “informing the ancestors”, held before the ancestral tablets in the main hall with great solemnity.
- **Taiwan**: “bowing to the ancestors” after the wedding is obligatory, with the further custom of the parents-in-law giving gold ornaments.
- **Ethnic minorities**: many peoples have no system of presentation at a temple (no family shrine, no lineage sacrifice), substituting their own “acknowledgement of kin” or religious ceremonies. Thus Tibetan weddings include “bowing to the Buddha and to the elders”; the Dai use “tying the thread” to confirm the relation of the couple to the family. Their social structures differ, and the “becoming a wife” function of the temple presentation is discharged by other mechanisms.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Dates, chestnuts and dried seasoned meat**: the gifts offered at the presentation to the parents-in-law.
- **Sweet wine and dishes**: what the parents-in-law use to receive the wife.
- **Incense, candles, offerings and ancestral tablets**: what the temple presentation uses.
- **The lineage genealogy**: the new wife may be entered in it once she has joined the lineage.
- **The words informing the ancestors (invocation)**: read at the temple presentation; see “Document Templates / Model Ancestral Invocation”.
