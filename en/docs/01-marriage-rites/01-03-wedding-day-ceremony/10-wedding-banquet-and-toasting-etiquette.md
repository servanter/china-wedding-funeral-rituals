# Wedding Banquet and Toasting Etiquette

## 1. Definition

The wedding banquet is the entertainment of guests at a wedding, called in the old ritual “feasting those who escort” and “sweetening the guests”. The toasting is the couple’s return of thanks to the guests.

Although the banquet is not a central ceremony in the ritual classics, its place in custom is very great: **the social recognition of a wedding rests half on the bowing in the hall and half on the banquet.** The attendance and the seating of the guests are a public display of the social relations of both families.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Shihunli* records “the parents-in-law feast the wife” and “feast those who escort”; “feasting” being entertainment with wine and food, this is the ritual source of the wedding banquet.
- Tang and Song wedding banquets were lavish. The Song *Dongjing menghua lu*, “Taking a Wife”, records that after the reception “wine and food were set out”, the guests seated in order.
- Ming and Qing banquets insisted on the “three days of feasting” and the “flowing banquet”, a marriage becoming a great event for lineage and village alike.
- In former times the banquet had rules such as “separate tables for men and women”, “the elders in the seats of honour” and “the couple not at table”, the couple appearing only at particular points to toast.
- Contemporary banquets are mostly held in hotels, the seating ordered by closeness and generation, and the toasting procedure highly standardised.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Taking seats**: the guests sit according to closeness and generation. The old rule reserved the “upper seats” for the maternal uncle’s family (“the maternal uncle is greatest”), the matchmaker and the lineage elders.
2. **Opening**: firecrackers are set off and the banquet opens, cold dishes first and hot dishes after.
3. **Order of courses**: the rule is “cold before hot, salt before sweet, soup to finish”; in some regions the first dish is the “head bowl” or the “round balls” (in the sense of reunion), and the last is fish (in the sense of “surplus”) or a sweet soup.
4. **The couple toast the guests**: beginning with the elders, in order of generation and seniority, addressing each in turn (the “change of address”), the person toasted replying with a blessing and a red packet.
5. **Toasting the matchmaker**: in some regions a stage of “wine of thanks to the matchmaker”.
6. **Speeches by the elders**: an elder of the lineage or the principal of the marriage speaks (the “admonition”, the “bestowal of blessing”).
7. **The couple’s thanks**: the couple thank all present together, and the banquet disperses.
8. **Dispersal and return gifts**: wedding sweets and cakes are distributed; in some regions thank-you cards are given in return.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the “eight great bowls” and “four great pieces” are insisted upon, with finger-guessing games at table for amusement.
- **Jiangsu and Zhejiang**: “three soups and four stir-fries”, “sixteen dishes arranged around”, the dishes fine and the “omen” much attended to.
- **Fujian and Guangdong**: banquets generally of “twelve courses”; in Guangdong “omen dishes” are insisted upon (hair vegetable, dried oysters, suckling pig, fish); in Chaoshan there are set rules for “sitting at table” and seating order is very strict.
- **Hakka areas**: the custom of “da dou si” (pooling contributions to give a banquet).
- **Ethnic minorities**: the Miao “long-table banquet”, the Dong “gathering banquet” and the Mongolian “whole-sheep banquet” differ in scale and form and are generally combined with the collective activity of the village, song and wine being the chief media — an essential difference from the domestic Han wedding banquet.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **The banquet table and dishes**: the menu and the arrangement of seats.
- **Wedding wine, sweets and cakes**: distributed after the banquet.
- **The seating plan**: showing each guest’s place.
- **The gift register**: recording the congratulatory gifts.
- **Thank-you cards**: to thank the guests after the banquet.
