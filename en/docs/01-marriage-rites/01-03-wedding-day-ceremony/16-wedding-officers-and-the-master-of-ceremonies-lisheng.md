# Wedding Officers and the Master of Ceremonies (Lisheng)

## 1. Definition

The wedding officers are the collective name for those who discharge the practical duties on the day of the wedding, chiefly:

- **The master of ceremonies / the chanter of the rites (*lisheng*)**: presiding over the ceremonies and chanting the ritual text.
- **The receiver of guests**: welcoming guests, showing the way and keeping the gift register.
- **The accountant**: managing income and outlay.
- **The kitchen**: preparing the banquet.
- **Chair-bearers and the band**: the insignia and the music.

Of these, the chanter of the rites is the master of the ceremonial language, and the words he chants determine the pace of the ceremony.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Shihunli* records the “assistant” and the “usher”: the assistant aided in performing the rites, the usher conveyed words between host and guest. This is the ritual source of the master of ceremonies.
- The Song *Dongjing menghua lu* records “ushers” at the reception (trade personnel who undertook the business of a wedding), already showing the professionalisation of the wedding officers.
- In the Ming and Qing the *lisheng* presided over Confucian ritual, generally a literate gentleman versed in the rites, presiding over household sacrifices, the three offerings and the dotting of the ancestral tablet; the wedding chanter has the same source.
- *Qing bai lei chao* records the chanted words of weddings in various places, mostly in verse, with attention to rhyme, auspiciousness and completeness.
- In modern times the wedding master of ceremonies moved from the Confucian ritual system to professional hosting, and a wedding-hosting trade has formed today.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Opening**: firecrackers are set off, and the chanter announces “the auspicious hour has come; the wedding begins”.
2. **Chanting the rites**: the words are chanted in the order of the ceremony — “first bow to Heaven and Earth”, “second bow to the high hall”, “bride and groom bow to one another”, “escort them to the bridal chamber”.
3. **Guiding**: gestures and chanted words direct the couple’s positions, turns and bows.
4. **Speeches**: speaking on behalf of the principals and the witnesses, or reading out congratulatory messages.
5. **Linking the stages**: auspicious talk between the stages, to keep the atmosphere warm.
6. **Improvisation**: handling contingencies (late guests, adjustments to the ceremony).
7. **Closing**: declaring the ceremony complete and arranging the seating for the banquet.

### Characteristics of the chanted text

- Largely in verse, two lines to a rhyme, for ease of delivery.
- Largely auspicious phrases: dragon and phoenix, mandarin ducks, twined trees, one heart, an early noble son.
- Both fixed formulas and improvisation; an adept can compose on the spot to fit the occasion.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: called the “great receiver of guests” or “master of ceremonies”, often a eloquent member of the lineage.
- **Jiangsu and Zhejiang**: called “master of ceremonies” or “chanter of the rites”, with insistence on “one chants and all respond”, the guests answering.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: two systems, the “ritual master” (a Daoist who is also master of ceremonies, presiding over chanting and liturgy) and the “chanter of the rites”, depending on whether the wedding has a religious element.
- **Guangdong**: the *da jin jie* also acts as master of ceremonies, the chanted words rhyming in Cantonese.
- **Ethnic minorities**: the presider is usually an elder of the group or a song master — the Dong “song master”, the Miao “village elder”, the Mongolian “pronouncer of blessings” (who recites the words of blessing). Their verbal form is chanted poetry rather than “chanting the rites”, the function akin to the Han master of ceremonies and the form different.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **The chanter’s text / the programme of ceremonies**: the script of the ritual order that the master follows.
- **Gift register and gift list**: used by the receiver of guests and the accountant.
- **Red felt, kneeling cushions, incense table**: the furnishings of the ceremony.
- **Wedding couplets and double-happiness characters**: for decoration.
