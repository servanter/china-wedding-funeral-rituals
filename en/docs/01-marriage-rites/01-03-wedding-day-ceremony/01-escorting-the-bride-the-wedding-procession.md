# Escorting the Bride: The Wedding Procession

![亲迎花轿迎娶仪仗 / The wedding procession with the bridal sedan](../../../../assets/images/06-wedding-sedan-procession.png)


## 1. Definition

This entry records the **popular** development of the personal reception: the composition of the escort party, the order of its insignia, the route it takes and the taboos of the hour.

Its difference from the entry “The Six Rites / Qinying”: the latter follows the *Shihunli* and records the ritual ceremony in which the groom alone rides out, lays down the goose and returns; this entry records the popular form, developed after the Tang and Song, of “a whole company walking the road”. **In the ritual classic the point is the person; in popular custom the point is the insignia.**

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Shihunli*: “two following carriages, candle-bearers before the horses.” This is the seed of the procession — following carriages and a candle-lit vanguard.
- In the Tang and Song the bride was fetched with flowered carrying-poles and drums and music; the Song *Dongjing menghua lu*, “Taking a Wife”, records that at the reception there were “lead men for the flowered carrying-poles” and “music played”.
- In the Ming and Qing the reception used “attendants” (banners, tablets, umbrellas, fans, golden gourds, axes) and bands of drums and music, on a scale set by the family’s means, the forms borrowed directly from the insignia of officials — hence the suspicion of “presumption”, repeatedly forbidden and repeatedly revived.
- In modern times rickshaws and decorated cars replaced the bridal palanquin and the insignia were simplified, but the customs of “letting off firecrackers”, “going by a roundabout route” and “scattering lucky coins” survived.
- In contemporary weddings a fleet of cars replaces the procession, the insignia converting into the ordering of the cars, the pasting of double-happiness characters and the hanging of red silk.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Announcing to the ancestors**: before setting out, the ancestors are informed (in some lineages).
2. **Assembly**: the master of ceremonies (or the *da jin jie*) counts the people and the gear, and the party sets out with firecrackers.
3. **Order of the procession** (the traditional sequence): drums and music leading → banners, tablets, umbrellas and fans → the bridal palanquin in the middle → those escorting the bride and carrying the trousseau following.
4. **Taboos of the route**: going back the way one came is avoided (out and back by different roads); passing temples, ancestral halls and graveyards is avoided; at bridges, wells and stone lions firecrackers are set off or lucky coins scattered (“to press down evil”).
5. **Arrival at the bride’s house**: firecrackers announce the arrival, and the party forms up outside the gate.
6. **The return**: the palanquin is raised once the bride is seated (or the car started); the custom that “the return does not follow the same road” is generally kept.
7. **Arrival at the groom’s house**: the palanquin is set down (the car stops) and the bride is helped in by the matron of honour.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: gongs, drums and *suona* lead, and the company insists on being “long, red and loud”; in old Beijing the insignia were supplied by carrying-houses and awning-shops.
- **The Jiangnan water country**: in the water towns the bride is fetched in a “flower boat”, its prow hung with red and stuck with coloured flags, with gongs and drums in attendance.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: the *da jin jie* leads the party, which must carry the “thank-you basket” (with ceremonial cakes, incense and candles).
- **Guangdong**: the cars are pasted with double-happiness characters and tied with coloured rosettes, with insistence on “go odd, return even”.
- **Ethnic minorities**: the Miao and Dong receive the party with “gate-blocking wine” and antiphonal singing; the Yi have “carrying the bride on the back”; the Zhuang reception party sings antiphonal songs. The forms of the procession differ from the Han, song and wine generally standing in for drums and insignia.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Bridal palanquin or decorated car**: the chief vehicle of the reception.
- **Attendants’ insignia**: banners, tablets, umbrellas, fans and the like.
- **Bands of gongs, drums and *suona***: the music.
- **Firecrackers and lucky coins** (coins and red paper): scattered along the way.
- **Red silk, wedding couplets and coloured rosettes**: the decoration of the cars and the palanquin.
- **The reception card or gift list**: documents travelling with the party.
