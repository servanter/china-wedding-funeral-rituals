# Blocking the Door and the Door-Opening Gift

## 1. Definition

Blocking the door is the custom by which, when the escort party reaches the bride’s gate, the bride’s family keeps it shut, and the groom’s side must answer with songs, verse contests and riddles, and pay the “door-opening money” (a lucky gift), before being admitted.

Its substance is **obstruction as play**: through a game-like blocking, the bride’s family symbolises its reluctance to let a daughter go, at the same time testing the groom’s sincerity and quickness of speech, and finally sealing a transactional reconciliation with the “lucky money” (red packet). Blocking the door is the most comic element of the wedding.

## 2. Historical Origins

- Its origin may be traced to the Tang custom of “blocking the carriage”: the woman’s family barred the way of the escort’s carriages and horses and demanded money, food and drink, as the Dunhuang text “Blocking the Carriage” attests.
- The “putting down of the son-in-law” (the teasing of the groom) is a near source: Tang weddings had the customs of “putting down the son-in-law” and “making sport of the son-in-law”.
- The Song *Dongjing menghua lu*, “Taking a Wife”, records “blocking the door” among the reception customs, alongside “scattering grain and beans” and “sitting in the empty tent”.
- In the Ming and Qing “blocking the door” became general and a fixed stage of the wedding; *Qing bai lei chao* records the customs of demanding red packets at the door in various places.
- Today it has become “the door-blocking games”, “finding the wedding shoe” and “tests of declaration”, the form changed but the function unchanged.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **The door shut**: the escort party reaches the gate and the bride’s family shuts it, guarded from within by the bride’s sisters and women friends.
2. **Calling at the door**: firecrackers are set off outside and the purpose of the visit proclaimed.
3. **Setting the tests**: those inside pose questions — antiphonal songs, verse contests, riddles, questions to answer, declarations (such as “who will cook after the wedding”).
4. **Passing in red packets**: the groom’s side pushes the “door-opening money” (lucky money) through the crack of the door; the door opens a little and more is repeatedly demanded, producing a comic haggling.
5. **The door opens**: when the demands are met or the tests passed, the door is fully opened.
6. **Entering**: the escort party enters, bows to the elders of the bride’s family, and presents the gift list.
7. **Seeking the shoe and other objects**: in some regions further stages are set, such as “finding the wedding shoe” or “finding the veil”.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: called “calling at the door”, mostly a matter of demanding red packets and speaking auspicious words, in a boisterous atmosphere.
- **Jiangsu and Zhejiang**: verse contests and antiphonal songs are insisted upon, and are especially valued in literary families.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: called “blocking the door”, guarded by the bride’s brothers, with the groom’s side required to pay a “door-opening gift”.
- **Guangdong**: called “passing the three barriers”, with three doors each having its own checkpoint.
- **Hakka areas**: chiefly “antiphonal songs at the blocked door”, the two sides forming rival teams and the singing continuing without pause.
- **Ethnic minorities**: the Dong and Miao have “gate-blocking wine”, barring the escort party with bowls of wine (or a formation of bowls) and singing gate-blocking songs, admission to the village following the drinking; the Yi “splashing water to welcome the party” serves a similar function. The basis of such customs is the participation of the whole village community, and both their scale and their meaning exceed the domestic door-blocking of the Han.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Red packets (lucky money)**: the door-opening money.
- **Red paper for the door god, and wedding couplets**: the decoration of the door.
- **Wine (gate-blocking wine)**: the door-blocking device in some regions.
- **Songbooks and set replies**: for the tests.
- **The gift list**: presented on entering.
