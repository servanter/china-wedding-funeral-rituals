# The Contemporary Chinese Wedding: An Integrated Programme

![婚礼当日仪节流程图 / Flowchart of the wedding-day rites](../../../../assets/diagrams/婚礼当日仪节流程图.svg)

*Figure 2. The wedding-day rites in sequence: reception — bowing in the hall — sharing the cup — banquet — bridal chamber — meeting the kin.*

## 1. Definition

The “contemporary Chinese-style wedding” is the form of wedding popular since the twenty-first century, rearranged out of traditional ceremonies as its material, usually reconstructed with reference to the *Shihunli* of the *Yili* and to Ming-dynasty weddings — but a **performative reconstruction**: the ceremonies are retained, simplified and newly invented.

This entry gathers its current programme, noting for each stage its traditional source and its alterations, for comparative study.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **The 1990s**: the wedding trade arose, “Chinese-style” and “Western-style” weddings becoming available styles, the Chinese-style usually a hybrid of “phoenix crown and rosy cape with a prop palanquin”.
- **The 2010s**: the *hanfu* movement and the revival of traditional culture brought the “Han-style wedding”, reconstructing ceremonies according to the ritual books (the three bowings and three yieldings, sharing the victim and the cup, binding the hair).
- **At the same time**: the protection of intangible heritage listed a number of local wedding customs (such as the “ten *li* of red dowry” of Ninghai in Zhejiang and the “Huian woman’s wedding custom” of Fujian), promoting the revival of local customs.
- **Today**: three forms coexist — Western (diamond ring, witnesses, champagne tower), Chinese (bowing in the hall, sharing the cup, binding the hair) and hybrid (the reception in Chinese style, the ceremony in Western style).

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

A fairly complete programme of a contemporary Chinese-style wedding:

1. **Informing the ancestors**: before the groom leaves, the ancestors are informed (“gaomiao”).
2. **The reception**: the party sets out, with the games of “blocking the door” and “hunting for the wedding shoe”.
3. **Leave-taking**: the bride bows farewell to her parents, her mother “ties her sash”, and the bride “laments”.
4. **Mounting the chair (or car)**: she takes her seat, “not going back the way one came”.
5. **Entering**: on reaching the husband’s house, “crossing the brazier” and “treading on tiles” (in some regions).
6. **Bowing in the hall**: first to Heaven and Earth, second to the high hall, then the couple to one another.
7. **Sharing the victim and the cup**: eating at one table and the rite of the cup of crossed arms.
8. **Binding the hair**: each cuts a lock, knotted with red cord and placed in a brocade bag.
9. **Thanks to the guests**: the couple bow in thanks to those present.
10. **Entering the bridal chamber**: sitting in the curtain and scattering the bed.
11. **Offering tea and changing address**: tea is offered to both sets of parents and they are addressed as “father, mother” (often merged with the bowing in the hall).
12. **Toasting at the banquet**: taking seats and toasting, and the banquet disperses.

### Differences from the traditional programme

| Traditional (from the *Yili* onwards) | Contemporary Chinese-style wedding |
| --- | --- |
| Presentation at the temple three months after the wedding; presentation to the parents-in-law the next day | Usually merged into the wedding day |
| Personal reception at dusk (“at the beginning of the dusk”) | Usually in the morning |
| The Six Rites in stages, over several months | Compressed into one day |
| The shared cup using half gourds (*jin*) | Twin cups, glass cups |
| No “witnessing” or “exchange of rings” | Chinese and Western combined, coexisting |

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **The cities**: wedding companies have differing formulas, adjusting them to local habit and the couple’s preference.
- **Local customs of the intangible-heritage type**: in some regions weddings follow the traditional order, as with the Zhejiang “ten *li* of red dowry” (a reconstructed display of the dowry procession) and the southern Fujian “ancient-ritual wedding”.
- **Ethnic minorities**: the various peoples too have contemporary revivals of “national weddings”, such as the Mongolian grassland wedding, the Tibetan traditional wedding and the Miao “Sisters’ Festival” collective wedding. Their reconstructions rest on the customs of the people concerned and not on Han ritual; the difference should be observed and the two should not be conflated.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- ***Hanfu*, phoenix crown and rosy cape, *qun kwa***: wedding dress.
- **Incense table, Heaven and Earth table, the chanter’s text.**
- **Hejin cups, red veil, wedding steelyard, scissors and red cord** (for binding the hair).
- **The marriage contract (contemporary version)**: some weddings include a stage of “signing the marriage contract”, imitating the old format.
- **The programme of the wedding**: the schedule of ceremonies supplied by the wedding company.
