# Returning to the Bride’s Family (Huimen)

![新妇回门（归宁）/ The bride's return visit to her natal home](../../../../assets/images/10-bride-returning-huimen.png)


## 1. Definition

Returning to the bride’s family, also “gui ning” (“homecoming”) or “returning to the mother’s house”, is the rite in which the newly married couple return after the wedding to visit the bride’s parents.

**The boundary with the entry “Post-Wedding Custom / Third-Day Return”**: this entry belongs to the closing stage of the wedding chain, and treats the origin, the journey and the ceremony of the homecoming; the entry “Post-Wedding Custom / Third-Day Return” treats specifically the typological differences and derivative customs (the double return, the month-long stay and so on) that the third-day return assumed in various places.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Shijing*, “Zhou Nan: Kudzu Vine”: “Which shall I wash and which not? To go home and greet my parents.” The term “gui ning” comes from this, an early expression of a woman’s visit to her parents.
- The *Shihunli* records that after the wedding “the wife enters, and after three months the sacrifices proceed”, connected with the system of the new wife’s dealings with her own family.
- In the Tang “gui ning” had a clear ritual order; the *Tang hui yao* records the rules for a princess’s homecoming, and popular usage called it “huimen”.
- After the Song the “third-day return” and the “seventh-day return” became the norm, the number of days varying from place to place (one, three, six, nine days or a full month).
- The return is **the expression of the bilateral character of marriage**: it both establishes the new wife’s place in the husband’s household and maintains her connection with her own. Formerly there were also two opposite customs, “no overnight stay on the return” (in some regions) and “staying a month” (in others), reflecting different measures of “returning” in different places.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Choosing the day**: the day of the return is fixed after the wedding, variously the first, third, sixth or ninth day, or the full month.
2. **Preparing gifts**: the couple bring gifts to the bride’s house, in pairs (wine, meat, cakes, sweets), insisting on “not going empty-handed”.
3. **Arriving at the gate**: the bride’s family welcomes them with firecrackers, the younger members coming out to meet them.
4. **Greetings**: the couple greet the bride’s parents and elders.
5. **The banquet**: the bride’s family holds a banquet, called the “return wine”.
6. **Returning the same day** (in most regions): they return to the husband’s house before sunset, the saying being “not to see the lamps of the mother’s house”.
7. **Staying a month** (in some regions): the new wife stays at her parents’ house for a month or several days, and the husband’s family may not press for her return.
8. **Return gifts**: the bride’s family give gifts in return (mostly food and useful articles) to be taken back to the husband’s house.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: called “huimen”, usually on the third day after the wedding; the rule is “husband and wife go together and come back together”.
- **Jiangsu and Zhejiang**: called “huimen” or “wangchao”, with the saying “return on the third day, pay the morning visit on the sixth”.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: called “zuoke” or “huimen”, ceremonious, the bride’s family preparing a “return basket”.
- **Guangdong**: called “huimen”, the bride’s family holding a “return banquet” in honour of the new son-in-law.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples have a system in which the new wife returns to her parents’ house for a longer or shorter stay (as with “not taking up residence in the husband’s house”), which may last for years, and is fundamentally different in nature from the Han return of a single day; the Yi, Miao and others likewise have their own names and ceremonies for the return. See the entries for each people.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Return gifts**: wine, meat, cakes, sweets and fruit, which must be in pairs.
- **The return basket (gift pannier)**: the vessel of the gifts.
- **The return banquet**: held by the bride’s family.
- **The new son-in-law’s gifts**: in some regions the new son-in-law also prepares gifts for the younger members of his wife’s family.
