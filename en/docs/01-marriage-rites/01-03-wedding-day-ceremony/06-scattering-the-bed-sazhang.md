# Scattering the Bed (Sazhang)

## 1. Definition

Scattering the bed is the custom in which, after the couple have entered the curtain (sitting in the bridal chamber), those present throw dates, chestnuts, peanuts, longans and lotus seeds into the canopy and sing “scattering songs”, to wish an early noble son and many descendants.

Its core mechanism is **homophonic blessing**: *zao* (dates) sounds like *zao* “early”, *li* (chestnuts) like *li* “to stand”, *guiyuan* (longans) like *gui* “noble”, *lianzi* (lotus seeds) like *lianzi* “in succession a son” — together “an early noble son”, “sons in succession”.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The custom flourished in the Tang and Song. The Song *Dongjing menghua lu*, “Taking a Wife”, records scattering when the couple “sit in the empty tent”; *Mengliang lu*, “Marriages”, records scattering with “five-coloured paper money, flowers and fruit”.
- The Song had a special “scattering song”, setting out the blessings in verse, which may be regarded as an early form of the ceremonial wedding song.
- Its predecessor is the custom of “scattering grain and beans”: at the reception, grain and beans were scattered before the gate to “press down the three baleful influences of the green sheep, the black cock and the black ox”, an apotropaic act; the later “scattering the bed” turned from averting evil to praying for offspring.
- In the Ming and Qing the custom was universal, the objects scattered varying from place to place; in Fujian and Guangdong coins and sugar were scattered as well, adding the sense of “scattering money”.
- Contemporary weddings mostly scatter petals, coloured paper or sweets, the sense of blessing remaining.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Entering the curtain**: the couple enter the bridal chamber and sit within the canopy (man on the left, woman on the right).
2. **Preparing the fruit**: dates, chestnuts, peanuts, longans and lotus seeds are prepared in advance (or the five grains, coins, sugar).
3. **Scattering**: a woman of full fortune (or relatives and friends) throws them into the canopy, singing blessings as she scatters.
4. **Singing the scattering song**: as “first scatter gold, second scatter silver, third scatter an early noble son leaping the dragon gate”.
5. **Picking up the fruit**: children scramble for the scattered fruit, popularly “snatching the wedding fruit” to obtain blessing.
6. **Ending**: the ceremony done, the couple share the bed and rest.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: dates, chestnuts and peanuts are scattered, insisted upon “in pairs”; in some places wheat and grain as well.
- **Jiangsu and Zhejiang**: dates, chestnuts, longans and lotus seeds, with the scattering song, whose words have set patterns.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: sugar, coins and rice, adding the sense of “abundant money and grain”.
- **Guangdong**: lotus seeds and lilies (in the sense of “a hundred years of harmony”), red dates and peanuts.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples have rites of scattering grain, water or rice for blessing, but their object is generally the gods or the ancestors rather than the couple, and the mechanism of meaning differs; they must be understood separately and not lumped together as “scattering the bed”.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Dates, chestnuts, peanuts, longans and lotus seeds**: the central objects scattered.
- **The five grains** (wheat, millet, broomcorn, panicled millet, rice): the objects scattered in some regions.
- **Coins and sugar**: supplementary objects in Fujian and Guangdong.
- **The canopy, wedding quilt and wedding pillow**: where the scattering takes place.
- **The scattering song**: orally transmitted verses, written down in some places.
