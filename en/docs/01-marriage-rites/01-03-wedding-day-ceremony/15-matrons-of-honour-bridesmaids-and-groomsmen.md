# Matrons of Honour, Bridesmaids and Groomsmen

## 1. Definition

- **Matron of honour**: also the *da jin jie*, the bridesmaid or the “lady who escorts the bride” — the female attendant who helps the bride through the ceremonies and looks after her daily needs.
- **Groomsman**: the male attendant who helps the groom, formerly also the “escorting gentleman” or “male attendant”.
- **Woman of full fortune**: the old rule required that a wedding attendant be a “woman of full fortune” — whose parents are both living, whose marriage is harmonious and who has children. Those incomplete (widows, the childless, the divorced) could not serve, being reckoned inauspicious.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Shihunli* records the “female attendants” (the nieces and younger sisters who accompanied the bride), the “governess” and the “servants” — the ritual germ of the wedding attendants.
- From the Tang and Song the names “matron of honour” and “flower-planting woman” appear. The Song *Dongjing menghua lu* records “ushers” and “maids” at the reception.
- The *da jin jie* of Fujian and Guangdong is the professional presider over wedding custom: generation after generation they transmit the knowledge of ceremonies, felicitous sayings and taboos, combining the four roles of master of ceremonies, bridesmaid, nurse and mediator — an important body of transmitters of traditional wedding knowledge.
- In the Ming and Qing the north distinguished the “lady who escorts the bride” (appointed by the bride’s family, accompanying her to the husband’s house) from the “lady who fetches the bride” (appointed by the husband’s family, in charge of the reception).
- In the contemporary wedding trade, “bridesmaids” and “groomsmen” are mostly unmarried friends, and the “woman of full fortune” requirement has relaxed.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Before the wedding**: helping prepare the trousseau, arranging it, checking the gift list, preparing the ceremonial articles.
2. **The coiffure**: on the morning of the wedding (or the night before) combing the bride’s hair, coiling the bun and adding the ornaments (the “shangtou”), reciting auspicious words.
3. **The reception**: the matron of honour guards the bride beside the chair (or car) and accompanies her to the husband’s house.
4. **Entering**: helping the bride into the hall, to sitting in the curtain and to the sharing of the cup.
5. **Attendance**: looking after the bride’s food and drink and her visits to the privy throughout (in former times a bride could not readily move about alone).
6. **Keeping the measure**: mediating in the blocking of the door and the chamber roughhousing, and keeping them within bounds.
7. **The close**: presiding over the next day’s stages such as “ranking by generation” and “offering tea and changing address”.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Fujian and Guangdong**: the professional *da jin jie* is at its most developed, wearing particular colours (mostly red and black) and reciting sets of auspicious sayings.
- **North China**: the “lady who fetches” and the “lady who escorts” are distinguished, usually filled by women of full fortune from the lineage.
- **Jiangsu and Zhejiang**: called “matron of honour” or “bridesmaid”, with insistence on being “able to sing and to talk”.
- **Taiwan**: called “the matchmaker woman” or *da jin*, combining the offices of matchmaker and attendant.
- **Ethnic minorities**: many peoples have no professional wedding attendant, the role being filled by an elder sister-in-law, an aunt or a specialist “song master”. The Miao “song master” and the Dong “song mistress” are responsible for the antiphonal singing at weddings; their function is close to the matron of honour, but their skill is singing rather than the conduct of ceremonies.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Dressing implements**: comb, fine comb, hairpin, clasp.
- **Songbooks of auspicious words**: some *da jin jie* have songbooks or formula books handed down from their teachers.
- **Red packets and thank-offerings**: the remuneration of the matron of honour and the bridesmaid.
- **Gift list and trousseau inventory**: for checking and counting.
