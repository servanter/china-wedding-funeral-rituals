# Sitting in the Curtained Bed and Sharing the Bed

## 1. Definition

- **Sitting in the curtain**: the rite in which the couple, having entered the bridal chamber, sit together within the canopy to receive congratulations. The *Dongjing menghua lu* calls it “sitting in the empty tent”.
- **Sharing the bed**: the couple sleeping together. In custom and ritual this is not merely a private matter but the sign that the marriage has formally taken effect in the life of the lineage.

Under the old system in which the parties did not know one another before marriage, sitting in the curtain was often the first genuine meeting and being together, so its significance far exceeded today’s “entering the bridal chamber”.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Shihunli*: “The head of the house takes off his shoes in the chamber, and the accompanying concubine receives them; the wife takes off her shoes in the room, and the attendant receives them.” It also records “sharing the victim” and “sharing the cup”, followed by “the head of the house goes out” and “the concubine feasts”. Later ritual scholars took the “taking off of shoes in chamber and room” as an early record of the rite of sharing the bed.
- The Song *Dongjing menghua lu*, “Taking a Wife”, records that after the bride entered she “sat in the empty tent, which is called sitting in the curtain”, and that “when the man and woman had bowed to one another they went to the bed, the woman sitting to the left and the man to the right, and the women scattered coloured fruit and money, which is called scattering the bed”.
- The Ming and Qing kept the names “sitting in the curtain” and “sitting on the bed”, the ceremony varying by region.
- Popular belief attached further customs to “sharing the bed”: scattering dates and chestnuts when the bed was dressed, engaging a woman of full fortune to dress it, placing the bride’s shoes under the bed.
- The Song also had the custom of “listening at the chamber”, relatives and friends listening outside the window to the couple’s words and movements in jest — an extension of the roughhousing in the bridal chamber.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Entering the chamber**: the bowing in the hall completed, the matron of honour leads the couple into the bridal chamber.
2. **Sitting in the curtain**: the two sit together within the canopy, the man on the left and the woman on the right.
3. **Scattering the bed**: dates, chestnuts, flowers and fruit are thrown into the canopy and scattering songs sung.
4. **Sharing the cup**: the rite of the cup of crossed arms is performed.
5. **Removing the ornaments**: the matron of honour removes the bride’s jewellery and outer garments (“taking off the paint”, “removing the dress”).
6. **Sharing the bed**: the couple sleep together.
7. **After the scattering**: relatives and friends outside listen and roughouse until late at night.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: called “sitting in the curtain”, with insistence that “one should sit long in the curtain”, it being said that long sitting brings long blessing.
- **Jiangsu and Zhejiang**: called “sitting on the bed”, usually done together with the scattering.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: called “sitting in the room”; on entering, the couple first bow to the ancestors in the hall and then enter the chamber to sit in the curtain.
- **Guangdong**: called “entering the room”, insisting that “the couple must not sit alone on the bed”, a bridesmaid sitting with them.
- **Ethnic minorities**: many peoples do not share the bed on the first night. Thus the old custom of “not taking up residence in the husband’s house” (in parts of Zhuang and Li territory), where the woman continues to live in her parents’ home after marriage and stays at the husband’s only at festivals or harvest, moving there permanently only after a child is born; among Tibetans and the Mosuo the forms of marital residence are more various still. Such systems differ fundamentally from the Han view that sharing the bed constitutes the marriage, and must be understood separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **The canopy (bed curtain)**: the central furnishing.
- **Wedding quilt, wedding pillow and sheets**: newly made, embroidered with auspicious motifs.
- **Dates, chestnuts, peanuts and longans**: for scattering.
- **Hejin cups and wine**: for sharing the cup.
- **Red candles (wedding candles)**: what the phrase “wedding candles in the bridal chamber” refers to, kept burning all night.
