# Announcing the Favourable Divination (Naji)

## 1. Definition

Naji is the third of the Six Rites. The groom’s family divines in the family temple; when the omen is favourable, it sends an envoy with a goose to inform the bride’s family, and the betrothal is from that moment formally concluded. “Naji” means “conveying the favourable omen to the bride’s family”.

Naji is the point in the Six Rites at which negotiation turns into settlement: nacai and wenming may still be broken off, but after naji both parties are bound by the ritual order and by public opinion, and may not lightly go back on their word.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Shihunli*: “Naji: use a goose, as in the rite of nacai.”
- Zheng Xuan’s commentary: “Divining on return in the temple, the omen being favourable, an envoy is again sent to inform them; the matter of the marriage is thereby settled.” This shows that the substantial function of naji is to settle the marriage.
- The *Liji*, “The Meaning of Marriage”, sums up the spirit of the Six Rites as “reverence, care, gravity and correctness”, of which naji is the turning point.
- In the Tang dynasty the binding force of naji was supported by law. The *Tang Code with Subcommentary*, “Households and Marriage”: “In all cases where a daughter has been promised in marriage, if a marriage letter has been sent or a private agreement made and the promise is then broken, sixty blows with the rod.” The “marriage letter” corresponds to the document of engagement after naji, and the breaking of the promise was a punishable offence.
- After the Song, naji was either merged with nazheng or omitted; in popular practice the rites called “guo xiaoding” and “xiading” took over its function.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Divination**: the head of the groom’s household divines in the family temple (or by yarrow stalks) upon the name and birth date obtained at wenming, and obtains a favourable omen.
2. **Sending the envoy**: the groom’s family sends an envoy with a goose to the bride’s house.
3. **The formula**: “You have been so gracious as to give your command; so-and-so has laid it before the divination, and the omen is favourable; he has sent me to presume to inform you.” (In substance: since you consented to the marriage, I have divined, and the omen is favourable; I have come to tell you.)
4. **The reply**: the host answers: “My daughter has not been taught, and I fear only that she may prove unequal to the task. Since you have a favourable omen, I share in it, and I dare not refuse.” (His daughter lacking instruction, he fears she may not be equal to the duties of a wife; since the omen is favourable, he shares in the good fortune and does not presume to decline.)
5. **Goose and entertainment**: the ceremonial is as at nacai; the guest withdraws and the host escorts him beyond the gate.
6. **Exchange of tokens**: thereafter the two families exchange betrothal gifts, called in popular practice “dingqin” or “xiaoding”.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: called “guo xiaoding”, “xiaoding” or “huan xiaotie”; the groom’s family sends jewellery, cloth and sweets, and the bride’s family returns shoes, hats and stationery.
- **Jiangsu and Zhejiang**: called “xiading”, “wending” or “chuanhong”; the “returning of the tray” (*huipan*) is insisted upon, and gifts are given in pairs.
- **Fujian and Guangdong**: called “wending” or “guocha”; the man’s family sends tea cakes and jewellery, and the woman’s family entertains with tea — “having drunk the man’s family’s tea” is itself the sign of consent.
- **Parts of Hunan and Jiangxi**: called “guocha” or “dinggeng”.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples substitute “settling the match over wine” or the giving of silver ornaments for the goose; the Yao have a custom of “handing over tokens”.
- The number of betrothal gifts is usually even, odd numbers being avoided; umbrellas (homophone of “scatter”) and clocks (homophone of “end”) are avoided as inauspicious.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **The goose**: the gift prescribed by the ritual classic.
- **Betrothal gifts**: in later practice chiefly jewellery (bracelets, rings, earrings), cloth, sweets, wine and tea cakes.
- **The engagement card or marriage letter**: the written proof of the engagement, recording three generations on each side, the amount of the betrothal gift and the witnessing matchmaker, and carrying a binding force in the sense of popular law.
- **The reply card**: the document by which the bride’s family acknowledges receipt.
