# Requesting the Wedding Date (Qingqi)

## 1. Definition

Qingqi is the fifth of the Six Rites. The groom’s family having divined a date for the wedding, it sends an envoy with a goose to inform the bride’s family and to “request” their approval. It is called a “request” because in the ritual order the wedding date requires the bride’s family’s final consent, so that the groom’s side may not presume to decide alone.

In ritual scholarship the essence of qingqi is the “ceremony of deference”: although the date has already been fixed, the groom’s family still approaches it as a request, in keeping with the “reverence, care, gravity and correctness” of the *Liji*. After the Song this rite was usually changed to “announcing the date” (*gaoqi*) or “sending the date”, with the groom’s family taking the initiative and the bride’s family receiving the command — a reflection of the changed relation of authority between the two households.

## 2. Historical Origins

- The *Shihunli*: “Qingqi: use a goose. The host declines; the guest consents, and announces the date, as in the rite of nazheng.”
- The formula: “You have given your gracious command, and so-and-so has repeatedly received it. Only because of the uncertainties of the three collateral kin, he has sent me to request an auspicious day.” (In substance: having more than once received your consent, and fearing that the elders of the lineage, being advanced in years, may be overtaken by change, he asks that an auspicious day be fixed early.)
- The host’s answer: “So-and-so has already received your command; he will do nothing but hear and obey.” (Having already consented to the marriage, he will follow your instruction in all things.) This is the ideal response imagined by the ritual books; if the bride’s family has objections, the matter must be negotiated afresh.
- Zhu Xi’s *Family Rituals* simply changes “requesting the date” to “announcing the date”, on the ground that the date has already been fixed by the groom’s family’s divination and need not be requested again — a reflection of the reality of the husband’s family’s initiative under the lineage system of the Song.
- Ming and Qing ritual books kept the name qingqi; the *Comprehensive Rituals of the Great Qing* gives the wedding of officials a section on qingqi.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Choosing the day**: the groom’s family chooses an auspicious day, generally by the yellow-path auspicious days, the “zhoutang” diagram and the birth characters of both parties, with an eye to the farming calendar and the solar terms.
2. **Sending the envoy**: the envoy, holding a goose, comes to the bride’s house and delivers the formula of qingqi.
3. **Granting the date**: if the bride’s family agrees, it answers “we will hear and obey”; if not (for instance because the household still has preparations to make), the matchmaker mediates and another day is chosen.
4. **Sending the date slip**: the groom’s family writes the “date slip” or “marriage card” on red paper, setting out the auspicious day and hour, the hour of the reception, and the zodiac animals and taboos to be avoided by each household, and sends it to the bride’s house.
5. **Preparing the trousseau**: on the strength of the date slip the bride’s family prepares the dowry and begins “hurrying the trousseau” and “making the wedding clothes”.
6. **Informing relatives and friends**: both families inform their relatives and friends and send out invitations.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: called “song rizi”, “xia rizi” or “tongxin”; the date slip is written on red paper folded double, and “looking at days” is insisted upon.
- **Jiangsu and Zhejiang**: called “tonghua”, “song quitie” or “dingri”; the wedding is usually set in a double month on a double day.
- **Southern Fujian**: called “song ritou”; the “day slip” is sent to the bride’s house together with the ceremonial cakes.
- **Some peoples of the south-west**: the date is fixed by antiphonal singing or by divination. The Miao have a rite of “fixing the wedding date”, which the village elders of both sides must agree upon.
- **Taboos**: a “year without spring” (a widow’s year) is avoided, as is a date clashing with the birth dates of either set of parents, and marriage on the “Sanniang sha” and similar days — these belong to the culture of date selection.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **The goose**: the gift prescribed by the ritual classic.
- **The date slip or marriage card**: the red-paper document setting out the auspicious day and hour and the taboos — one of the few “practical documents” among the Six Rites.
- **Almanacs and books of date selection**: the basis of popular choice of days, chiefly the *Yuxia ji* and *Xieji bianfang shu* traditions.
- **Invitations**: sent at the same time as the date is fixed, to inform relatives and friends.
