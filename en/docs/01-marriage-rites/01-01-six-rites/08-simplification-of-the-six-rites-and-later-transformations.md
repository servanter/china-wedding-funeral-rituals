# Simplification of the Six Rites and Later Transformations

## 1. Definition

Over more than two thousand years of transmission the Six Rites were repeatedly merged and adjusted. This entry sets out the institutional changes from the Song onwards — “Six Rites → Three Rites → Four Rites” — down to the transformation of the civilised wedding of the modern age and the contemporary Chinese-style wedding, showing how the ritual order was reshaped along with the lineage system and social structure.

## 2. Historical Origins

### The Song: the establishment of the Three Rites

- Sima Guang’s *Book of Ceremonies* reduced the Six Rites: the negotiation of the marriage, the asking of the name and naji were all gathered into “nacai”; nazheng was called “nabing”; with “qinying” added, this is in effect three rites.
- Zhu Xi’s *Family Rituals*, “Wedding Rites”, fixes the three rites as “nacai, nabing, qinying”, and changes “requesting the date” to “announcing the date”. The *Family Rituals* had immense influence and became the basic model of household ritual thereafter.
- The substance of the reduction to three: **the negotiation of the marriage was compressed into nacai**, and **qingqi lost its sense of “requesting”, the husband’s family having taken the initiative**.

### The Ming: the division between officials and commoners

- The *Collected Rites of the Great Ming* and the *Collected Statutes of the Ming* prescribe the Six Rites for the weddings of officials, while commoners “perform only four rites” (nacai, nabing, qinying, and the bride’s presentation to her parents-in-law) — a ritual reduction for the common people.
- In popular practice the “three stages” of “tiqin — dingqin — quqin” prevailed, and the ritual books drifted further from practice.

### The Qing: fixed in the Comprehensive Rituals

- Official weddings: nacai, nabing, qingqi, qinying — four sections.
- Weddings of knights and commoners: nacai, nabing, qingqi, qinying, the ceremonial reduced but the sequence similar.
- Contemporaries already spoke generally of “pinli” and “jiaqu”, and the “Six Rites” became an archaic literary name.

### The modern age: ritual order withdraws from law

- In the late Qing and early Republic the “civilised marriage” and the “new-style wedding” arose, with bowing, the exchange of rings and a public witness — the ceremonies of the Six Rites being replaced wholesale.
- The Republican *Civil Code*, “Part on Kinship”, established the marriage contract and marriage by consent, and the ritual Six Rites ceased to be a constitutive element of marriage.

### The present: the re-arrangement of symbols

- The industrialised wedding trade’s “Chinese-style wedding” requisitions the symbols of the Six Rites afresh: the bows in the hall, the shared cup, the tying of the hair, the lifting of the veil — mostly performed reconstructions.
- The promotion of “simple weddings” and “collective weddings” in many places has compressed the ceremonies further.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

The correspondence between the Three Rites and the modern wedding:

| Three Rites (Song) | Source in the Six Rites | Modern equivalent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nacai | Nacai + wenming + naji | Meeting, becoming acquainted, negotiating the marriage |
| Nabing | Nazheng | Engagement, betrothal, giving the bride-price |
| Qinying | Qingqi + qinying | Fixing the date, fetching the bride, holding the wedding |

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Fujian and Taiwan**: the name “Six Rites” survives to this day, with stages called wenming, songding, wanpin, qingqi and yingqin.
- **North China**: usually “tiqin — xiangqin — dingqin — kan rizi — quqin”.
- **Jiangsu and Zhejiang**: “shuoqin — xiangqin — xiading — song rizi — yingqu”.
- The correspondence between the wedding customs of the various peoples and the Six Rites differs from one to another; the structure of negotiation, engagement and reception among most peoples may be compared with the Six Rites, but the content of the ceremonies and their religious background differ, and simple equivalence is not appropriate.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- The simplification to three or four rites also changed the documentary system: the horoscope card, the betrothal letter and the date slip were reduced to “marriage letter”, “gift list” and oral agreement.
- From the modern age onwards “marriage certificates” and “deeds of engagement” appeared — legal rather than ritual documents.
