# Names for the Matchmaker (Moon Old Man, Red Maid, Ice Man)

## 1. Definition

The names for the matchmaker are unusually rich, dividing into **elegant names** and **popular names**, each with its own source and use.

| Term | Source / origin | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mei / meiren / meishuo** | the *Shijing*; the “Matchmaker” office of the *Zhouli* | the ordinary term |
| **Old Man of the Moon** | Li Fuyan (Tang), *Xu xuanguai lu*, “The Shop of Fixed Marriages” | elegant, for a union fated beforehand |
| **Crimson Maiden** | Wang Shifu (Yuan), *Romance of the Western Chamber* | elegant, especially of an eager go-between |
| **Ice man** | *Jinshu*, “Biography of Suo Dan”: “above the ice is yang, below it yin” | a literary elegant name |
| **Cutter of the axe handle** | *Shijing*, “Bin Odes: Cutting the Axe Handle”: “How does one cut an axe handle? Without an axe one cannot” | an elegant name |
| **Keeper of the judgement** | *Zhouli*: “the Matchmaker has charge of the judgement of the myriad people” | a literary term |
| **Introducer / string-puller** | modern general usage | a popular term |
| **Matchmaker woman / matchmaker man** | popular usage | often jocular or pejorative in colour |
| **Da jin jie** | the Lingnan region | the woman who attends and assists at the wedding |

**Function**: “to bring the good of two surnames together” — to propose, carry the horoscope cards, negotiate the betrothal gift, fix the wedding date, and attend the ceremonies.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Zhou**: the *Zhouli* established the office of the “Matchmaker”; the *Shijing*: “How does one take a wife? One must tell one’s parents… without a matchmaker one cannot”, reflecting the necessity of the go-between.
- **Jin and Tang**: the allusions of the “ice man” and the “Old Man of the Moon” appear.
- **Song and Yuan**: matching became a profession, and the name “matchmaker woman” appears in storybooks and drama (the “Crimson Maiden” of *Romance of the Western Chamber*).
- **Ming and Qing**: matchmakers were distinguished as “official” and “private”, and the gazetteers commonly record “matchmaking”.
- **Today**: “introducer”, “Crimson Maiden” and “marriage agencies” run in parallel; with online dating there has appeared the “online matchmaker”.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **The proposal**: the matchmaker conveys the wish to marry to each side.
2. **Carrying the cards**: the horoscope cards are carried back and forth.
3. **Negotiating the betrothal**: the amount of the betrothal gift is settled through the go-between.
4. **Fixing the date**: the wedding date is agreed through her mediation.
5. **Attending**: on the wedding day she assists at the ceremonies, and receives the “thanks to the matchmaker”.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: called “matchmaker”; thanks are given with a hock, wine and meat.
- **Jiangnan**: called “matchmaker” or “Old Man of the Moon”, with the proverb “eighteen hocks for the matchmaker”.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: “matchmaker woman”; the wedding includes the “matchmaker’s gift”.
- **Guangdong**: the *da jin jie* attends the wedding, combining the offices of matchmaker and ceremonialist.
- **Ethnic minorities**: among some peoples a village elder or the maternal uncle acts as go-between (the “matchmaker man”), with different names and duties; these must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: the horoscope card, the marriage letter (bearing the matchmaker’s signature), the gift list.
- **Objects**: the matchmaker’s thank-offering (wine and meat, cakes, red packets).
- **Textual basis**: the *Zhouli*, “Offices of Earth: the Matchmaker”; the *Chengwei lu*.
