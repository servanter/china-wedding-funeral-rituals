# The Titles of Groom and Bride

## 1. Definition

| Term | Period / origin | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Xinlang / xinniang (groom / bride)** | Tang and Song onwards | *Lang* and *niang* are affectionate terms for the young; *xin* marks the wedding |
| **Xinren (the new couple)** | Tang and Song | the collective term for the couple on the wedding day |
| **Xinfu (new wife)** | Han and Wei onwards | at first the newly married woman, later also used of a wife and of a daughter-in-law |
| **Guanren** | Tang and Song | the wife’s term for her husband (with a note of respect) |
| **Niangzi** | Tang and Song | the husband’s term for his wife; later generalised |
| **Liangren** | pre-Qin | the *Shijing*: “What night is this, that I see this good man?” |
| **Waizi / neizi** | literary | the husband as “the outer one”, the wife as “the inner one” |
| **Zhuojing / jiannei** | literary humble terms | the husband’s way of speaking of his wife to others |
| **Fujun / xianggong** | Ming and Qing | the wife’s terms for her husband |
| **Ana / darling** | today | affectionate borrowings under foreign influence |

**The ritual meaning of the terms**: the pairing of “official man” and “lady” embodies the **coexistence of hierarchy and affection** between husband and wife; “the outer one” and “the inner one” embody the household order of **divided spheres within and without**.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: “good man” and the “wedding rite” appear in the *Shijing* and the *Yili*.
- **Han, Wei and the Six Dynasties**: “new wife” was general; “lord” and “lady” gradually became terms of address.
- **Tang and Song**: “groom”, “bride”, “official man” and “lady” settled and spread.
- **Ming and Qing**: “xianggong” and “niangzi” were used together; in modern times “Mr”, “Mrs” and “lover” appeared.
- **Today**: “hubby”, “wifey”, “Mr”, “Mrs”, “lover” and “partner” are used together.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **After engagement**: “fiancé” and “fiancée”.
2. **The wedding day**: “groom”, “bride”, “the new couple”.
3. **The rite of changing address**: the couple address one another and change their address to the elders of both sides.
4. **After the wedding**: an honorific is chosen by occasion (outwardly) or an affectionate name (inwardly).
5. **After children are born**: the form generally shifts to “their father” and “their mother”.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: “the one who runs the house”, “daughter-in-law”, and in parts of the north-west “*poyi*”.
- **Jiangnan**: “Mr”, “lady”, “the person in the room”.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: “hand-in-hand” (the couple’s term for one another — the most distinctive local usage).
- **Guangdong**: “hubby”, “wifey” and “Mrs” are general.
- **Ethnic minorities**: each people’s terms for husband and wife differ (as with “*axa*” and “*azhu*”); these must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: in marriage letters and wedding cards the two parties are called “qianzao” (the male destiny) and “kunzao” (the female destiny).
- **Textual basis**: the *Erya*, “Explaining Kin”; the *Shiming*; the *Chengwei lu*.
