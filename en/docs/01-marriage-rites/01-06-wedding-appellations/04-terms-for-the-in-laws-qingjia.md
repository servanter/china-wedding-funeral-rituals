# Terms for the In-Laws (Qingjia)

## 1. Definition

“**Qingjia**” is **the mutual term between the parents of the two sides joined by a marriage**, the direct expression in nomenclature of the conclusion of the marriage.

| Term | Refers to | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Qingjia / qingjia gong** | the other party’s father | mutual |
| **Qingjia mu / qingjia po** | the other party’s mother | mutual |
| **Yuefu / yuemü (zhangren / zhangmüniang)** | the wife’s parents | the son-in-law’s terms |
| **Gonggong / popo (gongpo)** | the husband’s parents | the daughter-in-law’s terms |
| **Jiugu** | the husband’s parents (old term) | classical ritual usage (“the wife is presented to the *jiugu*”) |
| **Waijiu / waigu** | the wife’s parents (old term) | literary |
| **Taishan / taishui** | father-in-law / mother-in-law | an elegant literary usage (the allusion of “Mount Tai” comes from Duan Chengshi’s *Youyang zazu*, Tang) |

**The relativity of the terms**: the same person is called different things according to who is speaking — **the husband’s family looks at the daughter-in-law, the wife’s family at the son-in-law** — so a term also marks a “standpoint”.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the *Erya*, “Explaining Kin”: “the wife’s father is *waijiu*, the wife’s mother *waigu*”; “*jiugu*” means the husband’s parents.
- **Tang and Song**: the elegant “Mount Tai” for father-in-law (a Tang allusion).
- **Ming and Qing**: “qingjia”, “qingjia gong” and “qingjia mu” became the ordinary terms; the *Chengwei lu* lists them in detail.
- **Today**: after the “change of address” at the wedding the two sets of parents call one another “qingjia”, and the couple call the other’s parents “father” and “mother”.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **The negotiation**: each side addresses the other as “honoured in-laws”.
2. **After engagement**: they formally call one another “qingjia” and exchange greetings.
3. **The wedding day**: the couple perform the “rite of changing address”, calling the other’s parents “father” and “mother”.
4. **Dealings after the wedding**: at the seasons they “visit the kin”, the terms remaining in use.
5. **After children are born**: they rise in rank to “maternal grandfather / grandmother” and “grandfather / grandmother”.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: “qingjia” and “qingjia mu”; the son-in-law calls them “zhangren” and “zhangmüniang”.
- **Jiangnan**: “qingjia”, “zhangren” and “yuemü” are used together.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: “qingjia”, “zhangren ba” and “zhangmüniang”; the daughter-in-law calls her parents-in-law “the great one” and “the great official”.
- **Guangdong**: “waifu” and “waimu” (the wife’s parents); the daughter-in-law calls them “jiagong” and “jiapo”.
- **Ethnic minorities**: each people’s terms for the senior affines answer to its kinship system; these must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: marriage letters (setting out the names of both sets of parents), wedding cards, gift lists.
- **Textual basis**: the *Erya*, “Explaining Kin”; the *Shiming*; the *Chengwei lu*.
