# Terms for Marriageable Age (Jiji, Ruoguan, Daizi, Chuge)

## 1. Definition

| Term | Age / stage | Source and meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Zongjiao / chuitiao** | childhood | the hair styles of children |
| **Doukou (“cardamom”)** | a girl of thirteen or fourteen | Du Mu (Tang): “the cardamom tips in the second month” |
| **Jiji (“hairpin-taking”)** | a girl of fifteen (the **hairpin ceremony**) | *Liji*, “Inner Regulations”: “at fifteen she takes the hairpin”, meaning she may marry |
| **Daizi (“awaiting the character”)** | past the hairpin and unmarried | *zi* means being promised in marriage |
| **Ruoguan (“the weak cap”)** | a man of twenty (the **capping ceremony**) | *Liji*, “Summary of Ritual”: “at twenty he is weak; he takes the cap” |
| **Chuge (“leaving the chamber”)** | a woman’s marriage | *ge* means the women’s quarters |
| **Yugui (“going home”)** | a woman’s marriage | *Shijing*: “this girl goes home, fit for her house and home” |
| **Jiefa (“binding the hair”)** | first marriage | Han and Wei onwards: “hair-bound as husband and wife” |
| **Huajia / guxi** | old age | not a term of marriageable age, but used of long marriages (as “the sixtieth year renewed”) |

**Ritual basis**: a man took the cap at twenty and a woman the hairpin at fifteen, marking adulthood and fitness to marry. “A man marries at thirty, a woman at twenty” (the *Zhouli*) was the ideal age; actual ages were lower throughout the dynasties.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the *Liji* set the cap and hairpin ceremonies and the idea of marriageable age.
- **Han and Tang**: early marriage was general (many men marrying at fifteen or sixteen and women at thirteen or fourteen).
- **Song, Ming and Qing**: the ritual codes urged “marriage in good time”, but actual ages varied by region and class.
- **Modern times**: the statutory age rose step by step; the Marriage Law of 1950 set twenty for men and eighteen for women.
- **Today**: the Civil Code sets twenty-two for men and twenty for women; later marriage is the trend.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **The hairpin ceremony (girls of fifteen)**: the hair is bound up and the hairpin taken, childish dress changed for adult dress.
2. **The capping ceremony (men of twenty)**: the cap is taken and a courtesy name given, marking adulthood.
3. **Negotiation of marriage**: after the hairpin and the cap, entry into the negotiation (“awaiting the character in the women’s quarters”).
4. **Promise in marriage**: after nazheng, the *zi* (the promise).
5. **Leaving the chamber (going home)**: marriage and the completion of the rites.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Han regions**: by the Ming and Qing the cap and hairpin ceremonies were mostly simplified among the people, leaving only survivals such as “shangtou” and “the coiling of the hair”.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: before the wedding there is the custom of “shangtou” (combing the couple’s hair), connected with the vestiges of the hairpin ceremony.
- **Ethnic minorities**: each people’s coming-of-age rites (as the Yao “taking the precepts”, the Miao coming-of-age rite, the Tibetan “putting on the head”) relate to ideas of marriageable age; these must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: the hairpin, the cap, ceremonial dress.
- **Documents**: marriage letters (recording the ages of both parties), horoscope cards.
- **Textual basis**: the *Liji*, “Inner Regulations” and “Summary of Ritual”; the *Zhouli*.
