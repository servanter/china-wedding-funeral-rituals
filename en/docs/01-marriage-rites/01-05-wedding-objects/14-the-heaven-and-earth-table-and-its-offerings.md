# The Heaven-and-Earth Table and Its Offerings

## 1. Definition

“**Bowing to Heaven and Earth**” is one of the core ceremonies of the traditional wedding, and its furniture is called the “**Heaven-and-Earth table**” (the incense table).

**What is set out**:
- **Tablet of Heaven and Earth** — inscribed “the seat of Heaven, Earth, the ruler, parents and teacher” (or “the seat of Heaven and Earth”).
- **Ritual vessels** — incense burner, candlesticks (a pair of dragon-and-phoenix candles), incense holders, offering trays.
- **Offerings** — fruit, cakes, wine, tea (in pairs, on trays).
- **Others** — the grain measure (holding the five grains), steelyard, rule, mirror, bow and arrows (in parts of the north), the seat of the “god of joy”.

**Ritual meaning**: bowing to Heaven and Earth is to **announce the completion of the marriage** to “Heaven, Earth, the ruler, parents and teacher”, to the ancestors and to the gods, giving the marriage a sanction beyond the family.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the *Shihunli* has the section of “**announcing at the temple**” (sacrificing to inform the ancestors), the ritual ancestor of “bowing to Heaven and Earth”.
- **Tang and Song**: an incense table was set out for the wedding ceremony; the name “bowing in the hall” appears in Tang and Song notebooks.
- **Ming and Qing**: “bowing to Heaven and Earth” became general, the setting of the table settled, and the gazetteers commonly record the “Heaven-and-Earth tablet” and the “incense table”.
- **Today**: Chinese-style weddings set a “Heaven-and-Earth table” and perform the “three bows” (to Heaven and Earth, to the parents, and the couple to one another).

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Setting the table**: the Heaven-and-Earth table is set before the hall, with the tablet and vessels displayed.
2. **Lighting and incense**: the dragon-and-phoenix candles are lit and incense offered.
3. **Announcing to the temple / to Heaven and Earth**: the chanter of the rites chants and the bows are performed.
4. **The three bows**: first to Heaven and Earth, second to the parents, then the couple to one another.
5. **Clearing the table**: when the ceremony is done the offerings are removed and either shared out or kept.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the table bears the tablet “Heaven, Earth, ruler, parents and teacher”, with bow and arrows, measure and steelyard.
- **Jiangnan**: the incense table is simply and elegantly set, the offerings chiefly fruit and cakes.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: at the bowing the “ancestral tablets” and “Heaven and Earth” are honoured side by side, with insistence on the continuity of incense and fire.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples sacrifice to Heaven and Earth and the ancestors in their own ways (the hearth sacrifice, the sacrifice at the ancestral tree), which must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: the Heaven-and-Earth tablet, incense burner, candlesticks (dragon-and-phoenix candles), incense holders, offering trays, grain measure, steelyard, rule, mirror.
- **Offerings**: fruit, cakes, wine, tea.
- **Related documents**: the marriage letter, the words of announcement (to Heaven, Earth and the ancestors), the gift list.
