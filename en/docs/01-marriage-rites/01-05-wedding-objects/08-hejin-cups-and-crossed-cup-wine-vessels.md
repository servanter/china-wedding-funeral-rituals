# Hejin Cups and Crossed-Cup Wine Vessels

## 1. Definition

“**Hejin**” is a core ceremony of the wedding recorded in the *Shihunli* of the *Yili*: one gourd is split into two ladles (the *jin*), and husband and wife each take one and drink, figuring “**two made one**”. In later ages cups replaced the ladles, and the rite became “**the crossed-cup wine**”.

**Forms of the vessels**:
- **The *jin* (ladle)**: a gourd (*hulu*) split in two, the handles joined with cord.
- **The hejin cup**: two cups joined (as with “twin cups” or “conjoined cups”), sometimes linked by a coloured ribbon.
- **The Ming “hejin cup”**: in jade and in porcelain, the twin vessels carefully modelled (examples in museum collections).

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Pre-Qin**: the *Shihunli*: “eating from the same victim, drinking from the shared cup”.
- **Han and Tang**: the “*jin*” evolved into the “cup”; the custom of the “crossed cup” appears in Tang records.
- **Song and Yuan**: the “crossed-cup wine” spread; the *Mengliang lu* records the “crossed cup” at Lin’an weddings.
- **Ming and Qing**: the hejin rite and the “crossed-cup wine” ran in parallel; hejin cups became prized craft objects.
- **Today**: Chinese-style weddings perform the “crossed-cup wine” (or “the hejin rite”), often displaying cups tied with coloured ribbon.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Setting out the vessels**: wine vessels are set out in the bridal chamber or the hall.
2. **Hejin**: the couple each take one vessel, first drinking separately and then exchanging (the “crossed cup”).
3. **Completion**: when the drinking is done the cups are thrown or tied, and all present offer congratulations.
4. **The banquet**: performed together with the rite of “the same victim” (eating together from one animal), mostly omitted in recent times.
5. **Keeping**: the hejin cups are often kept as a memento.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the “crossed-cup wine” is performed, in some places with the two cups tied with red thread.
- **Jiangnan**: the hejin rite is emphasised, using twin cups.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: the crossed-cup wine runs parallel with “drinking tea” (the bride offering tea).
- **Ethnic minorities**: the shared-drinking rites of weddings differ (drinking with arms linked, drinking from one bowl) and are not of the same origin as the Han hejin; they must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: the *jin* (ladle), the hejin cup (twin cup), wine cups, coloured ribbon.
- **Companion objects**: the Heaven-and-Earth table, auspicious candles, wine.
- **Related documents**: the marriage letter, the hejin gift list.
