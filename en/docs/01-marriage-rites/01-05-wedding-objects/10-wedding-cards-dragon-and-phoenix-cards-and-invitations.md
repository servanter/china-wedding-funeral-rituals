# Wedding Cards (Dragon-and-Phoenix Cards) and Invitations

## 1. Definition

The wedding card, also the “**dragon-and-phoenix card**”, the “auspicious card” or the “invitation card”, is **the core document of a marriage**, comprising two kinds: the “dragon-and-phoenix card” of the engagement and the “invitation card” for guests.

**Form and layout**:
- **Dragon-and-phoenix card (marriage letter)**: of red paper or red silk, printed with dragons and phoenixes and double happiness; it records the names of both parties, their birth data, native place, the introducer and the names of the parents.
- **Invitation card**: bearing words such as “I have the honour to announce that on such a day of such a month my son so-and-so will be married; a banquet is respectfully prepared, and your whole household is invited to attend”; distinguished as the “**double card**” (each side inviting separately) and the “**joint card**” (held jointly).
- **Rules**: written in red or gold; white paper and writers of “poor hand” are avoided; delivery insists on **personal delivery** and on even numbers.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Zhou and Han**: the “marriage letter” rested on the documents of the Six Rites (the *Zhouli* gives the Matchmaker charge of marriage writings).
- **Tang and Song**: the forms of the marriage letter and the invitation were gradually completed; the name “dragon-and-phoenix card” spread from the Song onwards.
- **Ming and Qing**: the formats of the marriage letter and the invitation settled, with a distinction between the “great card” and the “small card” in different places.
- **Modern times**: lithographed and letterpress invitations became common.
- **Today**: paper and electronic invitations run in parallel; the marriage certificate has legal effect.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Sending the card (engagement)**: at “nacai” the dragon-and-phoenix cards are exchanged (“exchanging cards”).
2. **Sending the date card**: at qingqi the “date card” (bearing the wedding date) is sent.
3. **Sending invitations**: before the wedding invitations are delivered separately (the groom’s family inviting male guests, the bride’s family female guests, or jointly).
4. **Delivery**: delivered in person by a kinsman or a messenger, with wedding sweets accompanying the card.
5. **Reply**: those invited reply by card or by word of mouth.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: invitations are usually single red-paper cards with concise wording.
- **Jiangnan**: wedding cards insist on calligraphy and overprinting, with “full cards” and “half cards”.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: the marriage writing is called the “deed of marriage” or the “twelve-card letter”, with an antique layout.
- **Guangdong**: the invitation is called the “auspicious card”, with modern adjuncts such as the “cake card”.
- **Ethnic minorities**: guests are mostly invited orally or with particular tokens; there are also forms of marriage document, which must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Documents**: dragon-and-phoenix card (marriage letter), date card, invitation, gift list, thank-you card.
- **Objects**: the card box, red paper, seal paste, wedding sweets.
- **Collection catalogues**: actual marriage letters and invitations in museum collections.
