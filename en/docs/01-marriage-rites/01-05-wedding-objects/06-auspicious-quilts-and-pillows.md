# Auspicious Quilts and Pillows

## 1. Definition

Auspicious quilts and pillows are the general name for **the bedding of the bridal chamber**; because they are mostly embroidered with “dragons and phoenixes”, “double happiness” and “mandarin ducks”, they are called the “dragon-and-phoenix quilt” and the “mandarin-duck pillow”.

**Rules**:
- **Quilt cover**: scarlet silk or satin, embroidered with dragons and phoenixes, double happiness, peony, mandarin ducks and pomegranate (for many sons).
- **“Sewing the quilt”**: the sewing of the auspicious quilt must be presided over by a “**woman of full fortune**” (a woman whose parents are living and who has both sons and daughters); widows and pregnant women are avoided in some regions.
- **Number of pieces**: pairs are auspicious, normally “four laid and four covered” or “six laid and six covered” (the numbers of the trousseau).
- **“Dressing the bed”**: the bed is dressed at an auspicious hour before the wedding by the woman of full fortune, with “dates, chestnuts, peanuts and longans” placed among the bedding, taking the sense of “an early standing son” and “peanuts, noble sons” (a pun on “flowering and bearing in turn”).
- **Taboos**: the bed must not be left empty after dressing (“no empty room”); pregnant women must not sit on it; no napping on the bridal bed by day.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **From the pre-Qin onwards**: bedding was an important item of the trousseau (“quilts” and “pillows” appear in the ritual classics).
- **Tang and Song**: the custom of “dressing the bed” appears in the notebooks (“scattering the bed”, “furnishing the chamber”).
- **Ming and Qing**: the gazetteers commonly record the customs of “dressing the bed”, of the “woman of full fortune” and of “scattering the bed”.
- **Today**: in the cities it is simplified into “a new bed and new bedding”, but “dressing the bed” and “scattering the bed (scattering wedding fruit)” are still seen in Chinese-style weddings.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Sewing the quilt**: a woman of full fortune is asked to sew the auspicious quilts, in pairs.
2. **Sending the trousseau**: the quilts and pillows are sent to the groom’s house with the dowry.
3. **Dressing the bed**: the bed is dressed at the auspicious hour by the woman of full fortune, who lays out the bedding and pillows.
4. **Scattering the bed**: dates, chestnuts, peanuts and longans are scattered on the bed while those present speak blessings.
5. **The bridal chamber**: on the wedding night the bed must not be left empty, nor may others sit on it.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: “four laid and four covered” is emphasised, and the scattering is chiefly dates and chestnuts.
- **Jiangnan**: the “dressing of the bed” is done by a woman of full fortune, and fine embroidery on the quilt cover is insisted upon.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: the trousseau is called “the bridal furnishings”, the quilts and pillows insisting on pairs, with the custom of choosing an auspicious day for “setting the bed”.
- **Ethnic minorities**: the arrangements of bedding and sleeping quarters at weddings differ (some peoples practising “not taking up residence in the husband’s house”, where the “bridal bed” has a different meaning); these must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Auspicious quilt and pillow**: scarlet silk or satin covers embroidered with dragons and phoenixes, double happiness and mandarin ducks.
- **Objects scattered on the bed**: dates, chestnuts, peanuts, longans, lotus seeds.
- **Related documents**: the trousseau list (list of the dowry), the marriage letter.
