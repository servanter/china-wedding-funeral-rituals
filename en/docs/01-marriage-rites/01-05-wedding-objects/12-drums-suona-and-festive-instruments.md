# Drums, Suona and Festive Instruments

## 1. Definition

Wedding music (the “**blowing and beating**”, the “drum-and-wind band”, the “blowers and drummers”) is the indispensable sound-marker of the traditional wedding.

**Make-up**:
- At the core are the **suona (the shawm)** and **percussion (gong, drum, cymbals, hand-bells)**.
- An expanded band adds the **sheng, flute, guanzi, erhu and banhu**; in the music of the south, **silk-and-bamboo** ensembles (the “Jiangnan silk and bamboo”).
- The wedding as a whole divides into **the reception, the bowing in the hall, the banquet and the bridal chamber**, each with its own tunes.

**Common tunes**: “A Hundred Birds Pay Homage to the Phoenix”, “Lifting the Bridal Chair”, “Brimming with Joy”, “Stepping Higher”, “Dragons and Phoenixes Bring Prosperity” and others; wedding and funeral tunes may not be interchanged.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Han and Wei**: ceremonial wind-and-drum music (the “**gu-chui**”) was already used in ritual, at first military and later for weddings and funerals.
- **Tang and Song**: popular “**drum music**” spread into weddings (the *Dongjing menghua lu* records music at Bianjing weddings).
- **Ming and Qing**: the suona became the leading wedding instrument; the “**blowers and drummers**” became a specialised trade, commonly recorded in the gazetteers.
- **Modern times**: Chinese and Western bands were used together; in the later twentieth century cities turned to recorded sound, while the countryside still engaged drum bands.
- **Today**: Chinese-style weddings engage a “folk music band” or play suona tunes; under intangible-heritage protection the art of the suona is much valued.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Engaging the band**: the drum band (the “blowers and drummers”) is engaged in advance.
2. **Music at the reception**: the band leads the way when the chair sets out and the bride is fetched.
3. **Music at the bowing**: the “bowing tune” is played during the ceremony.
4. **Music at the banquet**: joyful tunes are played as guests are entertained; in some places with “sung airs” added.
5. **Music of farewell**: the “farewell tune” is played as the ceremony ends and the guests disperse.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: suona with gongs and drums, loud and warm in atmosphere.
- **Jiangnan**: silk-and-bamboo (flute, vertical flute, erhu) predominates, the melodies soft.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: the “northern pipe” and “southern pipe” repertoires are used at weddings; the “array” drum music is lively.
- **Guangdong**: the “eight-tone band” (gongs, drums and eight tones) is common at weddings.
- **Ethnic minorities**: the *lusheng*, the elephant-foot drum, the horse-head fiddle and the *dutar* are the wedding instruments of their own peoples and do not belong to the Han festival-music system; they must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Instruments**: suona, sheng, flute, gong, drum, cymbals, erhu, silk-and-bamboo instruments.
- **Related documents**: the card engaging the band, the list of insignia (setting out the officers and the band), the names of the tunes.
- **Collection catalogues**: folk instruments in museum collections.
