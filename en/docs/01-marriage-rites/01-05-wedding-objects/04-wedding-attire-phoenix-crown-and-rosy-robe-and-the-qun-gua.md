# Wedding Attire: Phoenix Crown and Rosy Robe, and the Qun Gua

## 1. Definition

Traditional wedding dress falls into **men’s ceremonial dress** and **women’s ceremonial dress**, the forms and gradations differing from dynasty to dynasty.

**Women’s wedding dress**:
- **Phoenix crown and rosy robe** — current in the Ming and Qing: a phoenix crown (adorned with dragons and phoenixes, pearls and kingfisher feathers), a rosy robe (a stole-like embroidered band), and a red skirt. The custom of “**borrowed dress**”: commoners borrowed the crown and robe of titled ladies, whence the general spread of the “**phoenix crown and rosy robe**”.
- **Qun gua** — current in the late Qing and Republican period in Lingnan (Cantonese and Chaoshan areas): **red skirt and black jacket** (the jacket embroidered with dragons, phoenixes and auspicious clouds, in grades called “empress *gua*” and “queen *gua*”), today called the “dragon-and-phoenix *gua*”.
- **Qipao and the Xiuhe dress** — Chinese wedding dress as it evolved in modern times.

**Men’s wedding dress**:
- Ming: the “**black gauze cap, round-collared robe and blue gown**”; Qing: the “**long gown and riding jacket**”; Republican: the “**Sun Yat-sen suit or Western suit**”.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Han and Tang**: wedding dress favoured the colours “dark and crimson” (black and red), strongly marked by the ritual order.
- **Song**: weddings had the saying “red for the man, green for the woman”; the phoenix crown gradually arose.
- **Ming and Qing**: the phoenix crown and rosy robe, and the red skirt, settled; the custom of borrowed dress made wedding dress uniform.
- **Late Qing and Republican period**: the Lingnan *qun gua* took shape; the Western wedding gown came in from the coast.
- **Today**: Chinese-style weddings use the Xiuhe dress and the dragon-and-phoenix *gua*, Western-style weddings the gown, and hybrid weddings a combination (gown, toasting dress, *qun gua*).

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Preparing the wedding clothes**: the bride’s family prepare her clothes (part of the trousseau); the groom’s family prepare the groom’s dress.
2. **Dressing after the coiffure**: on the wedding day the dress is put on after “shangtou” (the coiling of the hair).
3. **Ready to leave**: phoenix crown, rosy robe, red shoes and veil all complete.
4. **Changing**: traditional weddings changed into “ordinary clothes” after the ceremony; contemporary weddings have separate “main gown, toasting dress and farewell dress”.
5. **Keeping**: the wedding dress is kept as a memento.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: phoenix crown and rosy robe, red jacket and red skirt; in modern times the long gown and riding jacket.
- **Jiangnan**: fine embroidery, with attention to the patterns of the rosy robe and the red skirt.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: the bride wears the “dragon-and-phoenix *gua*” or the Xiuhe dress, with insistence on gold-thread embroidery.
- **Guangdong**: the *qun gua* is graded as “empress *gua*, queen *gua*, greater five blessings, middle five blessings, lesser five blessings”.
- **Ethnic minorities**: each people’s wedding dress is chiefly its own full festival dress (Miao silver costume, Tibetan *pulu* robe and so on); not of the same origin as the Han system, and to be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Women’s dress**: phoenix crown, rosy robe, red skirt, *qun gua*, embroidered shoes.
- **Men’s dress**: black gauze cap, round-collared robe, long gown and riding jacket, formal hat.
- **Companion objects**: the veil, the wedding steelyard, the marriage letter.
- **Collection catalogues**: actual wedding dress and pictorial catalogues in museum collections.
