# The Bridal Veil (Gaitou)

## 1. Definition

The bridal veil, also “the red veil”, “the red head-covering” or “the face-covering”, is **the red fabric that covers the bride’s face**, worn from the personal reception until it is lifted in the bridal chamber.

**Construction**: usually a square of red silk or satin, the four corners finished with tassels, sometimes embroidered (dragons and phoenixes, double happiness, peony).

**Symbolism and explanation**:
1. **The apotropaic account** — red and concealment serve to “avoid the ill and tend towards the good”.
2. **The ritual-transition account** — concealing and revealing is a classic “**rite of passage**”: the change from daughter of her parents’ house to wife of her husband’s, cutting off the old identity.
3. **“Lifting the veil”** — in the bridal chamber the groom lifts it with a **steelyard beam (the wedding steelyard)** or a *ruyi* sceptre, taking the sense of “exactly as one would wish”.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Han, Wei, Tang and Song**: the wedding rites had the idea of “screening the face”, the object not yet fixed in form (a fan “to be waved aside”, or a curtained hat).
- **Song and Yuan**: records of the “veil” in weddings increase (the *Mengliang lu* records the wedding customs of Lin’an).
- **Ming and Qing**: the red veil and the rite of “lifting the veil” settled, becoming indispensable to the wedding.
- **Today**: Chinese-style weddings keep the red veil; Western-style weddings treat it as decoration or omit it.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Putting on the veil**: the bride’s family cover her face before she mounts the chair (or before the ceremony).
2. **The journey**: the veil covers her face to the groom’s house.
3. **Lifting the veil**: in the bridal chamber the groom lifts it with the wedding steelyard, and all present offer congratulations.
4. **Keeping it**: in some regions the veil is kept as a memento and a “lucky object”.
5. **Sharing the cup**: after the lifting, the rite of the cup of crossed arms follows.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: a red veil embroidered with double happiness and dragons and phoenixes; lifted with the wedding steelyard.
- **Jiangnan**: the veil insists on fine embroidery, and the lifting may be done with a *ruyi* sceptre.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: the veil is used together with “a black umbrella over the head” and “a rice sieve over the face”.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples have customs of covering or binding the head (with meanings different from the Han veil), which must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **The veil**: red silk or satin with tassels at the four corners.
- **Companion objects**: the wedding steelyard, the *ruyi* sceptre, the wedding fan (for waving aside).
- **Related documents**: the marriage letter, the wedding card.
