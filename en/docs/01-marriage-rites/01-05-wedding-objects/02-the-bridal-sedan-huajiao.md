# The Bridal Sedan (Huajiao)

## 1. Definition

The bridal sedan, also called the “wedding chair”, the “painted chair” or the “flowered chair”, is **the chair in which the bride is carried at the personal reception**. It is one of the most emblematic objects of the traditional wedding.

**Construction and decoration**:
- A wooden cabin, surrounded with red silk and embroidered panels; the roof decorated with a “roof flower” and a “precious canopy”.
- The decoration insists on “**red, gold and colour**”: satin-embroidered curtains, tassels, woollen balls and glass mirrors.
- Inside are a cushion and a footrest, together with the “**chair rice**” or a hand-warmer.

**Carrying regulations**: rank is set by the number of bearers — **two bearers (the small chair), four bearers (the ordinary bridal chair), eight bearers (the wealthy)**; official families had the “**great eight-bearer chair**” in the form of an official’s chair. The bearers were supplied by the specialised “**chair house**” (the bridal-chair shop), which also let out insignia.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Tang and Song**: the chair (the “shoulder carriage”) came gradually into wedding use; by the Southern Song the fetching of the bride by chair was general.
- **Ming and Qing**: the custom of the bridal chair settled, and the “chair house” became a specialised trade; the gazetteers commonly record “bridal chairs” and “chair-bearers”.
- **Modern times**: bicycles, carriages and cars came in; in the later twentieth century cities turned to cars (“flower cars”).
- **Today**: “fetching the bride by bridal chair” is occasionally seen (mostly as a Chinese-style performance or a wedding in a scenic area).

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Letting the chair**: the chair is ordered from the chair house before the wedding, the colour, insignia and number of bearers being fixed.
2. **Sending out the chair**: it sets out at the auspicious hour, drums and music leading to the bride’s house.
3. **Mounting**: the bride is carried onto the chair by the matron of honour or an elder brother, her face covered by the veil.
4. **Raising the chair**: firecrackers are let off and wedding fruit scattered as the chair is raised for the return (setting it down being avoided — “the chair does not touch the ground”).
5. **Setting down**: at the groom’s gate the rites of “crossing the brazier” and “kicking the chair door” are performed, and the bride alights.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the bridal chair is carried with drums and wind music, the “great eight-bearer chair” being insisted upon.
- **Jiangnan**: the chair is finely decorated, with the customs of “chair rice” and “mounting balls”.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: the chair comes with a full set of insignia (the “reception array”), with “a bronze mirror hung before the chair”.
- **Guangdong**: the chair is combined with the “dragon-and-phoenix *gua*”, the procession accompanied by the *da jin jie*.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples use chairs at weddings (under Han influence), while others fetch the bride by horse, boat or ox-cart; these must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **The bridal chair**: cabin, curtains, roof flower, tassels.
- **Companion objects**: the veil, hand-warmer, chair rice, wedding fruit, firecrackers, insignia (banners, tablets, umbrellas and fans).
- **Related documents**: the engagement letter, the marriage letter, the list of insignia (the “duty list”).
