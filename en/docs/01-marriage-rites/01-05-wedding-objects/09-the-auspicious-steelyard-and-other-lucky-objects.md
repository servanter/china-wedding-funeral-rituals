# The Auspicious Steelyard and Other Lucky Objects

## 1. Definition

The **auspicious objects** used in wedding rites mostly work by homophony and symbol, forming a material system of “auspicious vocabulary”.

| Object | Use | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Wedding steelyard (the beam)** | Lifting the veil | *Cheng* sounds like *cheng* “to suit”: “exactly as one would wish” |
| ***Ruyi* sceptre** | Lifting the veil, display | “All as one would wish” |
| **Grain measure (the wedding measure)** | Display, holding grain | “A measure of gold a day”, abundance |
| **The rule (foot rule)** | Placed with the dowry, measuring the bed | “Rule” and “measure” |
| **Scissors and mirror** | Dowry, apotropaic object | Averting evil, “a bright mirror hung high” |
| **Abacus** | Dowry | Able to run a household, good at managing money |
| **Bow and arrows** | The “shooting of arrows” rite (in parts of the north) | Driving off evil, protecting the house |
| **Measure, rule, steelyard, scissors, mirror** | Together called the “six attestations” or “five attestations” | The apotropaic set placed with the dowry |

## 2. Historical Origins

- **From the pre-Qin onwards**: ritual vessels and wedding objects coexisted (the *fan* and *si* baskets appear in the ritual classics).
- **Tang and Song**: the homophonic use of auspicious objects became systematic.
- **Ming and Qing**: the gazetteers and notebooks record in detail “lifting the veil with the wedding steelyard” and the “six attestations of the trousseau”.
- **Today**: Chinese-style weddings use the steelyard or a *ruyi* sceptre to lift the veil; the “six attestations” appear in wedding exhibitions and displays.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Preparing the objects**: the wedding steelyard, the *ruyi* sceptre and the like; the “six attestations” (measure, steelyard, rule, scissors, mirror and so on) are placed among the dowry.
2. **Display**: they are set out on the Heaven-and-Earth table or in the bridal chamber.
3. **Lifting the veil**: the groom lifts the veil with the wedding steelyard (or a *ruyi* sceptre).
4. **Rites using the objects**: the “shooting of arrows” (in some regions), “passing the measure”, “measuring the bed”.
5. **Keeping**: the objects are kept with the dowry.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the custom of the “shooting of arrows” (three arrows shot at the chair or the door).
- **Jiangnan**: the “six attestations” of the dowry are emphasised; the wedding steelyard is generally used to lift the veil.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: the dowry objects are normally “needle and thread, scissors, rule, mirror”.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples have different systems of symbolic objects at weddings (knives, bows and arrows, saddles), which must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: the wedding steelyard, the *ruyi* sceptre, the grain measure, the peck measure, the rule, scissors, the mirror, the abacus.
- **Dowry documents**: the trousseau list.
- **Related documents**: the marriage letter, the gift list.
