# Wedding Shoes (Caitang Xie)

## 1. Definition

Wedding shoes are **the embroidered shoes worn by the bride**, also called “embroidered shoes”, “hall-treading shoes” or “chair-mounting shoes”. Their rules of making combine practical use, auspiciousness and the symbolism of “averting the baleful”.

**Principal rules**:
- **Red shoes**: scarlet embroidered shoes, embroidered with peony, mandarin ducks and double happiness.
- **“Chair-mounting shoes”** — worn when mounting the chair, changed at the groom’s gate for the “hall-treading shoes” (or the reverse).
- **“Objects hidden in the shoes”** — dates and chestnuts or coins placed inside, in the sense of “an early standing son” and “wealth and honour”.
- **“Hall-treading shoes”** — worn at the ceremony (the bowing in the hall); in some regions never worn again after the wedding, but kept as a memento.
- **“The shoes do not touch the ground”** — in some regions the bride’s feet must not touch the earth when mounting or alighting, red felt being trodden or the bride carried on someone’s back.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Tang and Song**: the custom of embroidered shoes at weddings appears in the records; “silk socks” and “embroidered shoes” in poetry are often connected with courtship and marriage.
- **Ming and Qing**: the gazetteers and notebooks record the “chair-mounting shoes”, the “hall-treading shoes” and the placing of objects inside them.
- **Modern times**: embroidered shoes gave way to leather shoes and high heels, but the insistence on red and on embroidery continued.
- **Today**: Chinese-style weddings use embroidered shoes or red high heels; “hunting for the shoe” (the groom seeking the bride’s wedding shoe) has become a favourite stage of contemporary weddings.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Making the shoes**: the bride’s family prepare the embroidered shoes (or the groom’s family sends the “chair-mounting shoes”).
2. **Mounting the chair**: the chair-mounting shoes are worn, the feet not touching the ground (red felt trodden, or the bride carried on her brother’s back).
3. **Changing the shoes**: at the groom’s house the hall-treading shoes are put on, and the bowing in the hall performed.
4. **Hiding the shoes**: in contemporary weddings the hiding and seeking of the shoes is a playful stage.
5. **Keeping**: the wedding shoes are kept as a memento.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the distinction between chair-mounting shoes and hall-treading shoes is comparatively clear.
- **Jiangnan**: the embroidery is fine, the patterns normally peony, mandarin ducks and double happiness.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: “a rice sieve over the face” and “a black umbrella over the head” are used alongside the shoe customs.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples have particular shoe customs at weddings (embroidered shoes, clogs), with various meanings; these must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Wedding shoes**: red embroidered shoes, chair-mounting shoes, hall-treading shoes.
- **Companion objects**: red felt, rice sieve, black umbrella, wedding fruit.
- **Related documents**: the trousseau list (setting out the embroidered shoes and other items of the dowry), the marriage letter.
