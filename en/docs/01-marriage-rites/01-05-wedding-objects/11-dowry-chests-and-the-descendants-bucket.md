# Dowry Chests and the Descendants Bucket

## 1. Definition

The **carrying vessels** of the dowry are chiefly chests and panniers, together with a number of objects of symbolic meaning.

| Object | Description | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Chest (camphor-wood chest)** | Holding clothing and bedding | Camphor resists insects; precious and practical |
| **Descendants bucket (chamber pot)** | A red-lacquered wooden pail, holding dates, chestnuts, red eggs and toilet articles | “Many sons and grandsons”; *tong* “pail” sounds like *tong* “in succession” |
| **Red foot-basin (bath pail / foot basin)** | An indispensable pail of the dowry | Abundance of life |
| **Jewellery box, dressing table** | The bridal furnishings | Adornment complete |
| **The “cornucopia”** | A display piece of the dowry | Wealth gathering |

The “**descendants bucket**” is the most symbolically charged item of the dowry: red eggs, dates, chestnuts and peanuts are placed inside, and a child of the groom’s family is asked to “carry the bucket” (to draw good fortune), the carrying fee belonging to the child — hence “**carrying the descendants bucket**”.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **From the pre-Qin onwards**: the system of the dowry (the accompanying concubines, the bridal furnishings) appears in the ritual classics and histories.
- **Tang and Song**: the sending of the bridal furnishings was at its height, and the notebooks record the display of “the furnished chamber”.
- **Ming and Qing**: the customs of the camphor-wood chest and the descendants bucket settled, and the gazetteers record them commonly.
- **Modern times**: chests gave way to leather cases and wardrobes, and the descendants bucket disappeared from the cities.
- **Today**: dowries are chiefly electrical appliances, furniture and jewellery; the “descendants bucket” survives in wedding displays.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Preparing the trousseau**: the bride’s family prepare the chests, pails, clothing and jewellery.
2. **Sending the dowry**: it is sent to the groom’s house the day before or on the wedding day, and checked off item by item against the list.
3. **Furnishing the chamber**: the dowry is displayed in the bridal chamber.
4. **Carrying the descendants bucket**: a child of the groom’s family carries the bucket to draw good fortune and receives the “carrying fee”.
5. **Keeping**: the objects pass into the new household for use.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: camphor-wood chests in pairs; the dowry insists on “four laid and four covered”.
- **Jiangnan**: fine bridal furnishings, with the custom of “bottoming the chest” (silver placed at the bottom of the chest).
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: the dowry is called “the bridal furnishings”, and the descendants bucket is common.
- **Guangdong**: the dowry includes the “dragon-and-phoenix quilt”, the “descendants bucket” and the “cornucopia”.
- **Ethnic minorities**: dowries take different forms (livestock, silver ornaments, textiles), not belonging to the Han system of bridal furnishings; these must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: camphor-wood chest, descendants bucket, red foot-basin, jewellery box, dressing table, cornucopia.
- **Related documents**: the trousseau list (dowry list), the gift list, the marriage letter.
