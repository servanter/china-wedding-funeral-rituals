# Auspicious Motifs: Dragon and Phoenix, Mandarin Ducks, Pomegranate, Double Happiness

## 1. Definition

Auspicious motifs are **a language system that expresses blessing through images**, appearing widely on wedding dress, quilts and pillows, chair curtains, dowry objects, cakes, window flowers and vessels.

| Motif | Meaning | Where it appears |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Dragon and phoenix** | “Dragons and phoenixes bring prosperity”; the matching of man and woman | Wedding dress, wedding candles, chair curtains |
| **Mandarin ducks** | Conjugal harmony, never apart | Quilts and pillows, embroidery |
| **Pomegranate / grape** | Many sons | Quilt covers, paper cuts, cakes |
| **Double happiness (囍)** | Double joy at the door | Doors and windows, objects, wedding cards |
| **Peony** | Wealth and honour | Wedding dress, embroidery |
| **Twin lotus on one stem** | One heart | Embroidery, paper cuts |
| **Fish** | “Surplus year after year”, also homophonous with “abundance” | Vessels, cake moulds |
| **Bat / deer** | “Blessing” and “emolument” | Dowry objects, wood carving |
| **Magpie on the plum** | “Joy reaching the eyebrows” | Window flowers, embroidery |
| **Gourd and butterfly** | “Vines of gourds unending”, flourishing descendants | Embroidery, paper cuts |

**Principle of combination**: chiefly **homophony** (fish for surplus, bat for blessing, deer for emolument) and **symbol** (pomegranate for many sons, mandarin ducks for harmony), forming a system in which “every image has a meaning, and every meaning is auspicious”.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Han and Tang**: auspicious motifs already took shape in brocade and bronze mirrors (the mirror “long fit for sons and grandsons”).
- **Song and Yuan**: flower-and-bird motifs and the system of homophonic meaning matured.
- **Ming and Qing**: wedding motifs settled and were widely used on dress and objects; “every image must have a meaning” became the rule.
- **Today**: traditional motifs continue in Chinese-style weddings, in cultural products and in intangible-heritage crafts.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Choosing the motif**: the motif is chosen by use (dragon and phoenix for dress, mandarin ducks and pomegranate for quilt covers).
2. **Making**: embroidery, brocade, paper-cutting and printing according to the craft.
3. **Display**: arranged on the principles of pairing and symmetry.
4. **Giving**: given to the couple as dowry or congratulatory gift.
5. **Transmission**: the motifs are handed down by craftsmen, embroiderers and paper-cut artists from generation to generation.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: paper-cut window flowers mostly “magpie on the plum” and “the pomegranate opening a hundred seeds”.
- **Jiangnan**: embroidery (Su and Gu schools) with fine motifs.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: “the eight immortals banner” and “dragon and phoenix” are used together, with fine embroidery.
- **Guangdong**: the *qun gua* is embroidered in gold thread with dragons and phoenixes, auspicious clouds and bats.
- **Ethnic minorities**: each people’s motifs form their own system (as with the Miao “butterfly mother” and geometric dragon-and-phoenix patterns in silverwork and embroidery); not of the same origin as Han wedding motifs, and to be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Where the motifs appear**: wedding dress, quilts and pillows, chair curtains, dowry objects, window flowers, wedding cards, cake moulds.
- **Related documents**: pattern books (embroidery patterns, paper-cut designs), the decoration of marriage letters.
- **Collection catalogues**: textiles and paper cuts in museum collections.
