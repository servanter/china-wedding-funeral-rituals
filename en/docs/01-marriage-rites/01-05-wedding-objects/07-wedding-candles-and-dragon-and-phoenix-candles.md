# Wedding Candles and Dragon-and-Phoenix Candles

## 1. Definition

Wedding candles, also “auspicious candles” or “dragon-and-phoenix candles”, are **the paired candles lit at the Heaven-and-Earth table and in the bridal chamber**, painted or carved with dragons and phoenixes, double happiness and peony. The phrase “the night of wedding candles in the bridal chamber” comes from them.

**Points**:
- **In pairs**: one dragon candle and one phoenix candle, figuring husband and wife; they must be lit together and put out together.
- **Rites of lighting**: the “Heaven-and-Earth candles” are lit at the bowing in the hall, the “bridal-chamber candles” on entering the chamber.
- **Taboos**: they must not go out midway (inauspicious — “a broken candle” is to be avoided); a single candle is avoided; white candles are avoided.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Northern and Southern Dynasties**: the wedding rites already used “candles” (the *Shishuo xinyu* records a wedding scene).
- **Tang and Song**: “wedding candles” became a common term of the wedding rites (in Tang poetry, “bridal-chamber candles”).
- **Ming and Qing**: the dragon-and-phoenix candle settled in form, and the gazetteers commonly record the customs of “lighting the wedding candles” and “keeping the candles”.
- **Today**: Chinese-style weddings light dragon-and-phoenix candles; Western-style weddings light a “unity candle”, with a similar function.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Preparing the candles**: a pair of dragon-and-phoenix candles is bought.
2. **Lighting**: the Heaven-and-Earth candles are lit at the bowing in the hall, and the bridal-chamber candles in the chamber.
3. **Keeping the candles**: they must burn out or stay alight all night (“keeping the auspicious candles”).
4. **Putting them out**: blowing them out is avoided; they are usually extinguished with a cover or pinched out with the fingers (to avoid “blowing away” the blessing).
5. **Keeping the ends**: in some regions the stubs are kept as mementos.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **North China**: the dragon-and-phoenix candles are used together with an “ever-burning lamp”, insisting that they not go out all night.
- **Jiangnan**: the wedding candles come with the customs of “keeping the candles” and “looking at the bride”.
- **Fujian and Taiwan**: dragon-and-phoenix candles are lit at the bowing, together with “incense and fire” for continuity.
- **Ethnic minorities**: some peoples have particular fire and lamp customs at weddings (the hearth, pine torches), whose meanings differ from the Han wedding candles; these must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Wedding candles**: dragon-and-phoenix candles, double-happiness candles, candlesticks.
- **Companion objects**: the Heaven-and-Earth table, incense burner, ever-burning lamp.
- **Related documents**: the marriage letter, the gift list.
