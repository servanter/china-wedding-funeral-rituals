# Beijing Marriage Customs

## 1. Definition

Beijing marriage custom fuses **the ritual order of the capital, the customs of the banner people and the manners of the market-place**; it insists on “propriety” and “display”, and its stages are clearly ordered. Its principal features:

1. **Two grades of “placing the betrothal”** — the “minor betrothal” (the preliminary engagement, with rings and jewellery) and the “major betrothal” (the formal engagement, with the “dragon-and-phoenix card”, jewellery and clothing).
2. **“Communication” and “the presentation”** — the groom’s family sends the “communication gift” (fixing the wedding date) and the “presentation” (the betrothal gift, in four or eight poles).
3. **“Sounding the house” and “showing the chair”** — on the day before the wedding the band plays to “sound the house” and the bridal chair is displayed to “show the chair”.
4. **“Reception — bowing in the hall — sitting in the curtain — roughhousing”** — the bride is fetched by bridal chair, the three bows are performed, and the couple “sits in the curtain” in the bridal chamber while kin “roughhouse”.
5. **“Ranking by generation”** — on the day after or the third day after the wedding the new wife greets her husband’s elders, her forms of address and status being established.
6. **“Return visit” and “meeting the kin”** — the third-day return, at which the kin of both sides meet.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Yuan and Ming**: the wedding ceremonies of Dadu and Beijing followed the *Collected Statutes of the Ming*, absorbing the customs of migrants from elsewhere.
- **Qing**: banner and Han customs fused to form a “Beijing school” of wedding custom, and specialised trades flourished — bridal-chair shops, awning shops and the like.
- **Republican period**: old and new coexisted; the “civilised marriage” ran alongside the old-style wedding.
- **Today**: city weddings are chiefly hotel banquets and wedding photography; the old rites are partly revived in the suburbs and in Chinese-style weddings.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. **Negotiation**: the matchmaker proposes, and “matching the marriage” examines the eight characters and the zodiac animals.
2. **Minor betrothal**: the groom’s family sends rings and jewellery (an intention to engage).
3. **Major betrothal**: the formal engagement, with the exchange of “dragon-and-phoenix cards”.
4. **Communication and presentation**: the wedding date is fixed and the betrothal gift sent.
5. **Reception**: bridal chair, drums and music; “sounding the house” with the band.
6. **Bowing and sitting in the curtain**: the three bows, then the bridal chamber, sitting in the curtain and roughhousing.
7. **Ranking by generation and the return visit**: greeting the elders, the third-day return and the meeting of kin.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **The old city**: many rules, insisting on “propriety” and “display”.
- **The inner suburbs (Chaoyang, Haidian)**: the old customs simplified, mostly following village usage.
- **The outer suburbs (Mentougou, Fangshan)**: old customs such as “the reception band” survive.
- **Manchu and Hui**: Beijing has other peoples’ marriage customs, not belonging to the Han system; these must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: bridal chair, gongs, drums and *suona*, dragon-and-phoenix candles, veil, wedding steelyard, dowry chests.
- **Documents**: dragon-and-phoenix card (marriage letter), communication card, gift list, invitations.
- **Trade objects**: the wares of bridal-chair shops, awning shops and wedding-garment shops.
