# Guizhou Marriage Customs

## 1. Definition

The Han Chinese of Guizhou are mostly **descendants of the early-Ming garrison migrants (the *tunpu* people) and of Ming-Qing migrants**; because of their enclosed environment their marriage custom **preserves much of the ancient rite**, and blends with the local peoples’ customs. Its principal features:

1. **“The ancient *tunpu* custom”** — around Anshun the *tunpu* people (descendants of early-Ming Jiangnan migrants) keep archaic wedding custom, the rite close to the surviving Jiangnan usage of the Ming.
2. **“Proposal — betrothal — sending the gift — reception”** — a clear vestige of the “Six Rites”.
3. **“Bridal lament”** — girls lament before marriage.
4. **“Bowing in the hall, sitting in the curtain, roughhousing”** — the sequence is complete.
5. **“Return visit”** — the third-day homecoming.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Early Ming**: Zhu Yuanzhang’s “transferring the north to campaign in the south” stationed Jiangnan troops and people in Guizhou, forming the *tunpu* settlements and their distinctive folkways.
- **Ming and Qing**: migrants continued to arrive; the gazetteers (the Guizhou general gazetteer and the prefectural and county gazetteers) record wedding ceremonies.
- **Today**: city weddings predominate; the old *tunpu* customs survive in the villages (partly as cultural-tourism content).

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. Proposal and matching the marriage.
2. Betrothal (exchange of cards).
3. Sending the gift (the betrothal gift, the gift list).
4. Bridal lament (before the wedding).
5. Reception: bridal chair and band; bowing in the hall, sitting in the curtain, roughhousing.
6. Return visit.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Central Guizhou (Anshun, Guiyang)**: a concentration of *tunpu* custom, with much ancient rite preserved.
- **Northern Guizhou (Zunyi)**: close to Chongqing and Sichuan, with a Sichuan-Guizhou colouring.
- **Eastern Guizhou (Tongren)**: close to Hunan, with similar customs.
- **Southern and south-western Guizhou**: the Han live among the Bouyei, Miao and Dong, with mutual permeation of custom.
- **The various peoples**: the Miao, Bouyei, Dong, Sui and Yi marriage customs form their own systems and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: bridal chair, dragon-and-phoenix candles, veil, dowry chests.
- **Documents**: the horoscope card, gift list, marriage letter, genealogy.
