# Taiwan Marriage Customs

## 1. Definition

The Han people of Taiwan are mainly **descendants of Minnan (Zhangzhou-Quanzhou) and Hakka migrants**; their marriage custom comes down from the Fujian and Guangdong rites and is preserved very completely — it may be called **a living specimen of the traditional Han Chinese wedding**. Its principal features:

1. **“Discussion — sending the betrothal — completing the betrothal — reception”** — the Minnan “Six Rites” system; “sending the betrothal” (the minor betrothal), “completing the betrothal” (the great engagement).
2. **“Eating the sisters’ table”** — before leaving, the bride shares a parting meal with her sisters; a local speciality.
3. **“Covering with the rice sieve” and “splashing water”** — the bride covers her head with a rice sieve on leaving; after she mounts the chair her family “splashes water” (a daughter married out is like water splashed out).
4. **“Crossing the fire” and “treading the tiles”** — on entering she steps over the brazier and crushes tiles.
5. **“Bowing in the hall” and “taking tea”** — after bowing, the bride serves tea to the elders and receives gifts (“taking tea”).
6. **“Returning to the maternal home (*guining*, *zuoke*)”** — the homecoming visit after the wedding.
7. **“Roughhousing”** — the chamber is roughhoused on the wedding night (now mostly limited to “teasing the newly-weds”).

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Ming and Qing**: Fujian and Guangdong migrants crossed to Taiwan, bringing their native custom; the Qing gazetteers (the Taiwan prefectural gazetteer and the county gazetteers) record wedding ceremonies.
- **Japanese occupation**: the custom was suppressed and reformed, yet the people kept it up of themselves.
- **After 1945**: the custom continued, gradually moving to a form where “civil/church wedding + Chinese banquet” run side by side.
- **Today**: wedding photography and hotel banquets are widespread, but the “sending the betrothal”, “bowing in the hall”, “taking tea” and “returning to the maternal home” still survive among the people.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. Discussion (proposal, matching the marriage).
2. Sending the betrothal (minor betrothal): rings and jewellery are sent.
3. Completing the betrothal (great engagement): the betrothal gift and wedding cakes are sent, and the wedding date fixed.
4. Reception: bridal chair or wedding car; covering with the rice sieve, splashing water, crossing the fire, treading the tiles.
5. Bowing in the hall and taking tea: the rites, serving tea and receiving gifts.
6. Returning to the maternal home (*guining*, *zuoke*): the homecoming.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **The south (Tainan, Kaohsiung, Pingtung)**: the most completely preserved custom.
- **The centre (Changhua, Taichung)**: both Zhangzhou-Quanzhou and Hakka custom.
- **The north (Taipei, Keelung)**: urbanised earlier, with simplified ceremony.
- **The Hakka (Taoyuan, Hsinchu, Miaoli, Kaohsiung Meinong)**: strong lineage colouring, with stress on ancestor worship.
- **The various peoples**: the marriage customs of Taiwan’s indigenous peoples each form their own systems and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: bridal chair or wedding car, rice sieve, brazier, tiles, dragon-and-phoenix candles, wedding cakes, gold ornaments.
- **Documents**: the horoscope card, gift list, marriage letter, ancestral tablet (for the post-wedding report of sacrifice).
