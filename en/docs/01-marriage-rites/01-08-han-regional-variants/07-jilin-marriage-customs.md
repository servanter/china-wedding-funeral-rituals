# Jilin Marriage Customs

## 1. Definition

The Han Chinese of Jilin are chiefly **descendants of migrants who “crossed the passes”**; their marriage custom belongs to the **north-eastern type**, essentially the same as that of Liaoning and Heilongjiang. Its principal features:

1. **“Proposal — exchange of cards — presentation — reception”** — the sequence follows Shandong and Hebei.
2. **The “mother-parting meat” and the urging gift** — the material customs complete.
3. **“Building the awning and laying the tables”** — an awning is put up in the courtyard, the banquet large.
4. **“Pressing the bed”, “sitting in the curtain”, roughhousing** — the full sequence.
5. **“Return visit”** — the third-day return.
6. **Korean-Han cohabitation** — some Han customs were influenced by Korean usage in dress and food.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Qing**: migrants from Shandong and Hebei came in from the middle period; with the opening of waste land at the end of the Qing the Han population grew sharply.
- **Republican period**: with agricultural settlement the villages settled into form, and marriage custom converged with that of Liaoning and Heilongjiang.
- **Today**: city weddings are chiefly hotel banquets; the countryside keeps the old rites.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. Proposal and matching the marriage.
2. Exchange of cards and betrothal.
3. The presentation (betrothal gift, mother-parting meat).
4. Urging the trousseau (clothing sent before the wedding).
5. Reception (bridal chair or cart); bowing in the hall, sitting in the curtain, roughhousing.
6. Return visit (third day).

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **The centre (Changchun, Jilin)**: city marriage custom modernised comparatively early.
- **The east (Yanbian, Tonghua)**: Korean and Han live together, and Han custom was influenced by Korean usage.
- **The west (Baicheng, Songyuan)**: close to Inner Mongolia, with traces of Mongol-Han fusion.
- **The various peoples**: the Korean, Manchu, Mongolian and Hui customs form their own systems and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: bridal chair or cart, dragon-and-phoenix candles, veil, mother-parting meat, dowry chests.
- **Documents**: horoscope card, gift list, invitations, marriage letter.
