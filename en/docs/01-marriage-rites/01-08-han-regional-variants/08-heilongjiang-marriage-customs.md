# Heilongjiang Marriage Customs

## 1. Definition

The Han Chinese of Heilongjiang are chiefly **migrants of the late Qing and early Republican period** (from Shandong, Hebei and Liaoning); their marriage custom is of the **north-eastern type**, tending to simplicity because of the climate and the conditions of the reclaimed lands. Its principal features:

1. **“Proposal — exchange of cards — presentation — reception”** — following the Shandong-Hebei sequence.
2. **“Building the awning and laying the tables”** — mostly an awning in the courtyard; in winter the tables are laid indoors.
3. **The “mother-parting meat”** — a material custom.
4. **“Sitting in the curtain” and roughhousing** — the full sequence.
5. **“Return visit”** — the third-day return.
6. **The character of the reclaimed and forest regions** — the weddings of state farm and forestry workers tended to be plain and collective (the work unit holding the celebration).

## 2. Historical Origins

- **The opening of waste land in the late Qing**: migrants poured in (“crossing the passes”), bringing the marriage customs of their Shandong and Hebei homes.
- **Republican period**: towns along the Chinese Eastern Railway rose, and city marriage custom grew simpler.
- **Today**: hotel weddings predominate; the farming and forestry regions keep the tradition of collective celebrations.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. Proposal and matching the marriage.
2. Exchange of cards and betrothal.
3. The presentation (betrothal gift, mother-parting meat).
4. Preparing the tables and the awning.
5. Reception; bowing in the hall, sitting in the curtain, roughhousing.
6. Return visit.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Harbin, Qiqihar**: city marriage custom modernised, the ceremonies simplified.
- **The Songnen plain reclamation area**: farm workers’ weddings collective.
- **The Greater and Lesser Khingan forest regions**: forest marriage custom, the ceremonies simplified.
- **The various peoples**: the Manchu, Korean, Mongolian, Daur, Oroqen and Hezhen customs each have their own systems and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: bridal chair or cart, dragon-and-phoenix candles, veil, mother-parting meat, dowry chests.
- **Documents**: horoscope card, gift list, invitations, marriage letter.
