# Hong Kong and Macau Marriage Customs

## 1. Definition

The residents of Hong Kong and Macau are mainly **Cantonese (Cantonese speakers)**; their marriage custom inherits the **Cantonese tradition**, yet because of modern urbanisation and the meeting of China and the West it has formed a “**urban traditional wedding**”. Its principal features:

1. **“The great presentation (*guodali*)”** — the betrothal gift is sent (wedding cakes, seafood, the three sacrificial animals, gold ornaments), itemised on the gift list.
2. **“Coming of age (*shangtou*)”** — before the wedding the couple’s hair is combed and faces threaded; an essential stage.
3. **“The great aunt (*dajin jie*)”** — the woman who accompanies and assists at the wedding (also performing the role of master of ceremony).
4. **“Reception — serving tea”** — fetching the bride, entering the house, serving tea to both sets of parents and elders (“pouring tea”).
5. **“Return visit”** — the homecoming three days after the wedding, with a roast pig and other gifts.
6. **Chinese and Western side by side** — civil/church weddings and the Chinese banquet run side by side; in Macau Catholic weddings and the traditional Chinese wedding coexist.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Ming and Qing**: Hong Kong and Macau belonged to Guangzhou prefecture and Xiangshan county; the custom was identical to the Cantonese.
- **After the ports opened**: under British Hong Kong and Portuguese Macau, Western weddings were introduced (civil registration, church weddings).
- **20th century**: the Chinese banquet was held in restaurants and banquet-houses; the customs of “coming of age” and “the great presentation” continued.
- **Today**: civil marriage, church weddings, hotel banquets and Chinese ceremony combine; the traditional observances are presided over by the “great aunt”.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. Discussion (proposal, matching the eight characters).
2. The great presentation (civil betrothal): the betrothal gift and wedding cakes are sent.
3. Coming of age: on an auspicious day before the wedding the couple’s hair is combed and faces threaded.
4. Reception: fetching the bride, playing “the door-opening *lai see*”, serving tea.
5. The wedding banquet: held in a hotel or restaurant.
6. Return visit: the homecoming three days later.

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Urban Hong Kong**: a Chinese-Western blend, the ceremony presided over by an etiquette company.
- **The New Territories (Yuen Long, Tai Po, Sha Tin)**: the indigenous inhabitants (the walled-village and Hakka people) keep lineage wedding custom and ancestor worship.
- **The outlying islands (Cheung Chau, Lamma)**: island customs, with a simpler ceremony.
- **Macau**: Chinese weddings and Catholic weddings run side by side; the custom is close to the Cantonese.
- **The various peoples**: Hong Kong and Macau also have the marriage customs of South-Asian and Filipino groups, not belonging to the Han system; they must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: wedding cakes, gold ornaments, the dragon-and-phoenix gown, the implements of coming of age (comb, red cord), the roast pig, the wedding card.
- **Documents**: the horoscope card, the gift list (the great-presentation list), the marriage letter, the marriage certificate (registration system).
