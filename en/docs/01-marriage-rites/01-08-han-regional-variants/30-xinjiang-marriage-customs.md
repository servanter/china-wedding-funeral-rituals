# Xinjiang Marriage Customs

## 1. Definition

The Han Chinese of Xinjiang are mostly **descendants of the Qing-dynasty garrison-reclamation migrants, Republican-era migrants and the 1949-after border-support migrants**; their native places cover every province, so the marriage custom shows a “**mixed array of provincial usages, seeking simplicity**” face, with no unified local form. Its principal features:

1. **The influence of native place** — in a single village one may see the wedding practices of Henan, Shandong, Gansu, Sichuan and others differing by native place.
2. **Simplicity and pragmatism** — constrained by transport and personnel, the ceremony is reduced.
3. **Unit- and corps-organised weddings** — the corps, factories and mines organise group weddings or assist with the celebration; the wedding is collectivised.
4. **Multi-ethnic coexistence** — the customs of the Uyghur, Kazakh and Hui run in parallel over the long term without mixing.
5. **“Weddings held in two places”** — because kin are scattered, the banquets are often held separately in two places.

## 2. Historical Origins

- **Qing dynasty**: after the Qianlong reign the frontier was opened by garrison farming; Shaanxi-Gansu migrants entered Xinjiang.
- **Republican era**: war and migration increased the Han population.
- **After 1949**: border-support and garrison-reclamation settlement, the corps system took shape, and group weddings were once in vogue.
- **Today**: hotel weddings and travel weddings go side by side; the old rites survive only in simplified links.

## 3. Ceremonial Procedure

1. Marriage negotiation (mostly through introductions by kin and friends).
2. Betrothal (a meeting gift, bride price, varying by native place).
3. Choosing the day (mostly a holiday).
4. The wedding: held in a hotel or the unit auditorium, with a banquet.
5. Return visit (if the kin are in the same place).
6. Post-wedding exchanges (simplified).

## 4. Regional and Ethnic Variants

- **Northern Xinjiang (Urumqi, Changji, Ili)**: diverse migrant native places, mixed customs.
- **Southern Xinjiang (Kashgar, Aksu)**: fewer Han people, mostly relying on the unit and fellow-provincial organisations.
- **The corps reclamation areas**: weddings organised by the regiment farm and company, the ceremony plain and uniform.
- **The various peoples**: the Uyghur (reciting the “Nikah”, the ahong officiates), Kazakh and Hui marriage customs form their own systems and must be recorded separately.

## 5. Objects and Documents

- **Objects**: the bridal chair (rare), dragon-and-phoenix candles, the wedding gown and Chinese dress, the banquetware.
- **Documents**: the horoscope card (rare), gift list, invitations, the marriage letter (the marriage certificate).
